# Choosing a tech career path: which roadmap is right for you?

"Learn tech" is too big a goal. Nobody gets hired for "tech". People get hired as a **web developer**, a **data analyst**, an **IT support technician**, a **security analyst** or a **mobile app developer**. Each of these jobs needs a different set of skills, in a different order.

This subject turns the free lessons on the Marzley Tech learning hub into **roadmaps**: ordered lists of steps that take you from zero to job-ready (or client-ready, if you plan to freelance). Every step links to the lessons to take and says what to build to prove you can do it.

The method behind every roadmap is the same:

> **LEARN → PRACTISE → BUILD → SHOW → WORK → EARN**

1. **Learn** a skill from the lessons.
2. **Practise** it with the exercises and quizzes until it feels normal.
3. **Build** a real project with it (see the *Build real projects* subject).
4. **Show** the project: GitHub, a live link, a short write-up, your CV.
5. **Work**: apply for jobs, internships, attachments or freelance gigs.
6. **Earn**, then keep learning the next step on the roadmap.

:::warning Honest expectations
- A roadmap shows *what* to learn. It does not make the learning fast. Most people need **6–18 months** of steady practice (1–3 hours a day) to become employable in a technical role. Some do it faster, many take longer.
- No course, certificate or roadmap guarantees a job. Employers hire people who can **show** they can do the work. That is why every roadmap ends with projects and a portfolio.
- Pay varies widely by company, city, experience and whether you work for local or international clients. Look at current adverts on BrighterMonday, LinkedIn, MyJobMag and company career pages to see real numbers for your area. Don't trust anyone who promises a fixed salary after a short course.
:::

## Questions to help you choose

Answer honestly and score each roadmap from 1 to 5:

| Question | Web | Data | Security | Mobile | IT support |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do I enjoy making things people can see and click? | 5 | 2 | 1 | 5 | 2 |
| Do I enjoy numbers, tables and spotting trends? | 2 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| Do I like figuring out how things break? | 3 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Do I like helping people face to face? | 2 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| Do I want to freelance for local businesses soon? | 5 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Do I only have a phone right now? | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 |

The table is a starting point, not a test. The best predictor of success is **which subject you still want to practise on a boring Tuesday night**.

## Skills every roadmap needs

These are the foundations. Do them first, whichever path you pick:

1. **Computer basics and typing**: [what a computer is](./?track=it-basics&lesson=what-is-a-computer), [files and folders](./?track=it-basics&lesson=files-windows), [keyboard shortcuts and typing](./?track=it-basics&lesson=keyboard-typing-shortcuts). Aim for 35+ words per minute.
2. **Online safety**: [passwords and 2FA](./?track=digital-literacy&lesson=passwords-2fa) and [scams](./?track=digital-literacy&lesson=scams). Employers trust people who don't get phished.
3. **Professional email and cloud tools**: [email](./?track=google-workspace&lesson=email) and [Google Drive](./?track=google-workspace&lesson=drive-collaboration).
4. **A CV and cover letter**: [CVs and letters in Word](./?track=ms-word&lesson=cv-letters).
5. **Git and GitHub** (for every technical path): [what Git is](./?track=git&lesson=what-is-git) and [GitHub](./?track=git&lesson=github). Your GitHub profile becomes your portfolio.
6. **Using AI tools well**: [prompting](./?track=ai-tools&lesson=prompting). AI speeds up learning, but you must still understand what it gives you.

## How to study so it actually sticks

- **Little and often beats long weekends.** One to two focused hours every day is better than eight hours on Saturday.
- **Type the code yourself.** Copying and pasting feels fast and teaches almost nothing.
- **Do every exercise and quiz** in the lessons. If you get a quiz question wrong, reread that section.
- **Build something after every 4–6 lessons**, even if it is small.
- **Keep a learning log** (a Google Doc or a GitHub README): date, what you learned, what confused you. This is also great material for interviews.
- **Join a community**: a campus tech club, Ajira Digital centres, Google Developer Groups, local meetups or online communities. Ask good questions: say what you tried and what happened.

| Day | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Mon–Thu | 1.5 h | One lesson + its exercise + quiz |

## Jobs, attachments and freelancing

Every roadmap ends the same way:

- **Attachment / internship**: if you are a student, apply early. Your projects make your application stand out from classmates who only have grades.
- **Entry-level jobs**: apply with a one-page CV that links to **2–4 projects**. Tailor the CV to each advert's keywords.
- **Freelancing**: start with people you can reach (family businesses, churches, schools, chamas), deliver well, ask for a testimonial. Follow the step-by-step [Freelancing from Kenya](./?track=freelancing&lesson=freelancing-roadmap) subject.

:::warning Avoid "pay to get a job" offers
Legitimate employers never ask you to pay for an interview, a "placement fee" or "training before starting". Fake job adverts targeting tech graduates are common. Verify the company, never send money, and never share OTPs or M-Pesa PINs.
:::

## Summary

- Pick a **specific** role, not "tech".
- Every path follows **Learn → Practise → Build → Show → Work → Earn**.
- Do the shared foundations first, then follow one roadmap step by step.
- Build projects and publish them; that is what gets you hired.
- Expect months, not weeks, and be wary of anyone promising guaranteed jobs or salaries.
