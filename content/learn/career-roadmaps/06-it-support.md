# IT support and networking roadmap: helpdesk, technician and network jobs

Every organisation with computers needs someone to keep them running: setting up laptops, fixing printers, resetting passwords, connecting the office Wi-Fi, managing email accounts, backing up data and removing viruses. IT support is one of the **fastest ways into a tech career** in Kenya, and it's a strong base for networking, system administration, cloud and cybersecurity later.

You'll find these jobs in schools and universities, hospitals, SACCOs and banks, county offices, NGOs, hotels, cyber cafés, computer shops, ISPs (internet providers) and managed IT companies. There's also self-employment: repairs, installing networks and CCTV for small businesses, and maintenance contracts.

## Who this suits

- You like solving practical problems and working with your hands.
- You're patient with people who are stressed because "the computer isn't working".
- You can explain technical things simply.

**Checkpoint:** you can replace a hard disk with an SSD, install Windows from a USB stick, install drivers and set up a printer, without help.

## Stage 3: Office software and accounts (2–3 weeks)

Users will ask you for help with these every day:
- [Word](./?track=ms-word&lesson=formatting), [Excel basics](./?track=excel&lesson=basics), [PowerPoint](./?track=powerpoint&lesson=first-presentation)

- Admin skills to learn next: creating users in **Microsoft 365** or **Google Workspace** admin consoles, shared mailboxes, resetting passwords, turning on 2FA for staff.

## Stage 4: Networking (6–8 weeks)

Networking is where IT support pay and responsibility grow.
- [What a network is](./?track=networking&lesson=what-is-a-network), [devices and cables](./?track=networking&lesson=devices-cables) (including crimping RJ45 cables)
- [OSI and TCP/IP](./?track=networking&lesson=osi-tcpip), [IPv4 addresses](./?track=networking&lesson=ipv4-addresses), [subnetting step by step](./?track=networking&lesson=subnetting-step-by-step)
- [DNS and DHCP](./?track=networking&lesson=dns-dhcp), [NAT and routing](./?track=networking&lesson=nat-routing), [switching and VLANs](./?track=networking&lesson=switching-vlans)
- [Wi-Fi](./?track=networking&lesson=wifi): planning coverage, securing access points
- [Network commands](./?track=networking&lesson=network-commands) and [troubleshooting](./?track=networking&lesson=troubleshooting)
- [Firewalls and VPNs](./?track=networking&lesson=firewalls-vpns)
- Labs: [Packet Tracer first lab](./?track=networking&lesson=packet-tracer-first-lab) (Cisco Packet Tracer is free with a Cisco Networking Academy account)

**Checkpoint:** in Packet Tracer, you can build an office network with two VLANs (staff and guests), DHCP, a router and Wi-Fi, and troubleshoot it with `ping`, `ipconfig` and `tracert`.

## Stage 7: Soft skills and ticketing

Support is a people job:
- **Ticketing**: log every request (who, what, when, what you did). Free tools like osTicket or a shared spreadsheet work for small offices.
- **Documentation**: write short how-to guides ("How to connect to the office printer") so the same question doesn't come back every week.
- **Communication**: no jargon, confirm the problem is fixed, follow up.
- **Inventory**: keep a list of every device, serial number, user and warranty date (an [Excel table](./?track=excel&lesson=sort-filter-tables) is enough to start).

## Certifications

| Certification | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **CompTIA A+** | The classic IT support certificate: hardware, operating systems, troubleshooting |
| **Cisco CCNA** | Networking; respected by ISPs and corporates. Cisco Networking Academy courses run at many Kenyan colleges |
| **CompTIA Network+** | Vendor-neutral networking |
| **Microsoft 365 Fundamentals / Endpoint Administrator** | Managing Microsoft 365 users and devices |
| **Google IT Support Certificate** | Online (Coursera); a good beginner structure |

Not required to start, but they help you get past HR filters. Projects and practical skill still matter most in interviews, and many employers give a practical test.

## Your portfolio

IT support portfolios are different from developer portfolios. Show:
1. **A home lab write-up**: Packet Tracer network diagram with VLANs and DHCP (screenshots and the .pkt file on GitHub).
2. **Repair log**: before and after photos of machines you upgraded or fixed (with owners' permission), what was wrong and what you did.
3. **How-to guides** you wrote (PDFs or a simple website).
4. **A small business IT plan**: a realistic plan for a 10-person office (devices, network, backups, email, security, budget).
5. **A script**: a PowerShell or bash script that automates something (e.g. a backup).

## Getting work

- **Attachment** in an ICT department (schools, hospitals, county offices, companies) is the classic entry route for students.
- Entry titles: *IT support technician, helpdesk officer, ICT assistant, field technician, junior network technician, NOC technician* (ISPs).
- **Self-employment**: laptop repairs and upgrades, network installation for small offices, maintenance contracts with schools and businesses. Price per job, offer a monthly maintenance plan, and keep receipts and a client list.

## Where it leads

IT support → **network engineer** (CCNA, then CCNP) · **system administrator** (Windows Server, Linux) · **cloud** (AWS, Azure) · **cybersecurity** (see the [cybersecurity roadmap](./?track=career-roadmaps&lesson=cybersecurity-roadmap)).

## Summary

- IT basics → hardware → office and accounts → networking → security → Linux → soft skills.
- Practise on real machines and Packet Tracer; document everything you fix.
- CompTIA A+ and CCNA are the classic certificates; attachments are the classic entry route.
