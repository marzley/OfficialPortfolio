# Show your projects: portfolio, GitHub, CV, case studies and interviews

Building a project is half the job. The other half is making sure the right people **see it and understand it in under a minute**. Recruiters and clients are busy: they skim. This lesson turns your projects into job offers and clients.

## 1. Choose your best 3–5 projects

Quality beats quantity. Pick projects that:
- match the job you want (a data analyst shows analyses and dashboards, not a to-do app);
- are **finished** and **live** (or have a demo video);
- show different skills (front-end, database, payments, an API, a mobile app);
- solve a believable problem, ideally a Kenyan one (M-Pesa, schools, SACCOs, shops, bookings).

Move unfinished experiments to a "Learning" section or leave them unpinned on GitHub.

## 2. Write a case study for each project

On your portfolio site, each project gets a short page or section:

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | "A Thika salon lost customers to double bookings and missed calls." |
| **Solution** | "An online booking system where customers pick free times and pay a KSh 200 deposit with M-Pesa." |
| **My role** | "Solo project: design, PHP/MySQL back-end, M-Pesa integration, deployment." |
| **Tech** | PHP 8, MySQL, JavaScript, Daraja API, cPanel |
| **Key challenges** | "Preventing double bookings when two people book at once: solved with a transaction and row lock." |
| **Results** | Only real results: "Used by the salon for 2 months; 140 bookings made online." For a practice project: "Tested with 10 friends; fixed 6 usability issues." |
| **Links** | Live demo · Source code · 1-minute video |
| **Screenshots** | Phone and desktop views |

:::warning Never invent results or clients
"Increased sales by 300%" with no evidence, fake testimonials, or calling a practice project "client work" will be found out, and it ends opportunities. Practice projects are respected; just call them what they are.
:::

## 3. Make your GitHub profile work for you

- **Profile README**: create a repository with the same name as your username; its README shows on your profile. Include: one line about you, what you're focused on, your tech, and links to your portfolio and LinkedIn.
- **Pin** your 4–6 best repositories.
- Each repository: a clear name (`salon-booking-system`, not `project2-final-final`), a description, topics/tags, a README with screenshots and live link (template in [How to build projects](./?track=projects&lesson=how-to-build-projects)), and a licence.
- **Commit history**: small, meaningful commits over weeks look far better than one giant upload ([good commits](./?track=git&lesson=gitignore-good-commits)).
- **No secrets** in any repository, ever. Check old commits too.
- A green contribution graph is nice but not essential; quality matters more than daily streaks.

## 4. The one-page tech CV

Use [CVs and letters in Word](./?track=ms-word&lesson=cv-letters) for formatting. For a tech CV:

1. **Header**: name, town, phone, email, portfolio link, GitHub, LinkedIn.
2. **Summary** (2–3 lines): "Junior full-stack developer building PHP/MySQL and M-Pesa-integrated web apps. Looking for a junior developer or attachment role in Nairobi or remote."
3. **Skills** (grouped, only what you can use): *Languages:* JavaScript, PHP, SQL · *Tools:* Git, VS Code, cPanel · *Other:* Daraja API, responsive design.
4. **Projects** (before education if you have little work experience): 3–4 projects, each with one line of description, tech, and a link. Use action verbs and specifics: "Built an inventory system with stock movements and transactional sales; deployed on cPanel."
5. **Experience**: attachments, freelance work, IT roles, even non-tech jobs (customer service, sales) briefly, since they show reliability.
6. **Education and certificates**.

Tailor the CV to each advert: mirror its keywords (if it says "REST APIs" and you built one, use those words). Many companies use applicant tracking systems that filter by keywords. Save as PDF named `Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf`.

## 5. LinkedIn

- Headline: "Junior Web Developer | PHP, JavaScript, M-Pesa integrations" beats "Student at …".
- Featured section: your best 3 projects with links.
- Post when you finish a project: the problem, a screenshot or short video, one thing you learned. These posts get you noticed by recruiters and potential clients.
- Connect with developers, recruiters and tech communities in Kenya; comment thoughtfully.

## 6. Demo videos

A 60–90 second screen recording (free tools: OBS Studio, or the phone's screen recorder for apps) showing the main flow, with your voice explaining it, is powerful, especially when a project can't stay online (e.g. the M-Pesa sandbox or a free host that sleeps). Upload to YouTube as "unlisted" and link it in the README.

## 7. Talking about projects in interviews

Prepare to answer these for every project on your CV:

| Question | What they're checking |
|---|---|
| "Walk me through this project." | Can you explain clearly, from problem to solution? |
| "What was the hardest part?" | Real problem-solving experience |
| "Why did you choose PHP/Flutter/…?" | That you make decisions, not just copy tutorials |
| "How did you secure it?" | Passwords, SQL injection, secrets, authorisation |
| "What would you do differently?" | Self-awareness and growth |
| "Show me some code you're proud of." | That you understand your own code |

Use the **STAR** method for stories: **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, **R**esult. Practise aloud. Many companies also give a **practical test** or a take-home task; your projects are the best preparation for those.

## 8. Getting your first client with your portfolio

- Start with people who already trust you: family businesses, your church, school or chama, friends' shops.
- Use a finished project as proof: "I built a booking system for salons; here's a demo. I can set one up for you."
- Agree scope, price, deposit (e.g. 50%) and timeline in writing before starting.
- Deliver, ask for a testimonial and permission to show the work, then ask for referrals.

See [profile and proposals](./?track=make-money-online&lesson=profile-and-proposals), [pricing](./?track=make-money-online&lesson=pricing), [web design business](./?track=make-money-online&lesson=web-design-business) and [getting paid in Kenya](./?track=make-money-online&lesson=getting-paid-kenya).

## 9. Keep it updated

Once a month: add new projects, remove weak ones, check every link still works, update your CV and LinkedIn. A portfolio with broken links tells a recruiter you don't maintain your work.

## Final checklist

- 3–5 finished projects with live links or demo videos
- Each has a case study: problem, solution, role, tech, challenges, honest results
- GitHub profile README; best repositories pinned; READMEs with screenshots; no secrets
- One-page CV with projects, links and keywords from the advert, saved as PDF
- LinkedIn headline, featured projects, a post per project
- You can explain every project, including its security, in 2 minutes

## Summary

- Show your best few projects, finished and live, with honest case studies.
- Make GitHub, CV and LinkedIn point to the same strong projects.
- Prepare to explain and defend every project in interviews; practise with STAR.
- Use your portfolio to win first clients from people who already trust you.
