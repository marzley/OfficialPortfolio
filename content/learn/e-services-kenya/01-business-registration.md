# Registering a business online and getting it ready to trade

Registering your hustle as a business makes it easier to open a business bank account, get a Till number, apply for tenders and LPOs, work with corporate clients and build trust. In Kenya most steps are done online through **eCitizen**. Requirements and fees change, so always confirm on the official sites before paying.

> Only pay through the official eCitizen payment page. Never send "registration fees" to an individual's phone number, and beware of fake "agents" and websites.

## 1. Choose a business type

| Type | In short | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Business name** (sole proprietorship or partnership) | Registers the trading name; you and the business are legally the same | Small hustles, freelancers, shops |
| **Private limited company (Ltd)** | A separate legal entity; owners' personal assets are protected | Growing businesses, investors, tenders |
| **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** | Partners with limited liability | Professional firms |
| **Community group / CBO / chama** | Registered through the relevant government office | Self-help groups |

Get advice (e.g. from an accountant or a Huduma Centre) if you're unsure.

## 2. Register on eCitizen (Business Registration Service)

The general steps:

1. Log in at **ecitizen.go.ke** (create an account with your ID if you don't have one).
2. Go to the **Business Registration Service (BRS)**.
3. **Name search and reservation**: propose a few names; approval takes some time. Unique, simple names get approved faster.
4. Fill in the registration form: owners' details, business address, nature of business.
5. Upload the required documents (e.g. ID copies, passport photos, KRA PINs of owners).
6. **Pay** through eCitizen (M-Pesa is accepted).
7. Download your **certificate** once approved.

Companies also get a **CR12** (list of directors and shareholders), often requested by banks and clients.

## 3. KRA PIN and tax obligations

- The business (for companies) needs its own **KRA PIN**; sole proprietors use their personal PIN.
- Know which taxes apply: many small businesses fall under **Turnover Tax**; others under income tax; **VAT** registration is required above a turnover threshold.
- File returns on **iTax** on time, even NIL returns, to avoid penalties.

(See the *KRA iTax* lesson. Rules and thresholds change: check KRA's website or ask an accountant.)

## 4. County permits and licences

Most businesses need a **county single business permit** from their county government (many counties now apply online). Some sectors need extra licences: food handling, health, pharmacies, transport, liquor, etc.

## 5. Get paid professionally

- Open a **business bank account** (you'll need the certificate and KRA PIN).
- Apply for an M-Pesa **Till number** (Buy Goods) or **Paybill** for your business.
- Create simple **invoices and receipts** with your registered name and PIN.
- Keep records of all income and expenses (a spreadsheet is fine to start).

## 6. Get online

- A **Google Business Profile** so customers find you on Google Maps (see the Digital marketing tutorial).
- **WhatsApp Business** with a catalogue.
- A simple **website** and a professional email (`info@yourbusiness.co.ke`).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Better |
|---|---|
| Paying "agents" outside eCitizen | Pay only through official channels |
| Not filing iTax returns because "there was no income" | File NIL returns |
| Mixing personal and business money | Separate accounts and records |
| Ignoring county permits | Check your county's requirements early |
