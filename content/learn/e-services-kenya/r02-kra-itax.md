# KRA PIN and iTax returns: registering, filing nil and employment returns, and staying compliant

Almost every adult in Kenya with an ID needs a **KRA PIN**: for jobs, bank accounts, SIM-related services, business registration, buying land or vehicles, and tenders. And every PIN holder is generally required to **file an annual income tax return** by **30 June**, even if they had no income (a "nil return"). Missing the deadline attracts penalties. This unit explains KRA, the PIN, iTax, how to file nil and employment returns step by step, and how freelancers and small businesses should think about tax.

:::warning Rules and menus change
Tax laws, rates, deadlines and the iTax interface change. This lesson explains the general process; always confirm current requirements on **kra.go.ke** / **itax.kra.go.ke**, or consult KRA or a tax professional for your situation.
:::

## KRA and why taxes matter

The **Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA)** collects taxes on behalf of the government. Taxes fund roads, schools, hospitals, security and public services. Main taxes you'll hear about:

| Tax | Who pays |
|---|---|
| **PAYE** (Pay As You Earn) | Employees (deducted from salary by the employer) |
| **Income tax** (individuals, companies) | People and businesses with taxable income |
| **Turnover Tax (TOT)** | Small businesses within a set turnover range (check current thresholds and rate) |
| **VAT** (Value Added Tax, standard 16%) | VAT-registered businesses charge it on sales |
| **Withholding tax** | Deducted at source on certain payments (e.g. some professional/consultancy fees) |
| **Rental income tax** | Landlords with residential rental income |
| **Excise duty, customs duty** | On specific goods and imports |

## The KRA PIN

:::define KRA PIN
A unique Personal Identification Number (letter + 9 digits + letter, e.g. A012345678Z) that identifies you or your business for all tax matters.
:::

You need it for employment, opening certain bank accounts, business registration, land and vehicle transactions, tenders and more.

### Registering for a KRA PIN (individual)

1. Go to **itax.kra.go.ke** (type it yourself).
2. Choose **New PIN Registration**.
3. Select **Taxpayer type: Individual** and **Mode: Online form**.
4. Enter your ID details (ID number, date of birth), contacts (email, phone) and address details as requested.
5. Choose **obligations** if asked (for most employed people: Income Tax - Resident Individual).
6. Submit; your PIN certificate is sent to your **email**. Download and keep it safely.

Tips: use **your own** active email (iTax sends passwords and certificates there). If you already have a PIN (many people were registered by employers or banks), don't create a second one: retrieve it instead.

## Logging in to iTax

1. Go to **itax.kra.go.ke**.
2. Enter your **KRA PIN** → Continue.
3. Enter your **password** and the security stamp (arithmetic question) → Log in.

**Forgot password:** click "Forgot Password/Unlock Account", enter your PIN, answer the security stamp; a new password is sent to your registered email. If you no longer have access to that email, you'll need to update it through KRA's support process.

Change the default password to a strong, unique one after logging in.

## Tax obligations

An **obligation** is a type of tax you're registered for, for example:
- **Income Tax - Resident Individual** (most individuals)
- **Income Tax - PAYE** (employers)
- **VAT**, **Turnover Tax**, **Residential Rental Income**

You file returns for each obligation you have. Wrong obligations cause confusion (e.g. a VAT obligation means monthly VAT returns are expected). If you have obligations you don't need, contact KRA about amending them.

## Filing a nil return (no income)

A **nil return** declares that you had no taxable income in the year (for example, students or unemployed people with a PIN). It must still be filed by **30 June** for the previous year (January–December).

Steps (typical flow):
1. Log in to iTax.
2. Go to **Returns** → **File Nil Return**.
3. Select the **Tax Obligation**: Income Tax - Resident Individual → Next.
4. The **return period** is shown (e.g. 01/01/2025 – 31/12/2025); confirm.
5. **Submit**.
6. Download the **e-Return Acknowledgement Receipt** and keep it.

:::warning Only file nil if you truly had no income
If you earned salary, business income, rent or other taxable income, filing a nil return is incorrect and can lead to penalties. Employees must file their employment income using their **P9** form.
:::

## Filing an employment income return (salaried employees)

If you're employed, your employer deducts PAYE monthly, but you still file an annual return to declare your income and the tax already deducted.

### What you need

- Your **P9 form** from each employer for the year (shows your gross pay, deductions, taxable pay and PAYE).
- Details of reliefs/exemptions if applicable (e.g. insurance relief, disability exemption certificate, pension contributions).
- Your iTax login.

### Steps (typical flow)

1. Log in → **Returns** → **File Return**.
2. Select **Income Tax - Resident Individual** → Next.
3. Choose the online form for employment income (KRA provides a simplified online form for employment-only income; others use the downloadable Excel form).
4. Enter the **return period**.
5. Enter **employer details and amounts from your P9**: employer PIN, gross pay, taxable pay, tax paid (PAYE). iTax may pre-fill data submitted by employers; **check it carefully against your P9**.
6. Add any reliefs/exemptions you qualify for (with supporting details).
7. Review the computation: tax due vs tax paid. Often the result is zero (PAYE already covered it); sometimes there's tax payable or a refund claim.
8. **Submit** and download the acknowledgement receipt.
9. If tax is payable, generate a **payment slip** and pay (via M-Pesa paybill or bank) using the payment registration number before the deadline.

Multiple employers in a year? Include all P9s. Side income (business, freelance, rent) needs additional sections or a different form; get guidance.

## Deadlines and penalties

| Item | Usual rule (confirm current law) |
|---|---|
| Individual income tax return | By **30 June** for the previous calendar year |
| Late filing | Penalties apply (KRA sets the amounts; for individuals, a fixed minimum or a percentage of tax due, whichever is higher) |
| Late payment | Penalty and interest on unpaid tax |
| Monthly returns (VAT, PAYE for employers, TOT) | Usually by the 20th of the following month |

File early: iTax gets very busy in the last days of June.

## Tax Compliance Certificate (TCC)

A **TCC** shows you've filed returns and paid taxes; it's required for many jobs (especially public service), tenders and licences. Apply on iTax (Certificates → Apply for Tax Compliance Certificate). It's issued if your returns and payments are up to date.

## Freelancers, side hustles and small businesses

Online income (Upwork, Fiverr, YouTube, transcription, design clients) and small business income are **taxable** like other income.

- **Keep records:** invoices, platform statements, M-Pesa statements, receipts for business expenses (data, equipment, rent).
- **Declare** income in your annual return under the appropriate section (business income), or register for the relevant regime (such as Turnover Tax for eligible small businesses), as advised.
- **Business registration:** register a business name (BRS via eCitizen) as you grow; open a separate business account/line.
- **eTIMS:** KRA has introduced electronic invoicing requirements (eTIMS) for many businesses; check whether they apply to you.
- **Get advice:** a qualified accountant or KRA's taxpayer support can confirm which taxes apply to your situation.

:::think A university student registered a KRA PIN to apply for an internship but never earned income. Do they need to file returns?
Generally yes: PIN holders are required to file an annual return by 30 June, and someone with no income files a **nil return**. Not filing can lead to penalties. If they start earning (internship stipend, freelance income), they must declare that income instead of filing nil.
:::

## Avoiding iTax scams

- KRA won't ask for your iTax password, M-Pesa PIN or OTPs by phone, SMS or email.
- "Tax refund" messages with links are common phishing: log in to iTax yourself to check.
- Use only `itax.kra.go.ke` and `kra.go.ke`.
- If a cyber café files for you, let them guide you but enter your password yourself, and change it afterwards.

## Summary

- KRA collects taxes; main taxes include PAYE, income tax, Turnover Tax, VAT and withholding tax.
- A KRA PIN (letter + 9 digits + letter) is needed for jobs, banking, business and property; register on itax.kra.go.ke.
- Log in with your PIN and password; reset via registered email.
- Obligations define which returns you file; most individuals have Income Tax - Resident Individual.
- Nil returns are for no income; employees file with their P9; deadline **30 June** for the previous year.
- Late filing and payment attract penalties; a TCC proves compliance.
- Freelancers and small businesses must keep records and declare income; get professional advice when unsure.
