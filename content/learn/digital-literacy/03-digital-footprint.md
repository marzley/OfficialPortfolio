# Your digital footprint, social media and online reputation

Everything you post, like, comment on and search leaves a trail: your **digital footprint**. Employers, universities, clients and even landlords look people up online. Managing your footprint protects your privacy and opens doors.

## Active vs passive footprint

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| What you deliberately share: posts, photos, comments, CVs, reviews | What's collected about you: location history, searches, cookies, app usage, being tagged |

Both can be found, copied and screenshotted, even after you delete them.

## Google yourself

Search your name (in quotes, e.g. "Brian Kiprop") with your town or school. Check Google Images too. What would a potential employer think?

## Clean up and lock down

1. **Review old posts**: delete or archive anything offensive, embarrassing or that shares private details.
2. **Privacy settings**: Facebook, Instagram and TikTok → Settings → Privacy. Limit who sees posts, who can tag you, and who can find you by phone number.
3. **Hide personal details**: don't publish your phone number, ID number, home location, date of birth or children's school.
4. **Turn off location** on photos and posts, and be careful posting while you're away from home ("On holiday for 2 weeks!").
5. **Remove old accounts** you no longer use.

## Build a positive footprint

Your footprint can also **help** you:

- A professional **LinkedIn** profile: clear photo, headline ("ICT student | Web designer | Nakuru"), skills, projects and certificates.
- A simple **portfolio** website or GitHub for developers (see our HTML and Git tutorials).
- Share useful content about your field: what you're learning, projects you finished, helpful tips.
- Consistent, professional usernames: `amina.designs` beats `sexy_amina254` for work.

## Think before you post: the 5-question test

1. Would I be happy for my parent, teacher or future boss to see this?
2. Is it true, and is it kind?
3. Am I sharing someone else's private information or photo without permission?
4. Could this be used to find or harm me (location, routine, ID)?
5. Would I say this to the person's face?

## Online reputation and the law

In Kenya, laws such as the **Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act** and the **Data Protection Act** cover issues like cyberbullying, harassment, sharing intimate images without consent, and publishing false information. Posts can have real legal consequences, not just social ones.

## Cyberbullying

If you or someone you know is being bullied online:

- **Don't reply** in anger.
- **Keep evidence**: screenshots with dates.
- **Block and report** the account on the platform.
- **Tell someone you trust**: a parent, teacher, counsellor.
- Serious threats: report to the police.

## Digital wellbeing

Social media is designed to keep you scrolling. Use Android's **Digital Wellbeing** or iPhone **Screen Time** to see your usage and set app timers, turn off non-essential notifications, and keep phones away at bedtime.
