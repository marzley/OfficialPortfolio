# Using the internet well: getting connected, finding information and doing everyday tasks online

The internet is now how Kenyans pay bills, apply for jobs and government services, learn, sell products, stay in touch with family and get news. Using it **well** means getting connected affordably, finding reliable information quickly, doing everyday tasks confidently, and avoiding the traps. This unit is a practical guide for anyone, whether you use a phone, a laptop or a cyber café computer.

:::note What you will learn
- What the internet is and how you connect (phone, Wi-Fi, hotspot)
- Buying and managing data bundles; saving data
- Browsers and apps; URLs and how to recognise real websites
- Searching effectively and judging information
- Everyday online tasks: payments, government services, learning, shopping, communication
- Using shared/public computers safely
- Online etiquette and wellbeing
:::

## What is the internet?

:::define Internet
A worldwide network connecting billions of computers and phones, so they can exchange information. Services that run on it include the **web** (websites), **email**, **messaging** (WhatsApp), **video calls**, **mobile banking apps** and **streaming**.
:::

When you open a website, your phone sends a request through your network (Safaricom, Airtel, Telkom, or your Wi-Fi provider) to a server somewhere in the world, and the page comes back, usually in a second or two.

## Getting connected

| Option | Best for | Tips |
|---|---|---|
| **Mobile data** | Phones anywhere with signal | 4G/5G is faster than 3G; signal varies by area |
| **Wi-Fi at home/office** (fibre or wireless) | Heavy use: videos, classes, downloads | Often cheaper per GB; share with the family |
| **Phone hotspot** | Getting a laptop online using your phone's data | Settings → Hotspot; set a strong password |
| **Public Wi-Fi** (cafés, malls, campuses, hotspots) | Light browsing | Less secure: avoid banking and logging into important accounts on open networks |
| **Cyber café** | No personal device | Log out and clear data when done (see below) |

### Data bundles

- Buy bundles through your provider's menu or app (e.g. dialling the USSD code or using the My Safaricom/My Airtel app). Daily, weekly and monthly bundles differ in price per GB; monthly bundles are usually cheaper per GB if you use a lot.
- Check your **balance** before big downloads.
- **Out-of-bundle** browsing (when the bundle runs out) can be expensive; some lines let you block it.

### Saving data

- Download big things (updates, movies, course videos) on Wi-Fi.
- Turn off **auto-play** of videos on Facebook, TikTok, Instagram and WhatsApp (auto-download media settings).
- Lower **YouTube quality** (480p uses far less than 1080p).
- Use **data saver** settings on Android and in apps; use "Lite" apps.
- On laptops, set your connection as **metered** to stop automatic big updates.
- Check which apps use most data: Settings → Network → Data usage.

## Browsers, apps and web addresses

A **browser** (Chrome, Safari, Opera Mini, Firefox, Edge) opens websites. Many services also have **apps** (Play Store/App Store), which are often easier on phones. Download apps only from official stores and check the developer name (e.g. "Kenya Revenue Authority" for KRA's app).

### Reading a web address (URL)

```
https://www.ecitizen.go.ke/services
  │       │           │
  │       │           └ the page/path
  │       └ domain name (who runs the site)
  └ https = encrypted connection
```

How to recognise the real site:
- **Government** sites end with **`.go.ke`** (e.g. `kra.go.ke`, `ecitizen.go.ke`, `kuccps.ac.ke` is an academic `.ac.ke` site for placement).
- Banks and companies use their known domain; check spelling carefully (`safaricom.co.ke`, not `safaricom-promo.com`).
- Scam sites copy logos and colours but use strange domains or extra words.
- **HTTPS (padlock)** means the connection is encrypted, not that the site is honest.

:::tip Bookmark important sites
Bookmark the real eCitizen, KRA, HELB, bank and school portal sites, and always open them from your bookmarks instead of links in SMS or WhatsApp messages.
:::

## Finding information

### Search well

- Use **specific words**: `KUCCPS application deadline 2026` instead of "when do we apply".
- Put phrases in **quotes**: `"good conduct certificate" requirements`.
- Search one site: `site:go.ke business name registration`.
- Find documents: `KCSE timetable filetype:pdf`.
- Use **Tools → Any time → Past year** for recent results.

### Judge what you find

Ask:
1. **Who** published it? An official body, an expert, a news organisation, or an anonymous page?
2. **When?** Fees, deadlines and procedures change; check dates.
3. **Why?** Is it selling something or trying to make you angry or scared?
4. **Can I confirm it** on an official site or two other reliable sources?

AI chatbots can explain things helpfully, but they can be wrong or out of date. Confirm important facts (deadlines, fees, health and legal information) on official websites.

## Using shared or public computers safely

Cyber cafés, school labs and office shared PCs are common. Before leaving:

1. **Log out** of every account (email, eCitizen, iTax, social media). Closing the tab isn't enough.
2. Use a **private/incognito window** so history and passwords aren't saved.
3. **Never click "Remember password"** on shared computers.
4. **Delete downloaded files** (IDs, certificates, CVs) from Downloads and the Desktop, and empty the Recycle Bin.
5. **Don't leave flash disks** behind; scan them for viruses afterwards.
6. Watch for people looking over your shoulder when typing passwords.

## Online etiquette ("netiquette")

- Be respectful; written messages lack tone, so avoid sarcasm that can be misunderstood.
- Avoid ALL CAPS (reads as shouting).
- Respect group rules in WhatsApp and Facebook groups; don't spam or forward chain messages.
- Ask before posting photos of other people, especially children.
- Think before posting: content can be screenshot and shared forever (see the digital footprint lesson).

## Wellbeing online

- Set limits for social media and gaming; use screen time tools.
- Take breaks from news and arguments that cause stress.
- Keep phones out of the bedroom at night for better sleep.
- Report harassment and block abusers; talk to someone you trust.

:::think You receive an SMS with a link saying "Your KUCCPS application is incomplete. Click to fix it now or lose your placement." What should you do?
Don't click the link. Open the official KUCCPS website yourself (from a bookmark or by typing the address you know), log in and check your application. Urgent messages with links are a common scam pattern; official bodies won't ask you to pay or give passwords through random links.
:::

## Practice tasks

1. Check your data usage by app on your phone and turn off auto-play/auto-download in two apps.
2. Bookmark the official websites you need (eCitizen, KRA, your bank, your school portal).
3. Use `site:go.ke` to find an official page about a service you need.
4. Compare information about one topic from three sources and note any differences.
5. Practise the shared computer checklist the next time you use a cyber café or lab.

## Summary

- The internet connects devices worldwide; connect with mobile data, Wi-Fi, hotspots or cyber cafés.
- Manage bundles, avoid out-of-bundle charges, and save data with Wi-Fi downloads, lower video quality and data saver settings.
- Recognise real websites (`.go.ke` for government, correct spellings); HTTPS means encrypted, not trustworthy; use bookmarks.
- Search specifically (quotes, `site:`, `filetype:`) and judge sources by who, when, why and confirmation.
- Do everyday tasks online confidently, and log out and clean up on shared computers.
- Practise good netiquette and protect your wellbeing.
