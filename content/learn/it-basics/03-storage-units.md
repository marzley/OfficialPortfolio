# Storage and data units: bits, bytes, MB, GB and cloud storage

"Is 32 GB enough?" "Why did this video eat my bundles?" Understanding data sizes helps you buy the right phone or laptop, manage storage and budget for internet bundles.

## Bits and bytes

- A **bit** is the smallest unit: a single 0 or 1.
- A **byte** is 8 bits: enough to store one character like `A`.

| Unit | Size (approximately) | Holds about |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Byte (B) | 8 bits | One letter |
| 1 Kilobyte (KB) | 1,000 bytes | A short SMS-length note is well under 1 KB |
| 1 Megabyte (MB) | 1,000 KB | A compressed photo, 1 minute of MP3 music |
| 1 Gigabyte (GB) | 1,000 MB | About 1 hour of standard-definition video streaming |
| 1 Terabyte (TB) | 1,000 GB | Hundreds of thousands of photos |

> Computers also count in powers of 2 (1 KB = 1,024 bytes). That's why a "1 TB" hard disk shows about 931 GB in Windows: same size, different counting.

## Bits vs bytes in internet speeds

Internet speed is measured in **bits per second** (Mbps), but file sizes are in **bytes** (MB). Divide by 8:

- A **10 Mbps** connection downloads about **1.25 MB per second** at best.
- So a 500 MB file takes roughly 500 ÷ 1.25 = 400 seconds, about 7 minutes.

## Types of storage

| Type | Speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **RAM** (memory) | Fastest | Temporary: cleared when power goes off. More RAM = more apps open smoothly. |
| **HDD** (hard disk drive) | Slow | Spinning disks; cheap per GB; fragile if dropped |
| **SSD** (solid-state drive) | Very fast | No moving parts; makes a computer start and open apps much faster |
| **Flash drive / memory card** | Medium | Portable; easy to lose; can spread viruses |
| **Cloud storage** | Depends on internet | Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud: available anywhere, good for backups |

> RAM is not storage space. "8 GB RAM, 256 GB SSD" means 8 GB of working memory and 256 GB for files.

| Video editing, design, gaming | 16–32 GB | 1 TB SSD |

An **SSD** matters more than a faster processor for how fast an everyday laptop *feels*.

## Freeing up space

- Windows: Settings → System → **Storage** → Temporary files, and turn on **Storage Sense**.
- Phones: clear WhatsApp media (Settings → Storage and data → Manage storage), delete duplicate photos, move photos to the cloud.
- Uninstall apps you don't use.

## Why storage and data units matter

Data units appear everywhere in daily life: data bundles (1 GB, 5 GB), phone storage (64 GB, 128 GB), file size limits on email (often around 25 MB), upload limits on eCitizen and job portals (often 2 MB or 5 MB per document), internet speeds (10 Mbps) and cloud storage plans. Understanding them helps you buy the right phone or laptop, avoid running out of space or bundles, and fix "file too large" errors when applying for jobs or government services.

## The units in order

| Unit | Size | Holds roughly |
|---|---|---|
| Bit (b) | 0 or 1 | A yes/no |
| Byte (B) | 8 bits | One simple character |
| Kilobyte (KB) | About 1,000 bytes | A short text document |
| Megabyte (MB) | About 1,000 KB | A compressed photo (1 to 5 MB), a 1-minute song |
| Gigabyte (GB) | About 1,000 MB | Around an hour of HD video streaming, depending on quality |
| Terabyte (TB) | About 1,000 GB | Hundreds of thousands of photos |

Computers technically use powers of 2 (1 KB = 1,024 bytes), which is why a "128 GB" phone shows a little less usable space, and the operating system also takes space.

## Converting between units

Multiply by 1,000 (or 1,024) to go to a smaller unit; divide to go to a bigger one:

```
2 GB      = 2 × 1,000      = 2,000 MB (approximately)
3,500 MB  = 3,500 ÷ 1,000  = 3.5 GB
1.5 MB    = 1.5 × 1,000    = 1,500 KB
```

Example: you have 1 GB of data. A 5-minute YouTube video at 480p uses roughly 40 to 50 MB, so 1 GB allows roughly 20 such videos (actual use varies by app and quality).

## Download time calculations

Internet speeds are in **bits** per second; file sizes are in **bytes**. Divide the speed by 8 to get megabytes per second:

```
Speed 20 Mbps ÷ 8 = 2.5 MB per second
A 500 MB file ÷ 2.5 MB/s = 200 seconds ≈ 3 minutes 20 seconds
```

Real downloads are usually slower than the advertised speed because of network congestion, Wi-Fi signal and the server's speed.

## Fixing "file too large" problems

| File type | How to make it smaller |
|---|---|
| Photo (JPG/PNG) | Resize (most phones and Windows Photos can resize); compress with free tools like Squoosh; save as JPG or WebP instead of PNG |
| Scanned document | Scan in black and white or greyscale at 150 to 200 DPI instead of colour 600 DPI |
| PDF | Use "Reduce file size" or "Compress PDF" tools (some free online tools; avoid uploading confidential documents to unknown sites) |
| Word document with images | Compress pictures (File → Compress Pictures in Word) |
| Video | Lower the resolution (1080p → 720p) or use a compression app |
| Many files | Zip them (right-click → Send to → Compressed folder) |

For job applications and eCitizen uploads, a scanned ID or certificate at 150 to 200 DPI in greyscale is usually clear and small.

## Types of storage compared

| Storage | Speed | Typical use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| RAM | Fastest | Running programs | Temporary; cleared at power off |
| SSD (NVMe) | Very fast | Laptop/desktop main drive | Boots in seconds; no moving parts |
| SSD (SATA) | Fast | Upgrading older laptops | Big speed boost over HDD |
| HDD | Slower | Cheap large storage, backups | Moving parts; damaged by drops |
| Flash drive / SD card | Varies | Moving files, phone storage | Easy to lose; can carry viruses |
| External HDD/SSD | Varies | Backups | Keep away from heat and drops |
| Cloud (Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud, Dropbox) | Depends on internet | Sync and backup | Accessible anywhere; needs data |

Upgrading an old laptop from HDD to SSD is one of the cheapest ways to make it feel much faster.

## The 3-2-1 backup rule

Losing a phone or a hard disk failure can wipe years of photos, assignments and business records. Follow the **3-2-1 rule**:

- **3** copies of important data (the original plus two backups)
- on **2** different types of storage (e.g. laptop + external drive)
- **1** copy off-site or in the cloud (in case of theft, fire or flooding)

Turn on automatic backup: Google Photos or Google One for Android phones, iCloud for iPhones, OneDrive for Windows Documents and Desktop folders.

## Where does phone storage go?

Check in Settings → Storage. Common space users:

- WhatsApp media (videos and photos from groups): in WhatsApp, Settings → Storage and data → Manage storage, delete large files and turn off auto-download.
- Duplicate photos and videos already backed up to the cloud: use "Free up space" in Google Photos.
- App caches (social media apps can store gigabytes): clear cache in app settings.
- Downloads folder: old PDFs, APKs and videos.
- Offline maps, music and movies.

:::think Your internet plan says 10 Mbps, but a 100 MB file takes about 90 seconds, not 10 seconds. Is the internet provider cheating?
Not necessarily. 10 Mbps is megabits per second, which is about 1.25 megabytes per second, so 100 MB takes at least 80 seconds even at full speed. Real conditions (Wi-Fi signal, congestion, server speed) add a little more. The confusion comes from bits (b) versus bytes (B).
:::
