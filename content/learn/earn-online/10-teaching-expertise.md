# I. Teaching and expertise (ways 92–100): tutoring, teaching languages, courses, coaching and communities

If you know something well, a school subject, a language, a profession, a software tool or a business skill, someone will pay to learn it from you. Online teaching ranges from one-on-one tutoring over Google Meet to selling recorded courses to thousands of people. Teachers, university students, professionals and skilled hobbyists can all earn this way. This unit covers nine methods.

:::note What makes a good online teacher
- Deep knowledge of the topic, **and** the patience to explain from the learner's level
- Clear structure: goals, small steps, practice, feedback
- Reliable setup: stable internet (with a backup), a headset, good lighting, a quiet space
- Tools: Google Meet/Zoom, a digital whiteboard (Google Jamboard alternatives, Miro, a drawing tablet or a phone as a document camera), Google Classroom or WhatsApp groups for materials
:::

---

### 92. Online tutoring (school, college and university)

**What it is.** One-on-one or small group lessons for CBC/CBE, KCSE, IGCSE/A-level, university units, and professional exams (CPA, ACCA modules).

**How to start, step by step:**
1. Choose subjects and levels you can teach confidently (e.g. Grade 7–9 Maths and Integrated Science, KCSE Chemistry).
2. Prepare a simple lesson structure: quick check of previous work → new concept → worked examples → practice → homework.
3. Find students: parents' WhatsApp and Facebook groups, your church and estate, schools (holiday tuition), referrals; international platforms like **Preply** and others for students abroad.
4. Price per hour or monthly packages (e.g. 8 lessons a month); take payment in advance (M-Pesa).
5. Report progress to parents monthly (topics covered, scores, areas to improve).

**Rules.** Follow child protection good practice: lessons with minors in visible settings, parents informed, professional communication only through parents' numbers where appropriate. Check current Ministry of Education rules on holiday tuition for school-going children.

### 93. Teaching English online

**What it is.** Teaching English to learners abroad (adults and children) who want conversation practice, business English or exam preparation (IELTS, TOEFL).

**How to start:**
1. Consider a **TEFL/TESOL certificate** (120-hour courses are common); many platforms and schools ask for one.
2. Join tutoring marketplaces such as **Preply** (preply.com) and **italki** (italki.com) as a tutor; some platforms prefer "native speakers" from specific countries, so check each platform's requirements honestly.
3. Record a strong **intro video**: clear voice, good light, smile, explain who you help and how.
4. Start with competitive rates to get first reviews, then raise them.
5. Specialise: business English, IELTS speaking, English for children, interview English.

### 94. Teaching Kiswahili and other languages

**What it is.** Teaching Kiswahili (and Kikuyu, Dholuo, Somali, Arabic, French, etc.) to foreigners: diplomats, NGO staff, missionaries, tourists, diaspora children and students of African languages.

**How to start:** list yourself on italki and Preply as a Kiswahili teacher; prepare a beginner curriculum (greetings, numbers, shopping, travel, grammar basics like noun classes); make short Kiswahili lessons on YouTube/TikTok to attract students; contact language schools and expatriate communities in Nairobi.

**Why it's an opportunity.** Kiswahili is spoken by tens of millions of people across East and Central Africa and is used by the African Union, so interest in learning it is real.

### 95. Selling online courses

**What it is.** Recording a course once and selling it many times.

**Where:** **Udemy** (huge marketplace, lower prices), **Teachable**, **Thinkific**, **Podia** (your own course site), **Selar** (popular in Africa with local payment options), or your own website/WhatsApp community + M-Pesa.

**How to start, step by step:**
1. **Validate demand:** ask your audience, check what people search and buy, run a free live class first.
2. **Outline:** clear outcome ("Build your first website in 30 days"), modules, short lessons (5–15 minutes), exercises.
3. **Record:** screen recordings (OBS Studio, free) for software topics, or phone video for practical skills; good audio is essential.
4. **Edit and upload** with worksheets and quizzes.
5. **Market:** free content on YouTube/TikTok/WhatsApp, email list, testimonials, launch discounts.
6. **Update** the course and support students.

**Good Kenyan course topics:** Excel for jobs, Canva design, digital marketing for small businesses, poultry farming business, Kenya tax filing (iTax) explained, coding, CV and interview skills, photography with a phone.

### 96. Live workshops and bootcamps

**What it is.** Paid live sessions or multi-week cohorts (Zoom/Google Meet or in person), e.g. "Weekend Excel bootcamp", "4-week freelancing class".

**How to start:** choose a practical topic with a clear result, plan sessions and assignments, set a price and a seat limit, collect payment via M-Pesa, record sessions for attendees, and gather testimonials. Cohorts create accountability, so students finish more often than with recorded courses.

---

### 97. Coaching and consulting

**What it is.** Selling your **expertise and judgement**: business consulting for SMEs, career coaching, fitness coaching, financial literacy, agribusiness advice, IT consulting, HR consulting, mentoring for students.

**How to start:** define a specific problem you solve for a specific client ("I help small restaurants cut food waste and increase profits"); publish helpful content to show expertise (LinkedIn, YouTube); offer a free 20-minute discovery call; sell packages (e.g. 4 sessions); collect testimonials.

**Rules.** Stay inside your qualifications. Medical, legal, financial investment and psychological advice are regulated professions in Kenya; don't present yourself as licensed if you aren't.

---

### 98. Selling your own notes, revision papers and study guides

**What it is.** Creating and selling **original** study materials: topic notes, revision guides, practice questions with marking schemes, past-paper walkthroughs you wrote yourself, flashcards.

**How to start:** create high-quality original materials, format them well (Word/Canva → PDF), sell via WhatsApp/M-Pesa, Selar, Payhip or your own site, and give free samples.

**Copyright and ethics.** Don't sell copyrighted textbooks or materials that belong to others (KNEC papers and publishers' books have rights holders), and never sell leaked exams: that's illegal and harms students. Your own original notes and questions are fine.

---

### 99. Interview and exam preparation services (ethical)

**What it is.** Mock interviews, aptitude test practice, public service interview preparation, preparing graduates for job assessments, exam technique coaching.

**How to start:** combine with CV writing (method 8); prepare question banks for common roles; run mock interviews by video with feedback. **Ethical line:** you help candidates prepare; you never sit exams or tests on their behalf.

---

### 100. Paid communities and memberships

**What it is.** A members-only group where people pay monthly for access to you, resources, accountability and each other: e.g. a freelancers' community, a farming club, a coding study group, a fitness group.

**Where:** WhatsApp Communities/Telegram (with M-Pesa subscriptions managed manually or via tools), Skool, Patreon, YouTube memberships, Discord.

**How to start:** build a free audience first; define what members get (weekly lives, resources, feedback, networking); start with a founding-member price; deliver consistently; keep the community safe and moderated.

## Pricing teaching and expertise

| Model | Example |
|---|---|
| Per hour | Tutoring, consulting calls |
| Package | 8 lessons/month; 4 coaching sessions |
| Per course | One-time course purchase |
| Cohort | 4-week bootcamp |
| Subscription | Monthly community membership |

Raise prices with results and testimonials. Specialists (exam board, profession, niche) earn more than generalists.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Teaching without lesson plans | Outline goals and steps for every session |
| Poor audio/video setup | Headset, light facing you, stable internet |
| Not collecting payment in advance | Prepaid packages |
| Selling copyrighted or leaked materials | Only original materials |
| Overpromising results ("guaranteed A") | Promise effort and a clear method |
| Making a big course nobody asked for | Validate demand first |

## Practice tasks

1. Plan a 60-minute tutoring lesson for one topic you know, with practice questions.
2. Record a 60-second tutor intro video.
3. Outline a 6-module online course with lesson titles.
4. Create a 5-page original revision guide and export it as a PDF.
5. Write a one-paragraph offer for a coaching or consulting service.

## Summary

- Teaching and expertise: tutoring, teaching English, teaching Kiswahili and other languages, courses, workshops, coaching/consulting, original study materials, interview prep and paid communities.
- Good structure, reliable setup and results-based reputation are key.
- Use Preply/italki for language tutoring, Udemy/Teachable/Selar for courses, WhatsApp/Telegram/Skool for communities.
- Stay ethical: original materials, no leaked exams, no sitting tests for others, stay within your qualifications.
