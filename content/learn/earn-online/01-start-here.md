# Start here: how to earn online from Kenya safely (and the full list of 130+ ways)

Thousands of Kenyans earn real money online every month: writers, transcribers, designers, developers, virtual assistants, AI trainers, content creators, online sellers and tutors. Thousands of others lose money to fake "online jobs", pyramid schemes and trading scams. The difference is usually **knowledge**: knowing which work is real, what skills it needs, how to start, how to get paid and how to spot a scam.

This subject explains **more than 130 ways to earn online**, grouped into 13 families. Each method has: what it is, who it suits, step-by-step instructions to start, where to find work, honest pay expectations and the risks. Read this first unit fully before choosing a method.

:::warning The honest truth first
- **There is no "easy money" online.** Real online income is payment for **work, skills or products** that someone values.
- **Nobody legitimate asks you to pay to get a job.** "Registration fees", "activation fees" and "starter kits" for online jobs are the number one scam in Kenya.
- **Most beginners earn little in the first 1–3 months.** Income grows with skills, reviews and consistency.
- **Trading (forex, Deriv, crypto) is not a job or salary.** Most retail traders lose money. Treat it as a high-risk activity to study carefully, never as a way to pay rent.
:::

## What you need to start

| Need | Why | Minimum |
|---|---|---|
| **A device** | To do the work | A smartphone works for some methods (social media, selling, some microtasks); a **laptop** opens most freelance work |
| **Reliable internet** | Deadlines and video calls | Mobile data bundles or home Wi-Fi; a backup (hotspot) |
| **Email address** | All platforms use it | A professional Gmail: `firstname.lastname@gmail.com` |
| **National ID / passport** | Identity verification (KYC) on platforms and payment services | Your real ID; never use someone else's account |
| **M-Pesa** | Receiving local payments and withdrawals | Registered in your own name |
| **PayPal, Payoneer, Wise or a bank account** | Receiving international payments | See "Getting paid" below |
| **KRA PIN** | Tax compliance, some clients ask | Free on iTax |
| **Basic computer skills** | Typing, files, Google Docs/Word | See the IT basics and Word subjects |
| **English writing** | Most international work is in English | Clear, correct sentences |

:::tip Phone-only? Start here
If you only have a phone: social media management for local businesses, selling on WhatsApp/Facebook Marketplace, content creation, some AI and microtask apps, customer support on chat, and tutoring by WhatsApp or Google Meet. Save part of your first income towards a used laptop (good refurbished laptops are available from around KSh 20,000–35,000).
:::

## Getting paid in Kenya

| Method | Used for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **M-Pesa** | Local clients, some platforms, withdrawals | Instant; keep business and personal records separate |
| **PayPal** | Many international clients and platforms | In Kenya you can withdraw PayPal balance to M-Pesa via the PayPal option in the M-Pesa app, or to a bank |
| **Payoneer** | Upwork, Fiverr and many marketplaces | Withdraw to a Kenyan bank account |
| **Wise** | Receiving from clients abroad | Withdraw to bank or M-Pesa where supported |
| **Bank transfer (SWIFT)** | YouTube AdSense, big clients, salaries | Fees per transfer; ask your bank about USD accounts |
| **Platform wallets** | Upwork, Fiverr, Jumia, etc. | Each has its own withdrawal options and fees |

Always use accounts **in your own name**. "Account renting" (using someone else's Upwork or AI-platform account, or letting someone use yours) breaks platform rules, gets accounts banned and often ends with the worker not being paid.

## Ten rules for staying safe

1. **Never pay to get a job.** No real employer charges registration or activation fees.
2. **Too good to be true is false.** "Earn KSh 5,000 daily by liking posts" is a scam.
3. **No guaranteed returns.** Anyone promising fixed weekly profits from forex, crypto or "investment" is running a scam or a Ponzi scheme.
4. **Keep payments on the platform.** Clients asking to pay "outside Upwork/Fiverr" often disappear, and it breaks the rules.
5. **Verify companies:** check their website, reviews, and LinkedIn; search "[company] scam".
6. **Protect your data.** Don't send copies of your ID to random WhatsApp recruiters.
7. **Beware of fake cheques and overpayments.** "I sent too much, refund the difference" is a classic trick.
8. **Never share OTP codes, PINs or passwords.**
9. **Check licences** for anything involving your money: the **CMA** (Capital Markets Authority) lists licensed brokers and fund managers; the **CBK** regulates banks and payment providers.
10. **When in doubt, ask** in trusted communities (Ajira Digital clubs, your college, experienced freelancers) before acting.

## Taxes and records (the short version)

- Online income is taxable in Kenya like any other income. As a freelancer you are generally responsible for declaring it and filing your **annual return on iTax by 30 June**.
- Keep a simple record: date, client, amount, fees, and what you spent on work (data, equipment).
- Rules and rates (income tax bands, turnover tax, withholding tax) change; check **kra.go.ke** or ask a tax adviser. The **Taxes, records and managing money** lesson in the Make Money Online subject goes deeper.

## How to choose your first method

Answer honestly:

1. **What skills do I already have?** (Typing, writing, design, a language, teaching, selling, coding, a profession like nursing or accounting.)
2. **What device do I have?** (Phone only, or laptop.)
3. **How much time per week?** (5 hours, 20 hours, full time.)
4. **Do I want quick small income or slower, bigger income?** Microtasks pay quickly but little; skills like design, development and marketing take months to learn but pay far more.
5. **What do I enjoy?** You'll quit work you hate.

| Your situation | Good first methods |
|---|---|
| Phone only, little experience | WhatsApp/Facebook selling, social media help for local businesses, microtask apps, UGC videos |
| Good English and typing | Transcription, proofreading, writing, AI rating, VA work |
| Creative | Canva graphics, thumbnails, short videos, photography |
| Teacher or strong student | Online tutoring, notes, YouTube lessons |
| Technical | Websites, WordPress, IT support, data analysis, coding for AI |
| Professional (nurse, lawyer, accountant, engineer) | Expert AI training, consulting, specialist writing |
| Have stock or a business | Jumia, own shop, WhatsApp catalogue |

:::think Why do microtasks and surveys usually pay less than skills like web development?
Because almost anyone can do them, so there are many workers competing for the same small tasks, and each task creates little value for the client. Skills like web development take months to learn, fewer people can do them well, and the result (a website that brings customers) is worth a lot to a business. Pay follows **value** and **scarcity** of skill.
:::
