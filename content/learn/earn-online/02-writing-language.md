# A. Writing and language work (ways 1–12): articles, copywriting, editing, translation and more

Every website, app, advert, email, product page and video script starts as **words**. Businesses worldwide pay for clear, persuasive writing, and Kenyans have an advantage: strong English education, often a second or third language (Kiswahili and mother tongues), and time zones that overlap with Europe and partly with the US. This unit explains 12 writing and language methods step by step.

:::note Before you start
- **Skills needed for all writing work:** correct grammar and spelling, clear sentences, research skills, meeting deadlines, and taking feedback without getting upset.
- **Tools (free):** Google Docs, Grammarly or LanguageTool (free versions), Hemingway Editor (readability), Google Search for research.
- **AI tools:** ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can help you brainstorm and edit, but clients pay for **your** judgement, accuracy and voice. Never submit unedited AI text as original work if the client expects human writing; many clients check, and trust is your business.
:::

## How to build a writing portfolio (do this first)

Clients hire writers by reading samples. You need 3–5 strong samples before applying anywhere.

1. **Choose 2–3 niches** you know or can learn: technology, personal finance, health and fitness, travel in Kenya, agriculture, education, real estate, e-commerce.
2. **Write samples** (800–1,500 words each) as if for a real client: "How to start poultry farming in Kenya: costs and profits", "10 budget phones under KSh 20,000 compared".
3. **Publish them** free on **Medium**, **LinkedIn articles**, a free **Google Sites** or **WordPress.com** blog, or a Google Drive folder with view-only links.
4. **Make a one-page portfolio**: your photo, niches, links to samples, contacts (email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp).

:::tip Quality beats quantity
Three excellent, well-researched samples with clear headings, sources and no errors beat twenty rushed ones. Read each sample aloud before publishing.
:::

### 1. Blog and article writing

**What it is.** Writing informative articles for company blogs, websites and online magazines: guides, how-tos, lists, news explainers.

**Who it suits.** Good English, curious researchers, people who enjoy explaining things.

**How to start, step by step:**
1. Build your portfolio as above in 2–3 niches.
2. Create profiles on **Upwork** (upwork.com), **Fiverr** (fiverr.com) and **PeopleperHour** (peopleperhour.com). Write a clear headline: "Tech and personal finance writer: clear, researched blog posts".
3. Check job boards: **ProBlogger job board** (problogger.com/jobs), LinkedIn Jobs ("content writer remote"), and Kenyan agencies and digital marketing firms that hire freelance writers.
4. Pitch Kenyan businesses directly: many SMEs, schools and SACCOs need blog posts but have no writer. Email them a short pitch with a sample and a suggested topic list.
5. Deliver on time, with headings, short paragraphs, sources and an attractive title. Ask satisfied clients for a review and repeat work.

**Pay (honest).** Beginners on marketplaces often start low (a few dollars per 500 words). Writers with good samples and niches commonly earn far more per article; specialists in finance, tech or health can earn several times beginner rates. Long-term clients matter more than one-off jobs.

**Watch out.** Avoid "content mills" that pay extremely little for huge volume, and "test articles" demanded unpaid by unknown clients (scammers collect free articles this way). A short test of 200–300 words is reasonable; a full unpaid article isn't.

### 2. SEO content writing

**What it is.** Writing articles designed to rank on Google for specific searches (keywords), so the client gets free traffic.

**Skills.** Normal writing skills plus SEO basics: keyword research, search intent, headings, internal links, meta titles and descriptions.

**How to start:**
1. Learn SEO basics (the Marketing subject on this hub, Google's free SEO Starter Guide, HubSpot Academy's free SEO course).
2. Practise with free tools: Google's "People also ask", Google Trends, Ubersuggest/AnswerThePublic free tiers, Google Search Console on your own blog.
3. Write a sample targeting a real keyword, e.g. "how to apply for a KRA PIN", and explain your keyword choice in your portfolio.
4. Offer "SEO blog posts" on Fiverr/Upwork and to local businesses; show results (e.g. "this post reached page 1 for X") when you have them.

**Pay.** Higher than general writing because results bring customers. Bundle services: keyword research + article + meta description.

---

### 3. Copywriting

**What it is.** Writing words that **sell**: website pages, adverts, product launch emails, sales pages, social media ads, brochure text, slogans.

**Skills.** Understanding customers, persuasion, short punchy writing, testing what works.

**How to start:**
1. Study classic copywriting principles: **AIDA** (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action), benefits over features, clear calls to action.
2. Rewrite real examples for practice: take a weak Kenyan business website and rewrite its home page (don't publish their brand as your client, just show "before/after" as a practice piece).
3. Offer services: "website copy", "Facebook ad copy", "product descriptions", "sales emails".
4. Partner with web designers (they often need someone to write their clients' website text).

**Pay.** One of the best-paid writing skills because it directly makes money for clients; experienced copywriters charge per project rather than per word.

:::example Features vs benefits
- Feature: "Our phones have a 5,000 mAh battery."
- Benefit: "Go two full days without charging, even with WhatsApp and YouTube."
Copywriting turns features into benefits the customer cares about.
:::

### 4. Ghostwriting

**What it is.** Writing content that is published under someone else's name: books, LinkedIn posts for executives, speeches, blog posts for founders, memoirs.

**How to start:**
1. Get comfortable writing in other people's voices: interview someone and write a post as them.
2. Offer "LinkedIn ghostwriting" to busy professionals, coaches and CEOs (a monthly package of posts).
3. For books: start with short e-books, interview the client, create an outline they approve, then write chapter by chapter.
4. Use contracts: agree on deadlines, number of revisions, confidentiality and payment milestones (e.g. 30% upfront).

**Ethics.** Ghostwriting for businesses and individuals is a normal profession. **Writing school or university assignments, theses or exams for students to submit as their own is academic dishonesty**: it can get the student expelled, it harms education, and many "academic writing" arrangements in Kenya leave writers unpaid with no legal protection. Build your career on legitimate writing.

### 5. Technical writing

**What it is.** Writing instructions and documentation: user manuals, help centre articles, software documentation, API docs, standard operating procedures (SOPs), training guides.

**Who it suits.** People with technical or IT knowledge who can explain clearly; engineers, IT graduates, developers, health workers (for medical device manuals).

**How to start:**
1. Learn the basics: plain language, numbered steps, screenshots, consistent terms. Google's free **Technical Writing courses** (developers.google.com/tech-writing) are excellent.
2. Create samples: a "How to set up M-Pesa Till on a POS" guide with screenshots, or documentation for an open-source project on GitHub.
3. Learn tools: Markdown, Google Docs, basic HTML, screenshots and annotations, and documentation sites (GitBook, Docusaurus).
4. Apply to software companies, SaaS startups and documentation agencies; contribute to open-source docs to get visible experience.

**Pay.** Technical writing is generally among the better-paid writing fields, especially for software documentation.

### 6. Proofreading and editing

**What it is.** Finding and fixing errors (proofreading) or improving clarity, structure and flow (editing) in documents, books, websites, reports and CVs.

**How to start:**
1. Test yourself honestly: can you spot errors in grammar, punctuation, consistency (e.g. "KSh" vs "Kshs"), and style?
2. Learn a style guide basics (e.g. British vs American spelling; clients specify which).
3. Learn **Track Changes** in Word and **Suggesting mode** in Google Docs; clients expect it.
4. Offer services to: authors self-publishing books, students (proofreading their own work is fine; rewriting it is not), businesses, NGOs, and non-native English speakers abroad.
5. Price per word or per page; give a quick sample edit of one page to win clients.

**Tip.** Specialise: academic proofreading (language only), business documents, fiction, website copy.

### 7. Translation

**What it is.** Converting written text from one language to another: English ↔ Kiswahili is in steady demand (NGO materials, apps, health information, marketing for East Africa), plus French, Arabic, Somali, Amharic and local languages.

**Who it suits.** Truly fluent in both languages, careful with meaning and culture.

**How to start:**
1. Choose your language pairs and specialities (health, legal, marketing, software).
2. Create samples (translate a public-domain text and show both versions).
3. Join translation platforms and agencies: **ProZ** (proz.com, the largest translator directory), **TranslatorsCafe**, **Gengo**, **Smartling/agency** roles, and localisation jobs on Upwork.
4. Learn a CAT tool (Computer-Assisted Translation) such as the free version of **Matecat** or **OmegaT**.
5. For Kiswahili, offer **localisation** (adapting apps and websites, not just word-for-word translation) and **reviewing machine translation** (post-editing), a growing service.

**Watch out.** Machine translation (Google Translate) is fast but makes meaning errors; clients pay for accuracy. Never deliver raw machine translation as human work.

### 8. CV, cover letter and LinkedIn writing

**What it is.** Writing professional CVs, cover letters and LinkedIn profiles for job seekers, a strong local market (graduates, people applying abroad, public service applicants).

**How to start:**
1. Learn modern CV standards: 1–2 pages, achievements with numbers, ATS-friendly format (simple layout, standard headings, no text in images).
2. Create 3 sample CVs (with fictional names) for different levels: graduate, mid-career, executive.
3. Offer services on Fiverr, LinkedIn, Facebook groups for job seekers, and campuses.
4. Package: CV + cover letter + LinkedIn update. Add an interview prep call as an upsell (method 99).

**Pay.** Locally, CV writing is often priced from a few hundred to a few thousand shillings; international clients pay more.

**Ethics.** Never invent qualifications or experience. Present real achievements well.

---

### 9. Grant and proposal writing

**What it is.** Writing funding applications for NGOs, community groups, startups, researchers and schools; and business proposals and tenders for companies.

**Who it suits.** Strong writers who understand projects, budgets and logical frameworks; people with NGO or business experience.

**How to start:**
1. Learn grant structure: problem statement, objectives, activities, logframe, monitoring and evaluation, budget, sustainability.
2. Study real open calls (foundations, embassies, development agencies) and their requirements.
3. Volunteer to write one proposal for a community group or youth group to build a sample (with permission).
4. Market to NGOs, CBOs, startups and consultants; network on LinkedIn.

**Pay.** Usually per proposal. Avoid "success fee only" arrangements as a beginner (you work for free if the grant isn't won), and never promise that a grant will be won.

---

### 10. Product descriptions

**What it is.** Writing the text for products in online shops: Jumia and Kilimall sellers, Shopify stores, supermarkets going online, Amazon sellers abroad.

**How to start:**
1. Write samples for 10 products in one category (phones, beauty, kitchen): title, 3–5 benefit bullets, specifications, and a short paragraph.
2. Learn marketplace rules (title length, keywords, banned claims).
3. Offer bulk packages to online sellers: "50 product descriptions".
4. Find clients: Kenyan sellers on Jumia/Kilimall, Instagram shops, e-commerce agencies.

---

### 11. Email newsletter writing

**What it is.** Writing regular emails for businesses and creators: newsletters, welcome sequences, promotions.

**How to start:**
1. Learn email basics: subject lines, one main message, clear call to action, mobile-friendly.
2. Learn a tool: **Mailchimp**, **MailerLite** or **Brevo** (free tiers).
3. Start your own small newsletter (e.g. weekly tech tips) as proof.
4. Offer monthly packages ("4 newsletters per month") to businesses, schools and coaches; combine with method 73 (email marketing).

---

### 12. Scriptwriting

**What it is.** Writing scripts for YouTube videos, adverts, explainer videos, podcasts, radio ads and TikTok content.

**How to start:**
1. Study successful videos: write down how they hook viewers in the first 10 seconds, structure, and end with a call to action.
2. Write sample scripts: a 60-second advert, a 7-minute YouTube explainer, a 30-second TikTok.
3. Offer to YouTubers (many need scripts for faceless channels), agencies and businesses making video ads.
4. Learn the format: two-column scripts (visuals | audio) for video.

## Turning writing into a stable income

1. **Niche down:** "fintech writer" earns more than "general writer".
2. **Raise prices gradually** as reviews grow.
3. **Retainers:** offer monthly packages (e.g. 8 blog posts a month) for predictable income.
4. **Upsell:** writing + images + publishing on WordPress; copy + design via a designer partner.
5. **Keep learning:** SEO, marketing, a speciality subject.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Applying without samples | Build 3–5 samples first |
| Copying from other websites | Plagiarism ends careers; always write originally and cite sources |
| Submitting unedited AI text | Use AI as an assistant only; fact-check and rewrite |
| Missing deadlines | Promise realistic dates; communicate early if delayed |
| Writing assignments for students | Choose legitimate writing work |
| Accepting long unpaid "tests" | Offer a short paid test or your samples |

## Practice tasks

1. Choose two niches and write one 1,000-word sample in each.
2. Rewrite the home page of a small business website (as practice) using AIDA.
3. Translate a 300-word health notice from English to Kiswahili (or another language you know).
4. Write a CV for a fictional graduate using ATS-friendly formatting.
5. Create an Upwork or Fiverr profile draft: headline, overview, three portfolio items.

## Summary

- Writing work includes articles, SEO content, copywriting, ghostwriting, technical writing, editing, translation, CVs, grants, product descriptions, newsletters and scripts.
- Build 3–5 excellent samples first; publish them in a simple portfolio.
- Find work on Upwork, Fiverr, PeopleperHour, ProBlogger, ProZ, LinkedIn and by pitching Kenyan businesses directly.
- Avoid plagiarism, unedited AI text, unpaid long tests and academic cheating.
- Grow income by specialising, retainers and upselling.
