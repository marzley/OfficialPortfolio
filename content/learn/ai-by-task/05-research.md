# Best AI for research: answers with sources, academic papers and deep research

Research is where AI can save you hours, and also where it can embarrass you most. A chat assistant answering from memory can **confidently invent facts and references**. The best research tools search the web or real papers and **show their sources**, so you can check.

## The best AI tools for research

| Tool | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Perplexity** | Quick answers with numbered sources | Good everyday research tool; click the sources |
| **Deep research modes** in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude | Long reports that search many websites | Takes minutes; useful for market research and background reading; still check sources |
| **NotebookLM** | Answers only from documents *you* upload | Great for reports, policies, books, your own notes; less risk of invented facts |
| **Elicit** | Academic research: finding papers and pulling out findings | Built for literature reviews |
| **Consensus** | "What does the research say about…?" from scientific papers | Shows how studies agree or disagree |
| **Semantic Scholar** / **Google Scholar** | Finding real papers to read and cite | Scholar is not "AI chat" but is essential |
| **Scite** | Seeing whether a paper is supported or disputed by later papers | Useful for academic writing |

**Best for most people:** Perplexity (or your chat assistant's search mode) for quick answers; NotebookLM for your own documents; Elicit or Consensus plus Google Scholar for academic work.

## A reliable research workflow

1. **Ask a clear question**, with place and time: "What are the KRA requirements for registering a small business in Kenya in 2026?"
2. **Use a tool that shows sources.**
3. **Open the sources.** Prefer official sites (KRA, eCitizen, Kenya Law, ministries, universities) over blogs.
4. **Check dates.** Laws, fees and prices change.
5. **Cross-check** important facts in a second source.
6. **Write your own conclusion**, citing the sources you actually read.

## Ready prompts

**Background research**
```text
Give me an overview of the poultry farming market in Kenya: demand, typical costs, main risks and regulations.
Cite your sources with links, prefer official and recent sources, and tell me what you're unsure about.
```

**Compare options**
```text
Compare M-Pesa Till, Paybill and Pochi la Biashara for a small shop: fees, setup steps and who each suits.
Use a table and link to Safaricom's official pages where possible.
```

**Academic search**
```text
Find peer-reviewed studies (2018 or later) on mobile money and household savings in East Africa.
List title, authors, year and a one-line finding for each, with a link. Don't invent papers.
```

## Fake references: how to spot them

- Search the exact title in Google Scholar. Can't find it? Assume it's fake.
- Check the journal exists and the authors are real.
- Watch for impossible details: future dates, wrong page numbers, a famous author in the wrong field.
- Never cite a paper you haven't opened.

:::warning Research that affects health, money or the law
For medical, legal, tax or investment questions, use AI only to understand the topic and prepare questions. Confirm with official sources and a qualified professional (doctor, advocate, accountant).
:::

:::think You ask an AI for statistics on youth unemployment in Kenya for a proposal. It gives a precise figure with no source. What do you do?
Don't use it as is. Ask for the source, or search official sources yourself (Kenya National Bureau of Statistics, World Bank, ILO). Use the official figure with its year and a citation. If you can't verify the number, don't put it in the proposal.
:::

## Summary

- Use tools that show sources (Perplexity, deep research modes) and open the sources.
- NotebookLM answers from your own documents.
- Elicit, Consensus, Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar and Scite for academic work.
- Check dates, prefer official sources, never cite papers you haven't read.
