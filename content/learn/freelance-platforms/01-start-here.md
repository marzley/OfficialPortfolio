# Start here: how freelance platforms differ, fees, getting paid in Kenya and choosing where to start

There are dozens of places to find freelance work. They're not all the same: some are open marketplaces where anyone can bid, some are **vetted networks** that test you first, some are **specialist** sites for one skill, and some are **job boards** for remote contracts and jobs. This subject reviews **37 of them** in detail.

For step-by-step setup of the two biggest, see [Upwork step by step](./?track=freelancing&lesson=upwork-step-by-step) and [Fiverr step by step](./?track=freelancing&lesson=fiverr-step-by-step).

## Four kinds of platform

| Kind | How it works | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| **Open marketplace** | You bid on jobs or list services; clients choose | You're starting and need reviews |
| **Vetted network** | You pass skills tests and interviews; they match you with clients | You're experienced (usually developers) |
| **Specialist site** | One skill only: design, translation, WordPress, editing, voice | You have a clear niche |
| **Remote job board** | Companies post contracts and jobs; you apply directly | You want longer contracts or a remote job |

## Fees, in plain words

- Marketplaces take a **service fee** from your earnings (Fiverr is a flat 20%; Upwork charges 0–15% depending on the contract; others vary and change: check each site).
- Some also charge for **bids** or “connects” to apply for jobs.
- Vetted networks are usually **free for freelancers**: the client pays more.
- Job boards are free to apply; the employer pays to post.

**Price your work after fees.** If a platform takes 20%, a KSh 10,000 job pays you about KSh 8,000 before withdrawal charges.

## Getting paid in Kenya

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| **M-Pesa** | Some platforms (for example Upwork) let you withdraw to M-Pesa directly |
| **Payoneer** | Widely supported; withdraw to a Kenyan bank |
| **PayPal** | Withdraw to M-Pesa through the official M-Pesa–PayPal service |
| **Bank transfer** | Direct to a Kenyan bank account, with fees |
| **Wise** | Receive and convert foreign currency, withdraw to a Kenyan bank |

More detail: [Getting paid in Kenya](./?track=make-money-online&lesson=getting-paid-kenya).

## Rules that protect you

1. **Your own account, real ID.** Buying, renting or “borrowing” accounts breaks every platform's rules and you can lose all earnings.
2. **Keep payments on the platform** until you have a solid relationship and the rules allow otherwise. Off-platform payment is the most common way freelancers get scammed and banned.
3. **No fake reviews**, no paying for reviews.
4. **Never pay a “client” to start a job** (for training, software or equipment).
5. **Use a written scope** for every project (see [Discovery calls, quotes and contracts](./?track=freelancing&lesson=discovery-quotes-contracts)).

:::warning Details change
We last checked the key fees and payouts in **October 2026**. Fees, accepted countries and payment options change often. Confirm on each platform's official website before you rely on what's here.

## Summary

- Platforms come in four kinds: open marketplaces, vetted networks, specialist sites and remote job boards.
- Price after fees; vetted networks are usually free for freelancers.
- M-Pesa, Payoneer, PayPal and Wise are the main ways to get paid in Kenya.
- One real account, payments on the platform, no fake reviews, never pay a client.
