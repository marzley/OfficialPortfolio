# Specialist platforms by skill: 99designs, Dribbble, Behance, Reedsy, ProZ, Codeable, Voices and more

If you have a clear skill, a specialist platform puts you in front of clients who already want exactly that. Profiles and portfolios matter more than bids.

:::warning Check before you rely on it
Fees, accepted countries and payout options change often. Confirm on each platform's official website.
:::

## 15. 99designs (by Vista)

**What it is:** A design marketplace with contests and direct one-to-one projects.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | Logos, brand identity, packaging, web and app design |
| Fees for you | A platform fee per project |
| Getting paid | PayPal or Payoneer (check current options) |
| Kenya | Open to designers worldwide |
| Competition | High in contests |
| Best for | Graphic designers building a portfolio |

**How to start:** Enter a few contests to learn, then aim for direct projects once you have wins.
**Pros:** Lots of design briefs. **Cons:** Contests can mean unpaid work if you don't win.
**Watch out:** Never copy others' designs or use unlicensed fonts and images.

## 16. Dribbble

**What it is:** A design community with a jobs board and freelance project listings.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | UI/UX, product design, illustration, branding |
| Fees for you | Free to use; paid Pro features |
| Getting paid | Paid by clients directly or through the platform (depending on the feature) |
| Kenya | Open worldwide |
| Competition | High |
| Best for | UI/UX designers and illustrators |

**How to start:** Post polished shots regularly; complete your profile; apply to freelance listings.
**Pros:** Great for showing design work. **Cons:** Competitive.
**Watch out:** Verify clients before starting work off-platform.

## 17. Behance (Adobe)

**What it is:** Adobe's portfolio network, with a jobs and freelance hiring section.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | Design, illustration, photography, motion graphics |
| Fees for you | Free; freelance payments through Adobe may carry fees |
| Getting paid | Payment methods vary by country |
| Kenya | Open worldwide |
| Competition | High |
| Best for | Creatives who want a portfolio that clients discover |

**How to start:** Publish case-study style projects showing your process, not just final images.
**Pros:** Strong portfolio exposure. **Cons:** Discovery takes time.
**Watch out:** Watermark previews when sending work before payment.

## 18. Reedsy

**What it is:** A curated marketplace of editors, designers and marketers for authors and publishers.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | Book editing, proofreading, cover design, ghostwriting, book marketing |
| Fees for you | A fee on projects |
| Getting paid | Payment methods in your account |
| Kenya | Vetted; open to professionals worldwide |
| Competition | Selective |
| Best for | Experienced editors and book designers |

**How to start:** Apply with publishing credits or strong samples.
**Pros:** Serious clients. **Cons:** Hard to get accepted.
**Watch out:** Respect authors' confidentiality.

## 19. ProBlogger Jobs

**What it is:** A long-running job board for blog and content writers.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | Blog writing, content marketing, copywriting |
| Fees for you | Free for writers |
| Getting paid | Paid by the client directly |
| Kenya | Open worldwide; some jobs want specific locations |
| Competition | High |
| Best for | Writers with samples |

**How to start:** Keep a small portfolio of published posts; apply quickly to new listings.
**Pros:** Real writing jobs. **Cons:** Many applicants.
**Watch out:** Agree payment terms before writing; no unpaid “test articles” longer than a short sample.

## 20. ProZ.com

**What it is:** The largest community and job marketplace for translators and interpreters.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | Translation, localisation, interpreting, subtitling |
| Fees for you | Free basic membership; paid membership for more jobs |
| Getting paid | Paid by agencies or clients directly |
| Kenya | Open worldwide; Kiswahili and other African languages are in demand |
| Competition | Medium to high |
| Best for | Translators (including Kiswahili–English) |

**How to start:** Complete your profile, list language pairs, and check agencies' reputations on the platform's “Blue Board”.
**Pros:** Big translation network. **Cons:** Some low rates.
**Watch out:** Check the agency's rating before accepting work.

## 21. TranslatorsCafé

**What it is:** A translator community with job listings and an agency directory.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | Translation and localisation |
| Fees for you | Free and paid memberships |
| Getting paid | Paid by clients directly |
| Kenya | Open worldwide |
| Competition | Medium |
| Best for | Translators |

**How to start:** Build a profile and respond to listings.
**Pros:** More translation leads. **Cons:** Smaller than ProZ.
**Watch out:** Beware fake translation jobs that send overpayment cheques.

## 22. Codeable

**What it is:** A vetted marketplace for WordPress and WooCommerce experts.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | WordPress development, WooCommerce, site fixes |
| Fees for you | Clients pay the platform fee |
| Getting paid | Payment methods in your account |
| Kenya | Vetted; open to developers worldwide |
| Competition | Selective |
| Best for | Experienced WordPress developers |

**How to start:** Pass the technical screening with a strong WordPress portfolio.
**Pros:** High-quality WordPress clients. **Cons:** Strict vetting.
**Watch out:** Follow coding and security standards.

## 23. Kolabtree

**What it is:** A platform connecting scientists, researchers and data experts with businesses.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | Research, data analysis, statistics, medical writing |
| Fees for you | A fee on projects |
| Getting paid | Payment methods in your account |
| Kenya | Open to qualified experts worldwide |
| Competition | Selective |
| Best for | PhDs, statisticians, scientists |

**How to start:** Apply with your credentials and publications.
**Pros:** Uses deep expertise. **Cons:** Narrow audience.
**Watch out:** Don't do students' graded assignments: it's academic cheating.

## 24. Voices

**What it is:** A marketplace for voice-over talent.

| | |
|---|---|
| Work | Ads, explainers, e-learning, audiobooks, IVR |
| Fees for you | Paid membership options and fees on jobs |
| Getting paid | Payment methods in your account |
| Kenya | Open worldwide; African accents are requested for some projects |
| Competition | High |
| Best for | Voice actors with good home recording setups |

**How to start:** Record clean demos; audition quickly and professionally.
**Pros:** Big voice-over client base. **Cons:** Membership costs; many auditions.
**Watch out:** Never pay “agents” for guaranteed jobs.

## Summary

- Specialist platforms reward clear skills: 99designs, Dribbble and Behance for design; Reedsy and ProBlogger for writing and editing; ProZ and TranslatorsCafé for translation; Codeable for WordPress; Kolabtree for research; Voices for voice-over.
- A strong portfolio matters more than bidding.
- Respect copyright and confidentiality, and avoid academic cheating.
