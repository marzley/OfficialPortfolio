# Writing a CV, cover letter and formal letters: layout, content and examples

Your CV and cover letter are often the only things an employer sees before deciding whether to call you. Formal letters are still used for applications, complaints, requests to schools, banks, county offices and employers. This unit shows you exactly how to write and format a modern CV, a strong cover letter and standard formal letters in Word or Google Docs, with examples tailored to the Kenyan job market.

:::note What you will learn
- What a CV is for and how employers read it (seconds, and sometimes software)
- CV sections in order, with examples of strong wording
- Formatting a clean, ATS-friendly CV in Word/Docs
- Mistakes that get CVs rejected
- Writing a targeted cover letter
- Formal letter layout (block format), with an example
- Saving, naming and sending applications
:::

## How employers read CVs

- Recruiters often scan a CV in **seconds** at first, looking for relevant job titles, skills, education and achievements.
- Many organisations use **Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)**: software that reads CVs and filters them by keywords. Complicated layouts (text boxes, columns, images, tables for layout) can confuse ATS and lose information.
- So a good CV is **clear, relevant, easy to scan and simple to parse**.

## CV sections (in order)

### 1. Contact details (top)

```
WANJIKU KAMAU
Nairobi, Kenya · 0712 345 678 · wanjiku.kamau@gmail.com · linkedin.com/in/wanjikukamau
```

- Name in larger bold text.
- Phone (in a format employers can call), professional email, town (full address not needed), LinkedIn/portfolio link.
- **Don't include:** ID number, date of birth, marital status, religion, tribe, photo (unless specifically requested). This protects your privacy and avoids bias.

### 2. Professional summary (3–4 lines)

Who you are, your key skills and what you offer, tailored to the job:

> Accounting graduate (BCom, Finance) with internship experience in M-Pesa reconciliation, invoicing and QuickBooks. Skilled in Excel (pivot tables, XLOOKUP) and detail-oriented reporting. Seeking an accounts assistant role in a growing SME.

### 3. Skills

Group relevant skills; match words from the job advert (truthfully):

```
Technical: QuickBooks, Excel (pivot tables, XLOOKUP, charts), iTax filing, Google Workspace
Professional: financial reporting, reconciliation, customer service, time management
Languages: English (fluent), Kiswahili (fluent)
```

### 4. Work experience (most recent first)

For each role: job title, organisation, location, dates, then **3–5 bullet points of achievements**, not just duties.

```
Accounts Intern · Mavuno Hardware Ltd, Thika · Jan 2026 – Jun 2026
- Reconciled daily M-Pesa and bank transactions for 3 branches, reducing month-end errors.
- Prepared 120+ customer invoices monthly in QuickBooks.
- Built an Excel stock report that cut weekly reporting time from 3 hours to 45 minutes.
```

**Weak:** "Responsible for invoices." **Strong:** "Prepared 120+ invoices monthly in QuickBooks with 99% accuracy."

Use action verbs: *prepared, managed, organised, built, improved, reduced, trained, led, analysed, designed, sold, resolved*. Add numbers where possible (how many, how much, how fast).

No formal experience? Include **internships, attachments, volunteering, church/community roles, freelance work, school leadership, projects** (e.g. "Built a website for a local salon").

### 5. Education

```
Bachelor of Commerce (Finance) · Kenyatta University · 2022 – 2025 · Second Class Upper
Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) · Moi Forces Academy · 2021 · B+
```

Most recent first. For experienced professionals, education can come after experience; for fresh graduates, it can come before.

### 6. Certifications and training

Short courses, professional exams (CPA sections), Google/Microsoft certificates, Ajira Digital/Jitume training, first aid.

### 7. Optional sections

Projects, achievements/awards, volunteer work, publications, interests (only if relevant or interesting, e.g. "Captain, university chess club").

### 8. Referees

Two or three people who can confirm your work or character (supervisors, lecturers), with title, organisation, phone and email. **Ask permission first.** If space is tight, "Referees available on request" is acceptable unless the advert asks for referees.

## Formatting a clean CV in Word or Google Docs

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Length | **1 page** for students/graduates; **2 pages** for experienced professionals |
| Font | Calibri, Arial, Cambria or similar, 10.5–12 pt; headings 13–16 pt |
| Margins | 1.5–2.5 cm |
| Headings | Bold, maybe a thin line below (Borders → Bottom border) |
| Layout | Single column; avoid text boxes, images, icons for key information, and complicated tables (ATS-friendly) |
| Spacing | Consistent space between sections; use paragraph spacing, not empty lines |
| Bullets | Simple round bullets; consistent punctuation |
| Dates | Consistent format ("Jan 2026 – Jun 2026"), right-aligned with a right tab |
| File | Save as **PDF** unless the employer asks for Word; name it `CV-Wanjiku-Kamau.pdf` |

### Right-aligning dates with a tab

1. Type the job title and organisation.
2. Set a **right tab stop** at the right margin (click the tab selector on the ruler until it shows the right tab, then click near the right edge).
3. Press `Tab` and type the dates: they line up neatly on every line.

### Templates

Word (File → New → search "resume") and Google Docs (Template gallery) have CV templates. Choose simple ones; heavily designed templates with columns and graphics may not pass ATS.

## Common CV mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Spelling and grammar errors | Spell check + read aloud + ask someone to proofread |
| Unprofessional email | `firstname.lastname@...` |
| Generic CV for every job | Tailor the summary and skills to each advert |
| Duties instead of achievements | Action verbs + results + numbers |
| Too long (4+ pages) or crowded | 1–2 pages, white space |
| Personal details (ID, tribe, religion) | Remove them |
| Lying or exaggerating | Never: background checks and interviews expose it |
| Photos and fancy graphics | Simple, readable layout (unless photos are requested) |
| Sending `.docx` that looks broken on other devices | Send PDF |

## The cover letter

A cover letter (or application letter) explains **why you want this job** and **why you're a good fit**. Keep it to **one page**, 3–4 paragraphs.

```
Wanjiku Kamau
Nairobi · 0712 345 678 · wanjiku.kamau@gmail.com

2 October 2026

The Human Resources Manager
Mavuno Hardware Ltd
P.O. Box 1234-01000, Thika

Dear Hiring Manager,

RE: APPLICATION FOR ACCOUNTS ASSISTANT POSITION (REF: AA/2026/04)

I am writing to apply for the Accounts Assistant position advertised on
your website on 28 September 2026. As a Finance graduate with hands-on
experience in M-Pesa reconciliation and QuickBooks, I am confident I can
support your finance team from day one.

During my internship at a three-branch hardware business, I reconciled
daily M-Pesa and bank transactions, prepared over 120 invoices monthly,
and built an Excel stock report that reduced reporting time by 75%.
I enjoy accurate, organised work and solving problems with data.

I am particularly drawn to Mavuno Hardware's growth across Kiambu County,
and I would welcome the opportunity to contribute to efficient financial
operations as you expand.

My CV is attached for your consideration. I am available for an interview
at your convenience and can be reached on 0712 345 678.

Yours faithfully,

Wanjiku Kamau
```

Tips:
- **Address a person** if you can find the name ("Dear Ms. Wambui"); then close with "Yours sincerely". If you use "Dear Sir/Madam" or "Dear Hiring Manager", close with "Yours faithfully" (British convention common in Kenya).
- Reference the specific job and where you saw it.
- Show you researched the organisation.
- Match 2–3 of their requirements with evidence.
- Don't repeat your whole CV.

## Formal letters (block format)

Used for requests, complaints, resignations, applications to institutions and official communication.

```
[Your address]
P.O. Box 5678-00100
Nairobi

[Date]
2 October 2026

[Recipient's title and address]
The Principal
Highway Secondary School
P.O. Box 910-00200
Nairobi

[Salutation]
Dear Sir/Madam,

[Subject line, bold or underlined]
RE: REQUEST FOR TRANSFER LETTER FOR JOHN KAMAU (ADM. NO. 4521)

[Body: purpose first, details, request/action]
I am writing to request a transfer letter for my son, John Kamau, Form 2B,
Admission Number 4521, as our family is relocating to Eldoret in November.

I would be grateful if the letter, together with his academic report
for Term 2, could be ready by 25 October 2026.

Thank you for your assistance.

[Complimentary close]
Yours faithfully,

[Signature]

[Name]
Mary Wanjiru (Parent)
0722 000 000
```

Formatting: everything left-aligned (block format), one blank line (or paragraph spacing) between parts, a clear subject line, and a polite, concise tone.

### Common formal letter types

| Letter | Key content |
|---|---|
| Request | What you need, why, by when |
| Complaint | What happened (facts, dates, reference numbers), impact, what resolution you want |
| Resignation | Position, last working day (per notice period), thanks, handover offer |
| Recommendation request | Who you are, what it's for, deadline, attach CV/details |
| Internship/attachment application | Course, institution, period required, skills, attach introduction letter |

:::think A graduate sends the same CV and letter starting "Dear Sir" to 50 companies without changing anything. Why might they get few responses, and what would improve them?
Generic applications don't show fit for the specific role, often miss keywords that ATS and recruiters look for, and "Dear Sir" can seem careless. Tailoring the summary, skills and cover letter to each advert (keywords, the company's needs, the exact job title and reference) significantly improves the chances.
:::

## Sending applications

- Follow instructions exactly (email subject format, file type, reference number, deadline, portal).
- Name files clearly: `CV-Wanjiku-Kamau.pdf`, `Cover-Letter-Wanjiku-Kamau-AA-2026-04.pdf`.
- Email body: short and professional (see the email lesson); mention the attachments.
- Keep a spreadsheet of where you applied, dates and follow-ups.
- Beware of job scams: never pay to apply (see the scams lesson).

2. Rewrite three duties from your experience as achievements with action verbs and numbers.
3. Build a one-page ATS-friendly CV with right-aligned dates using a right tab.
4. Write a tailored cover letter for a real job advert.
5. Write a formal complaint letter about a delayed delivery, with a reference number and requested resolution.

## Summary

- A CV must be clear, relevant, scannable and ATS-friendly: contacts, summary, skills, experience (achievements), education, certifications, referees.
- Use action verbs and numbers; tailor to each job; remove personal details like ID and tribe; keep 1–2 pages; send as PDF.
- Cover letters are one page: the job and where you saw it, why you fit (evidence), why this organisation, and a polite close.
- Formal letters use block format: addresses, date, salutation, subject line, concise body, complimentary close and name.
- Follow application instructions, name files clearly and avoid job scams.
