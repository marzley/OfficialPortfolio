# Designing clear slides: layouts, text, colour, images, charts and the design rules professionals use

We've all sat through presentations with walls of tiny text, clashing colours and blurry clip art. Good slides do the opposite: they make your message instantly clear, support what you say, and make you look professional, whether you're presenting a school project, pitching to investors, training staff or reporting to a board. This unit teaches the principles and the PowerPoint/Google Slides tools to design clear, attractive slides.

:::note What you will learn
- The purpose of slides (support, not script)
- Planning: message, audience, structure
- Slide masters, layouts and themes for consistency
- Typography: fonts, sizes, and the "6×6" idea
- Colour and contrast
- Images, icons and visual hierarchy
- Turning data into clear charts and tables
- Alignment, spacing and Designer/Design Ideas
- Accessibility checks
:::

## What slides are for

Slides are **visual support** for a spoken message. The audience should **listen to you** and **glance at the slides** to understand or remember a point. If slides contain everything you say, people read instead of listening (and you might as well send a document).

| Slides should... | Slides shouldn't... |
|---|---|
| Show key points, visuals, numbers and structure | Contain your full script |
| Be readable from the back of the room | Have paragraphs of small text |
| Have one main idea each | Cram several topics |
| Look consistent | Mix fonts, colours and styles randomly |

:::tip Two documents, two purposes
If people need detailed information to take away, give them a **handout** or a PDF report. Keep the slides simple, and put extra details in the **speaker notes**.
:::

## Plan before you design

1. **Audience:** who are they, what do they know, what do they care about?
2. **Main message:** if they remember one sentence, what is it?
3. **Structure:** introduction (why it matters) → 3–5 key points → conclusion and call to action.
4. **Storyboard:** sketch slides on paper or write slide titles first (in PowerPoint's **Outline View**: View → Outline).

A common business structure: **Problem → Solution → Evidence → Plan → Ask**.

## Themes, layouts and the slide master

### Themes

**Design → Themes** apply a coordinated set of colours, fonts and backgrounds. Choose a **simple** theme; you can change colours (Design → Variants → Colors) and fonts (Variants → Fonts) to match a brand.

### Layouts

Each slide uses a **layout** (Home → Layout): Title Slide, Title and Content, Section Header, Two Content, Comparison, Picture with Caption, Blank. Using layouts (instead of random text boxes) keeps titles in the same place on every slide and makes slides accessible to screen readers.

### Slide Master

**View → Slide Master** controls the design of all layouts: fonts, colours, logo, footer, background. Change it once (e.g. add the company logo bottom-right, set title font), and every slide updates. Close Master View when done.

**Google Slides:** Slide → Edit theme (similar master editing).

## Typography

| Element | Recommended size (for room presentations) |
|---|---|
| Titles | 32–44 pt |
| Body text | 20–28 pt (never below about 18 pt) |
| Footnotes/sources | 12–14 pt |

- Use **one or two fonts** (e.g. a bold sans-serif for titles and a clean sans-serif for text).
- **Sans-serif fonts** (Calibri, Arial, Segoe UI, Montserrat) read well on screens.
- Avoid ALL CAPS paragraphs and decorative fonts.
- Left-align body text; centre only titles or short statements.

### Less text

The "**6×6 rule**" (a guideline): no more than about 6 bullets per slide and about 6 words per bullet. Better still: one key sentence and a visual.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Our company has experienced significant growth in revenue over the past financial year, with sales increasing from KSh 4.2 million to KSh 5.8 million, which represents an increase of approximately 38 percent." | **Sales grew 38% this year** (KSh 4.2M → 5.8M) with a simple bar chart |

Write slide titles as **messages**: "Mobile orders doubled after the M-Pesa button" beats "Sales data".

## Colour and contrast

- **High contrast:** dark text on a light background, or light text on a dark background. Avoid light grey on white or text over busy photos.
- **Limited palette:** 2–3 colours plus neutrals; use your brand or the theme's colours.
- **One accent colour** to highlight what matters (a key number, the best bar in a chart).
- **Projectors wash out colours:** test in the room if possible; dark text on light backgrounds is safest in bright rooms.
- **Colour meaning:** green for positive, red for negative/warnings (but don't rely on colour alone, for colour-blind viewers).

## Images and icons

- Use **relevant, high-quality** images; avoid stretched (distorted) or pixelated pictures. Hold `Shift` while resizing to keep proportions.
- **Full-bleed images** (covering the whole slide) with a short overlaid statement are powerful; add a dark transparent rectangle behind text for readability.
- **Icons** (Insert → Icons in Microsoft 365) explain ideas simply and consistently.
- **Crop** to focus (Picture Format → Crop); **Remove Background** for product photos.
- Use images you have rights to: your own photos, licensed stock, free sites (Unsplash, Pexels) or PowerPoint's stock images.
- **Compress pictures** (Picture Format → Compress Pictures) to keep files small for emailing.

## Visual hierarchy and layout

Guide the eye to what matters first:
- **Size:** the most important element is biggest.
- **Position:** people read top-left to bottom-right.
- **Contrast/colour:** highlight one thing.
- **White space:** empty space makes slides calm and focused.

### Alignment and spacing

- Use **Smart Guides** (dotted lines appear when objects line up) and **Arrange → Align** (left, centre, distribute horizontally) for neat layouts.
- Keep consistent margins; don't put things right at the edge.
- **Group** objects (`Ctrl+G`) that belong together.

### Designer / Design Ideas

In Microsoft 365, **Design → Designer** suggests professional layouts when you add pictures or lists. Google Slides has "Explore" suggestions in some versions. Use them as a starting point, then simplify.

## Data on slides

- Show **one key point per chart**; title the slide with that point.
- Choose the right chart: column/bar for comparisons, line for trends, pie only for a few parts (see the Excel charts lesson).
- **Simplify:** remove gridlines, unnecessary legends and 3D effects; label values directly.
- **Highlight** the important bar/line in an accent colour, grey for the rest.
- Avoid big tables: show the 3–5 most important numbers, or put the full table in a handout.
- **Insert → Chart** in PowerPoint (edits in an Excel sheet), or paste a chart from Excel (Paste Options: keep source formatting, or link data so it updates).

:::think A slide shows a 12-column table of monthly sales for 8 branches in 10-point text, titled "Branch performance". How could you redesign it?
Decide the key message (e.g. "Thika overtook Ruiru as our top branch in Q3"), make that the title, and show a simple bar or line chart of just the relevant branches/quarter with Thika highlighted. Move the full table to a handout or appendix slide.
:::

## Consistency checklist

- [ ] Same theme, fonts and colours throughout
- [ ] Titles in the same place (using layouts)
- [ ] Bullets and alignment consistent
- [ ] Images similar in style (all photos or all illustrations)
- [ ] Logo/footer from the Slide Master
- [ ] Slide numbers (Insert → Slide Number) for Q&A references

## Accessibility

- Use **slide layouts** with real titles (screen readers use them).
- Add **alt text** to images (right-click → View Alt Text).
- Ensure **contrast** and large fonts.
- Don't rely on colour alone.
- Use **Review → Check Accessibility** (PowerPoint) to find problems.
- Provide captions for videos.

## Practice tasks

1. Take a text-heavy slide and redesign it with one message title, 3 short bullets and an image.
2. Use the Slide Master to add a logo and footer to all slides.
3. Create a chart slide where the title states the insight and one bar is highlighted.
4. Build a 7-slide deck using Problem → Solution → Evidence → Plan → Ask.
5. Run Check Accessibility and fix the issues.

## Summary

- Slides support your talk; details go in notes and handouts.
- Plan audience, message and structure before designing.
- Use themes, layouts and the Slide Master for consistency.
- Large, simple text (titles 32+ pt, body 20+ pt); few words; message titles.
- High contrast, limited colours, one accent; quality images and icons; white space and alignment.
- Simplify charts and highlight the key point; avoid big tables.
- Check accessibility: layouts, alt text, contrast.
