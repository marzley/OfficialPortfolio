# Charts, SmartArt, tables, video and audio in slides

Numbers and processes are hard to follow as bullet points. PowerPoint's visual tools turn them into something an audience understands in seconds.

## Choosing the right chart

| Your message | Chart |
|---|---|
| Compare amounts (sales by town) | **Bar/column** chart |
| Change over time (enrolment 2020–2026) | **Line** chart |
| Parts of a whole (budget shares, max 5 parts) | **Pie/doughnut**, or a bar chart |
| Relationship between two numbers | Scatter chart |
| One key number | Just the number, big: **"KSh 2.4M raised"** |

## Inserting a chart

1. **Insert → Chart** → pick a type → a small Excel sheet opens.
2. Type or paste your numbers → close the sheet.
3. Or copy a chart from Excel and **paste** (choose "Use destination theme" to match your slides).

### Make charts readable

- Title the chart with the **conclusion**: "Nakuru sold the most in Q3".
- Delete clutter: gridlines, 3D effects, unnecessary legends.
- Highlight the important bar in your accent colour; keep others grey.
- Label values directly on bars when there are few.
- Large fonts: the back row must read it.

## Tables

**Insert → Table** for small comparisons (3–6 rows). For big tables, show only the key rows on the slide and share the full table as a handout or PDF.

## SmartArt: processes, cycles and hierarchies

**Insert → SmartArt** turns bullet text into diagrams:

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Process** | Register → Pay fees → Attend → Graduate |
| **Cycle** | Plant → Water → Harvest → Sell |
| **Hierarchy** | Organisation chart |
| **Relationship** | Venn diagrams, overlapping ideas |

Tip: type bullets first, then right-click the text box → **Convert to SmartArt**.

## Icons and shapes

- **Insert → Icons**: consistent, simple icons (e.g. phone, money, people) next to short points.
- **Shapes + text** for simple custom diagrams; align them with **Arrange → Align** and **Distribute**.

## Video and audio

- **Insert → Video → This Device** (or Online Video for YouTube, which needs internet during the talk).
- **Playback** tab: Trim, start automatically or on click, play full screen.
- **Insert → Audio** for background music or a recorded clip; you can also **Record Audio** narration per slide.
- **Slide Show → Record** turns your presentation into a narrated video (File → Export → Create a Video) for online classes or when you can't present live.

> Embedded videos make the file large. Compress with **File → Info → Compress Media**, and always test on the presentation computer beforehand.

## Accessibility

- **Alt text** on images and charts (right-click → View Alt Text).
- High contrast between text and background.
- Use the **Accessibility checker** (Review → Check Accessibility).

4. A column chart of expected monthly income for 6 months

## Why visuals matter in presentations

People understand and remember a picture or chart much faster than a paragraph of numbers. A sales trend, a budget split, a survey result or a project timeline is clearer as a chart or diagram. Donors, investors, managers and examiners often decide based on a few key visuals. But a cluttered or misleading chart damages trust, so knowing how to choose and design visuals is an important professional skill.

## Choosing the right chart: more detail

| Your message | Best chart | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Change over time (monthly sales) | Line chart | Pie chart |
| Comparing categories (sales by branch) | Bar/column chart, sorted largest first | 3D charts |
| Parts of a whole (budget split, 2 to 5 parts) | Pie or doughnut, or a 100% stacked bar | Pie with 10 slices |
| Relationship between two numbers (ads vs sales) | Scatter chart | Line chart |
| Progress to a target | Bar with a target line, or a big number | Gauges with tiny text |
| Single important number | A big number on its own ("2,450 new customers") | A chart with one bar |
| Ranking | Horizontal bar chart | Vertical columns with long labels |

If a chart needs a long explanation, it's probably the wrong chart or has too much in it.

## Making a chart tell one story

1. **Start with the message**: "Nakuru branch doubled sales in 6 months."
2. **Remove clutter**: delete gridlines, borders, unnecessary legends and decimals.
3. **Highlight**: colour the important bar or line in your brand colour; make the rest grey.
4. **Label directly**: put labels on bars or at line ends instead of a separate legend.
5. **Use the title as the message**.
6. **Start bar charts at zero**: a cut axis exaggerates small differences and can mislead.

## Linking charts from Excel

- Copy a chart in Excel → in PowerPoint, **Paste Special → Paste Link** (or use Keep Source Formatting & Link Data).
- When the Excel data changes, update the chart in PowerPoint (right-click → Edit Data / Refresh Data).
- Useful for monthly reports where the same slides are updated with new numbers.
- When sending the file to others, linked data may break; paste as a picture or embed for the final version.

## Tables that are easy to read

- Keep to essential rows and columns (5 to 7 rows is plenty on a slide).
- Right-align numbers, and use the same number of decimals.
- Light row banding or thin lines; no heavy borders.
- Highlight the key row or column.
- If the audience needs every number, give a printed handout instead.

## Diagrams beyond SmartArt

| Diagram | Use |
|---|---|
| Process (arrows) | Steps: register → pay → receive receipt |
| Timeline | Project milestones, history |
| Cycle | Repeating processes (plan → do → check → improve) |
| Hierarchy / org chart | Team structure |
| Venn diagram | Overlapping groups |
| Matrix (2 × 2) | Prioritising: urgent/important, cost/benefit |
| Funnel | Sales stages: visitors → leads → customers |

SmartArt can be converted to shapes (SmartArt Design → Convert → Convert to Shapes) for full design control.

## Icons and 3D models

- **Insert → Icons** (Microsoft 365) offers thousands of simple, editable icons; recolour them to match your theme.
- Use icons consistently (same style: all outline or all filled).
- **3D models** can be rotated live in a presentation, useful for product demos or technical subjects.

## Video and audio done right

| Tip | Why |
|---|---|
| Keep clips short (under 1 to 2 minutes) | Holds attention |
| Trim video inside PowerPoint (Playback → Trim Video) | Show only the relevant part |
| Set Start: Automatically or When Clicked | Control timing |
| Embed instead of linking local files | Links break when the file moves |
| Compress media (File → Info → Compress Media) | Smaller file for email and sharing |
| Test sound on the presentation computer | Many venues have audio problems |
| Have a backup screenshot or link | In case the video fails |
| Online videos (Insert → Video → Online Videos) need internet | Download permitted videos beforehand if the venue has poor Wi-Fi |

Only use videos and music you have the right to use; for YouTube content, linking or embedding through the official player is the safe approach.

## Recording a presentation with narration

**Slide Show → Record** (or Record tab) lets you record narration, webcam video and ink annotations, then **File → Export → Create a Video** produces an MP4. Useful for online classes, training videos and sending pitches to people who can't attend live.

## Accessibility for visuals

- Add **alt text** describing what a chart shows ("Line chart: sales rose from 120 in January to 310 in June").
- Don't rely on colour alone; add labels or patterns.
- Use high contrast and large fonts in charts.
- Run **Review → Check Accessibility** to find missing alt text, low contrast and reading-order problems.
- Add captions or subtitles to videos where possible.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| 3D pie chart with 12 slices | Bar chart sorted by size, or group small items as "Other" |
| Tiny chart labels | At least 18 points |
| Too many colours | One highlight colour plus grey |
| Copy-pasted blurry screenshots of tables | Rebuild the table or paste a linked chart |
| Huge embedded videos making a 300 MB file | Trim and compress media |
| Axis not starting at zero on bar charts | Start at zero to avoid misleading |

:::think A presenter shows a bar chart where Branch A's bar looks three times taller than Branch B's, but the numbers are 9,800 and 9,400. What's wrong?
The vertical axis doesn't start at zero (it probably starts around 9,200), which exaggerates a small 4% difference into what looks like a huge gap. Bar charts should start at zero; if the small difference matters, state it in the title or show the percentage change.
:::
