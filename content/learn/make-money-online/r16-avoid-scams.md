# Avoiding online money scams: the 15 most common traps in Kenya, how they work and how to protect yourself

Wherever people look for money online, scammers follow. Kenyans lose money every day to fake jobs, "investment" groups, forex signal sellers, task apps, SIM swaps and fake buyers. Scammers are organised, patient and good at psychology. The best protection is understanding **how** each scam works, so you recognise the pattern even when the story changes.

This unit is one of the most important in the subject. Read it fully, and share it with friends and family.

:::note What you will learn
- The psychology scammers use
- 15 common scams, how each works and the warning signs
- Checking whether a company or offer is real
- Protecting your M-Pesa, accounts and identity
- What to do if you've been scammed, and where to report
:::

## How scammers think

Almost every scam uses some of these levers:

| Lever | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| **Greed / big returns** | "Double your money in 7 days" |
| **Urgency** | "Offer ends tonight", "Pay now or lose the job" |
| **Authority** | Fake Safaricom, KRA, bank or police staff |
| **Social proof** | Screenshots of "payouts", fake testimonials, groups full of fake members |
| **Fear** | "Your account will be blocked", "There's a warrant for your arrest" |
| **Small first win** | Paying you small amounts at first to win trust before the big ask |

:::warning The golden rule
If you have to **pay money to receive money** (a fee to get a job, to withdraw earnings, to unlock tasks, to release a prize or loan), it's almost certainly a scam.
:::

## The 15 common scams

### 1. Fake job offers
"Hiring data entry clerks, KSh 3,000 per day, work from home." You pay for "registration", "training" or "a starter kit" and never hear from them again. **Signs**: pay to apply, chat-only interviews, salary far too high, Gmail addresses for a "big company".

### 2. Task / "like and earn" apps
You're paid to like videos or rate products. Small payouts arrive, then you're told to deposit money to unlock higher tasks or "complete a combination order". The deposits grow until you can't withdraw anything. **Signs**: deposits required, "VIP levels", Telegram groups with "mentors".

### 3. Ponzi and pyramid "investments"
Returns are paid from new members' money, not real business. They collapse, and most people lose. **Signs**: guaranteed high returns, rewards for recruiting, no clear product, pressure to reinvest.

### 4. Forex and crypto signal scams
"Account managers" promise to trade for you with 30% weekly profits, or sell "100% accurate signals". Fake dashboards show profits until you try to withdraw. **Signs**: guaranteed profits, unlicensed brokers, requests to send money to personal M-Pesa numbers. Check whether a forex broker is licensed by the **Capital Markets Authority (CMA)**.

### 5. Fake loan apps
You're asked to pay an "insurance" or "processing" fee before a loan is released. **Signs**: upfront fees, apps outside official stores, unrealistic approval. Licensed digital lenders are listed by the **Central Bank of Kenya**.

### 6. "Wrong number" M-Pesa reversal
You receive an SMS that looks like an M-Pesa credit, then a call: "I sent money to you by mistake, please send it back." No money actually arrived. **Protection**: always check your actual M-Pesa balance or statement. Genuine reversals are handled by Safaricom (via 456), not by sending money back to a stranger.

### 7. SIM swap and impersonation
Scammers call pretending to be Safaricom or a bank, ask for your PIN or a code sent to your phone, then take over your line or account. **Protection**: Safaricom and banks never ask for your PIN. Never share one-time codes.

### 8. Fake buyers and overpayment
A "buyer" sends a fake payment confirmation (or overpays) and asks you to send the "balance" back. **Protection**: confirm payments in your actual statement before releasing goods.

### 9. Fake sellers
Cheap phones, cars or land advertised online; you pay a deposit and the seller disappears. **Protection**: meet in safe public places, use pay-on-delivery, verify land at the registry with official searches, never pay large deposits to strangers.

### 10. Account recovery and "verification" scams
Messages say your Facebook/Instagram/WhatsApp will be disabled unless you "verify" through a link. The link steals your password. **Protection**: never log in through links in messages; turn on 2-step verification.

### 11. Freelance platform scams
A "client" asks you to move off Upwork/Fiverr to Telegram, pay for "software" to do the job, or do large unpaid "tests". **Protection**: keep communication and payment on the platform until you know the client well.

### 12. Fake scholarship, visa and overseas job agents
Fees for guaranteed visas, overseas jobs or scholarships. **Protection**: verify agencies with the **National Employment Authority (NEA)** for overseas jobs; real scholarships don't charge application fees in most cases.

### 13. Prize and promotion scams
"You've won KSh 50,000 in a Safaricom promotion, send KSh 1,000 to release it." **Protection**: you can't win a competition you didn't enter; real promotions don't ask for fees.

### 14. Romance scams
Someone builds an online relationship over weeks, then has an emergency: hospital fees, customs charges for a "gift". **Protection**: be very cautious with anyone who asks for money and avoids video calls.

### 15. Fake "courses" and "mentorship"
Gurus sell expensive programmes promising guaranteed income, mostly teaching you to recruit others into the same programme. **Protection**: look for real, verifiable results, free previews and refund policies.

## How to check if something is real

1. **Search** the company name + "scam", "reviews", "Kenya".
2. **Verify registration**: business registration via eCitizen/BRS; CMA for investment and forex; CBK for lenders and banks; NEA for recruitment agencies.
3. **Check contact details**: official website, physical address, company email domain, LinkedIn employees.
4. **Ask someone you trust** before paying. Scammers rush you because they know a second opinion stops them.
5. **Test with no money**: a real job or platform lets you start without paying.

## Protect your money and identity

- Never share your **M-Pesa PIN**, bank PIN, passwords or one-time codes.
- Use **2-step verification** on email, social media and freelance accounts.
- Use a **password manager** and unique passwords.
- Don't send ID photos and selfies to unverified "employers".
- Set an **M-Pesa** or bank transaction limit that fits your needs.
- Check your phone line works normally; if you suddenly lose network, contact your provider quickly (possible SIM swap).

## If you've been scammed

1. **Act fast**: call your bank or Safaricom (for M-Pesa, call 100/200 or use official channels) to request a hold or reversal if possible.
2. **Collect evidence**: screenshots, phone numbers, transaction codes, links, chat history.
3. **Report**: to the **DCI** (Directorate of Criminal Investigations) or your nearest police station; forward scam SMS to **333** (Safaricom's scam reporting short code); report fake accounts to the platform.
4. **Secure accounts**: change passwords, enable 2-step verification, check linked devices.
5. **Warn others** and don't be ashamed: scammers target everyone, including educated and experienced people.
6. **Beware "recovery" scammers** who promise to get your money back for a fee. They're another scam.

:::think A WhatsApp group shows daily screenshots of members earning KSh 10,000 from "AI trading robots". The admin says: "Deposit KSh 5,000 today, earn 20% daily, offer closes at midnight." Which levers and warning signs can you spot?
Greed (20% daily), urgency (closes at midnight), social proof (screenshots, group members) and probably authority ("AI robot"). Guaranteed daily returns are impossible in real trading. It's an investment scam or Ponzi; check CMA licensing and don't deposit.
:::

## Summary

- Scammers use greed, urgency, authority, social proof, fear and small first wins.
- If you must pay to receive money, it's almost certainly a scam.
- Learn the 15 common patterns: fake jobs, task apps, Ponzi schemes, forex/crypto signals, loan apps, M-Pesa reversal tricks, SIM swap, fake buyers and sellers, phishing, freelance, visa, prize, romance and guru scams.
- Verify with eCitizen/BRS, CMA, CBK and NEA; never share PINs or codes; use 2-step verification.
- If scammed: act fast, collect evidence, report to DCI and 333, secure accounts and ignore "recovery" offers.
