# AI training and data annotation work

Every AI system, from ChatGPT-style chatbots to self-driving cars and voice assistants, learns from **examples that humans label and check**. Companies pay people around the world to:

- **Label images and video**: draw boxes around cars, people, potholes; tag objects.
- **Rate and correct AI answers**: read two chatbot answers, say which is better and why, fix mistakes.
- **Write prompts and ideal answers**: write questions and model answers in your area (maths, coding, law, medicine, Kiswahili...).
- **Transcribe and record voice**: read sentences aloud, or type out recordings, often in African languages and accents.
- **Check search results and ads** for relevance and quality.

Kenya is one of the world's biggest hubs for this work. Nairobi has companies doing it at scale, and many Kenyans do it remotely from home.

## Skills that get you accepted (and paid more)

1. **Excellent written English**: most tests check grammar, reasoning and following long instructions exactly.
2. **Attention to detail**: guidelines can be 30+ pages. Reviewers reject work that skips rules.
3. **A specialty**: coding, maths, finance, medicine, law or a language (Kiswahili, Dholuo, Kikuyu, Somali...) unlocks better-paid projects.
4. **Basic computer skills**: fast typing, Google Docs/Sheets, a reliable laptop and internet.
5. **Honesty**: don't use AI to do AI-training tasks unless allowed; platforms detect it and remove people.

## How to start (step by step)

1. **Prepare**: update your CV, list your education and specialties, and get a stable email address.
2. **Apply to 3 to 5 platforms** from the table that accept your country. Complete the profile fully.
3. **Pass the assessment**: read the instructions twice, take your time, and do it in a quiet place. You often get only one try.
4. **Start small and accurate**: quality scores decide whether you get more tasks. Speed comes later.
5. **Track your hours and payments** in a spreadsheet (see the Excel subject).
6. **Get paid**: most pay through PayPal, Payoneer or direct bank transfer. See [Getting paid in Kenya](./?track=make-money-online&lesson=getting-paid-kenya).

## Practise for the assessments

The tests usually look like this. Try them:

```quiz
Q: A guideline says "Draw a box around every vehicle, including parked ones and motorbikes". In a photo with 2 moving cars, 1 parked lorry and 1 boda boda, how many boxes do you draw?
A: 4 | four
Q: A chatbot is asked "What is the capital of Kenya?" Answer A says "Nairobi". Answer B says "Mombasa is the capital and largest port". Which answer is better? (A or B)
A: A
Q: Answer B above is wrong about what? Write the one-word topic.
A: capital | the capital
Q: An instruction says "Do not rate a response higher than 2 if it contains a factual error." A response is well written but says Mount Kenya is in Uganda. What is the highest rating you may give?
A: 2 | two
Q: Is it allowed to share your platform account with a friend so you both work on it?
A: no
```

## Red flags

- "Pay KSh 2,000 to unlock tasks" or "buy a verified account": **scam / against the rules**.
- Telegram or WhatsApp groups promising guaranteed daily pay for "AI jobs".
- Being asked for your M-Pesa PIN or ID photos by individuals (not the platform's official verification page).

## Is it a career?

Annotation can be a good **entry** into tech. People who do well move into **quality review**, **team lead**, **data operations**, **prompt engineering** or **machine learning** roles. Learn Python and SQL in this hub to grow from labelling data to working with it.
