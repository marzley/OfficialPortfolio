# Online tutoring and small online tasks: teaching platforms, lesson setup, micro-task sites and realistic earnings

Two very different ways to earn online share this unit:

- **Online tutoring** uses what you already know (a school subject, English, coding, music, Excel) to teach learners by video call or recorded lessons. Pay can be good and it grows with your reputation.
- **Micro-tasks** are tiny jobs done online (tagging images, short surveys, testing an app, recording your voice) that pay small amounts each. They need little skill but pay little, so they're best as a side income or a stepping stone.

This unit explains both in detail so you can choose wisely and avoid the many fake "earn by clicking" sites.

- Who uses online tutoring and what subjects are in demand
- Tutoring platforms vs finding your own students

## Part 1: Online tutoring

### Who needs tutors?

| Learner | Typical needs |
|---|---|
| Kenyan pupils and students (CBC, KCSE, IGCSE) | Maths, sciences, languages, revision, homework help |
| Diaspora families | Swahili lessons for children, Kenyan curriculum support |
| International learners | English conversation, exam prep (IELTS/TOEFL), coding, Swahili for travel/work |
| University students | Statistics, programming, research methods |
| Adults and professionals | Excel, computer basics, digital marketing, public speaking |

### Platforms vs your own students

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Tutoring platforms** (e.g. Preply, italki for languages, Superprof, and local platforms) | Students find you; payments handled | Commission (often high at first), competition, platform rules and country support vary |
| **Your own students** (WhatsApp, Facebook groups, schools, referrals, your website) | You keep everything, set your own rates | You must market yourself and collect payments |

Most tutors start on a platform or with referrals, then build their own client base.

### Step 1: Choose your offer

Be specific: "KCSE Chemistry revision for Form 3–4", "Swahili for diaspora kids aged 6–12", "Excel for job seekers in 4 sessions". A specific offer is easier to market than "I teach anything".

### Step 2: Set up your tools

- **Video call**: Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams.
- **Whiteboard**: Google Jamboard alternatives such as Microsoft Whiteboard, Miro, or a pen tablet; a phone pointed at paper also works.
- **Materials**: Google Docs/Slides, past papers you're allowed to use, worksheets you created.
- **Scheduling**: Google Calendar or Calendly (free plan).
- **Setup**: stable internet, headset, good light, quiet room, plain background.

### Step 3: Plan a lesson

A 60-minute lesson plan:

```
1. Warm-up (5 min): quick recap questions from last lesson
2. Teach (15 min): one concept with clear examples
3. Guided practice (15 min): solve problems together
4. Independent practice (15 min): learner solves, you watch and correct
5. Wrap-up (5 min): summarise, assign homework
6. After the lesson (5 min): send notes and progress update to learner/parent
```

### Step 4: Price and get paid

| Type | Example rates |
|---|---|
| Local primary/secondary tutoring | KSh 500–2,000 per hour |
| Exam prep and specialist subjects | KSh 1,500–3,500+ per hour |
| International learners | $8–30+ per hour depending on subject and experience |

Sell **packages** (e.g. 8 lessons paid upfront at a small discount). Collect payment before lessons via M-Pesa, bank or the platform. Have a cancellation policy (e.g. 12 hours' notice or the lesson is charged).

### Child safety and professionalism

:::warning Protect children and yourself
- Communicate with parents, not directly with minors on private apps.
- Keep sessions professional, in a visible space; consider recording only with parental consent.
- Never ask for personal photos or information unrelated to lessons.
- Follow platform child-protection rules.
:::

### Growing as a tutor

- Collect reviews and results ("improved from D+ to B in 2 terms").
- Run group classes (5–10 learners at a lower price each) to earn more per hour.
- Turn your best lessons into recorded courses or revision packs (see the digital products lesson).

## Part 2: Micro-tasks

### Types of micro-tasks

| Task | What you do | Example platforms to research |
|---|---|---|
| **Surveys and research studies** | Answer questions for academic or market research | Prolific (availability depends on country), research panels |
| **Website and app testing** | Record yourself using a site and speak your thoughts | User-testing platforms |
| **Data labelling** | Tag images, classify text, check AI answers | Annotation platforms (see the AI annotation lesson) |
| **Voice and video recording** | Read sentences for speech datasets | Data collection platforms, sometimes project-based |
| **Search and ads evaluation** | Rate search results for relevance | Rater roles through large vendors |
| **Small gigs** | Quick tasks on Microworkers-style sites | Low pay; check reviews |

Country availability changes often; many platforms accept or pause Kenyan workers depending on project needs.

### Realistic earnings

Micro-tasks pay small amounts per task: a few cents to a few dollars. Many people earn a little pocket money; some with consistent access and high accuracy earn more. Treat it as:
- A way to earn while learning a bigger skill
- Practice with online payments (PayPal, Payoneer)
- An introduction to AI data work, which can lead to better-paid specialist projects

### How to do well

- Complete your profile honestly; many sites screen by demographics and skills.
- Read instructions fully; accuracy scores decide whether you keep getting work.
- Work at the times tasks are released (often US/Europe working hours).
- Track your hourly earnings; drop sites that pay less than your time is worth.

### Fake task sites: red flags

- "Earn KSh 2,000 a day by liking videos" or "rating products"
- You must **pay a registration fee** or "upgrade to VIP" to withdraw
- You must **recharge/deposit** to unlock bigger tasks (a well-known task scam)
- Earnings grow only when you recruit friends
- No clear company, address or real reviews

:::think A site shows you "earned" KSh 4,800 in a week of tasks, but says you must pay KSh 1,500 "withdrawal activation" first. What's happening?
It's a scam: the balance is fake, created to make you pay. Legitimate platforms never charge to withdraw your earnings. Don't pay; stop using it and warn others.
:::

## Summary

- Online tutoring uses your knowledge to teach local, diaspora and international learners; specific offers sell best.
- Use platforms or find your own students; set up video, whiteboard, scheduling and a clear lesson plan.
- Charge per hour or by package, collect payment upfront, have a cancellation policy, and protect children.
- Micro-tasks (surveys, testing, labelling, recording) pay small amounts; good for side income and learning.
- Never pay to register, unlock tasks or withdraw: those are scams.
