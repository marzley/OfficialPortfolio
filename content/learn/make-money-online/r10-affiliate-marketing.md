# Affiliate marketing step by step: programmes, content that converts, tracking and honest disclosure

**Affiliate marketing** means recommending someone else's product with a special tracking link. When a person clicks your link and buys (or signs up), the company pays you a **commission**. You don't stock products, deliver anything or handle customer service. Your job is to help people make a good buying decision.

It sounds easy, and that is exactly why most people fail at it: they spam links with no audience and no trust. This unit shows how affiliate marketing actually works, who earns from it, where to find programmes that pay people in Kenya, and the exact process from your first link to steady commissions.

## Why companies pay affiliates

Advertising is expensive and uncertain: a company can pay for a billboard and sell nothing. With affiliates, the company pays **only when a sale happens**. That makes affiliates one of the lowest-risk marketing channels, so businesses from global giants to Kenyan online shops run programmes.

| Who | Why they use affiliates |
|---|---|
| **Online stores** (electronics, fashion, books) | Pay per sale, reach new audiences |
| **Software and web hosting companies** | Pay high commissions because a customer stays for years |
| **Online courses and digital products** | No stock cost, so they can share 30–50% |
| **Banks, insurance and investment apps** | Pay per sign-up or funded account (check regulations) |
| **Travel and booking sites** | Pay per booking |

## How it works, step by step

1. You join a programme and get a unique link, e.g. `https://shop.example/product?ref=kelvin123`.
2. A visitor clicks your link. The shop saves a **cookie** (a small note in the browser) with your ID.
3. If the visitor buys within the **cookie window** (often 1–90 days), the sale is credited to you.
4. After the **return/refund period**, the commission is approved.
5. Once you pass the **payment threshold**, the company pays you (bank, PayPal, Payoneer or M-Pesa depending on the programme).

:::define Key terms
- **Commission rate**: percentage or fixed amount you earn per sale (e.g. 5%, or KSh 500 per sign-up).
- **Cookie window**: how long after the click a purchase still counts.
- **Conversion rate**: the share of clickers who buy.
- **EPC (earnings per click)**: total earnings ÷ total clicks. Useful to compare programmes.
- **Payout threshold**: the minimum balance before you are paid.
:::

## Programmes you can join from Kenya

Availability and terms change, so always read the current terms page before you build content around a programme.

| Type | Examples to look up | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kenyan online shops | Jumia affiliate programme (KOL), other local e-commerce sites | Local products, local payment; commissions vary by category |
| Global marketplaces | Amazon Associates and similar | Best for international audiences; check payment options for your country |
| Web hosting and software | Hosting companies, website builders, VPNs, email tools | Often high one-off or recurring commissions |
| Digital product networks | Selar, Gumroad creators, ClickBank, Digistore24 | Courses and e-books; judge quality before promoting |
| Affiliate networks | Impact, CJ, ShareASale/Awin, PartnerStack | One account, many brands; approval needed |
| Freelance and fintech platforms | Referral programmes of tools you already use | Sometimes pay per sign-up; check the rules |

:::warning Check before you promote
Avoid "affiliate" schemes where you earn mainly by recruiting people who pay to join. That is a pyramid structure, not affiliate marketing. Real programmes are free to join and pay you for customers' purchases.
:::

## Step 1: Pick a niche and an audience

Affiliate income follows **buying intent**. Someone searching "best laptop for campus students under KSh 50,000" is ready to buy; someone watching a dance video is not. Pick a niche where people research purchases:

- Phones, laptops and gadgets for students
- Home and kitchen appliances
- Farming equipment and inputs
- Web hosting, website tools and online business software
- Books, courses and learning resources
- Baby products, fitness gear, beauty products

Choose something you understand or will genuinely test. Your experience is what makes people trust your recommendation over a random list.

## Step 2: Choose where your audience lives

| Platform | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Blog/website** | Ranks on Google for buying searches for years | Slow to grow |
| **YouTube** | Reviews and comparisons are watched by buyers; links in description | Needs consistent video work |
| **TikTok/Instagram** | Fast reach; "TikTok made me buy it" effect | Short attention, links harder to place |
| **WhatsApp channel / Telegram** | Direct, high trust with local audiences | Easy to become spammy |
| **Email newsletter** | You own the list; strong conversions | Must build the list first |

Start with **one** platform. Most successful affiliates pair a searchable platform (blog or YouTube) with one social platform for reach.

## Step 3: Create content that converts

These formats consistently earn because they answer a buyer's question:

1. **Single product review**: what it is, who it's for, pros, cons, real photos or test results, price, your verdict.
2. **Comparison**: "Tecno vs Infinix vs Samsung under KSh 20,000", with a clear table.
3. **Best-of list**: "7 best budget laptops for programming students in 2026", grouped by need.
4. **Tutorial**: "How to start a blog with [hosting]", where the tool is naturally needed.
5. **Problem → solution**: "My phone storage is always full: 5 fixes", recommending a memory card or cloud plan as one fix.

A review structure that works:

```
Title: [Product] review after 3 months: is it worth KSh X?
1. Quick verdict (2–3 lines + who should buy / who should skip)
2. Key specs table
3. What I liked (with real examples)
4. What I didn't like (be honest)
5. Alternatives (cheaper and better options)
6. Final verdict + link ("Check the current price on ...")
Disclosure line near the top
```

:::tip Honesty sells
Listing real cons and recommending a cheaper alternative when it suits the reader makes your audience trust you. Trusted creators get far more clicks over time than those who praise everything.
:::

## Step 4: Place links well

- Put the link where the decision happens: after the verdict, in comparison tables, and in the first lines of a YouTube description.
- Use clear call-to-action text: "Check today's price on Jumia".
- Use a link shortener or your own redirect (e.g. `yoursite.com/go/laptop-x`) so links are tidy and easy to update when a programme changes.
- Don't hide links behind misleading buttons.

## Step 5: Track and calculate

Most dashboards show clicks, orders and commission. Keep your own simple sheet too:

| Content | Clicks | Sales | Commission (KSh) | EPC (KSh) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Budget phones video | 400 | 12 | 8,640 | 21.6 |
| Laptop blog post | 150 | 3 | 4,500 | 30.0 |

Example calculation: a video gets 20,000 views. 2% click (400 clicks). 3% of clickers buy (12 sales). Average price KSh 18,000, commission 4%:

12 × 18,000 × 0.04 = **KSh 8,640**.

The same formula tells you what to improve: more views, a better click rate (clearer call to action) or a better conversion rate (better-matched products).

:::think You have 1,000 clicks on a link. Programme A pays 3% on a KSh 30,000 product with a 2% conversion rate. Programme B pays 30% on a KSh 2,000 e-book with a 5% conversion rate. Which earns more?
A: 1,000 × 0.02 = 20 sales × 30,000 × 0.03 = KSh 18,000. B: 1,000 × 0.05 = 50 sales × 2,000 × 0.30 = KSh 30,000. B earns more here, which is why you compare EPC, not just commission rate.
:::

## Disclosure and the rules

- **Always disclose**: "Some links on this page are affiliate links. If you buy through them I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you." Put it near the top, not hidden at the bottom.
- Follow each programme's rules: many forbid using their brand name in paid ads, self-purchases, cookie stuffing or posting links in private messages to strangers.
- Platforms (YouTube, Instagram) have "paid partnership" or disclosure settings; use them.
- Never invent results, fake reviews or fake scarcity ("only 2 left!") you can't verify.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Posting bare links in Facebook groups | Build content people seek out; groups often ban spam |
| Promoting 50 random products | Focus on one niche and a few products you trust |
| No disclosure | Disclose clearly every time |
| Ignoring refunds and cookie windows | Read terms; expect pending earnings to drop slightly |
| Only one programme | Diversify so one policy change doesn't wipe out income |

## Summary

- Affiliates earn a commission when their referral buys; companies like it because they pay only for results.
- The process: join → get link → visitor clicks (cookie) → buys within the window → commission approved → paid at threshold.
- Pick a buying-intent niche and one main platform; reviews, comparisons, best-of lists and tutorials convert best.
- Track clicks, sales and EPC; compare programmes by EPC, not just rate.
- Disclose every time, follow programme rules, and avoid pay-to-join recruitment schemes.
