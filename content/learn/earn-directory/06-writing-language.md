# Writing and language work

Businesses, schools, NGOs, media houses, job seekers and authors all need words: website text, blog posts, product descriptions, CVs, reports, translations, proofreading. Strong writers in Kenya can earn locally and from international clients, and writing skills also lift almost every other method in this directory.

## What all writing methods share

- **Core skills:** clear, correct English (or Kiswahili and other languages), research, structure, meeting briefs and deadlines.
- **Tools:** a laptop, Google Docs or Word, a grammar checker, and plagiarism awareness.
- **Portfolio:** 3–5 samples in the type of writing you want to sell ([portfolio with no clients](./?track=freelancing&lesson=portfolio-without-clients)).
- **AI rules:** many clients forbid AI-generated text or require disclosure. Using AI to brainstorm is common; submitting unedited AI text as your own usually breaks contracts and damages trust.
- **Academic cheating:** writing essays, assignments or theses for students to submit as their own is academic dishonesty; it violates university rules and platform policies, and it's not covered in this directory.

See also: [writing and language work overview](./?track=earn-online&lesson=writing-language-work).

## 73. Blog and article writing

**What it is:** Writing informative or engaging articles for company blogs, websites and publications.
**How it works:** Client gives topic/keywords → you research, outline, write, edit → client reviews → revisions → payment per article or per word.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Research, structure, readable style, basic SEO |
| Cost & equipment | Laptop, internet |
| Where to find work | Freelance marketplaces, content agencies, businesses directly, LinkedIn |
| Payment | Per article/word; PayPal, Payoneer, M-Pesa (local) |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium; specialists (tech, finance, health) earn more |
| Time to first income | Weeks |

**How to start:** Publish 3 sample articles in one niche on a free blog or LinkedIn; pitch businesses in that niche.
**Pros:** Huge demand; builds expertise. **Cons & risks:** Low-paying "content mills"; AI has reduced demand for generic content.
**Scam warning:** "Writing accounts" for rent or sale, and "pay to get writing jobs" groups.
**Kenya notes:** Kenyan businesses increasingly need local SEO content; niche expertise is the way past cheap generic work.

## 75. Copywriting (sales pages, ads, landing pages)

**What it is:** Persuasive writing that makes people take action: buy, sign up, call, book.
**How it works:** Research the audience and offer → write headlines, benefits, proof, calls to action → test versions.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Persuasion, understanding customers, concise writing |
| Cost & equipment | Laptop |
| Where to find work | Businesses, marketers, agencies, start-ups |
| Payment | Per project; some performance bonuses |
| Realistic earnings | Medium to high for skilled copywriters with results |
| Time to first income | Weeks to months |

**How to start:** Rewrite 3 real (anonymised) landing pages or ads as before/after samples; study classic copywriting principles.
**Pros:** High-value skill. **Cons & risks:** Results-driven; needs proof.
**Scam warning:** Courses promising "$10k months" from copywriting.
**Kenya notes:** Kenyan SMEs selling online need clear Facebook/Instagram ad copy and landing pages, often in English and Kiswahili.

**How it works:** Interviews to capture their voice and ideas → drafts → revisions; confidentiality agreements.

| | |
|---|---|

| Cost & equipment | Laptop, recording app for interviews |
| Where to find work | Executives, pastors, coaches, entrepreneurs, publishers |
| Payment | Project fees in milestones; retainers for posts |
| Realistic earnings | Medium to high for experienced writers |

**Kenya notes:** Many leaders and pastors want books but lack time to write them.

## 83. Grant and proposal writing

**How it works:** Read the call carefully → plan with the organisation → write problem, solution, budget narrative, monitoring and evaluation → submit before deadlines.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Persuasive structured writing, understanding of donors and logframes, budgeting basics |
| Cost & equipment | Laptop |
| Where to find work | NGOs, CBOs, start-ups, researchers, consultants |
| Payment | Fixed fee per proposal (ethical practice; success-only fees are discouraged by many donors) |
| Realistic earnings | Medium to high for experienced writers with successes |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Volunteer to help a local CBO with a small grant; study successful proposals and donor guidelines.
**Pros:** High value. **Cons & risks:** No guarantee of funding; tight deadlines.
**Scam warning:** "Grant agents" demanding fees to "release" grants are scams.
**Kenya notes:** Kenya has many NGOs and youth/women groups applying for local and international funding.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Native-level fluency in the target language, subject knowledge |
| Cost & equipment | Laptop; free or paid CAT tools |
| Where to find work | Translation agencies, NGOs, businesses, freelance platforms |
| Payment | Per word |

**Kenya notes:** English–Kiswahili, French, Arabic, Somali and Amharic pairs are useful in the region.

## Summary

- Writing methods range from blogs, SEO and copywriting to technical, grant, CV, editing, translation, scripts, PR, journalism and creative work.
- Specialisation and proof of results raise pay; generic content is under pressure from AI.
- Follow client AI rules, never write academic work for students to submit, and avoid "writing account" schemes.
