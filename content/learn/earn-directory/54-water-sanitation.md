# Water, sanitation and hygiene services

Clean water and good sanitation are basic needs, and many Kenyan homes, schools and markets still lack them. These businesses earn while improving public health.

## What these methods share

- **Public health rules:** county public health departments regulate many of these services.
- **Safety:** chemicals, sewage and confined spaces need training and protective gear.
- **Trust:** reliable service and clean facilities bring repeat customers.

## 841. Water quality testing

**What it is:** Testing borehole, tank and tap water for safety using test kits or a partner lab.
**How it works:** Collect samples → test or send to lab → report and advise.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Water testing, sampling |
| Cost & equipment | Test kits; lab partnership |
| Where to find work | Households, schools, water vendors, farms |
| Payment | Per test |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Weeks |

**How to start:** Train in water testing; partner with an accredited lab for full analysis.
**Pros:** Important for health. **Cons & risks:** Kits don't replace full lab tests.
**Scam warning:** Never issue “safe” certificates without proper tests.
**Kenya notes:** Water vendors and refill stations need regular testing.

**How it works:** Test water → recommend system → install → service filters.

**Pros:** Recurring filter sales. **Cons & risks:** Must match system to water quality.
**Scam warning:** Avoid exaggerated health claims.
**Kenya notes:** High fluoride in parts of the Rift Valley makes treatment valuable.

| | |

**Kenya notes:** Most rural and many peri-urban homes rely on septic systems.

## Summary

- Water and sanitation businesses include testing, purification, pay toilets, biodigesters, sanitary bins, handwashing stations, greywater, septic tanks, laundromats, portable toilets, solar water heaters and dispenser servicing.
- County public health rules and NEMA waste rules apply.
- Protect water sources and use licensed disposal.
