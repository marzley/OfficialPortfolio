# More crops and livestock

Adding to the farming methods already covered (poultry, dairy, pigs, rabbits, goats, avocado, macadamia and more), these crops and animals suit different regions and markets.

## What these methods share

- **Market first:** know your buyer before planting or stocking.
- **Permits:** some animals (game birds, ostriches) need Kenya Wildlife Service permits.
- **Extension advice:** county agricultural officers and KALRO offer guidance.

## 891. Turkey farming

**What it is:** Raising turkeys for meat, especially for festive seasons and hotels.
**How it works:** Buy poults → brood → grow → sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Poultry management |
| Cost & equipment | Housing, feed, poults |
| Where to find work | Hotels, festive buyers, butcheries |
| Payment | Per bird |
| Realistic earnings | Medium |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Start small; plan sales for December and Easter.
**Pros:** Good prices per bird. **Cons & risks:** Sensitive young birds.
**Scam warning:** Buy poults from reputable breeders.
**Kenya notes:** Demand peaks during festive seasons.

## 892. Ducks and geese

**What it is:** Raising ducks and geese for meat and eggs.
**How it works:** Buy ducklings → raise with water access → sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Poultry management |
| Cost & equipment | Housing, water source, feed |
| Where to find work | Restaurants, households, Asian and hotel markets |
| Payment | Per bird or egg |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Start with a few; find buyers first.
**Pros:** Hardy birds. **Cons & risks:** Smaller market than chickens.
**Scam warning:** Avoid “guaranteed buyback” schemes.
**Kenya notes:** Some hotels and restaurants buy ducks regularly.

## 893. Guinea fowl farming (with permit)

**What it is:** Rearing domesticated guinea fowl for meat and eggs.
**How it works:** Get a KWS permit → buy keets → rear → sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Poultry management |
| Cost & equipment | Housing, feed, permit |
| Where to find work | Hotels, specialty buyers |
| Payment | Per bird |
| Realistic earnings | Medium |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Apply to the Kenya Wildlife Service for a game farming permit first.
**Pros:** Premium meat. **Cons & risks:** Permit needed; flighty birds.
**Scam warning:** Never take birds from the wild.
**Kenya notes:** Guinea fowl are classed as wildlife in Kenya, so farming needs a permit.

## 894. Ostrich farming (with permit)

**What it is:** Rearing ostriches for meat, leather, feathers and tourism.
**How it works:** Permit → breeding stock → rear → sell or show.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Large-animal management |
| Cost & equipment | Land, fencing, permits, high capital |
| Where to find work | Hotels, leather buyers, tourists |
| Payment | Per bird or product |
| Realistic earnings | Medium to high |
| Time to first income | Years |

**How to start:** Visit existing ostrich farms; get KWS permits.
**Pros:** Several income streams. **Cons & risks:** High capital; long cycle.
**Scam warning:** Check the legality of any “ostrich investment” scheme.
**Kenya notes:** A few ostrich farms run in Kenya, some as tourist attractions.

## 896. Watermelon farming

**What it is:** Growing watermelons for markets and juice businesses.
**How it works:** Plant → irrigate → harvest in about three months.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Horticulture |
| Cost & equipment | Seeds, irrigation, pest control |
| Where to find work | Markets, juice vendors, supermarkets |
| Payment | Per fruit or tonne |
| Realistic earnings | Medium |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Choose hot, dry areas with irrigation.
**Pros:** Fast crop cycle. **Cons & risks:** Price drops at peak harvest.
**Scam warning:** Plan harvest timing to avoid gluts.
**Kenya notes:** Common in Kitui, Makueni, Coast and Ukambani regions.

## 898. Moringa farming and products

**What it is:** Growing moringa and selling leaves, powder, seeds and oil.
**How it works:** Plant → harvest leaves → dry and powder → pack.

| | |
|---|---|

| Where to find work | Health shops, exporters, local buyers |
| Payment | Per kilo |

## 900. Sorghum contract farming

**What it is:** Growing sorghum under contract for brewers and processors.
**How it works:** Sign contract → plant certified seed → deliver harvest.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Crop farming |

| Where to find work | Brewers, millers, processors |
| Payment | Per tonne on delivery |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |

**Kenya notes:** Brewers source sorghum from dry regions like Eastern and Western Kenya.

## Summary

- More farm options include turkeys, ducks and geese, guinea fowl and ostriches (with KWS permits), strawberries, watermelons, dragon fruit, moringa, indigenous vegetables, sorghum contracts, pawpaw and aloe.
- Find buyers first and use certified seeds and stock.
- Some animals and plants need permits.
