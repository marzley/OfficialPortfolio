# Automotive trades and services

Kenya has millions of vehicles and motorbikes, and they all need maintenance, parts and services. Skilled automotive workers increasingly find customers through Google Maps, WhatsApp, TikTok repair videos and Facebook groups.

## What these methods share

- **Training:** TVET courses and apprenticeships build real skills; certifications add trust.
- **Genuine parts and honest quotes** build long-term customers.
- **Online presence:** a Google Business Profile, before/after videos and reviews bring customers ([Google Business](./?track=marketing&lesson=google-business)).
- **Safety and law:** tools, chemicals and road safety; vehicle modifications must stay legal.

## 431. Auto electrician

**What it is:** Diagnosing and repairing vehicle electrical systems: wiring, lights, starters, alternators, sensors.
**How it works:** Workshop or mobile call-outs with diagnostic tools.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Automotive electrical training, diagnostics |
| Cost & equipment | Multimeter, diagnostic scanner, tools |
| Where to find work | Car owners, garages, fleets, matatu operators |
| Payment | Per job |
| Realistic earnings | Low–medium to medium |
| Time to first income | Weeks (after training) |

**How to start:** Train at a TVET; apprentice with an experienced auto electrician.
**Pros:** Modern cars need electrical specialists. **Cons & risks:** Keeping up with new technology.
**Scam warning:** Never tamper with odometers or emissions systems illegally.
**Kenya notes:** Hybrid and imported cars bring demand for diagnostic skills.
