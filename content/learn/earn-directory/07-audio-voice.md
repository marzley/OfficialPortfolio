# Transcription, audio and voice work

Interviews, meetings, court sessions, podcasts, sermons, lectures and videos all produce audio that needs typing, captioning, editing or voicing. This category suits people with good ears, fast typing, clear voices or audio skills.

## What these methods share

- **Equipment:** good headphones; for voice work, a decent microphone and a quiet space (a wardrobe full of clothes makes a cheap "vocal booth").
- **Skills:** listening, accurate typing ([typing skills](./?track=it-basics&lesson=keyboard-typing-shortcuts)), grammar, and audio software basics.
- **Confidentiality:** you'll hear private information; treat it carefully.
- See [transcription and audio work overview](./?track=earn-online&lesson=transcription-audio-work).

## 98. General transcription

**What it is:** Typing audio or video recordings (interviews, meetings, podcasts) into text.
**How it works:** Listen and type following a style (clean verbatim or full verbatim), with speaker labels and sometimes timestamps; quality checked.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Typing 50+ wpm with accuracy, listening, grammar |
| Cost & equipment | Laptop, headphones, foot pedal optional, free transcription software |
| Where to find work | Transcription platforms, agencies, researchers, podcasters |
| Payment | Per audio minute |
| Realistic earnings | Low; rises with speed and specialisation |
| Time to first income | Weeks (tests) |

**How to start:** Practise with public talks; take platform tests; learn style guides.
**Pros:** Flexible; entry-level. **Cons & risks:** Hard audio pays the same; tests are strict; AI transcription reduces simple work.
**Scam warning:** "Transcription jobs" requiring payment for software, accounts or training.
**Kenya notes:** Accents and multilingual audio are areas where humans still add value.

## 99. Captioning (closed captions)

**What it is:** Creating time-synchronised captions for videos, including sound descriptions, for accessibility.
**How it works:** Transcribe and time captions in caption software; follow reading-speed and line rules.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Transcription, timing, caption standards |
| Cost & equipment | Laptop, caption software (free options) |
| Where to find work | Captioning companies, YouTubers, educators, media |
| Payment | Per video minute |
| Realistic earnings | Low to low–medium |
| Time to first income | Weeks |

**How to start:** Caption a short video; learn SRT/VTT formats.
**Pros:** Growing accessibility demand. **Cons & risks:** Detail-heavy.
**Scam warning:** As above.
**Kenya notes:** Local creators and educators benefit from captions for wider reach.

## 100. Correcting AI transcripts (transcript editing)

**What it is:** Reviewing machine-generated transcripts against the audio and fixing errors.
**How it works:** Listen while reading, correct words, names, speakers and punctuation.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Listening, grammar, speed |
| Cost & equipment | Laptop, headphones |
| Where to find work | Transcription companies, media firms, researchers |
| Payment | Per audio minute |
| Realistic earnings | Low |
| Time to first income | Weeks |

**How to start:** Learn to use auto-transcription tools yourself and offer fast, accurate cleaned transcripts.
**Pros:** Faster than typing from scratch. **Cons & risks:** Lower rates than full transcription.
**Scam warning:** As above.
**Kenya notes:** Offer cleaned transcripts of Kenyan accents and code-switching (English/Kiswahili).

|---|---|

| Where to find work | Medical transcription companies, hospitals |
| Payment | Per line, minute or salary |
| Realistic earnings | Low–medium (decreasing as speech recognition improves) |

**Kenya notes:** Nurses and clinical officers with typing skills can apply medical knowledge.

## 104. Voice-over for ads and corporate videos

**What it is:** Recording your voice for adverts, explainer videos, corporate training, IVR phone systems and documentaries.
**How it works:** Receive a script → record and edit clean audio → deliver files; often directed live.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Clear, expressive voice; reading naturally; basic audio editing |
| Cost & equipment | USB/XLR microphone, headphones, quiet treated space, free software (Audacity) |
| Where to find work | Voice marketplaces, ad agencies, production houses, radio stations, freelance platforms |
| Payment | Per project, word or finished minute; usage rights affect price |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium; strong demos and repeat clients matter |
| Time to first income | Weeks to months |

**How to start:** Record a 60–90 second demo reel with varied styles; treat your recording space.
**Pros:** Creative; repeat clients. **Cons & risks:** Equipment investment; competitive.
**Scam warning:** Agencies charging big fees for "voice certification".
**Kenya notes:** Kiswahili and Kenyan English voice-overs are needed for local ads, NGOs and phone systems.

## Summary

- Audio methods include general, research, medical and legal transcription; captions and transcript editing; voice-over, audiobooks and IVR; podcast editing and audio cleanup; music production, mixing, jingles, sound design, session vocals, teaching and hosting.
- Local languages and good equipment create advantages; AI reduces simple transcription work, so add skills.
- Beware fees for "certification", software or label deals.
