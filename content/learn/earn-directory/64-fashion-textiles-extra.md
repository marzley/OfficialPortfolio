# More fashion and textile businesses

Adding to fashion and beauty (methods 463–477) and tailoring (267), these textile businesses suit both artisans and traders.

## What these methods share

- **Quality:** neat finishing and durable materials bring repeat buyers.
- **Deposits:** take deposits on custom orders.
- **Originality:** don't copy other brands' designs or logos.

## 924. Tie-dye and batik

**What it is:** Dyeing fabric into colourful patterns for clothes, décor and souvenirs.
**How it works:** Prepare fabric → wax or tie → dye → finish.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Dyeing techniques |
| Cost & equipment | Dyes, wax, fabric |
| Where to find work | Boutiques, tourists, online buyers |
| Payment | Per piece |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Weeks |

**How to start:** Learn from artisans; sell online and at craft markets.
**Pros:** Unique products. **Cons & risks:** Dye chemicals need safe handling.
**Scam warning:** Use colour-fast dyes so products don't run.
**Kenya notes:** Batik and tie-dye are popular with tourists and young buyers.

## 925. Knitting and crochet products

**What it is:** Making sweaters, scarves, hats, baby clothes and décor by hand or machine.
**How it works:** Take orders or make stock → sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Knitting, crochet |
| Cost & equipment | Yarn, needles or knitting machine |
| Where to find work | Parents, boutiques, online |
| Payment | Per item |
| Realistic earnings | Low |

**How to start:** Start with baby items and beanies; post on social media.
**Pros:** Low start-up. **Cons & risks:** Time-consuming by hand.
**Scam warning:** Get deposits for custom orders.
**Kenya notes:** Cold-season demand in highland towns.

**What it is:** Renting wedding gowns, bridesmaid dresses and evening wear.
**How it works:** Buy quality stock → rent with deposit → clean and alter.

| | |
|---|---|

| Payment | Per rental |

## 928. Mitumba bale wholesaling

**What it is:** Importing or buying bales of second-hand clothes and selling them to retailers.
**How it works:** Source bales → sell whole or graded to traders.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Trade knowledge, grading |
| Cost & equipment | High: capital, storage |
| Where to find work | Mitumba retailers, online sellers |
| Payment | Per bale |
| Realistic earnings | Medium |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Learn the trade at major markets before investing.
**Pros:** Large volumes. **Cons & risks:** Import rules and quality risk.
**Scam warning:** Follow import regulations and pay duties.
**Kenya notes:** Kenya's mitumba trade is huge; retailers rely on wholesalers.

## Summary

- More textile businesses include tie-dye and batik, knitting and crochet, bridal and suit hire, mitumba bale wholesaling and school sweater knitting.
- Quality, deposits and written rental terms matter.
- Respect originality and import rules.
