# Neighbourhood shops and retail

Every estate and village needs shops. Retail is competitive, so location, stock control and customer service decide success.

## What these methods share

- **Licences:** a county single business permit; some goods need extra licences.
- **Stock control:** track what sells; avoid dead stock.
- **M-Pesa:** a till number and good records help you grow.

## 866. Neighbourhood shop (duka) or minimart

**What it is:** Selling everyday goods (sugar, flour, soap, airtime) to nearby households.
**How it works:** Buy from wholesalers → sell at small margins → restock daily.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Stock management, customer service |
| Cost & equipment | Premises, shelves, stock |
| Where to find work | Estates, villages, near schools |
| Payment | Daily sales |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Days |

**How to start:** Choose a busy spot without strong competition; stock what neighbours ask for.
**Pros:** Daily cash flow. **Cons & risks:** Thin margins; credit to customers.
**Scam warning:** Limit credit to people you trust.
**Kenya notes:** Wholesalers and distributors now deliver to shops, often with digital ordering.

## 867. Hardware shop

**What it is:** Selling building materials, tools, paint and plumbing and electrical items.
**How it works:** Stock popular items → serve builders and homeowners → offer delivery.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Product knowledge, stock control |
| Cost & equipment | High: premises, stock, transport |
| Where to find work | Growing estates, rural centres |
| Payment | Daily sales |
| Realistic earnings | Medium |

**How to start:** Start near construction activity; build relationships with fundis.
**Pros:** Steady demand. **Cons & risks:** High capital tied in stock.
**Scam warning:** Avoid counterfeit electrical goods.
**Kenya notes:** Fast-growing towns create demand for hardware shops.

## 868. Cereals and grains shop

**What it is:** Selling maize, beans, rice, green grams and flour by weight.
**How it works:** Buy in bulk → store well → sell by kilo.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Grain quality, storage |
| Cost & equipment | Premises, storage, verified scale |
| Where to find work | Estates, markets |
| Payment | Daily sales |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Weeks |

**How to start:** Buy at harvest when prices are low; store properly.
**Pros:** Staple demand. **Cons & risks:** Price swings; pests.
**Scam warning:** Use a verified weighing scale: cheating customers is illegal.
**Kenya notes:** Cereal shops are common in every Kenyan market.

## 869. Milk ATM and milk bar

**What it is:** Selling fresh pasteurised milk from a dispensing machine, plus yoghurt and mala.
**How it works:** Buy from processors or coolers → dispense → sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Hygiene, customer service |
| Cost & equipment | Milk dispenser, Kenya Dairy Board licence, premises |
| Where to find work | Estates, campuses |
| Payment | Daily sales |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Weeks to months |

**How to start:** Get the Kenya Dairy Board licence; source from licensed processors.
**Pros:** Daily customers. **Cons & risks:** Spoilage if cooling fails.
**Scam warning:** Never sell raw unprocessed milk from unlicensed sources.
**Kenya notes:** Milk ATMs are popular in Kenyan towns.

## 874. Wines and spirits shop (licensed)

**What it is:** Selling alcoholic drinks legally.
**How it works:** Get a county liquor licence → stock → sell within legal hours.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Retail management, compliance |
| Cost & equipment | Premises, licence, stock |
| Where to find work | Towns, estates |
| Payment | Sales |
| Realistic earnings | Medium |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Apply for the county liquor licence and follow trading hours.
**Pros:** Good margins. **Cons & risks:** Strict rules; social responsibility.
**Scam warning:** Never sell to under-18s or sell illicit or counterfeit alcohol.
**Kenya notes:** The Alcoholic Drinks Control Act and county laws regulate licences and hours.

## 875. Mitumba (second-hand clothes) market stall

**What it is:** Selling second-hand clothes from a physical stall at a market.
**How it works:** Buy bales → sort grades → price and sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Sorting, pricing, selling |
| Cost & equipment | Stall rent, bale capital |
| Where to find work | Markets, roadside stalls |
| Payment | Daily sales |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Days |

**How to start:** Learn grading from experienced traders before buying expensive bales.
**Pros:** Popular with buyers. **Cons & risks:** Bale quality is a gamble.
**Scam warning:** Buy from reputable bale dealers.
**Kenya notes:** Selling online is method 230; a stall reaches walk-in buyers.

## Summary

- Neighbourhood retail includes dukas, hardware, cereals, milk ATMs, stationery, kitchenware, fabrics, baby shops, licensed wines and spirits, and mitumba stalls.
- Get the right county and product licences.
- Control stock and credit, and use honest scales.
