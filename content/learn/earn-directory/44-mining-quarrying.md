# Mining, quarrying and building materials (licensed)

Kenya's building boom needs stone, ballast, sand and murram, and several counties have gold, gemstones and other minerals. These are physical, regulated businesses: done legally and safely they create good jobs; done illegally they destroy land and rivers and risk lives.

## What these methods share

- **Licences:** minerals are governed by the Mining Act (2016) through the Ministry of Mining, with permits for artisanal and small-scale miners. Quarries and sand harvesting also need county permits and NEMA environmental approval.
- **Safety:** collapsing pits and quarries kill workers every year. Protective gear, safe slopes and no work in unstable pits are essential.
- **Land and community:** get the landowner's written agreement and engage the community.
- **Rehabilitation:** restore land after extraction (backfilling, trees).

## 722. Artisanal gold mining (licensed)

**What it is:** Small-scale gold mining by individuals or groups with an artisanal mining permit.
**How it works:** Dig and process ore → sell gold to licensed dealers.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Mining skills, safety awareness |
| Cost & equipment | Tools, permit, safety gear |
| Where to find work | Licensed gold dealers and buying centres |
| Payment | Per gram |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium; very uncertain |
| Time to first income | Weeks to months |

**How to start:** Join or form a registered artisanal miners' group; get the county permit.
**Pros:** Possible high value. **Cons & risks:** Dangerous pits; mercury poisoning.
**Scam warning:** Sell only to licensed dealers; 'gold' sold to you outside the system is a common scam.
**Kenya notes:** Migori, Kakamega, Siaya, Turkana and West Pokot have artisanal gold mining. Avoid mercury; safer methods exist.

## 723. Gemstone mining and dealing (licensed)

**What it is:** Mining or trading gemstones such as tsavorite, ruby, tourmaline and garnet.
**How it works:** Mine or buy rough stones → grade → sell to licensed buyers or cutters.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Gem identification |
| Cost & equipment | Licences (mining or dealer's licence), tools, testing equipment |
| Where to find work | Licensed dealers, exporters, jewellers |
| Payment | Per carat or piece |
| Realistic earnings | Medium to high; risky |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Learn gemology basics; partner with a licensed dealer before trading.
**Pros:** High value. **Cons & risks:** Fraud and fake stones.
**Scam warning:** Fake and glass 'gems' are common; test before paying.
**Kenya notes:** Taita Taveta and parts of the Rift Valley are known for gemstones; dealing needs a licence.

**How it works:** Quarry → cut stones → sell to builders and hardware stores.

**How to start:** Work in an established quarry first; then lease a licensed site.

**Kenya notes:** Machakos, Juja, Kiambu and Nyeri supply Nairobi's building stones.

## Summary

- Mining and building materials include artisanal gold, gemstones, building stone, ballast, sand, murram, paving, lime, equipment hire, testing and field work.
- Licences (Mining Act, county permits, NEMA) and safety are non-negotiable.
- Rehabilitate land and avoid mercury and illegal sand mining.
