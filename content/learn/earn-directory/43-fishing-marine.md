# Fishing, marine and lake economy

Lake Victoria, Lake Turkana, Lake Naivasha, the Indian Ocean coast and thousands of fish ponds support a big fishing economy. Beyond catching fish, there is money in trading, processing, ice, gear, boats, transport and tourism. These methods suit people living near water, and some (trading, processing, cold chain) work inland too.

## What these methods share

- **Licences and rules:** fishing and fish trading are regulated by the Kenya Fisheries Service (KeFS) and county governments, and landing beaches are run with Beach Management Units (BMUs). Passenger boats are regulated by the Kenya Maritime Authority (KMA).
- **Hygiene and cold chain:** fish spoils within hours without ice; buyers and supermarkets demand clean handling.
- **Seasons and closed seasons:** catches vary by season, and some species or areas have closed seasons to let stocks recover.
- **Safety on water:** life jackets, weather checks and seaworthy boats save lives.

## 708. Licensed small-scale fishing

**What it is:** Catching fish with a boat and legal nets or lines and selling at the landing beach.
**How it works:** Fishing trips → landing → sale to traders, hotels or processors.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Fishing skills, swimming, boat handling |
| Cost & equipment | Boat (or crew share), legal nets, licence, life jackets |
| Where to find work | Landing beaches, fish traders, hotels |
| Payment | Cash or M-Pesa per catch |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium; varies with season |
| Time to first income | Days (as crew) |

**How to start:** Start as crew with an experienced fisher; join your BMU; use only legal mesh sizes.
**Pros:** Daily cash income. **Cons & risks:** Dangerous weather, falling catches, theft of nets.
**Scam warning:** Don't buy 'permits' from people who aren't BMU or KeFS officials.
**Kenya notes:** Undersized mesh and monofilament nets are illegal and destroy fish stocks.

## 709. Fish trading (buying at the beach, selling in towns)

**What it is:** Buying fresh fish at landing beaches and selling to markets, estates and restaurants.
**How it works:** Buy early at the beach → ice → transport → sell same day.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Negotiation, quality checking, customer care |
| Cost & equipment | Cool boxes, ice, transport, trading capital |
| Where to find work | Town markets, estates, hotels, WhatsApp groups |
| Payment | Cash, M-Pesa |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Days |

**How to start:** Start small with one cool box and a few regular customers; build trust with beach suppliers.
**Pros:** Steady demand. **Cons & risks:** Spoilage without ice; price swings.
**Scam warning:** Beware of 'supplier' who asks for full payment before you see the fish.
**Kenya notes:** Omena, tilapia and Nile perch are the big sellers; many traders are women.

## 710. Fish smoking, drying and processing

**What it is:** Smoking, sun-drying or frying fish to last longer and sell further away.
**How it works:** Buy fresh fish → clean → smoke/dry on racks → pack → sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Processing and hygiene |
| Cost & equipment | Drying racks (raised, off the sand), smoking kiln, packaging |
| Where to find work | Markets, supermarkets (with certification), inland towns |
| Payment | Per kg or packet |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Weeks |

**How to start:** Use raised racks and improved kilns; package in clean branded packs.
**Pros:** Longer shelf life, sells inland. **Cons & risks:** Firewood costs; quality complaints.
**Scam warning:** Never sell spoiled or chemically treated fish.
**Kenya notes:** Dried omena sells across Kenya and to neighbouring countries.

## 711. Ice supply for fishermen and traders

**What it is:** Making or reselling ice for fish preservation.
**How it works:** Ice machine or bulk purchase → sell blocks or flakes at beaches and markets.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Basic business and maintenance |
| Cost & equipment | Ice machine or freezers, power (solar or grid), insulated storage |
| Where to find work | Landing beaches, fish traders, events |
| Payment | Per block or bag |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Weeks |

**How to start:** Survey demand at your nearest beach; start by reselling, then invest in a machine.
**Pros:** Essential and recurring. **Cons & risks:** Power costs and outages.
**Scam warning:** As usual: keep records and avoid credit to unknown buyers.
**Kenya notes:** Solar-powered ice and cold rooms are expanding on Lake Victoria beaches.

**How it works:** Stock gear → sell to fishers; mend nets for a fee.

| | |
|---|---|

| Where to find work | Fishers, BMUs, beach shops |

**How to start:** Learn net mending; stock only legal mesh sizes.
**Pros:** Constant demand. **Cons & risks:** Credit sales.
**Scam warning:** Selling illegal nets can lead to confiscation and fines.
**Kenya notes:** Life jackets are a valuable stock item: many fishers still lack them.

## 713. Boat building and repair

| | |
|---|---|

| Where to find work | Fishers, transporters, tourism operators |

**Scam warning:** Agree deposits in writing.
**Kenya notes:** Boats carrying passengers must meet KMA safety standards.

## 715. Passenger and cargo boat transport (licensed)

**What it is:** Carrying people and goods between islands, beaches and towns.
**How it works:** Scheduled trips or hire; fares per person or cargo.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Boat handling, safety, navigation |
| Cost & equipment | Seaworthy boat, life jackets for all, KMA licence and insurance |
| Where to find work | Island communities, traders, tourists |
| Payment | Fares |
| Realistic earnings | Medium |
| Time to first income | Months (licensing) |

**How to start:** Get KMA registration, safety equipment and insurance; train crew.
**Pros:** Steady income on busy routes. **Cons & risks:** Accidents are deadly if overloaded.
**Scam warning:** Never overload; life jackets for every passenger, every trip.
**Kenya notes:** Overloading and no life jackets cause many lake tragedies; safety builds trust and bookings.

## 716. Sport fishing charters

**What it is:** Taking tourists deep-sea or lake fishing.
**How it works:** Bookings via hotels and online → trip → photos and catch-and-release.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Fishing expertise, hospitality, boat skills |
| Cost & equipment | Suitable boat, gear, licences, insurance |
| Where to find work | Coastal hotels, tourists, online travel platforms |
| Payment | Per trip |
| Realistic earnings | Medium to high |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Crew on an established charter; build reviews online.
**Pros:** High-value clients. **Cons & risks:** Seasonal; costly equipment.
**Scam warning:** Take deposits only through clear booking terms.
**Kenya notes:** Malindi, Watamu and Shimoni are known for sport fishing; follow catch-and-release where required.

## 717. Seaweed farming

**What it is:** Growing seaweed in shallow coastal waters for sale to processors.
**How it works:** Plant on lines → harvest after weeks → dry → sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Farming, patience |
| Cost & equipment | Ropes, stakes, seedlings, drying space |
| Where to find work | Processors, cosmetics and food buyers, cooperatives |
| Payment | Per kg |
| Realistic earnings | Low |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Join a seaweed farmers' group (common in Kwale).
**Pros:** Low capital; suits women's groups. **Cons & risks:** Price depends on few buyers.
**Scam warning:** Avoid buyers who change prices after harvest; use group contracts.
**Kenya notes:** Kwale County seaweed farming has grown with community groups and processors.

**What it is:** Moving fish in insulated or refrigerated transport to supermarkets and institutions.
**How it works:** Contracts → collection → cold transport → delivery.

| | |
|---|---|

## Summary

- Fishing jobs go far beyond catching fish: trading, processing, ice, gear, boats, engines, transport, tourism, seaweed and supply.
- Follow KeFS, BMU and KMA rules; use legal nets; respect closed seasons.
- Ice and hygiene protect profits; life jackets protect lives.
