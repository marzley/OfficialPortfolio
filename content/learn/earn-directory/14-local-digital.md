# Local digital services

Not all digital income is "online". Across Kenya, people earn by bringing digital services to their neighbourhood: printing and typing, online applications, phone repairs, M-Pesa agencies, photo studios, Wi-Fi hotspots and more. These businesses often need a location, some equipment and licences, but serve steady local demand.

## What these methods share

- **Location and trust:** a visible, accessible spot and a reputation for honest service.
- **Licences and permits:** county business permits and, for some services, specific licences (e.g. M-Pesa agent requirements, CA licensing for some telecom services). Check what applies.
- **Customer data:** handle IDs, documents and passwords responsibly; never keep customers' passwords.
- See [local services online](./?track=earn-online&lesson=local-services-online) and [online government services](./?track=e-services-kenya&lesson=ecitizen).

## 246. Cyber café / business centre

**What it is:** A shop offering internet access, printing, photocopying, scanning, typing, lamination and online applications.
**How it works:** Walk-in customers pay per service.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Computer skills, customer service, knowledge of online services |
| Cost & equipment | Computers, printer/copier, scanner, laminator, internet, premises, permits |
| Where to find work | Walk-in customers near schools, offices, markets |
| Payment | Cash/M-Pesa per service |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium (location matters) |
| Time to first income | Immediately once open; setup takes weeks |

**How to start:** Research the location; start with essential equipment; add services over time.
**Pros:** Steady daily demand. **Cons & risks:** Equipment maintenance; competition.
**Scam warning:** Don't help customers with fraudulent applications or fake documents.
**Kenya notes:** KUCCPS, HELB, eCitizen, KRA and job applications drive traffic seasonally.

**What it is:** Running an agent outlet for deposits, withdrawals and other mobile money services.
**How it works:** Apply through the operator's agent process (often via a dealer), meet requirements, maintain float, earn commissions.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Cash handling, accuracy, security awareness |
| Cost & equipment | Float capital, premises, permits, documents required by the operator |
| Where to find work | Your outlet's customers |
| Payment | Commissions from the operator |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium (location and float matter) |
| Time to first income | Weeks to months (application) |

**How to start:** Apply through official channels; learn fraud prevention.
**Pros:** Steady transactions. **Cons & risks:** Fraud and robbery risks; float management.
**Scam warning:** Fraudsters impersonate customer care to trick agents into reversing transactions; follow official procedures only.
**Kenya notes:** Follow Safaricom's and other operators' official agent requirements and anti-fraud rules.
