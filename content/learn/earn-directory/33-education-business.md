# Education businesses

Parents in Kenya invest heavily in education. Beyond individual tutoring (methods 204–222), there are owner-level education businesses: daycares, tuition centres, schools, colleges and support services. They require registration and careful compliance because they serve children and learners.

## What these methods share

- **Registration and standards:** schools register with the Ministry of Education and county authorities; TVET institutions with TVETA; daycares with county governments.
- **Child safeguarding:** background checks, safe premises, clear policies.
- **Reputation:** results, safety and communication drive enrolment.

## 557. Daycare centre

**What it is:** Caring for babies and toddlers while parents work.
**How it works:** Licensed premises, trained caregivers, structured routines.

| | |
|---|---|
| Skills | Childcare, first aid, management |
| Cost & equipment | Premises, toys, safety equipment, licences |
| Where to find work | Working parents in your area |
| Payment | Monthly fees |
| Realistic earnings | Low to medium |
| Time to first income | Months |

**How to start:** Meet county licensing and safety requirements.
**Pros:** High demand. **Cons & risks:** Huge responsibility.
**Scam warning:** As usual: honest marketing, no hidden fees.
**Kenya notes:** Estates near business districts have strong demand.

## Summary

- Education businesses include daycares, schools, tuition centres, TVET and computer colleges, school transport, supplies, educational toys, reading clubs, education consultancy, exam printing, school software, special needs services and holiday camps.
- Register properly, protect children, and never promise grades, admissions or visas.
