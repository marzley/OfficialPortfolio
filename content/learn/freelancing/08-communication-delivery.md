# Client communication, delivery, revisions, difficult clients and getting reviews

Clients rarely leave because the work was 5% worse than perfect. They leave, or leave bad reviews, because they felt **ignored, surprised or confused**. Great communication turns average projects into repeat clients and referrals. This lesson covers the whole delivery journey.

## The golden rules

1. **No surprises.** Bad news early is a problem solved together; bad news on the deadline is a broken promise.
2. **Respond within one working day**, even if it's just "Got it, I'll reply properly by tomorrow noon."
3. **Write things down.** Decisions made on calls get summarised in writing afterwards.
4. **Under-promise, over-deliver.** Quote realistic deadlines and deliver a little early.
5. **Be the calm professional**, even when the client isn't.

## Onboarding: start strong

Within 24 hours of the deposit:
- Send a **welcome message**: thank them, confirm the timeline, list what you need from them and by when, and how updates will work.
- Share a **content checklist** (logo files, photos, text, logins) with a deadline.
- Set up a shared space: a Google Drive folder, a Trello board, or a simple shared document with the plan.
- Agree **communication channels**: e.g. WhatsApp for quick questions, email for approvals and files, a weekly 15-minute call for bigger projects.

Example welcome message:

> Hi Grace, deposit received, thank you! We're officially started.
>
> Here's what happens next:
> 1. By Friday I'll share the homepage design for your feedback.
> 2. I need your menu, prices and 5–10 photos by Wednesday (upload them here: [Drive link]).
> 3. I'll send a short progress update every Monday and Thursday.
>
> If anything changes on your side, just let me know. Excited to build this!

## Progress updates

A short update twice a week (or at every milestone) prevents anxious "any progress?" messages:

> **Update (Thursday):**
> Done: homepage and menu page designed.
> Next: contact page and WhatsApp ordering (by Monday).
> Need from you: delivery areas and fees.
> On track for launch on the 20th.

Done, next, needed, status. Four lines. Clients love it.

## Getting feedback that's actually useful

Vague feedback ("make it pop", "I don't like it") is common. Guide it:
- Share work with **specific questions**: "Is the menu order right? Do these colours match your brand? Is anything missing on the first screen?"
- Ask for feedback in **one consolidated message** per round, not 15 separate ones over a week.
- For visual work, tools like Google Docs comments, Figma comments or annotated screenshots keep feedback clear.
- When feedback is unclear, ask: "Can you show me an example you like?" or "What should a visitor feel or do here?"

## Revisions within scope

- Remind kindly how many rounds are included: "This is revision round 1 of 2."
- Group changes and do them in one go.
- If requests go beyond the agreed scope, use the scope creep script from [discovery, quotes and contracts](./?track=freelancing&lesson=discovery-quotes-contracts).

## Delivering the work

A professional delivery includes:
1. The **deliverables**, clearly named and organised (folders, file formats they asked for).
2. A short **summary**: what's included, how to use it, any logins (shared securely, not in public channels).
3. **Instructions or training:** a short video (screen recording) or guide on how to update or use it.
4. **Next steps:** final invoice, support period, care plan or ongoing services.

> Hi Grace, your website is live: [link] 🎉
> - Admin login details are in the secure note I just sent.
> - Here's a 6-minute video showing how to update prices and photos: [link]
> - Your final invoice is attached (due in 7 days).
> - Support: any bugs in the next 30 days are fixed free.
>
> It was a pleasure working on this. If you're happy with the result, a short review would mean a lot: [Google review link]

## Asking for reviews and testimonials

- Ask **right after** a successful delivery, when the client is happiest.
- Make it easy: send the direct link (Google Business review link, platform review, or "reply to this message with two or three sentences I can quote").
- Prompt them with questions: "What was the problem before? What changed? Would you recommend me, and to whom?"
- Ask permission to use their name, business name and logo in your portfolio.
- Never write fake reviews or pay for them.

## Difficult situations (and what to say)

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Client goes silent** for days | Friendly reminder at 3 days, another at 7 days explaining the timeline will move: "Since we're waiting on the content, the launch date moves to the 28th." Put the project on pause politely after that. |
| **Client keeps changing their mind** | Summarise the latest decision in writing; point to the agreed scope and revision rounds; quote extra work. |
| **Client is rude** | Stay calm and factual. Don't reply angrily; wait an hour. If abuse continues, finish the current milestone and end the project professionally. |
| **You made a mistake** | Own it quickly, fix it, explain how you'll prevent it. Clients forgive honesty much more than excuses. |
| **You'll miss a deadline** (illness, power outage) | Tell the client **as soon as you know**, with a new realistic date and what you've done so far. |
| **Client won't pay** | Send a polite reminder on the due date, a firmer one after 7 days referencing the agreement, then a final notice. Keep final files until paid. On platforms, use their dispute process. |
| **Client asks for unpaid "small extras" after the project ended** | "Happy to help! That's outside the original project. I can do it for KSh X, or include it in a monthly care plan." |

## Ending a project well (and turning it into more work)

- **Close properly:** confirm everything is delivered, payments complete and access handed over.
- **Offer ongoing value:** maintenance, monthly content, social media management, SEO, support ([retainers and scaling](./?track=freelancing&lesson=scale-retainers-agency)).
- **Check in after 30–60 days:** "How is the website working for you? Anything you need?" Many repeat projects start with that message.
- **Write the case study** for your portfolio while details are fresh.

## Professional habits that set you apart

- Proper greetings, clear grammar, no voice notes for important decisions (send text so there's a record), and respect for the client's time zone and working hours.
- Use the client's name and refer to their business goals, not just tasks.
- Keep your own notes for each client: preferences, logins (in a password manager), decisions.
- Protect client data: strong passwords, 2FA, don't share their files or logins with anyone else ([passwords and 2FA](./?track=cybersecurity&lesson=passwords-2fa)).

## Summary

- Onboard clearly, send short regular updates (done, next, needed, status) and write decisions down.
- Guide feedback with specific questions; handle revisions within scope.
- Deliver with files, instructions, invoice and next steps; ask for reviews right away.
- Handle silence, changes, mistakes and late payments calmly and in writing; end projects in a way that brings repeat work.
