# Freelance tools, workspace and a productive workday

Freelancers don't have an office manager, an IT department or a boss setting the schedule. Your tools and habits **are** your office. The good news: almost everything you need has a free version.

## The essential toolkit

| Need | Free or low-cost tools | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Email and calendar** | Gmail + Google Calendar | Use a professional address; turn on 2FA |
| **Files and sharing** | Google Drive, OneDrive | One folder per client; share links, not attachments |
| **Documents and quotes** | Google Docs / Word, Canva for designed PDFs | Keep quote, invoice and contract templates |
| **Project tracking** | Trello, Notion, a Google Sheet | Columns: To do · Doing · Waiting on client · Done |
| **Client tracking (mini CRM)** | Google Sheets, HubSpot free CRM, Notion | Prospects, status, follow-up dates ([finding direct clients](./?track=freelancing&lesson=finding-direct-clients)) |
| **Video calls** | Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams | Test camera, mic and lighting before important calls |
| **Messaging** | WhatsApp Business, email, platform messages | Separate business number if possible ([WhatsApp Business](./?track=marketing&lesson=whatsapp-business)) |
| **Time tracking** | Toggl Track, Clockify, Upwork's app for hourly contracts | Know how long tasks really take, which is essential for pricing |
| **Invoicing** | Templates in Docs/Sheets, Wave, Zoho Invoice | Number every invoice; keep copies for KRA |
| **Passwords** | Bitwarden, the browser's password manager | Never reuse passwords; share client logins securely |
| **Screen recording** | OBS Studio, Loom (free tier), phone screen recorder | Training videos and explanations for clients |
| **Backups** | Google Drive/OneDrive sync + an external drive | Losing a client's files is a disaster ([backups](./?track=cybersecurity&lesson=backups-ransomware)) |
| **AI assistants** | General AI chat tools | Drafting, ideas, checking; you're responsible for accuracy ([AI at work](./?track=ai-tools&lesson=ai-office-productivity)) |

Plus your skill-specific tools (VS Code, Figma, Canva, CapCut/DaVinci Resolve, Excel, QuickBooks, Express Scribe…).

:::tip Use legal software
Cracked software can contain malware that steals your accounts (including platform and M-Pesa-linked accounts) and can get you into trouble with clients. Use free alternatives or legitimate licences. Many tools have free tiers or student discounts.
:::

## Your workspace

- **A dedicated spot**, even a small table in a quiet corner, signals "work mode" to you and the people you live with.
- **Chair and screen height:** your eyes should be level with the top of the screen; a laptop stand or a pile of books plus an external keyboard and mouse saves your neck.
- **Lighting for calls:** face a window or a lamp, not with the light behind you.
- **A headset with a microphone** makes calls much clearer than laptop speakers.
- See [ICT health and safety](./?track=it-basics&lesson=ict-health-safety-ethics) for posture and eye care.

## Power and internet: the Kenyan reality

Outages happen. Clients abroad don't care that KPLC is down; they care that you delivered. Plan for it:

| Risk | Backup plan |
|---|---|
| Power outage | A laptop with good battery life; a power bank that can charge your phone (and laptop, if supported); a small UPS for your router; know a nearby café or co-working space |
| Internet down | Mobile data bundles on a different network from your home Wi-Fi; phone hotspot |
| Laptop breaks | Work saved in the cloud so you can continue on another machine; a repair contact ([computer maintenance](./?track=computer-maintenance&lesson=troubleshooting)) |
| Phone lost (with 2FA codes) | Backup codes stored safely; authenticator app with backup; SIM-swap protection ([SIM swap](./?track=cybersecurity&lesson=social-engineering-sim-swap)) |

Always build a **buffer** into deadlines (a day or two) so one outage doesn't break a promise. Ajira Digital centres, Jitume labs, libraries and co-working spaces can be useful backups in many towns.

## Planning your week

**Sunday or Monday morning (30 minutes):**
1. List all client deadlines for the next two weeks.
2. Choose your **top 3 priorities** for the week.
3. Block time in your calendar: deep work blocks for client work, a fixed daily block for marketing/pitching, short admin blocks.

## A productive freelance day (example)

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 7:30–8:00 | Plan the day; check messages quickly (reply only to urgent ones) |
| 8:00–11:00 | **Deep work** on the most important client task: phone on silent, notifications off |
| 11:00–11:30 | Break, then reply to messages and emails |
| 11:30–12:30 | **Marketing:** proposals, outreach, follow-ups, content |
| 12:30–13:30 | Lunch, away from the screen |
| 13:30–16:00 | Client work block 2 |
| 16:00–16:30 | Admin: invoices, tracker updates, updates to clients |
| 16:30 | Stop. Write tomorrow's top task. |

Adjust for clients in other time zones (e.g. afternoon/evening overlap with Europe or North America), but keep **fixed working hours** and tell clients what they are.

## Focus techniques that work

- **Time blocking:** every task gets a slot in the calendar.
- **Pomodoro:** 25 minutes focused, 5 minutes break; after four rounds, a longer break.
- **Batch messages:** check WhatsApp and email at set times (e.g. 11:00, 14:00, 16:00) instead of every five minutes.
- **One tab, one task:** close everything not needed for the current task.
- **"Eat the frog":** do the hardest or most dreaded task first.

## Avoiding burnout

Freelancers often overwork: saying yes to everything, answering messages at midnight, working weekends.
- Set working hours and a weekly day off; put them in your WhatsApp Business away message.
- Don't take on more work than you can deliver well; raise prices instead ([pricing](./?track=make-money-online&lesson=pricing)).
- Move your body daily; protect sleep.
- Connect with other freelancers (communities, Ajira/Jitume groups, local meetups). Isolation is real.

## Weekly review (Friday, 20 minutes)

1. What did I deliver this week? What's overdue?
2. How many pitches/proposals did I send? Any replies?
3. Money: invoices sent, payments received, outstanding.
4. One thing to improve next week.

## Summary

- A free toolkit covers email, files, projects, CRM, calls, time tracking, invoicing, passwords and backups.
- Plan for power and internet outages; build buffers into deadlines.
- Plan weekly, time-block deep work and marketing daily, batch messages.
- Keep working hours, protect your health, and review every week.
