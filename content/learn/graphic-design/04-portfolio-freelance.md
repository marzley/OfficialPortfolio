# Build a design portfolio and work with clients

Skills get you started; a **portfolio** and good **client habits** get you paid. This lesson covers how to show your work and handle design jobs professionally.

## Build a portfolio (even with no clients yet)

1. **Create practice projects** for realistic businesses: a logo and flyer for a salon in Kisumu, social posts for a boda-boda sacco, a menu for a café. Label them "concept work".
2. **Redesign** existing designs you think could be better (show before/after, respectfully).
3. **Do a few jobs cheaply or free** for a local church, school event or friend's business in exchange for permission to show the work and a testimonial.
4. **Show 8–12 of your best pieces**, not everything. Quality beats quantity.

### Where to show it

- **Behance** or **Dribbble** (free design portfolio sites)
- **Instagram** business account: consistent grid, your best work, stories showing your process
- A simple **website** (see the HTML portfolio project) with your WhatsApp link
- A **PDF portfolio** you can send on WhatsApp (keep it under ~10 MB)

### Present each project as a story

1. The client and the problem ("A new salon in Kisumu needed a brand that felt premium but affordable").
2. Your process (sketches, options).
3. The final design, shown in context: a mockup of the logo on a signboard, the flyer on a wall, posts on a phone.
4. The result ("Opening-day flyers brought 40 bookings" if you know it).

## Working with clients: the process

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| **1. Brief** | Ask the questions from the posters lesson. Write down everything. |
| **2. Quote** | Price, what's included, number of revisions (e.g. 2 rounds), timeline, files delivered |
| **3. Deposit** | Take 50% before starting, especially with new clients |
| **4. Concepts** | Present 2–3 options, explained |
| **5. Revisions** | Collect all feedback in one message per round |
| **6. Final approval** | In writing (WhatsApp is fine). Double-check spellings, dates, numbers. |
| **7. Balance and delivery** | Deliver final files after full payment |

## Files to deliver

- **Logo**: PNG (transparent), JPG, SVG/PDF (vector), in colour, black and white versions; a simple guide with colour codes and fonts.
- **Print designs**: print-ready PDF with bleed, plus PNG/JPG for sharing online.
- **Social media**: correctly sized PNG/JPG.
- Editable files (Canva link or source) only if agreed: many designers charge extra for these.

## Handling feedback

- Ask **why**: "Can you make it pop?" often means "the headline isn't big enough" or "the colours feel dull".
- Explain your design decisions calmly with reasons (readability, their audience).
- Extra revisions beyond the agreed rounds are **paid**: state this in the quote so it's not awkward later.

## Pricing (how to think about it)

Consider your time, skill, the client's size and how the design will be used (a logo for a big company is worth far more than a birthday flyer). Look at local rates, start reasonably, and raise prices as your portfolio and demand grow. Always give written quotes. The *Make Money Online* tutorial has a rate calculator.

## Copyright and ethics

- Don't copy other designers' work or use fonts/images without the right licence.
- Canva's free elements have licence terms: check before using them in a logo a client will trademark.
- Keep client files private and don't post confidential work without permission.

## Keep growing

Follow designers you admire, study good posters in town, practise daily challenges (e.g. a poster a day for 30 days), and learn a vector tool (Inkscape, Illustrator, Figma) for professional logo work.
