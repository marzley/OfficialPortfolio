# Photo editing basics

Good product and profile photos sell. You don't need expensive software: free tools on your phone or in the browser can fix most photos in minutes.

## Free tools

| Tool | Where | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Snapseed** | Android/iPhone | Powerful, free, no watermarks |
| **Phone's built-in editor** | Gallery / Photos app | Quick crops and light fixes |
| **Canva** | Web and app | Background remover (Pro), filters, text |
| **Photopea** | Browser (photopea.com) | Photoshop-like, free |
| **GIMP** | Windows/Mac/Linux | Full desktop editor, free |
| **remove.bg** | Browser | Quick background removal |
| **Lightroom Mobile** | App | Colour and light editing (free core features) |

## The editing order that works

### 1. Crop and straighten

- Remove distractions at the edges.
- Straighten horizons and walls.
- Use the **rule of thirds**: put the main subject on the grid lines, not always dead centre.
- Crop to the size you need (1:1 for Instagram posts, 4:5 portrait, 16:9 for website banners).

### 2. Fix the light (exposure)

| Setting | Does |
|---|---|
| **Exposure / Brightness** | Overall lightness |
| **Contrast** | Difference between darks and lights |
| **Highlights** | Recover bright areas (sky, white shirts) |
| **Shadows** | Bring out detail in dark areas (faces in shade) |

Go easy: small changes look natural.

### 3. Fix the colour

- **White balance / temperature**: remove yellow or blue tints. White things should look white.
- **Saturation / vibrance**: boost colours slightly. Vibrance is gentler on skin tones.
- For food and products, accurate colour matters more than dramatic filters.

### 4. Sharpen and clean up

- A little **sharpening** helps phone photos; too much looks crunchy.
- **Healing tool**: remove dust, spots and small distractions.

### 5. Export correctly

| Use | Format | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Website | WebP or JPG, quality ~80% | ~1200–1600 px wide, under 200 KB |
| Social media | JPG | 1080 px wide |
| Logos/graphics with transparency | PNG | as needed |
| Print | High-quality JPG or PDF | 300 DPI at print size |

Always keep the **original** photo; edit a copy.

## Removing backgrounds (product photos)

A clean white or plain background makes products look professional and consistent across your catalogue.

1. Photograph the product on a plain surface in good light.
2. Use Canva's background remover, remove.bg, or Photopea's "Select Subject".
3. Place it on white or your brand colour; add a soft shadow so it doesn't float.
4. Make all products the same size and position in the frame.

## Portraits and profile photos

- Face lit by a window, eyes sharp.
- Plain, uncluttered background.
- Crop at the shoulders; leave a little space above the head.
- Retouch lightly: remove a blemish, not the person's face shape. Over-edited photos lose trust.

## Ethics and honesty

Don't edit product photos to misrepresent them (making a phone screen look uncracked, or food bigger than it is). Customers will complain and trust is lost. For news and documents (IDs, certificates), editing is dishonest and can be illegal.

## Who edits photos professionally

Product photos sell items on Jumia, Instagram and WhatsApp catalogues; real estate agents need bright, straight property photos; event photographers edit hundreds of wedding and graduation photos; restaurants need appetising menu photos; and every business needs good team and profile photos. Photo editing is a skill you can sell directly, and it's essential for graphic and web designers.

## Taking better photos first

Editing can't fully fix a bad photo, so start well:

| Tip | Why |
|---|---|
| Use natural light near a window (avoid harsh midday sun) | Soft, even light; true colours |
| Clean the phone lens | Smudges cause haze |
| Hold steady or use a stand; tap to focus | Sharp images |
| Keep backgrounds simple | The subject stands out; easier to edit |
| Shoot at eye level for people, slightly above for food | Flattering, natural angles |
| Use gridlines (rule of thirds) | Better composition |
| Take several shots | Choose the best |

## Non-destructive editing

Professional editors keep the original untouched:

- Work on a **copy** of the file, or in apps that keep edits separate (Lightroom, Snapseed's edit history).
- In Photoshop/GIMP/Photopea, use **adjustment layers** and **layer masks** instead of painting directly on the photo.
- Save the working file (PSD/XCF) as well as the exported JPG/PNG/WebP.

## Understanding the histogram

The histogram shows the spread of tones from black (left) to white (right):

- Bunched on the left: underexposed (too dark).
- Bunched on the right: overexposed (too bright); detail may be lost in highlights.
- Spread across: good range of tones.

Use **Levels** or **Curves** to stretch the tones for better contrast.

## Curves and selective adjustments

- A gentle **S-curve** (lift highlights, lower shadows) adds contrast.
- **HSL / colour mixer**: adjust individual colours, e.g. make green vegetables greener without affecting skin tones.
- **Selective tools / masks**: brighten a face, darken a distracting background, sharpen only the product.
- **Vignette**: subtly darken edges to draw attention to the centre (don't overdo it).

## Retouching tools

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| Spot healing brush | Remove dust, small blemishes, stray threads |
| Clone stamp | Copy one area over another (removing objects) |
| Content-aware fill / generative fill | Remove larger objects; check results carefully |
| Dodge and burn | Lighten or darken specific areas |
| Frequency separation (advanced) | Professional skin retouching while keeping texture |

Keep retouching natural: remove temporary blemishes, not permanent features, unless the client specifically asks. Over-smoothed skin looks fake.

## Product photo workflow (e-commerce)

1. Shoot on a plain white or light background with even lighting.
2. Straighten and crop to a consistent ratio (square 1:1 is common for marketplaces).
3. Correct white balance so whites are white.
4. Adjust exposure and contrast; sharpen slightly.
5. Remove the background or clean it to pure white if the marketplace requires.
6. Add consistent padding so products appear the same size across the catalogue.
7. Export at the size the platform recommends, compressed (WebP/JPG), with descriptive file names (`black-leather-handbag-front.jpg`).

## Batch editing

When editing many photos from the same shoot (events, products):

- Edit one photo, then **copy/paste settings** or **sync** to others (Lightroom, Snapseed "copy edits").
- Use **presets** for a consistent look across a brand's social media.
- Use batch export or actions (Photoshop Actions, GIMP plugins) to resize and rename.

## AI editing tools: use with care

AI tools can remove backgrounds, extend images, remove objects and enhance low-quality photos in seconds. Use them to save time, but:

- Check details (hands, text, edges) for errors.
- Don't create misleading images of products (e.g. making food look different from what customers receive).
- Disclose heavy AI alteration where honesty matters (journalism, real estate, before/after claims).
- Respect people's likeness: get consent before editing and publishing photos of people.

## Export settings by use

| Use | Format | Size guide |
|---|---|---|
| Website hero | WebP/JPG | ~1600–2000 px wide, compressed |
| Product images | WebP/JPG | 1000–2000 px square |
| Instagram post | JPG | 1080 px wide |
| WhatsApp Status | JPG | 1080×1920 |
| Logo with transparency | PNG or SVG | As needed |
| Print | TIFF or high-quality JPG/PDF | 300 DPI at print size |

## Practice

1. Photograph a product in good window light and edit it into a clean marketplace image.
2. Use Curves to add contrast and HSL to adjust one colour.
3. Remove a small distracting object with the healing or clone tool.
4. Batch-edit five photos with the same preset and export them for Instagram.
5. Create before/after comparisons of three edits for your portfolio.

:::think A restaurant asks you to make their food photos look "like the adverts", making portions bigger and colours far brighter than reality. What's the risk, and what would you suggest?
Customers who receive smaller or different-looking food feel misled, which leads to complaints and bad reviews, and false advertising can create legal issues. Suggest professional-quality but honest editing: good lighting, styling, cropping, natural colour correction and appealing presentation that still matches what's served.
:::
