For every real online opportunity, there’s a scam copying it. Kenyans have lost savings, phones and even their identity to fake online jobs. Learn the patterns and you’ll recognise them instantly.

1. “Task apps” and “like and earn” schemes

You’re paid small amounts for liking videos, rating products or watching ads, then asked to deposit money to “upgrade your level” or “unlock withdrawals”. The money comes from new members, not real work. It always collapses.

2. Account selling and renting

People sell “verified” Upwork, Outlier or other accounts, or offer to “open an account for you” for a fee. It breaks platform rules, accounts get banned, and buyers often never receive anything.

3. Fake recruiters

A “recruiter” on WhatsApp, Telegram or LinkedIn offers a well-paid remote job, then asks for a training fee, equipment fee or medical fee, or for your ID and bank details. Real employers don’t charge you to work.

4. Fake payment messages

Sellers receive SMS messages that look like M-Pesa confirmations but come from normal phone numbers. Always confirm payments in your M-Pesa balance or statement before sending goods.

5. Overpayment and refund tricks

A “client” sends more than agreed and asks you to refund the difference. The original payment is later reversed or was fake.

6. “Investment” and forex schemes

Guaranteed daily returns, crypto “mining” or forex “account management”. If returns are guaranteed and high, it’s almost certainly a pyramid.

Warning signs

  • You must pay to earn.
  • Pay that’s far too high for easy work.
  • Pressure to act fast (“only 3 slots left today”).
  • Communication only on Telegram or WhatsApp, never an official email or platform.
  • Requests for your M-Pesa PIN, OTP codes or passwords.

If you’ve been scammed

  1. Stop sending money, even to “recover” what you lost (recovery scams are common).
  2. Call Safaricom on 100 or 200 quickly about fraudulent M-Pesa transactions; you can also forward scam SMS to 333.
  3. Report to the police (DCI) and keep screenshots and transaction codes.
  4. Change passwords and turn on two-step verification.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal to pay for an online job in Kenya?

No. Real employers and platforms pay you. Anyone asking for a registration, training or activation fee is almost certainly a scammer.

Are “like and earn” apps legit?

Apps that pay for likes or views and then ask you to deposit money to withdraw are scams.

Where can I report a fraudulent M-Pesa transaction?

Contact Safaricom customer care quickly, forward scam SMS to 333, and report to the police with your transaction codes.
