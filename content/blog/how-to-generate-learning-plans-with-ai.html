Preparing CBET documents (learning plans, session plans, information sheets and assessment tools) takes trainers in Kenya’s TVET institutions hours every week. AI can cut that time dramatically, if you use it carefully and keep the curriculum at the centre. This guide shows two ways: a general AI assistant with good prompts, and CBET Planner, a tool built specifically for this job.

What a good learning plan needs

A CBET learning plan spreads a unit of competency across your sessions. It should match the curriculum and typically shows, for each session or week:

  • The unit of competency, and its elements and performance criteria (from the curriculum)
  • Learning outcomes: what trainees will be able to do
  • Content to be covered
  • Learning activities and methods (demonstration, practical, discussion)
  • Resources: tools, equipment, materials, references
  • Assessment methods and evidence

Always follow your institution’s and the relevant curriculum body’s templates. AI should fill a template, not invent one.

Option 1: a general AI assistant

Step 1: give it the curriculum, not just the title

The biggest mistake is asking “Write a learning plan for Electrical Installation”. The AI will invent content that doesn’t match your curriculum. Instead, paste the unit’s elements, performance criteria and content from the official curriculum.

Step 2: a prompt that works

“Act as an experienced TVET trainer in Kenya who prepares CBET documents. Using ONLY the curriculum content below, draft a learning plan for [unit name] over [number] sessions of [duration] each, for [level] trainees. For each session give: session number, element, learning outcome, content, learning activities, resources and assessment. Present it as a table. Do not add content that is not in the curriculum; if something seems missing, list it separately as a question for me. Curriculum: [paste].”

Step 3: session plans from the learning plan

“Using session 3 of the learning plan above, write a session plan: objectives, introduction (5 minutes), development steps with trainer and trainee activities and timings, conclusion, resources, and how learning will be assessed. Total time: [duration].”

Step 4: information sheets and assessments

  • “Write a trainee information sheet for this session in simple English, with key terms, steps and safety precautions.”
  • “Create a practical assessment and an observation checklist aligned to these performance criteria: [paste].”
  • “Write 10 oral questions with model answers for this element.”

Step 5: check like a professional

  • Does every session map to the curriculum’s elements and performance criteria?
  • Are hours and session counts right?
  • Are safety points correct for your workshop?
  • Are resources ones your institution actually has?
  • Is the language clear for your trainees?

You are responsible for the final document. AI drafts; the trainer decides.

Responsible use

  • Don’t paste trainees’ personal information into public AI tools.
  • Follow your institution’s policy on AI use.
  • Keep the curriculum as the source of truth, and keep your professional judgement.

AI can draft one quickly if you give it the official curriculum content and a clear template. The trainer must check it against the curriculum before use.
