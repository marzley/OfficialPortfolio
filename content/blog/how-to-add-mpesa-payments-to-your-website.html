Most Kenyan customers would rather pay with M-Pesa than a card. If your website can send the payment prompt straight to their phone, you’ll close more sales. Here’s how it works, in plain language.

What is an M-Pesa STK push?

An STK push is the pop-up that appears on a customer’s phone asking for their M-Pesa PIN. On a website, it works like this:

  1. The customer enters their phone number at checkout.
  2. Your website asks Safaricom to send a payment request to that number.
  3. The customer sees the prompt on their phone and enters their PIN.
  4. Safaricom tells your website whether the payment succeeded, and your site confirms the order automatically.

You can try a real KSh 1 payment on my site to see exactly what your customers would experience.

What you need

  • A till or paybill number. Payments go straight to your business account.
  • A Safaricom Daraja account. Daraja is Safaricom’s system that lets websites connect to M-Pesa. It gives you the keys your website uses.
  • A website with SSL (https). Safaricom only sends payment results to secure addresses.
  • A server-side script. The part that talks to Safaricom must run on your server, never in the visitor’s browser, so your keys stay secret.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Putting your keys in the website code. Anyone can read them and misuse your account. Keep them in a private file on the server.
  • Trusting the browser. Only mark an order as paid when Safaricom confirms it, never just because the customer clicked a button.
  • No handling for failed payments. Customers cancel, enter the wrong PIN or have low balance. Your site should explain what happened and let them try again.
  • No record of payments. Save every M-Pesa receipt number with the order, so you can match payments and handle disputes.

M-Pesa, cards or both?

For most Kenyan businesses, M-Pesa should come first. If you also sell to customers abroad or corporate clients, add card payments through a gateway like Paystack or PayPal alongside it.

Can I add it to my existing website?

Usually, yes. Whether your site is custom-built or on WordPress, an M-Pesa checkout can be added without rebuilding everything. I’ve built it into online shops and a Wi-Fi hotspot billing system that connects customers automatically once they pay.
