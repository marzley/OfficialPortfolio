Many businesses in Kenya ask for “an app” when a fast web app would serve their customers better and cost far less. Here’s how to decide, what it costs, and how to choose a developer you can trust.

Three kinds of apps (and which one you need)

  • Web app: runs in the browser on any phone or computer. No download needed, one version for everyone, cheapest to build and update. Ideal for systems like bookings, school portals, stock and sales.
  • Progressive web app (PWA): a web app that can be installed on the home screen, loads fast and can work with a weak connection. Our own site is installable this way.
  • Android app: installed from an APK or Google Play. Useful when you need notifications, offline work or phone features. Most Kenyan users are on Android, so it’s usually the first native platform to build for.

For most businesses, a web app or PWA first is the smart choice. You can add an Android app later using the same system behind it.

What the best app developers do

  • Start with the problem, not the features. Who uses the app, and what must they get done in under a minute?
  • Design for cheap phones and slow data. Small downloads, fast screens, and clear buttons.
  • Handle payments properly. M-Pesa STK Push with automatic confirmation, and keys kept on the server, never inside the app.
  • Secure your data. Logins, roles, backups and privacy that follow the Kenya Data Protection Act.
  • Give you an admin area. You should manage users, content and reports without calling the developer.
  • Plan for updates. Apps need maintenance. Ask what support costs after launch.

Questions to ask any app developer

  1. Can I use an app you’ve built, live, today?
  2. Web app, PWA or Android: which do you recommend for me, and why?
  3. Who owns the code, the server and the Play Store account?
  4. How do you secure payments and user data?
  5. What does support cost after launch?
