Teachers and trainers who learn a little prompt engineering save hours every week on planning, notes and assessments, and spend that time with learners. Here are ready prompts for schools (including CBC) and TVET (CBET), with the checks that keep quality high.

The teacher’s prompt formula

Role + learners + curriculum content + task + format + checks.

“Act as an experienced [subject] teacher in Kenya. My learners are [level, class size, any needs]. Using this curriculum content: [paste], [task]. Format: [table/steps]. Point out anything in my request that doesn’t match the curriculum.”

Planning

  • Scheme of work / learning plan: “Spread this content across [n] lessons of [duration], with outcomes, activities, resources and assessment per lesson, as a table.” TVET trainers: see generating CBET learning plans with AI and CBET Planner.
  • Lesson or session plan: “Write a 40-minute lesson plan on [topic]: objectives, introduction, development with teacher and learner activities and timings, conclusion, assessment.”
  • Real-life examples: “Give 5 examples from everyday life in Kenya that illustrate [concept].”

Notes and resources

  • “Write learner notes on [topic] in simple English with key terms in bold and a summary box.”
  • “Rewrite these notes for learners who find reading difficult, keeping all key points.”
  • “Create a step-by-step practical procedure for [task] with safety precautions.”

Assessment

  • “Write 10 questions on [topic]: 4 recall, 4 application, 2 analysis, with a marking scheme.”
  • “Create a rubric with 4 levels for this practical task, aligned to these performance criteria: [paste].”
  • “Here is a learner’s answer: [paste, without names]. Give kind, specific feedback and one next step.”

Differentiation

  • “Give me three versions of this activity: support, core and extension.”
  • “Suggest ways to make this lesson accessible to a learner with low vision.”

Checks that keep you professional

  • Curriculum first: always paste the official content and check alignment.
  • Accuracy: verify facts, formulas, dates and safety procedures.
  • Local fit: examples, units (KSh, metres) and resources your school actually has.
  • Privacy: never paste learners’ names, marks or personal details into public AI tools.
  • Integrity: teach learners how to use AI honestly too.

Frequently asked questions

How can teachers use AI in Kenya?

For lesson and session plans, notes, questions, rubrics, differentiated activities and feedback, always checked against the curriculum and without sharing learners’ personal data.

Can AI make schemes of work and learning plans?

Yes, as a first draft from the official curriculum content you provide. The teacher must check alignment, timing and resources.
