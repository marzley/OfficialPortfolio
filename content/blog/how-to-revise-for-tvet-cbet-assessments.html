In Kenyan TVET colleges, many courses now follow competency-based education and training (CBET). Instead of one big exam at the end, you are assessed on whether you can actually do each part of the job. That changes how you should revise. This guide is from Kelvin Wanyoike, an ICT trainer who works with TVET learners.

How CBET assessment works

  • Your course is split into units of competency, for example “Apply computer applications” or “Install network systems”.
  • Each unit has elements and performance criteria: the things you must show you can do.
  • You are usually assessed with a written or oral test on the knowledge, and a practical where you do the task, plus evidence of your work collected in a portfolio of evidence.
  • The result is competent or not yet competent. If you're not yet competent, you can be reassessed.
  • National assessments for many CBET qualifications are run by TVET CDACC. Your trainers and college will tell you exactly which units, dates and requirements apply to you.

A simple revision plan

  1. Get the unit list and performance criteria from your trainer or the curriculum. Revise against these, not just your notes.
  2. Turn each performance criterion into a question you can answer out loud: “How do I crimp a network cable?” “What does VLOOKUP do?”
  3. Practise the practical, again and again, with a timer. Do the real task on a computer or with real tools, not just reading about it.
  4. Test yourself with practice questions before looking at the answers, then revise only what you got wrong.
  5. Keep your portfolio of evidence tidy and up to date: dated work, screenshots, photos of practicals and trainer sign-offs.
  6. Revise in short daily sessions (30 to 45 minutes) instead of one long night before.

Frequently asked questions

Are the revision packs past papers?

No. They are practice questions written for our lessons, to help you check what you know. Use them together with your trainer's notes and any past assessments your college gives you.

What if I am “not yet competent” in a unit?

Ask your trainer which performance criteria you missed, practise those, and book a reassessment as your college allows.
