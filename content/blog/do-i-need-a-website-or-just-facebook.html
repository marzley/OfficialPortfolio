Many Kenyan businesses start with a Facebook, Instagram or TikTok page, and that’s a great first step: it’s free and your customers are already there. But at some point most growing businesses ask: do I need a proper website? Here’s how to decide.

What social media does well

  • Reaching people who already follow you or see your posts.
  • Quick updates: new stock, offers and photos in seconds.
  • Conversation through comments and messages.

Where a website wins

  • People find you on Google. When someone searches “bakery in Thika” or “school uniform supplier”, Google shows websites and Business Profiles, not Facebook posts.
  • You own it. A social account can be hacked, suspended or buried by the algorithm. Your website and domain are yours.
  • It looks professional. A .co.ke address and a matching email (like info@yourbusiness.co.ke) build trust with bigger clients, tenders and institutions.
  • It sells for you. Customers can see prices, book, order and pay by M-Pesa any time, without waiting for you to reply to a DM.
  • Everything in one place. Services, prices, location, reviews and contacts, organised the way you want.

The best answer: both

Use social media to reach people, and your website as the home they land on. Every post can point to a page on your site where they can learn more, book or buy.

When is it time for a website?

  • You answer the same questions about prices and location again and again.
  • You want customers to find you on Google.
  • You’re pitching to companies, schools or government institutions.
  • You want to take orders or bookings and payments online.
