A domain name is your address online, like yourbusiness.co.ke. A .co.ke domain tells customers you are a Kenyan business, and it gives you a professional email such as info@yourbusiness.co.ke instead of a Gmail address. Here is how to get one.

.co.ke, .ke or .com?

  • .co.ke: for businesses in Kenya. The most common choice for local companies.
  • .ke: shorter and newer. Good if the name you want is free.
  • .com: international. Useful if you sell outside Kenya.
  • Some Kenyan extensions are restricted, for example .ac.ke for colleges and universities, .sc.ke for schools and .go.ke for government, and need documents to prove you qualify.

Kenyan domains are managed by KeNIC (the Kenya Network Information Centre). You buy them through accredited registrars, usually hosting companies, or through your web developer.

Choosing a good name

  • Short and easy to say over the phone.
  • Your business name, or close to it.
  • Avoid hyphens and numbers that people will get wrong.
  • Check social media handles at the same time, so your name matches everywhere.

What it costs

A domain is paid for every year. Prices for .co.ke vary between registrars and change over time, so compare a few. The renewal price matters more than a cheap first year. Keep auto-renewal on, or set a reminder: if a domain expires, your website and email stop working, and someone else can register the name.

Step by step

  1. Search for the name on a registrar's website to see if it's free.
  2. Register it in your own name or your business's name, with your own email and phone number. If someone registers it for you, make sure you are listed as the owner.
  3. Add hosting for your website and email, and point the domain to it.
  4. Set up email (info@, sales@, your name@) and turn on SSL so the site shows the padlock.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need documents for a .co.ke domain?

A .co.ke domain is open to businesses and individuals without the extra documents needed for restricted ones like .ac.ke or .sc.ke. Your registrar will tell you if anything else is needed.

Can I move my domain to another company later?

Yes. A domain can be transferred between registrars. Ask your current provider for the transfer (EPP) code.
