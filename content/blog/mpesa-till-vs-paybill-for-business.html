Almost every customer in Kenya pays with M-Pesa, so the way you receive money matters. Safaricom gives businesses three main options: a Buy Goods Till, a Paybill, and Pochi la Biashara. Here is what each one is for, so you pick the right one before you print it on your shop sign or connect it to your website.

Buy Goods Till: simple and fast

Customers pay through Lipa na M-Pesa → Buy Goods and Services and enter your till number. There is no account number, so paying is quick.

  • Best for: shops, salons, kiosks, restaurants, hardware stores: anywhere a customer pays and leaves.
  • Downside: you can't easily tell what a payment was for, only who paid and how much.

Paybill: when you need to know what each payment is for

Customers pay through Lipa na M-Pesa → Pay Bill, enter your business number and an account number. That account number can be an invoice number, a student's admission number, a house number or a member number.

  • Best for: schools and colleges, landlords, SACCOs and chamas, internet providers, anyone who sends invoices.
  • Why it helps: a system can match each payment to the right person or invoice automatically, so nobody has to check statements by hand.

Pochi la Biashara: for very small traders

Customers pay through Send Money → Pochi la Biashara to your phone number, but business money is kept apart from your personal M-Pesa. It suits mama mbogas, boda riders and small sellers who are just starting. It isn't built for websites or systems.

Which works with a website or system?

Both a Till and a Paybill can be connected to a website or system through Safaricom's Daraja API. The customer types their number, gets the M-Pesa PIN prompt on their phone (STK push), and the website confirms the payment automatically. See how M-Pesa payments on a website work.

  • Online shop: a Till is usually enough, because each order already has its own record.
  • School fees, rent, invoices or member contributions: a Paybill, so the account number links each payment to the right person.

How to apply and what it costs

You apply through Safaricom (online or at a Safaricom shop). The documents asked for depend on whether you are a sole trader or a registered business, and usually include your ID, KRA PIN and, for registered businesses, the registration certificate. Safaricom charges transaction fees according to its current tariff, which changes from time to time, so check the latest charges on Safaricom's website before you choose. Frequently asked questions

Can I have both a Till and a Paybill?

Yes. Many businesses use a Till at the counter and a Paybill for invoices or online orders.

Can customers pay on my website without typing the till number?

Yes. With an STK push integration, they only type their phone number and enter their PIN on the prompt. The website fills in the amount and confirms the payment for them.
