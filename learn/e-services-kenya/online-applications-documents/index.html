Online applications: documents, good conduct and avoiding mistakes

Jobs, scholarships, HELB loans, KUCCPS placement, passports, the Certificate of Good Conduct, business permits: more and more applications happen online. Most rejections and delays come from avoidable mistakes. This lesson is a practical guide to doing them right the first time.

Prepare a digital document folder

Create a folder on Google Drive (and a copy on your phone) with clear, scanned copies:

DocumentTips

Scanning well with a phone

Use Google Drive → Scan, Adobe Scan or Microsoft Lens: they crop, straighten and produce readable PDFs. Scan in good light, and check that every word can be read when zoomed in.

File size and format

Portals often limit size (e.g. 1–2 MB) and format (PDF or JPG). To reduce size: scan in greyscale, lower the resolution slightly, or use a free PDF compressor. Combine pages into one PDF when a portal allows only one upload.

Certificate of Good Conduct (police clearance)

Issued by the DCI, applied for through eCitizen. The general process is: apply and pay online, then complete fingerprinting at an approved DCI location or Huduma Centre, and download the certificate when ready. Check the current steps on eCitizen before you start, and apply early: processing takes time.

Filling in forms: avoid these mistakes

  1. Names exactly as on your ID, in the same order.
  2. Correct ID number, phone and email: you'll receive codes and results there. Use an email you check.
  3. Don't wait for the deadline day: portals slow down and crash.
  4. Read the instructions twice; answer every required field.
  5. Save and screenshot confirmation pages, reference numbers and M-Pesa payment messages.
  6. Log out afterwards, especially at cyber cafés.

Staying safe with applications

  • Use only official sites (they usually end in .go.ke for government, or the organisation's known domain).
  • Never pay for a "guaranteed" job, placement or loan approval: that's a scam.
  • Don't share your eCitizen, iTax or email password with cyber café attendants. If someone helps you, type the password yourself, then change it later.
  • Beware of WhatsApp messages with "application links": check the address carefully.
  • Verify job adverts with the employer's official website or phone number.

Writing short answers and cover letters

Many applications ask "Why do you want this?" or "Describe your experience":

  • Keep it specific and honest; give examples with results: "I built a website for my college's ICT club that 300 students use."
  • Match the requirements listed in the advert.
  • Check spelling and grammar (and have someone else read it).
  • Write it first in a document, then paste it: portals sometimes time out and lose what you typed.

After applying

  • Note deadlines and expected response dates in your calendar.
  • Check email (including Spam) and SMS regularly.
  • Follow up politely if a date passes with no response.
  • Keep improving: every application teaches you something.

Applying online confidently

Job applications, university and college placement, scholarships, internships, government services, bursaries and visa applications are increasingly done online. Small mistakes (a wrong ID number, a blurry certificate scan, a missed deadline) can cost opportunities. A prepared, organised approach saves time and stress, and helping others apply is a common service in cyber cafés.

Filling in forms accurately

  1. Read all instructions before starting; note deadlines and required documents.
  2. Use your name exactly as it appears on your ID and certificates (same order and spelling).
  3. Double-check ID number, KRA PIN, phone and email (a wrong email means you miss communication).
  4. Prepare long answers in a document first, then paste them in (forms can time out).
  5. Save drafts often if the portal allows.
  6. Review everything before submitting; you often can't edit afterwards.
  7. Save or print the confirmation page and reference number.

Writing strong short answers

QuestionApproach
"Why do you want this position/scholarship?"Connect your goals, the opportunity and what you'll contribute; be specific
"Describe a challenge you overcame"Use situation, task, action, result with real details
"What are your strengths?"Give 2–3 strengths with evidence (projects, results, roles)

Keep within word limits, avoid copying generic answers, and proofread (read aloud or ask someone else).

Protecting yourself from application scams

Red flagWhy
Asking for payment to "secure" a job, placement or scholarshipGenuine opportunities rarely charge for selection
Communication from personal email addresses or WhatsApp only, for official rolesReal organisations use official channels
Very high pay for little work, or no interviewClassic bait
Requests for M-Pesa PIN, passwords or OTPsNever legitimate
Websites with misspelled domains or no HTTPSPossible phishing

Government websites in Kenya typically end in .go.ke; verify links and call official numbers when unsure.

After applying

  • Track applications in a simple spreadsheet: organisation, role, date applied, deadline, status, contact, follow-up date.
  • Check email (including spam) and SMS regularly.
  • Prepare for interviews or aptitude tests early.
  • Follow up politely if the timeline has passed.
  • Learn from rejections: ask for feedback where possible and improve your documents.

Helping others in a cyber café (good practice)

  • Let customers type their own passwords; never store them.
  • Log out of all accounts and clear downloads after each customer.
  • Give customers copies of their documents and confirmations; don't keep personal documents longer than necessary.
  • Explain each step so customers learn; avoid submitting on their behalf without their review.

Practice

  1. Create your digital document kit with clear file names and check each file's size.
  2. Combine a two-page certificate scan into a single PDF under 1 MB.
  3. Write a 150-word answer to "Why do you want this opportunity?" for a real or sample application.
  4. Set up an application tracking spreadsheet.
  5. Identify the red flags in three job adverts you find online.
Think about it: An applicant's name on their application is "Jane W. Kamau" but their ID says "Jane Wanjiru Kamau" and their certificate says "Wanjiru Jane Kamau". Why might this cause problems, and what should they do?Show answer

Verification systems and officers compare names across documents; inconsistencies can delay or block the application or raise fraud concerns. Use the name exactly as on the ID, and if certificates differ, be prepared to explain or provide supporting documents (such as an affidavit) according to the institution's requirements.
