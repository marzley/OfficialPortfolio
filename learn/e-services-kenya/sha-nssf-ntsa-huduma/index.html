SHA health cover, NSSF, NTSA and Huduma services: registering, paying and checking statements

Beyond eCitizen and KRA, Kenyans regularly deal with health insurance, retirement savings, driving licences, vehicle records and in-person government services. These systems have changed significantly in recent years (for example, NHIF was replaced by the Social Health Authority (SHA) in October 2024). This unit explains what each body does, how to register and check your status online, and how to avoid scams.

Always confirm current details

Registration channels, contribution rates and procedures for SHA, NSSF and NTSA have changed recently and may change again. Use this lesson to understand the services, then confirm current requirements on the official websites or offices.

What you will learn

  • SHA: what it is, who must register, contributions and checking cover
  • NSSF: retirement savings, statements and benefits
  • NTSA: driving licences, vehicle searches and transfers through eCitizen
  • Huduma Centres and other in-person services
  • Keeping records and avoiding scams

Social Health Authority (SHA)

In October 2024, the National Health Insurance Fund (NHIF) was replaced by the Social Health Authority (SHA) under new health laws. SHA manages funds including the Social Health Insurance Fund (SHIF), a Primary Healthcare Fund and an Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund.

Who registers

Kenyan residents are expected to register, including household members (spouse, children). Employees' contributions are typically deducted by employers; people in informal work contribute based on assessed income through the official process.

How to register and check (typical channels)

  • The official SHA website (sha.go.ke) and its registration portal.
  • USSD (the official short code announced by SHA, e.g. *147#).
  • Assistance at health facilities, Huduma Centres and SHA offices.

You'll typically need your ID number, phone number and dependants' details (birth certificate numbers for children). After registering, you can check your membership and contribution status through the official channels.

Using cover

  • Visit SHA-contracted facilities; you may need to verify your identity (and sometimes an OTP to your phone).
  • Ask the facility which services are covered under SHA packages and whether any amount is payable by you.
  • Keep receipts and records of treatment.

Keep your contributions up to date

Cover can depend on being registered and up to date with contributions. Check your status before planned treatment, and update dependants when your family changes.

National Social Security Fund (NSSF)

NSSF is a statutory retirement savings scheme: employees and employers contribute monthly, and members receive benefits on retirement (and in certain situations such as permanent disability, emigration or to survivors).

  • Contributions are calculated under the NSSF Act, 2013, with rates and upper limits phased in over several years (amounts have increased in stages since 2024; check the current tier limits).
  • Employers deduct and remit employee contributions.
  • Check your statement: register on the NSSF self-service portal or use the official USSD/SMS services to confirm that your employer has remitted contributions. Missing contributions should be raised with your employer and NSSF.
  • Self-employed people can register and contribute voluntarily.
  • Consider additional retirement savings (registered personal pension schemes, employer pension schemes, SACCOs, money market funds) because NSSF alone may not be enough.

National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA)

NTSA services are accessed through eCitizen (NTSA section), replacing the older TIMS system.

Before buying a used vehicle

Do an official vehicle search on eCitizen/NTSA to confirm the owner, logbook details and any caveats (e.g. financing by a bank). Do the transfer through the official system, and never pay the full amount before confirming the seller's identity and the vehicle's records.

Huduma Centres and other in-person services

Huduma Centres (in counties across Kenya) offer many government services under one roof: ID applications and replacements, birth certificates, NSSF/SHA/KRA help, good conduct fingerprinting (as designated), business services and more. Useful when you need help with online processes or in-person verification.

Other important services:

  • National ID (Registration of Persons): new IDs at 18, replacements for lost IDs; Kenya has been rolling out the Maisha Namba/digital ID system, so check current procedures.
  • Civil registration: births and deaths.
  • County services: business permits, land rates, parking (often through county portals and M-Pesa paybills).

Avoiding scams

  • Use only official websites (.go.ke and the known official domains) and official short codes.
  • No agent can "speed up" your SHA cover, NSSF benefits or driving licence for extra money outside official fees.
  • Calls/SMS claiming your "SHA cover will be cancelled unless you pay to this number" are likely scams: check your status yourself through official channels.
  • Don't share OTPs sent during SHA verification at hospitals with anyone except the facility system process you initiated.
  • For vehicle purchases, beware of fake logbooks and "too cheap" deals.
Think about it: Your employer deducts NSSF from your payslip every month, but your NSSF statement shows no contributions for the last six months. What should you do?Show answer

Raise it with your employer's HR/payroll department first, with your payslips as evidence. If it isn't resolved, report it to NSSF (employers are legally required to remit deductions). Keep checking your statement regularly so problems are caught early.

Practice tasks

  1. Check your SHA registration status through the official channel and confirm your dependants are listed.
  2. Register on the NSSF self-service portal (or use the official USSD) and download or view your statement.
  3. Log in to eCitizen and find the NTSA services list.
  4. Write down the steps to do an official vehicle search before buying a car.
  5. Create a secure folder for your health, pension, licence and vehicle documents.

Summary

  • SHA replaced NHIF in October 2024; register yourself and dependants, keep contributions up to date and check status through official channels before treatment.
  • NSSF is statutory retirement savings; check statements regularly to confirm employer remittances; consider additional savings.
  • NTSA services (licences, vehicle searches and transfers) run through eCitizen; always search vehicles before buying.
  • Huduma Centres provide many services in person.
  • Keep records, use official channels, and avoid agents and scam messages.
