Earning as an app developer in Kenya

App development is one of the best-paid tech skills, locally and internationally. Here's how app developers earn, how to price work, and a roadmap from beginner to hired.

Ways app developers earn

PathHow
Client projectsBuild apps for businesses, schools, SACCOs, NGOs, startups (fixed price per project)
Maintenance retainersMonthly fee to update, fix and host apps you built
EmploymentMobile developer roles at banks, fintechs, telcos, startups, agencies
Remote workInternational companies via Upwork, Toptal, Andela, Turing, Arc
Your own appsAds, subscriptions, paid features (Google Play Billing for digital goods), or a SaaS for a niche (like CBET Planner for TVET trainers)
TeachingCourses, bootcamps, mentoring

What clients pay for (Kenya)

ProjectTypical range (KSh)
Simple web app / PWA40,000 to 100,000
Business app with login, payments and admin dashboard100,000 to 300,000
Marketplace or on-demand app (customers, providers, maps, payments)300,000 to 1,000,000+
Monthly maintenance5,000 to 50,000+

These are typical market ranges, not fixed prices. Always quote from a written scope, take a deposit (40 to 50%), and charge for changes outside the scope. See how much an app costs in Kenya.

Protect yourself on client projects

  • Written agreement: scope, timeline, payment stages, who owns the code and accounts, support period.
  • Milestones: design → working prototype → beta → launch, with payment at each.
  • Client owns their accounts: their Google Play account, their Daraja credentials, their hosting. You get access, they keep ownership. It builds trust and avoids disputes.
  • Hand over properly: source code, credentials, a short guide, and training.

The portfolio that gets you hired

  1. Three real apps beat twenty tutorial clones: a business tool (like the Duka stock app in the Flutter course), an app with login and an API, and one that solves a problem for someone you know.
  2. Publish at least one on Google Play (or share an APK and a demo video).
  3. GitHub with clean code and READMEs.
  4. Case studies: problem, what you built, results, screenshots.
  5. LinkedIn posts showing your progress and projects.

Where to find work

  • Local businesses and institutions you can reach in person or through your network.
  • Kenyan job boards (BrighterMonday, Fuzu, LinkedIn).
  • Freelance platforms (Upwork, Fiverr) and vetted networks (Toptal, Andela, Turing).
  • Developer communities: Google Developer Groups, Flutter and Android meetups, tech hubs.

The app developer career landscape

App developers work in startups, banks and fintechs, telecoms, agencies, NGOs and government digital services, or independently as freelancers for local and international clients. Demand comes from businesses wanting to sell online, accept M-Pesa, manage customers, digitise records and serve users on their phones. Skills compound: once you can build and publish an app with payments, you can serve many types of clients.

Career paths

Writing a strong proposal for a client

A clear proposal shows you understand the business, defines scope, and protects both sides.

Pricing your work

Consider:

  • Hours needed (with buffer), your hourly/day rate, and project complexity.
  • Third-party costs (store fees, hosting, SMS, design assets) charged separately and transparently.
  • Ongoing maintenance as a separate monthly or yearly agreement.
  • The value to the client (an app that processes thousands of orders is worth more than a simple brochure).

Avoid racing to the bottom on price; reliable delivery and good communication justify fair rates.

Contracts and protecting yourself

Keep communication in writing (email or a project channel), and send summaries after calls.

Interview preparation for developer jobs

  1. Portfolio walkthrough: be ready to explain one project in depth: problem, architecture, challenges, what you'd improve.
  2. Fundamentals: your language, data structures, APIs and HTTP, Git, basic SQL.
  3. Practical tasks: small take-home or live coding exercises; practise building a screen that fetches and displays data.
  4. System design (junior level): how would you design a simple booking or ordering app (screens, API, database, payments)?
  5. Behavioural questions: teamwork, handling feedback, a bug you struggled with and how you solved it.

Ask questions too: how the team reviews code, how releases work, what you'd learn in the first months.

Building a reputation

  • Publish projects on GitHub with good READMEs and screenshots.
  • Write short posts on LinkedIn about what you're building and learning.
  • Contribute to open source or community projects.
  • Join developer communities (meetups, tech hubs, online groups) and help others.
  • Ask satisfied clients for testimonials and referrals.

Think about it: A client asks you to publish their app under your personal Google Play developer account "to save time and money". What are the risks, and what should you advise?Show answer

The app would legally belong to your account: the client depends on you for every update, transferring it later is extra work, and any policy problem affects your whole account (and your other apps). Advise the client to create their own developer account (in the business name), invite you as a user with the right permissions, and keep payment and store accounts under their ownership.
