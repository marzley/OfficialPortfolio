Level 1: The map of AI tools (what to use for what)

There are thousands of AI tools, and new ones appear every week. You don't need to know them all. You need to know the categories and one or two good tools in each. Many have free plans; prices and features change often.

Chat assistants (the all-rounders)

ToolBest for
ChatGPTGeneral questions, writing, images, voice
ClaudeLong documents, careful writing, analysis, coding
GeminiGoogle Workspace (Gmail, Docs, Sheets), Android
Microsoft CopilotWindows and Microsoft 365 (Word, Excel, PowerPoint)
Meta AIQuick help inside WhatsApp and Instagram

Research and studying

ToolBest for
PerplexityAnswers with sources and links
NotebookLMUpload your notes/PDFs and ask questions only about them; makes study guides and audio summaries
Google Scholar (not AI, but essential)Finding real academic papers to cite

Images and design

ToolBest for
Canva (Magic Studio)Posters, social posts, removing backgrounds, AI images inside designs
ChatGPT / Gemini image generationQuick illustrations and concept images from a description
Adobe FireflyImages built for commercial use, inside Adobe apps
IdeogramImages with readable text (posters, logos ideas)
Microsoft DesignerFree social graphics and invitations

Video and audio

ToolBest for
CapCutAuto-captions, templates, AI effects for TikTok/Reels
RunwayAI video generation and editing
ElevenLabsRealistic voice-overs (use only voices you have the right to use)
DescriptEdit podcasts and videos by editing the transcript

Writing and office work

ToolBest for
GrammarlyGrammar, tone and clarity
Copilot in Microsoft 365 / Gemini in Google WorkspaceDrafting documents, Excel formulas, slide decks, email summaries
Otter.ai / Google Meet notesMeeting transcripts and summaries

Coding

ToolBest for
GitHub CopilotCode suggestions inside VS Code
Claude CodeAn AI coding agent in your terminal and editor
CursorAn AI-first code editor
Chat assistantsExplaining errors, writing small scripts, learning to code

Automation (connecting apps)

ToolBest for
Zapier"When X happens, do Y" between thousands of apps, with AI steps
MakeVisual automations, cheaper for many steps
n8nOpen-source automation you can host yourself

How to choose a tool

  1. Start with a chat assistant: it can do 80% of what most people need.
  2. Add a specialist tool only when you hit a limit (better images, video, meeting notes).
  3. Check the free plan, privacy policy and commercial-use terms before using it for clients.
  4. Don't chase every new app: depth with a few tools beats shallow use of many.
