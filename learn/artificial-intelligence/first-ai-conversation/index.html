Level 1: Your first conversation with an AI assistant

You don't need a laptop, money or technical skills to start. A smartphone and data are enough. This lesson walks you through your first real conversations with an AI assistant.

Getting started (5 minutes)

  1. Install the app from the Play Store (or open the website).
  2. Sign in with your Google account or email.
  3. Type your first message in the box at the bottom and send.
  4. Read the reply, then ask a follow-up. The assistant remembers the conversation so far.
  5. Start a new chat for a new topic (it keeps answers focused).

10 things to try today

Copy these, change the details to your own life, and see what happens.

  1. "Explain what inflation is like I'm 12 years old, with an example using unga prices in Kenya."
  2. "I have KSh 15,000 a month after rent. Help me make a simple budget for food, transport, savings and airtime."
  3. "Rewrite this message to my landlord so it's polite and clear: [paste your message]."
  4. "Give me 10 KCSE-style questions on photosynthesis, then mark my answers."
  5. "Translate this to Kiswahili, keeping it friendly: 'Our shop opens at 7am, and we deliver within Thika town.'"
  6. "I want to start a small chips business in Kitengela. List the costs I should plan for and the questions I should answer first."
  7. "Help me prepare for a job interview as a customer care agent. Ask me one question at a time and give feedback."
  8. "Summarise this article in 5 bullet points: [paste text]."
  9. "Suggest 5 names for a salon in Nakuru, and explain each."
  10. "What should I check before I accept an online job offer?"

Using voice and photos

  • Voice: tap the microphone or headphones icon and speak (in English or Kiswahili). Great when typing is slow or for practising conversation.
  • Photos: tap the camera or "+" icon to send a picture. Try: a photo of a homework question ("explain how to solve this, don't just give the answer"), a plant leaf ("what might be wrong with this tomato plant?"), or a poster ("check this flyer for spelling mistakes").

Reading answers wisely

Every answer is a draft from a fast assistant, not the final truth:

  • If it gives numbers, prices, laws or dates, check them (more in the fact-checking lesson).
  • Ask "How sure are you?" or "What could be wrong in your answer?" It often points out weak spots.
  • Ask "Where can I confirm this?" and check official sources (KRA, eCitizen, Ministry websites).

Keep yourself safe

  • Don't share your M-Pesa PIN, passwords, ID number, or other people's private information.
  • Don't paste confidential work documents into a personal AI account unless your employer allows it.
  • For health, legal or money decisions, use AI to understand and prepare questions, then talk to a professional.
  • Check the app's privacy settings (many let you turn off using your chats for training).

Practice

Do this now: open any assistant and have a 5-message conversation about something you want to learn this month. Ask follow-ups like "give me an example", "make it shorter", "now in Kiswahili", "quiz me".

How to write prompts that get good answers

The quality of an AI answer depends heavily on how you ask. A vague question gets a generic answer; a clear request with context gets something useful. A simple structure works for almost everything:

PartExample
Role (optional)"Act as a friendly maths tutor for a Form 2 student."
Task"Explain how to calculate simple interest."
Context"I'm in Kenya, and the example should use Kenyan shillings and a SACCO loan."
Format"Use 3 short steps and one worked example, under 150 words."
Constraints"Don't use difficult words. End with one practice question."

Weak vs strong prompts

WeakStrong
"Write a CV""Help me write a one-page CV for a junior accounts assistant role in Nairobi. Here are my details: [education, skills, attachment experience]. Use clear headings and bullet points."
"Tell me about farming""I have half an acre in Kiambu. Suggest three vegetables I could grow for the local market in the long rains, with rough planting-to-harvest times. Say which details I should confirm with an agricultural officer."
"Fix this""Check this email for grammar and make it more polite but keep it short: [email]"

Following up: AI chats are conversations

You rarely get the perfect answer first time. Improve it step by step:

  • "Make it shorter / simpler / more formal."
  • "Give me three alternative versions."
  • "Explain step 2 in more detail."
  • "Translate this into Kiswahili."
  • "Turn that into a table."
  • "What questions should I ask a professional about this?"

Start a new chat for a new topic, so earlier instructions don't confuse the answer.

Privacy habits

  • Don't paste ID numbers, passwords, PINs, bank details, medical records or other people's personal information.
  • Remove names from work documents before asking for help, unless your organisation allows that tool.
  • Check the tool's settings for chat history and whether conversations are used to improve the model; turn off what you don't want.
  • Remember that screenshots and shared chat links can be seen by others.

Practice

  1. Rewrite three weak prompts from your own life using the role–task–context–format–constraints structure.
  2. Ask an AI to quiz you on a topic you're studying, then check two answers in your notes or textbook.
  3. Use follow-up prompts to improve a draft message three times.
  4. Ask the same question to two different assistants and compare the answers.
  5. Find the privacy and history settings in the assistant you use.
