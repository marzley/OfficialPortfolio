C. AI training and data work (ways 22–32): annotation, AI rating, Remotasks-style tasks and data entry

Artificial intelligence learns from examples prepared by humans: photos with every car outlined, sentences labelled as positive or negative, chatbot answers ranked from best to worst. This human work is called data annotation or AI training, and Kenya has been one of the world's important hubs for it, with companies like Sama and CloudFactory employing data workers in Nairobi, and thousands of Kenyans working on platforms such as Remotasks before it left the country. This unit explains the eleven main types of AI and data work, how to get in, and the realities of pay, availability and working conditions.

What happened to Remotasks in Kenya

Remotasks (owned by Scale AI) was very popular with Kenyan workers. In March 2024 it stopped offering work in Kenya (and some other countries), leaving many workers without access. Lessons from that:

  1. Never depend on one platform. Projects and whole platforms can end suddenly.
  2. Never buy or rent accounts from others to get around country restrictions; accounts get banned and earnings are lost.
  3. Build transferable skills (accuracy, a subject speciality, coding, languages) that work on many platforms and in full-time jobs.

How AI training work works

  1. You apply to a platform or company and pass qualification tests.
  2. You're added to projects (each with its own guidelines, often 10–50 pages).
  3. You complete tasks in the platform's tool; reviewers check quality.
  4. Pay is per task or per hour; your quality score decides how much work you get.
  5. Projects end; you qualify for new ones.

The two keys to success

Read guidelines completely (most people skim and fail), and choose quality over speed. Accurate workers get promoted to reviewer roles and better projects; fast careless workers get removed.

22. Image and video annotation

What it is. Labelling images and video frames: drawing bounding boxes around objects (cars, people, products), tracing exact outlines (polygons / segmentation), marking points (keypoints on faces or bodies), tracking objects across video frames, and labelling road scenes for self-driving cars.

Skills. Patience, precision, a good screen and mouse (a laptop or desktop; a mouse is much more accurate than a touchpad).

How to start:

  1. Learn the tool types with free software: CVAT (cvat.ai) and Label Studio (labelstud.io) let you practise annotation on your own images.
  2. Apply to annotation companies and platforms above; take the qualification tests carefully.
  3. Build speed only after your accuracy is consistently high.

23. Text labelling and classification

What it is. Reading text and labelling it: sentiment (positive/negative), topic, intent ("wants a refund"), named entities (marking names, places, amounts), spam detection, and checking machine translations.

How to start: apply to Appen, OneForma, Toloka and Clickworker projects; practise with Label Studio. Language skills (Kiswahili and others) open extra projects.

24. AI response rating (RLHF)

What it is. Chatbots like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini improve partly through RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback): humans compare AI answers and rate which is more helpful, correct, safe and well written, and explain why.

Skills. Excellent English reading and writing, careful fact-checking, clear explanations of your ratings.

How to start:

  1. Use AI chatbots a lot and notice their mistakes: wrong facts, missed instructions, unsafe advice, poor formatting.
  2. Learn the common rating dimensions: helpfulness, honesty/accuracy, harmlessness, instruction following, writing quality.
  3. Apply to AI trainer roles (platforms above; search "AI trainer" and "AI writing evaluator" on LinkedIn and Upwork).
  4. In tests, justify your ratings specifically: "Response B is better because it answers both parts of the question and correctly states the KRA deadline, while A ignores the second part."

25. AI writing evaluation and prompt writing

What it is. Writing prompts (questions and instructions) that challenge AI models, writing ideal "gold" responses, and red-teaming (trying to make AI produce harmful content, so developers can fix it, under strict guidelines).

How to start: strong writing samples (method 1), practise writing complex prompts with clear constraints, and apply to AI training projects. Red-teaming projects require following safety rules strictly and handling disturbing content professionally.

26. Coding tasks for AI training

What it is. Writing, reviewing and rating code produced by AI; creating coding problems and solutions; explaining bugs. Languages commonly requested: Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, Java, C++, SQL, Go.

Who it suits. Developers and strong programming students. Pay is usually much higher than general annotation.

How to start:

  1. Get solid at one or two languages (Python and JavaScript subjects on this hub).
  2. Practise on coding challenge sites (LeetCode, HackerRank, Exercism) and review AI-generated code critically.
  3. Apply for "AI coding trainer" or "software engineer, AI training" roles; tests usually involve finding bugs and explaining fixes.

27. Expert AI training (maths, science, medicine, law, finance)

What it is. AI companies hire subject experts to write hard questions, check AI reasoning step by step and correct errors in specialised fields.

Who it suits. Graduates and professionals: maths, physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, law, accounting, engineering, economics. Often a degree is required, sometimes a master's or PhD for top projects.

How to start: apply to expert AI trainer programmes (e.g. Outlier and similar, where available, plus LinkedIn listings), highlight your degree and specialism, and prepare for subject tests.

In Kenya

Kenya has many well-qualified graduates in sciences, health and engineering who struggle to find jobs locally. Expert AI training is one of the few online fields where a degree in, say, chemistry or nursing directly leads to well-paid remote work, though availability depends on projects and country eligibility.

28. Search engine and ads evaluation (rater jobs)

What it is. Rating how relevant search results, ads, maps results or recommendations are for a query in your country and language, following detailed guidelines (Google publishes its own Search Quality Rater Guidelines publicly).

How to start: apply to rater roles at TELUS Digital, Appen and similar companies when they open for Kenya/English or Kiswahili; study the public rater guidelines before the exam. These are often part-time with set weekly hours.

29. Content moderation

What it is. Reviewing posts, images and videos against platform rules and removing harmful content, for social media companies and apps, usually through outsourcing companies (BPOs).

The reality. Content moderation can expose workers to violent and disturbing content. In Kenya, former moderators brought widely reported legal cases about working conditions and mental health support. If you consider this work:

  • Ask about psychological support, exposure limits, breaks and contracts before accepting.
  • Know your rights under Kenyan labour law.
  • It's fine to decide this work isn't for you.

30. Data entry

What it is. Typing or copying information into spreadsheets, databases, CRMs or online stores: product catalogues, survey results, invoices, contact lists.

How to start:

  1. Master Excel/Google Sheets basics (the Excel subject on this hub): formatting, sorting, filters, data validation, removing duplicates.
  2. Offer data entry on Upwork/Fiverr and to local businesses (stock lists, school records).
  3. Prove accuracy with a small sample task.

Watch out. "Data entry jobs" advertised on WhatsApp/Telegram that ask for a "registration fee" or ask you to "buy software" are scams. Real data entry is often low-paid; add Excel skills to move into better-paid data analysis (method 86).

31. Web research and lead lists

What it is. Finding information online for clients: building lists of potential customers (business names, websites, emails) for sales teams, researching competitors, collecting prices, verifying addresses.

How to start: learn advanced Google search operators (site:, quotes, filetype:), LinkedIn search, Google Maps business research, and spreadsheet organisation. Offer "lead generation lists" on Upwork/Fiverr. Follow data protection rules: use publicly available business information, not personal data scraped without permission.

32. Local-language data for AI

What it is. Creating and checking data in African languages: translating sentences, writing natural sentences, rating translations, transcribing local-language audio (method 16), recording speech (method 20).

How to start: list all languages you speak fluently on platform profiles (OneForma, Appen, Toloka, and others), watch for language projects, and join African language technology communities (such as Masakhane, a research community for African languages) to hear about projects and research.

A realistic plan to get into AI work

  1. Week 1: create accounts on 3–4 platforms that accept Kenya; complete profiles honestly (languages, education, skills).
  2. Week 1–2: take qualification tests slowly; read every guideline.
  3. Ongoing: keep a notebook of each project's rules; ask questions in project forums.
  4. Parallel: build a stronger skill (coding, a subject speciality, Excel) for higher-paying AI training and jobs.
  5. Always: keep other income options; projects end without warning.

Practice tasks

  1. Install Label Studio or use CVAT online and annotate 20 images with bounding boxes.
  2. Ask two AI chatbots the same tricky question and write a rating explaining which answer is better and why.
  3. List your languages and subject strengths; find two AI projects that match them.
  4. Clean a messy spreadsheet of 100 contacts (remove duplicates, fix formatting).
  5. Read the first chapter of Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines.

Summary

  • AI training work: image/video annotation, text labelling, AI rating (RLHF), prompt writing and red-teaming, coding and expert training, search rating, moderation, data entry, research and local-language data.
  • Kenya is a hub (Sama, CloudFactory and many platform workers), but platforms change: Remotasks stopped serving Kenya in March 2024.
  • Success = reading guidelines fully and prioritising accuracy.
  • Higher pay comes from coding, subject expertise and languages.
  • Never buy/rent accounts or pay to join; know your rights, especially in content moderation.
