F. Content creation (ways 56–67): YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, podcasts, UGC and brand deals

Kenyan creators now earn from YouTube ads, brand partnerships, sponsored TikToks, live gifts, affiliate links and their own products. Some have built real businesses. But for every creator who earns well, many post for months with little income. This unit explains twelve content-creation methods: how each platform pays, step-by-step setup, how to grow, and the honest timeline.

The honest timeline

Most creators earn little or nothing for the first 6–12 months. Platform payouts depend on audience location (advertisers pay more for viewers in the US and Europe than in Kenya), niche and watch time. In Kenya, the biggest creator income usually comes from brand deals and selling your own products/services, not platform payouts alone. Treat content as a long-term business.

56. YouTube (long videos)

How YouTube pays. Through the YouTube Partner Program (YPP). Requirements (check YouTube's current page, as they change):

  • Fan funding features (memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat) open at 500 subscribers with 3 public uploads in 90 days and either 3,000 watch hours in 12 months or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days.
  • Ad revenue sharing at 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million valid Shorts views in 90 days.
  • You also need to follow YouTube's monetisation policies, live in an eligible country (Kenya is eligible), and link an AdSense account (payments to your bank by wire transfer).

How to start, step by step:

  1. Pick a niche and plan 10 video ideas people actually search for (use YouTube search suggestions).
  2. Equipment: a smartphone (rear camera, landscape), a cheap clip-on microphone (audio quality matters more than video), natural light from a window.
  3. Script or outline every video: hook in the first 15 seconds, deliver value, call to action.
  4. Edit with CapCut (free, phone and PC) or DaVinci Resolve (free, PC).
  5. Create a strong thumbnail (method 53) and a searchable title ("How to apply for a KRA PIN in 2026: step by step").
  6. Publish consistently (e.g. one video weekly) for at least 6 months; study YouTube Studio analytics (click-through rate, average view duration).
  7. Reply to comments; collaborate with similar creators.

Pay reality. Revenue per 1,000 views varies widely by niche and audience country; finance, tech and business niches with international viewers earn more than entertainment watched mostly in Kenya. Many Kenyan YouTubers earn more from sponsorships than ads.

57. YouTube Shorts

What it is. Vertical videos up to 3 minutes. Shorts grow channels fast; Shorts ad revenue is shared from a pool and is usually much lower per view than long videos.

Strategy. Use Shorts to attract subscribers to your long videos and to your products/services; repurpose the same clips on TikTok, Reels and Facebook.

58. TikTok

How TikTok earns for Kenyan creators. TikTok's main creator payout programme (the Creator Rewards Program) has been available only in selected countries, and Kenya has not been on that list as of this writing (check TikTok's current eligibility). Kenyan TikTokers mainly earn through:

  • Brand deals (often the biggest source), including via TikTok's Creator Marketplace/TikTok One where available.
  • LIVE gifts (viewers send gifts that convert to "diamonds" and then money; you must meet age and follower requirements).
  • Selling your own products and services and driving followers to WhatsApp or a shop.
  • Affiliate links and promotions.

How to start:

  1. Choose a niche; study 20 top videos in it (hook, length, sound, captions).
  2. Post often (daily if possible at the start); the first 1–3 seconds decide whether people keep watching.
  3. Use on-screen text and captions (many watch without sound).
  4. Engage: reply to comments with videos, go LIVE when eligible.
  5. Build a media kit (followers, average views, audience location, past brand work) to pitch brands.

59. Facebook

How it pays. Meta offers monetisation tools such as in-stream ads, Stars (fans send Stars during videos and lives), subscriptions and performance-based programmes, and has been expanding eligibility country by country. Check the Monetisation section in your Facebook Professional Dashboard to see what's available to your page.

Why Facebook matters in Kenya. It has a very large adult audience, Facebook groups are powerful for communities and selling, and many brands still advertise there.

How to start: create a Page or switch your profile to professional mode, post consistent video content (Reels plus longer videos), build community in groups, and follow Meta's monetisation and content policies (reposting others' content can disqualify you).

60. Instagram

How it pays. Mostly brand partnerships (sponsored posts, Reels and stories), affiliate links and driving followers to products/services. Instagram also offers tools like subscriptions and gifts in some regions.

How to start: strong visual niche (fashion, beauty, food, travel, design), consistent Reels, a clear bio with a link (WhatsApp or website), and Highlights for products, prices and reviews.

61. Podcasting

What it is. Audio (and increasingly video) shows on Spotify, Apple Podcasts and YouTube.

How it pays. Sponsorships (most common), listener support, premium episodes, and building authority that sells your services.

How to start:

  1. Choose a format (solo, interviews, co-hosts) and a niche ("tech careers in Kenya", "farming business", "relationships").
  2. Record with a USB mic or phone + clip mic; edit (method 19).
  3. Host on a free podcast host (e.g. Spotify for Creators) that distributes to Spotify and Apple; publish video versions on YouTube.
  4. Publish weekly; cut short clips for TikTok and Reels.

62. Blogging with ads

What it is. Writing helpful articles on your own website and earning from Google AdSense or ad networks, affiliate links and sponsored posts.

How to start: buy a domain and hosting (or start free), install WordPress, write in-depth articles answering real searches (SEO, method 2), apply for AdSense once you have quality content and traffic. Blogging is slow (often 6–12+ months to meaningful traffic), and AI-generated search answers are changing traffic, so focus on unique, experience-based content.

63. UGC (user-generated content) creator

What it is. Creating authentic-looking videos and photos for brands to use in their own ads and pages, like a customer testimonial or unboxing. You don't need a big following, because the brand posts the content, not you.

How to start, step by step:

  1. Make 5–10 sample UGC videos with products you own: unboxing, "3 reasons I love...", before/after, tutorial. Natural lighting, clear voice, vertical format, 15–60 seconds.
  2. Build a simple portfolio (Google Drive folder or a free website) and a rate card.
  3. Pitch local brands (skincare, food, fashion, apps, fintechs) and global brands through UGC platforms and LinkedIn/Instagram DMs.
  4. Agree on usage rights (how long and where the brand can use your content) and price accordingly.

Why it's great for beginners. Brands pay for the content itself; follower count matters much less.

64. Influencer brand deals

How it works. Brands pay creators to promote products to their audience: single posts, campaigns, ambassador contracts, event appearances.

How to get deals:

  1. Build an engaged audience in a clear niche (engagement and audience fit matter more than raw follower count; micro-influencers with 5,000–50,000 engaged followers do get paid work).
  2. Create a media kit: who you are, audience demographics and location, average views, engagement rate, past work, contacts, packages.
  3. Pitch brands you already use, with a specific idea.
  4. Use simple written agreements: deliverables, deadlines, payment terms (deposit), usage rights, exclusivity.
  5. Disclose sponsored content ("#ad", "sponsored", or the platform's paid partnership label): it's required by platforms and builds trust.

Ethics. Never promote betting to minors, unlicensed investment schemes or "get rich quick" products. Your audience's trust is your business.

65. Live streaming and gifts

What it is. Earning from viewers' gifts and donations during live streams: TikTok LIVE gifts, YouTube Super Chat and memberships, Facebook Stars, and gaming streams on YouTube or Twitch.

Tips. Go live on a schedule, have a topic or activity (Q&A, tutorial, gaming, music), thank supporters by name, and moderate comments. Follow age rules; gifting features require you to be 18+.

66. Video editing for creators

What it is. Editing other people's videos: YouTube videos, Shorts/Reels/TikToks, podcasts, church services, adverts, weddings.

How to start: learn CapCut and then DaVinci Resolve or Premiere Pro; build a showreel; offer packages ("4 YouTube videos + 12 Shorts per month") to creators and businesses; find clients on Upwork, Fiverr, YouTube creator communities and locally. Editors with storytelling and retention skills are in high demand.

67. Faceless channels

What it is. Channels where the creator doesn't appear: voice-over with stock footage, screen recordings (software tutorials), animations, compilations, relaxing music, story narration.

Honest notes:

  • Platforms demonetise "reused" or mass-produced low-value content (e.g. other people's clips, repetitive AI slideshows). YouTube's policies on inauthentic and repetitious content apply.
  • Faceless channels succeed when they add real value: original scripts, research, good narration, useful tutorials.
  • AI tools can help with scripts and voice, but disclose realistic altered or synthetic content where platforms require it, and check facts.

Practice tasks

  1. Write your niche statement: "I make [type of content] for [audience] to help them [result]."
  2. Plan 10 video ideas with searchable titles.
  3. Film and edit one 60-second video with captions in CapCut.
  4. Make 3 UGC sample videos for products you own.
  5. Draft a one-page media kit.

Summary

  • Content income comes from platform payouts, brand deals, fan support, affiliate links, products and services.
  • YouTube's Partner Program has thresholds (500 subscribers for fan funding; 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours or 10M Shorts views for ads); Kenya is eligible.
  • TikTok's main payout programme has been limited to certain countries; Kenyan TikTokers earn mainly from brand deals, LIVE gifts and selling.
  • UGC lets beginners earn without a big audience.
  • Choose a niche, post consistently, prioritise audio and value, disclose sponsorships, and expect a long build-up.
