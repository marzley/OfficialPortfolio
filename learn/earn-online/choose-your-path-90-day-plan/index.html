Choose your path: a 90-day plan, pricing, getting clients, taxes and avoiding burnout

You've now seen more than 130 ways to earn online. The biggest mistake now would be trying ten at once. This final unit helps you choose, gives you a 90-day plan, explains pricing and finding clients, and covers money management, taxes and staying healthy.

Step 1: Choose one main method and one backup

Score each method you're considering from 1–5:

QuestionMethod AMethod BMethod C
Do I have or can I learn the skill in 1–3 months?
Does my device/internet support it?
Is there demand (local or international)?

Do I enjoy it?
Total

Pick the highest as your main method, and one quick-start method as a backup (e.g. main: web design; backup: transcription or social media help for local businesses).

Step 2: The 90-day plan

Days 1–30: Learn and build proof

  • Study the skill daily (1–3 hours). Use this hub's subjects and free courses.
  • Create 3–5 portfolio samples (or your first products/videos).
  • Set up profiles: LinkedIn, the main platform (Upwork, Fiverr, Preply, Jumia, YouTube...), WhatsApp Business.
  • Set up payments: M-Pesa, PayPal/Payoneer.

Days 31–60: Get first clients or sales

  • Apply/pitch every day: e.g. 3 proposals daily, or 5 local businesses visited per week, or daily content.
  • Do your first jobs extremely well; over-communicate; ask for reviews.
  • Track: applications, replies, jobs won, income, hours.

Days 61–90: Improve and grow

  • Analyse: which proposals or content worked? Do more of that.
  • Raise prices slightly after 3–5 good reviews.
  • Create packages and repeat-client offers.
  • Learn the next skill that increases your value (e.g. design → UI/UX; writing → SEO; VA → bookkeeping).

Pricing your work

ApproachWhen
Per hourOngoing support, VA, tutoring
Per project / packageWebsites, logos, content packages (usually better for you)
Per unitPer word, per audio minute, per product listing
Retainer (monthly)Social media management, maintenance, bookkeeping

How to set a price:

  1. Check what others with similar experience charge on the platform or locally.
  2. Calculate your minimum: monthly costs ÷ realistic billable hours.
  3. Start reasonably (not the cheapest), raise prices as reviews and results grow.
  4. Always define scope, revisions and deadlines in writing.

Finding clients: the four channels

  1. Platforms: Upwork, Fiverr, Preply, Jumia etc. Competitive but full of buyers.
  2. Local outreach: walk in, call, WhatsApp businesses near you with a specific offer.
  3. Content and social media: show your work publicly so clients come to you (LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram, YouTube).
  4. Referrals: happy clients recommend you; ask them directly.

A proposal template

Hi [Name], I read that you need [their need]. I've done similar work for [example/sample link], where I [result]. For your project I'd [2–3 specific steps]. I can deliver by [date]. One question: [smart question about their project]. Thanks, [Your name]

Short, specific, about them, with proof.

Money management for online earners

  • Separate accounts: keep business income separate from personal spending (a separate M-Pesa line or bank account helps).
  • Pay yourself a fixed amount; online income goes up and down.
  • Emergency fund: 3–6 months of expenses (a money market fund works well).
  • Save for tax and equipment (set aside a percentage of each payment).
  • Invest in skills and tools that increase income (a better laptop, courses, a backup internet line).
  • Avoid mobile loans for business except in clear, planned cases.

Taxes and registration

  • Online income is taxable in Kenya. Get a KRA PIN, keep records and file returns on iTax by 30 June each year.
  • As you grow, consider registering a business name on eCitizen (BRS), and get advice on which tax regime applies (rules for income tax, turnover tax and withholding tax change; check kra.go.ke or a tax professional).
  • Keep invoices, receipts, platform statements and M-Pesa statements.

Protecting yourself

  • Never pay to get work; never share OTPs and PINs; verify companies and brokers.
  • Use contracts and deposits; keep payments on platforms.
  • Back up your work (Google Drive) and secure accounts with strong passwords and two-factor authentication.
  • Don't rent or buy accounts.

Health and avoiding burnout

  • Set working hours and breaks; online work can swallow your whole day and night.
  • Look after your eyes (20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds), back (a proper chair and screen height) and sleep.
  • Connect with other freelancers (online communities, Ajira Digital clubs) for support and advice.
  • Rejection is normal: many proposals get no reply. Track and improve rather than giving up.

Your commitment

Write it down:

"For the next 90 days, my main method is [method]. I will practise [X] hours a day, build [number] samples, and send [number] proposals/pitches a week. My backup method is [method]. My first income goal is KSh [amount]."

Summary

  • Choose one main method and one backup using a simple scoring table.
  • Follow a 90-day plan: learn and build proof → get first clients → improve and grow.
  • Price per project or retainer where possible; define scope in writing; raise prices with reviews.
  • Find clients through platforms, local outreach, content and referrals.
  • Manage money (separate accounts, emergency fund, tax savings), register and file taxes, protect yourself and your health.
