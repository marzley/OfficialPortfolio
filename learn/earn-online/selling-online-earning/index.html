J. Selling online (ways 101–110): Jumia, Kilimall, WhatsApp, your own shop, digital products and crafts

Kenyans buy online every day: phones and electronics on marketplaces, clothes on Instagram, food on WhatsApp, second-hand items on Facebook Marketplace, and digital products like e-books and templates. Selling online can be a full business or a side income. This unit explains ten selling methods with practical steps, costs and the logistics of delivery and payment in Kenya.

The basics of any online selling business

  1. Product: something people want, at a price that leaves profit after all costs.
  2. Trust: real photos, reviews, clear prices, a physical location or reliable contacts, fair returns.
  3. Payment: M-Pesa (Till or Paybill makes you look professional), cash on delivery, cards.
  4. Delivery: riders (boda/courier apps), parcel services, matatu shuttles and courier companies for upcountry orders, or pickup points.
  5. Records: stock, sales, costs, profit (a simple spreadsheet).

101. Selling on Jumia

What it is. Listing products on Jumia (jumia.co.ke), Kenya's large online marketplace, which brings customers, handles payments and offers delivery options; you pay commissions and fees.

How to start, step by step:

  1. Register as a seller on Jumia's seller centre (you'll need business details, ID, KRA PIN, and bank or payment details; check current requirements).
  2. Complete seller training (Jumia provides guides and training).
  3. List products with excellent photos (white background for the main image), accurate titles, specifications and competitive prices.
  4. Understand commissions per category, fees, delivery and returns policies before pricing.
  5. Fulfil orders fast; good ratings improve visibility. Use promotions (campaigns like Black Friday) carefully, keeping profit in mind.

Best for: electronics, accessories, beauty, fashion, home and kitchen products.

102. Kilimall and other marketplaces

What it is. Kilimall (kilimall.co.ke) and other marketplaces and classified sites (e.g. Jiji for classifieds) also let you list products.

How to start: compare commissions, traffic and categories; list your best products on two or three channels; keep stock levels synchronised to avoid selling what you don't have.

103. WhatsApp, Facebook Marketplace and Instagram selling

What it is. Selling directly through social media, the most common online selling method in Kenya.

How to start, step by step:

  1. Set up WhatsApp Business with a catalogue (photos, prices, descriptions), greeting message and quick replies (method 75).
  2. Post products on WhatsApp Status daily, in a WhatsApp Channel, and in relevant groups (follow group rules).
  3. List on Facebook Marketplace (local buyers search it a lot) and in Facebook buy-and-sell groups.
  4. Build an Instagram shop page with Highlights for categories, prices, delivery info and reviews.
  5. Get an M-Pesa Till (Lipa na M-Pesa Buy Goods) for a professional payment experience.
  6. Arrange reliable delivery: local riders, parcel services for upcountry.
  7. Ask happy customers for photos and reviews; post them (with permission).

Protect yourself and your customers

  • Common scams against sellers: fake M-Pesa SMS messages ("confirm the SMS by checking your actual M-Pesa balance or statement before releasing goods"), and buyers asking you to send money back after "overpaying".
  • Don't ask customers to pay before delivery unless they trust you; consider pay-on-delivery for nearby orders or a deposit for custom items.

104. Your own online shop

What it is. A website store you control (WooCommerce, Shopify, or simpler tools), with M-Pesa and card payments.

Why. No marketplace commissions, your own brand and customer list. But you must bring your own traffic (social media, ads, SEO).

How to start: start selling on social media first; when you have steady sales, build a store (method 81) or hire a developer; connect M-Pesa; drive traffic with content and ads.

105. Local dropshipping

What it is. Selling products you don't stock: you market them, the customer orders and pays, then you buy from a supplier (wholesaler in town, or a supplier who delivers) and arrange delivery, keeping the margin.

How to start:

  1. Find reliable suppliers (wholesale shops, importers) who'll sell single items and can deliver or hold items for you.
  2. Test products with low risk: post photos (with permission), take orders, then purchase.
  3. Agree clearly with suppliers on stock confirmation, quality and returns.
  4. Be honest with customers on delivery times.

Risks. Suppliers running out, quality problems and slow deliveries damage your reputation. International dropshipping into Kenya faces long shipping times and import duties; local suppliers usually work better.

106. Digital products

What it is. Products sold as downloads: e-books, guides, templates (Canva, Excel budget planners, CV templates), printables, presets, music beats, stock packs, courses (method 95).

Where: Selar (selar.co), Payhip (payhip.com), Gumroad (gumroad.com), Etsy-style marketplaces, your own site, or WhatsApp + M-Pesa with manual delivery.

How to start:

  1. Solve a specific problem: "Excel budget template for Kenyan families", "Guide to starting a chicken business: costs and suppliers", "Wedding planning checklist".
  2. Create it well (Canva, Google Docs, Excel), with clear instructions.
  3. Set up a product page with a strong title, preview images and price.
  4. Promote with free valuable content (TikTok, YouTube, WhatsApp Channel).

Why it's attractive. Create once, sell many times; no delivery costs.

107. Print-on-demand merchandise

What it is. Selling custom T-shirts, hoodies, mugs and caps with your designs, printed only when ordered: through international POD platforms (method 48) or local printing partners in Kenya for your own brand.

How to start locally: design a small collection (Swahili phrases, campus merchandise, church and event merch, profession-themed designs); partner with a local printer for per-order printing; take pre-orders with deposits; sell through social media and events.

108. Selling Kenyan crafts and products to the world

What it is. Selling handmade items (Maasai beadwork, kiondo baskets, soapstone carvings, kikoys, leather sandals, jewellery, art) and products like Kenyan tea and coffee to customers abroad.

How to start:

  1. Source from artisans fairly (agree prices, quality and timelines; fair trade practices build lasting supply).
  2. Photograph products beautifully; tell the story of the artisans.
  3. Sell through international marketplaces that accept sellers from Kenya (check each platform's seller country list), your own website, Instagram and diaspora networks.
  4. Learn export basics: packaging, courier costs (Posta EMS, international couriers), customs documents and any export requirements for your product category (e.g. food and wood products may need certificates).

109. Reselling and thrift (mitumba) online

What it is. Buying quality second-hand clothes (mitumba), shoes, bags, electronics or furniture, cleaning/repairing and photographing them, and reselling online.

How to start: learn to pick quality items in markets (Gikomba, Toi, local markets), clean and photograph them well, post consistently on Instagram/TikTok/WhatsApp, and offer delivery. "Thrift drops" at set times create excitement. For electronics, test thoroughly and be honest about condition.

110. Farm produce online

What it is. Selling eggs, vegetables, fruits, honey, milk products, chicken and other produce directly to households, restaurants and offices through WhatsApp, social media and delivery, or through agri-marketplace apps.

How to start: ensure consistent supply and quality; set up a WhatsApp catalogue; offer weekly delivery routes/subscriptions (e.g. a weekly vegetable box); follow food safety and any licensing rules for your product (e.g. dairy and meat have stricter requirements).

Practice tasks

  1. Pick one product and calculate profit per item with all costs.
  2. Set up a WhatsApp Business catalogue with 5 products and quality photos.
  3. Write a Facebook Marketplace listing with title, price, description and delivery terms.
  4. Create a simple digital product (a one-page checklist or template) and price it.
  5. List three suppliers near you and their minimum orders and delivery options.

Summary

  • Selling methods: Jumia, Kilimall and classifieds, WhatsApp/Facebook/Instagram, your own shop, local dropshipping, digital products, print-on-demand, Kenyan crafts for export, reselling/thrift and farm produce.
  • Calculate profit after all costs; build trust with photos, reviews and reliable delivery.
  • Use M-Pesa Till/Paybill; verify payments in your M-Pesa account, not SMS screenshots.
  • Start small, find what sells, then scale.
