Freelance scams, account safety and handling disputes

Scammers target freelancers because freelancers are actively looking for work, often new, and sometimes desperate for a first client. In Kenya, fake "online jobs", account-rental schemes and fake clients cost people a lot of money every year. This lesson shows the common scams, how to protect your accounts, and what to do when a real client relationship goes wrong. See also avoid scams and online job scams in Kenya.

The one rule that stops most scams

Real clients pay you. You never pay to get work.

Any request for money before you earn (registration, activation, training, software, "tax", "clearance", "account upgrade") is almost certainly a scam.

How to check a client or job

  1. On platforms: payment verified, hiring history, reviews from other freelancers, location, money spent. Read the reviews they received.
  2. Off platforms: does the company exist? Website on its own domain, working phone number, registered business, real people on LinkedIn, Google reviews. Search "company name + scam".
  3. Email domain: "hr@realcompany.com" vs "realcompany.hr2026@gmail.com".
  4. The offer itself: pay far above normal for simple work is a red flag.
  5. Trust your gut: pressure and urgency are manipulation tactics.

Protecting your accounts

  • Unique strong passwords for email, platforms and payment accounts, stored in a password manager (passwords and 2FA).
  • Two-factor authentication everywhere, preferably an authenticator app (not just SMS).
  • Secure your email first: it can reset every other account.
  • Protect your SIM: a SIM swap can steal SMS codes and M-Pesa (SIM swap); set up an M-Pesa/SIM PIN and never share OTPs.
  • Never share your platform login, ID photos, selfies for "verification", or M-Pesa PIN with "agents", "account managers" or "clients".
  • Use your own account only, in your real name; one account per platform unless the platform allows more.
  • Keep devices clean: updates on, legal software, no unknown downloads (malware).

When a real project goes wrong: disputes

Most disputes come from unclear scope or communication. Prevention is in discovery, quotes and contracts and communication and delivery. When it happens anyway:

Step 1: Talk calmly

  • Find out what exactly the client is unhappy with.
  • Compare it to the written scope; separate genuine issues from new requests.
  • Offer a fair solution: fix genuine issues quickly; quote new requests.

Step 2: Put it in writing

Summarise the problem, the agreed scope, what you've delivered and your proposed solution, in a calm, factual message. Keep screenshots of important messages and files.

Step 3: Use the platform's process

  • Upwork: fixed-price milestones have a dispute and mediation process; hourly protection depends on using the time tracker correctly. Read the current rules in the help centre.
  • Fiverr: use the Resolution Center (extensions, modifications, cancellations) and contact Customer Support if needed. Don't cancel orders casually; cancellations affect your account.
  • Keep all communication on the platform, which is what support will look at.

Step 4: Direct clients who won't pay

  1. Polite reminder on the due date, with the invoice.
  2. Firm reminder after 7 days, referencing the agreement.
  3. Final notice with a deadline, stating next steps.
  4. Don't hand over final files/passwords until paid (as agreed in your contract).
  5. For larger amounts: a formal demand letter, and the Small Claims Court in Kenya, which handles claims up to a set limit with simpler procedures. Check the current limit and process on the Judiciary's website.

Step 5: Know when to let go

Sometimes the cost (time, stress) of chasing a small amount is higher than the amount. Learn from it (stronger deposits, clearer scope) and move on. Never threaten, insult or publicly shame a client; it can come back on you legally and professionally.

If you've been scammed

  • Stop sending money or information immediately.
  • Secure accounts: change passwords, turn on 2FA, log out other sessions.
  • Report: to the platform; to Safaricom (call 100 for M-Pesa issues, or forward scam SMS to 333); to your bank; to the police / DCI with screenshots and transaction codes.
  • Warn others in your communities (without sharing personal information).
  • Be kind to yourself: scammers are professionals, and reporting helps stop them.

Summary

  • Real clients pay you; never pay to get work, rent accounts or forward money for "clients".
  • Check clients and companies; stay on platforms until a contract exists; don't download unknown files.
  • Protect your email, SIM and platform accounts with strong passwords and 2FA.
  • Handle disputes calmly, in writing and through platform processes; use reminders, demand letters and the Small Claims Court for unpaid direct work.
