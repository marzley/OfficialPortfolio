Google Calendar, Meet and staying organised online

Managing time and meeting people online are core workplace skills. Google Calendar and Google Meet are free with any Google account, and the same ideas apply to Outlook, Microsoft Teams and Zoom.

Google Calendar

Create events

  1. Open calendar.google.com (or the app) and click a time slot, or + Create.
  2. Add a title, date/time, location or Add Google Meet video conferencing.
  3. Add guests by email: they get an invitation to accept or decline.
  4. Set notifications (e.g. 30 minutes before, 1 day before).
  5. Repeat for recurring events: every Monday, monthly on the 5th (chama meetings, fee reminders).

Google Meet: online meetings and classes

Start or join

  • From Calendar (the Meet link in the event), from meet.google.com, or the Meet/Gmail app.
  • Share the link: anyone with a Google account (and often without) can join.

Data use

Video calls use roughly 0.5–1.5 GB per hour. To save bundles: turn off your camera, lower the video quality in settings, or join by audio only.

Meeting etiquette

  1. Join a few minutes early and test your mic and camera.
  2. Use a quiet place, headphones if possible, and face a light source.
  3. Mute when not speaking; use chat or raise hand.
  4. Share an agenda before and action points after (who does what, by when).
  5. Start and end on time.

Personal productivity habits

  • Time-blocking: put focused work sessions in your calendar, not just meetings.
  • One to-do list (Google Tasks, Keep, or a notebook), reviewed every morning.
  • Inbox zero: in Gmail, archive or label emails once handled; use stars or Snooze for follow-ups.
  • Two-minute rule: if a task takes under two minutes, do it now.
  • Weekly review on Friday: what's done, what's next week.

Why calendars and online meetings matter

Being organised and reliable is one of the most valued work skills. Remote jobs, freelancing for international clients, online classes, team projects and interviews all depend on managing time well and joining meetings professionally. A shared calendar avoids double bookings, reminders stop missed deadlines, and online meetings save travel time and money in traffic-heavy cities like Nairobi.

When scheduling with international clients, always confirm the time in both time zones in writing: "Tuesday 3 pm EAT / 12 noon GMT".

Appointment schedules: let clients book you

Google Calendar's appointment schedules (availability depends on your account type) create a booking page:

  1. Create → Appointment schedule.
  2. Set duration (30 minutes), available hours and buffer time between meetings.
  3. Add booking form questions (phone number, reason for the meeting).
  4. Share the booking link on your website, WhatsApp Business profile or email signature.

Consultants, tutors, salons, clinics and freelancers use this to avoid back-and-forth messages about times.

Planning your week with time-blocking

Block time for important work like you would a meeting. Protect at least one or two focused blocks each day.

Data-saving tips for meetings

  • Turn off your camera when not speaking; video uses far more data than audio.
  • Lower video quality (Settings → Video → Send/receive resolution).
  • Close other apps and tabs that use the internet.
  • Use Wi-Fi when available; join by phone audio if your connection is very weak.
  • Download materials beforehand rather than during the meeting.

As a rough guide, a 1-hour video call can use several hundred MB to over 1 GB depending on quality; audio-only uses much less.

Professional online presence

  • Use a clear display name (first name and surname, not a nickname).
  • A neutral background and good lighting from the front (face a window).
  • Use earphones with a microphone to reduce echo and background noise.
  • Mute when not speaking, especially in noisy places.
  • Dress appropriately for interviews and client meetings.
  • Join a minute or two early to fix any problems.

Online interview tips

  1. Test the link, camera and mic the day before.
  2. Charge your laptop/phone and have a backup data bundle or hotspot.
  3. Position the camera at eye level; look at the camera when answering.
  4. Keep your CV and the job description open in another window (or printed).
  5. Have water nearby and notifications turned off.
  6. If the connection drops, rejoin quickly and apologise briefly; have the interviewer's phone or email as backup.

Practice

  1. Set up a weekly time-blocked schedule in Google Calendar with colours for work, study and personal time.
  2. Add a secondary time zone (e.g. GMT) and schedule a meeting showing both times.
  3. Create an appointment schedule (if your account supports it) or a booking form for 30-minute consultations.
  4. Run a 15-minute Google Meet with a friend: agenda, screen share, captions, and send minutes afterwards.
  5. Test how much data a 10-minute call uses with video on and off (check your phone's data usage).
Think about it: You agree to a "2 pm meeting" with a client in London by email, and they join while you're having lunch. What went wrong, and how do you prevent it?Show answer

The time zone wasn't specified: 2 pm in London (GMT/BST) is a different time in Kenya (EAT is 2 or 3 hours ahead depending on the UK's daylight saving). Always write both time zones ("2 pm UK / 4 pm EAT") and send a calendar invite, which converts the time automatically for each person.
