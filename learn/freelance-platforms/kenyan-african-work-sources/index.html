Kenyan and African sources of work: Ajira Digital, Jitume, BrighterMonday, Fuzu and MyJobMag

Local sources connect you with Kenyan businesses, NGOs and remote roles open to Kenyans, plus free training. Local clients pay in shillings, often via M-Pesa, and are a great place to start.

Check before you rely on it

Fees, accepted countries and payout options change often. Confirm on each platform's official website.

33. Ajira Digital

What it is: A government programme that trains young Kenyans for online and digital work and connects them to opportunities.

WorkTraining, mentorship, links to online work and Ajira centres
Fees for youFree
Getting paidNot a payment platform: you're paid by clients or platforms
KenyaKenya-wide, with physical centres
CompetitionN/A
Best forBeginners who want training and community

How to start: Register on the official site, join a centre near you, and follow the training paths. More: Ajira Digital and Jitume. Pros: Free training and community. Cons: You still need to find clients. Watch out: Ajira never charges for registration: fake “Ajira jobs” asking for money are scams.

34. Jitume Digital Labs

What it is: A government initiative providing digital labs and training in institutions across Kenya.

WorkDigital skills training, access to computers and internet for online work
Fees for youFree access through participating institutions
Getting paidNot a payment platform
KenyaKenya-wide in participating institutions
CompetitionN/A
Best forStudents and youth needing computers and internet

How to start: Find a Jitume lab near you (often in TVETs and universities). Pros: Free computers and internet. Cons: Limited hours and places. Watch out: Use official channels only.

35. BrighterMonday

What it is: A major Kenyan job site.

WorkFull-time, part-time and contract jobs, including remote roles
Fees for youFree for job seekers
Getting paidPaid by the employer
KenyaKenya
CompetitionHigh
Best forKenyan job seekers

How to start: Create a strong profile and set alerts for remote or contract roles. Pros: Many Kenyan employers. Cons: Mostly full-time office jobs. Watch out: Real employers don't charge interview or medical fees upfront.

36. Fuzu

What it is: A Kenyan and East African career platform with jobs and learning.

WorkJobs, internships and courses
Fees for youFree basic; paid extras
Getting paidPaid by the employer
KenyaKenya and East Africa
CompetitionHigh
Best forGraduates and early-career workers

How to start: Complete your profile and skill tests. Pros: Career resources. Cons: Competitive. Watch out: Same: no upfront fees to employers.

37. MyJobMag Kenya

What it is: A job listings site covering Kenya.

WorkJobs, internships, NGO roles
Fees for youFree
Getting paidPaid by the employer
KenyaKenya
CompetitionHigh
Best forJob seekers

How to start: Subscribe to alerts in your field. Pros: Many listings. Cons: Mostly local roles. Watch out: Verify the employer's details before applying.

Summary

  • Ajira Digital and Jitume offer free training, community and access; BrighterMonday, Fuzu and MyJobMag list Kenyan jobs and contracts.
  • Local clients pay in shillings, often via M-Pesa: great for first reviews and income.
  • Real government programmes and employers never charge registration or interview fees.
