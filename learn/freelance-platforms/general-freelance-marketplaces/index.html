General freelance marketplaces: Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer, PeoplePerHour, Guru, Contra and more

Open marketplaces accept almost any skill. They're where most Kenyan freelancers get their first reviews, and competition is high, so a sharp profile and a clear niche matter.

Check before you rely on it

Fees, accepted countries and payout options change often. Confirm on each platform's official website.

1. Upwork

What it is: The largest freelance marketplace, with hourly and fixed-price contracts from clients worldwide.

WorkWriting, design, development, virtual assistance, marketing, data, admin and more
Fees for youA service fee on each payment (Upwork has moved to a variable fee: check the current rate) and paid “Connects” to apply for jobs
Getting paidM-Pesa (directly in Kenya), Payoneer, bank or other methods in your account
KenyaOpen to Kenyan freelancers; profile approval isn't guaranteed
CompetitionHigh
Best forMost skills, especially longer contracts

How to start: Choose one niche, write a focused profile, add 2–3 portfolio pieces, and send short, tailored proposals. Full guide: Upwork step by step. Pros: Biggest client base; payment protection on contracts. Cons: Connects cost money; tough first jobs; profile approval can be slow. Watch out: Anyone selling “approved Upwork accounts” or asking you to work under their account is breaking the rules.

2. Fiverr

What it is: A marketplace where you list fixed-price services (“gigs”) and buyers come to you.

WorkDesign, writing, video editing, voice-over, programming, marketing, AI services
Fees for you20% from each order
Getting paidPayoneer, PayPal or bank (options vary)
KenyaOpen to Kenya
CompetitionHigh in popular categories
Best forClear, packaged services (e.g. “a logo in 3 days”)

How to start: Create 2–3 specific gigs with strong thumbnails and clear packages; reply fast. Full guide: Fiverr step by step. Pros: Buyers find you; good for repeatable services. Cons: 20% fee; ranking takes time. Watch out: Never deliver outside Fiverr or accept payment off the platform.

3. Freelancer.com

What it is: A large bidding marketplace and contest site.

WorkDevelopment, design, writing, data entry, engineering
Fees for youA fee per project (a percentage or a minimum amount) and paid membership options
Getting paidPayPal, Payoneer, bank or other methods
KenyaOpen to Kenya
CompetitionVery high, with many low-budget jobs
Best forPeople willing to bid a lot and enter contests

How to start: Bid only on jobs you can do well; verify your account; build a few reviews. Pros: Huge job volume. Cons: Price competition; some low-quality clients. Watch out: Clients asking you to pay a “registration” or “security deposit” are scammers.

4. PeoplePerHour

What it is: A UK-based marketplace with projects and fixed-price “offers”.

WorkDesign, web development, writing, marketing
Fees for youA service fee on earnings (tiers vary) and proposal credits
Getting paidPayPal, Payoneer or bank
KenyaOpen to freelancers worldwide including Kenya
CompetitionMedium to high
Best forDesigners, developers and writers wanting UK and European clients

How to start: Get your profile approved with a strong portfolio; post “offers” as well as bidding. Pros: Good-quality clients. Cons: Profile approval can be strict. Watch out: Keep all communication on the platform.

5. Guru

What it is: A long-running marketplace with flexible payment options and workrooms.

WorkProgramming, design, writing, admin, engineering
Fees for youA service fee that depends on your membership level
Getting paidPayPal, Payoneer or bank
KenyaOpen to Kenya
CompetitionMedium
Best forFreelancers who want a less crowded marketplace

How to start: Complete your profile, add portfolio samples, and use the free bids carefully. Pros: Less crowded than the biggest platforms. Cons: Fewer jobs. Watch out: Use SafePay (the platform's escrow) for every job.

6. Contra

What it is: A commission-free platform where freelancers build portfolio-style profiles and sell services.

WorkDesign, product, development, writing, marketing
Fees for youFree for freelancers (clients pay fees); paid upgrades optional
Getting paidPayment methods offered in your account
KenyaOpen to many countries; check payout options for Kenya
CompetitionMedium
Best forDesigners and creatives with strong portfolios

How to start: Build a beautiful portfolio profile and share it on LinkedIn and social media. Pros: Keep what you earn; great portfolio pages. Cons: You bring much of your own traffic. Watch out: Check payout methods work for you before taking a project.

7. Truelancer

What it is: A freelance marketplace popular in Asia and Africa.

WorkWeb development, design, writing, data entry
Fees for youA fee per project and membership plans
Getting paidPayPal or bank
KenyaOpen to Kenya
CompetitionHigh, budget-focused
Best forBeginners building first reviews

How to start: Bid on small projects you can deliver perfectly. Pros: Easy to start. Cons: Low budgets. Watch out: Avoid clients wanting work before funding the milestone.

8. Hubstaff Talent

What it is: A free directory where remote companies find freelancers.

WorkDevelopment, design, marketing, support, admin
Fees for youFree for freelancers and companies
Getting paidPaid by the client directly (agree how)
KenyaOpen to freelancers worldwide
CompetitionMedium
Best forFreelancers who want direct clients without fees

How to start: Create a detailed profile; apply to listed jobs. Pros: No fees. Cons: No payment protection. Watch out: Use written agreements and milestone payments for direct clients.

Summary

  • Upwork and Fiverr are the biggest and work for Kenyans; Freelancer, PeoplePerHour, Guru, Contra, Truelancer and Hubstaff Talent are alternatives.
  • Each charges differently: service fees, bid credits or memberships (Contra and Hubstaff Talent are free for freelancers).
  • Keep payments on the platform and never pay a client.
