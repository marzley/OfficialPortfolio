Sales funnels, lead follow-up and CRM: turning enquiries into paying customers

Many Kenyan businesses spend money on ads and posts, get lots of "How much?" messages on WhatsApp, and still make few sales. The problem is usually not marketing: it's what happens after someone shows interest. A sales funnel maps the steps from stranger to customer; lead follow-up and a simple CRM (customer relationship management system) make sure no enquiry is forgotten. This unit shows how to design funnels, respond to leads, follow up without being annoying, track everything, and improve conversion rates step by step.

What you will learn

  • Funnel stages and measuring drop-off at each step
  • Lead capture: forms, WhatsApp, calls, DMs
  • Speed to lead and response scripts
  • Qualifying leads
  • Follow-up sequences on WhatsApp, SMS, email and phone
  • Handling common objections (price, trust, timing)
  • CRMs: from a Google Sheet to HubSpot, Zoho and WhatsApp-integrated tools
  • Pipeline stages and sales metrics
  • Retention, upselling and referrals

Speed to lead

Leads go cold fast: the business that replies first often wins. Aim to reply within minutes during working hours, and set an automatic greeting outside hours ("Thanks! We'll reply by 8 am. Meanwhile, here's our price list: ...").

Qualifying leads

Not every enquiry is a buyer. Simple qualifying criteria (BANT):

  • Budget: can they afford the typical price range?
  • Authority: are they the decision-maker?
  • Need: does your product solve their problem?
  • Timing: when do they want it?

Prioritise hot leads; nurture warm ones with content and follow-ups; politely close cold ones.

Follow-up sequences

Most sales need several touches. A respectful sequence after sending a quote:

DayChannelMessage
0WhatsAppQuote + summary of benefits + next step ("Reply YES to book a site visit")
1WhatsApp"Did you get a chance to look at the quote? Any questions?"
3WhatsApp/emailA testimonial or case study from a similar customer
7CallFriendly check-in; answer objections
14WhatsAppLimited-time offer or financing option
30WhatsApp"Still interested? Happy to update the quote." then move to nurture list

Stop if they say no, and always make opting out easy. Use broadcast lists only for people who agreed to receive updates.

Handling objections

ObjectionResponse approach
"It's too expensive."Break down value and savings; offer packages or payment plans; compare cost of the problem
"I'll think about it."Ask what they need to decide; offer a summary; agree a follow-up date
"I don't trust online sellers."Reviews, physical address, pay on delivery, warranty, references
"Another company is cheaper."Explain differences (quality, warranty, support); don't badmouth competitors
"Not now."Ask when; schedule a reminder; add to nurture content

Retention, upselling and referrals

It's usually cheaper to sell again to happy customers than to find new ones:

  • Thank-you message and a how-to guide after purchase.
  • Check in after a week; fix problems quickly.
  • Ask for a Google review once they're happy.
  • Offer upgrades/add-ons when relevant (batteries, maintenance plans).
  • Referral programme: a reward for both the referrer and the new customer.
  • Occasional valuable updates (not spam) to stay remembered.
Think about it: A furniture shop gets 300 WhatsApp enquiries a month from ads but makes 10 sales. The owner replies in the evenings when free. Suggest four improvements.Show answer

Reply within minutes (assign a staff member, set a greeting and quick replies with prices and photos); qualify leads with a few questions; send clear quotes with delivery and payment options; follow up with a structured sequence (testimonials, offers, calls); track every lead in a sheet/CRM with stages and next actions; and measure conversion by ad to cut poor-quality campaigns.

Summary

  • A funnel moves people from visitors to leads, qualified leads, quotes, customers and referrals; fix the weakest step first.
  • Make capturing leads easy and reply within minutes with friendly scripts that qualify (BANT).
  • Follow up several times respectfully, handle objections with value and proof, and stop when asked.
  • Track every lead in a CRM (even a Google Sheet) with pipeline stages, next actions and lost reasons.
  • Measure response time, conversion, deal value, CLV and CPA; grow with retention, upsells and referrals.
