Branding and a digital marketing plan: positioning, brand identity, budgets and a 90-day plan

Tactics like SEO, social media and ads work much better when they serve a clear strategy: who you're for, why customers should choose you, what you'll say, where, with what budget, and how you'll measure it. This unit pulls the whole subject together: you'll define a brand and positioning, set goals, choose channels, plan content and budget, and write a practical 90-day digital marketing plan for a real business (yours or a client's).

What you will learn

  • What a brand is (more than a logo)
  • Target market, segments and personas
  • Positioning and the unique value proposition
  • Brand identity: name, logo, colours, fonts, voice
  • SMART marketing goals
  • The marketing funnel and choosing channels
  • Budgeting and forecasting results
  • A 90-day plan template with a worked example
  • Reviewing and improving monthly

What a brand is

A brand is what people think and feel about a business: its reputation, promise and personality. The logo and colours are signals of it. Strong brands are consistent (same quality, look and tone everywhere), distinctive and trusted.

Target market and personas

  1. Market: who could buy? (e.g. households in Nakuru needing reliable power)
  2. Segments: groups with different needs (homeowners, small shops, schools, farms)
  3. Target segment: who you focus on first (small shops with frequent outages)
  4. Persona: a detailed profile of a typical customer:

Positioning and value proposition

Positioning is the place you want to hold in customers' minds compared with competitors. A simple template:

For [target customer] who [need/problem], [brand] is the [category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternatives], because [proof].

Example: "For small shops in Nakuru that lose sales during blackouts, Mwangaza Solar is the installer that keeps tills and fridges running with flexible M-Pesa payment plans, unlike generic suppliers, because we offer 24-hour support and a 5-year installation warranty."

Your unique value proposition (UVP) is the short customer-facing version: "Never lose a sale to a blackout. Solar for shops, pay monthly via M-Pesa."

Brand identity

ElementGuidance
NameEasy to say, spell and remember; check domain and social handles; check it isn't trademarked
LogoSimple, works small (profile photo) and in one colour
Colours1–2 main colours + neutrals; consistent everywhere; check contrast for readability
Fonts1–2 fonts; readable on phones
ImageryReal photos of your work, team and customers beat stock images
Voice and toneFriendly, expert, playful or formal? Which languages (English, Swahili, Sheng)?
Brand guidelinesA one-page document so everyone (staff, designers, agencies) stays consistent

SMART goals

Goals should be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound:

  • Weak: "Get more customers online."
  • SMART: "Get 60 qualified WhatsApp enquiries and 15 installations from online channels between 1 October and 31 December."

The funnel and channels

Funnel stageGoalChannels and tactics
AwarenessGet noticedTikTok/Reels, Facebook/Instagram ads, YouTube, PR, partnerships
ConsiderationBuild trustWebsite, Google Business Profile, reviews, case studies, educational content, email
ConversionTurn interest into salesLanding pages, WhatsApp follow-up, offers, Google Search ads, retargeting
Retention and referralRepeat and recommendEmail/WhatsApp updates, loyalty offers, review requests, referral rewards

Choose channels where your persona is, that you can maintain, and that fit your budget. Most small businesses do well with: Google Business Profile + website (SEO) + one social platform + WhatsApp + email, adding ads when tracking is ready.

Budget and forecast

Plan money and time:

Start with small tests to get real cost-per-lead numbers, then adjust the forecast. Include costs like design, video production, tools (email platform, scheduling) and staff time.

Worked example: Mwangaza Solar (90 days)

Month 1: Foundations

  • Optimise Google Business Profile; collect 15 reviews from past clients.
  • Website: landing page "Solar for shops in Nakuru" with prices, financing, WhatsApp button; set up GA4 and conversion tracking.
  • Film 8 short videos: installations, customer testimonials, "how much solar does a shop need?"
  • Start a monthly email/WhatsApp update for past customers and leads.

Month 2: Launch campaigns

  • Facebook/Instagram Click-to-WhatsApp ads targeting business owners within 30 km (KSh 300/day), 3 creatives.
  • Google Search ads for "solar installation Nakuru", "solar for shops" with negatives (jobs, free, DIY).
  • Publish 2 blog guides (SEO): costs of solar for small businesses; solar vs generator.
  • Referral offer: KSh 2,000 M-Pesa reward for referrals that install.

Month 3: Optimise and scale

  • Pause underperforming ads; raise budget on the best creative by 25%.
  • Retarget website visitors and video viewers with testimonials.
  • Partner with a hardware shop and a SACCO for co-marketing.
  • Review: leads, cost per lead, installations, reviews; plan the next quarter.

KPIs: enquiries per channel, cost per lead, installations, revenue, Google reviews, website conversion rate.

Monthly review

Ask each month:

  1. Which channels produced leads and sales (not just likes)?
  2. What did each lead and sale cost?
  3. Which content/ads performed best, and why?
  4. What will we stop, start and continue?

Keep a simple dashboard (Google Sheets or Looker Studio) and adjust the plan; marketing improves through measured experiments.

Think about it: A new café in Eldoret has KSh 20,000 a month for marketing and the owner wants to "be on every platform". What plan would you recommend for the first 90 days?Show answer

Focus: a fully optimised Google Business Profile with photos and reviews (people search "café near me"), Instagram or TikTok with consistent short videos of food, ambience and events, WhatsApp Business for orders/bookings with a catalogue, and a small local ads budget (e.g. Click-to-WhatsApp or Instagram ads within 5–10 km) once tracking is set. Add a loyalty/referral offer and measure visits, orders and reviews monthly instead of spreading thin across all platforms.

Summary

  • A brand is customers' perception; build it consistently with a clear name, logo, colours, fonts, imagery and voice.
  • Define target segments and personas, then a positioning statement and a short UVP.
  • Set SMART goals, map channels to the funnel (awareness, consideration, conversion, retention), and focus on a few channels done well.
  • Forecast budgets with cost per lead, close rate and margin; test small before scaling.
  • Write a 90-day plan (situation, target, goals, channels, content, budget, tracking, timeline) and review monthly.
