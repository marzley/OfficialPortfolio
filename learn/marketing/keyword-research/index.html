Keyword research: find what customers search for

SEO starts with one question: what words do your customers type into Google? A bakery might think "artisanal confectionery", but customers search "birthday cakes in Thika". Keyword research finds the real words so your pages match them.

Types of keywords

TypeExampleNotes
Short / head"cakes"Huge competition, vague intent
Long-tail"chocolate birthday cake delivery Thika"Less traffic, but people ready to buy
Local"plumber near me", "plumber in Nakuru"Gold for small businesses
Question"how much does a website cost in Kenya"Great for blog posts and FAQs
Branded"Marzley Tech", "Naivas offers"People already know you

Small businesses win with long-tail and local keywords.

Understand search intent

For every keyword, ask what does the person want?

IntentExampleBest page
Informational"what is SEO"A blog post or guide
Commercial"best web designer in Nairobi"Comparison or service page with reviews
Transactional"buy solar panels Kisumu"Product page with price and "Order" button
Navigational"KRA iTax login"The official site

Google shows what satisfies the intent. Search your keyword and look at what ranks: if it's all blog posts, a product page won't rank, and vice versa.

Free ways to find keywords

  1. Google autocomplete: start typing "website design in..." and note the suggestions.
  2. "People also ask" boxes: real questions to answer in your content.
  3. Related searches at the bottom of the results page.
  4. Google Search Console: shows the searches that already bring people to your site (the best data you'll ever get, and free).
  5. Google Keyword Planner (in Google Ads, free account): search volume estimates.
  6. Google Trends: compare terms and see seasonality (e.g. "school fees" peaks in January).
  7. Ask customers how they'd search for you, and listen to the words they use on WhatsApp.

Build a keyword map

List keywords, then assign one main keyword per page so your pages don't compete with each other.

Using keywords well (without stuffing)

Put the main keyword naturally in:

  • The title tag (near the start) and meta description
  • The H1 heading and at least one H2
  • The URL: /birthday-cakes-thika
  • The first paragraph
  • Image alt text where it genuinely describes the image
  • Internal links from other pages, with the keyword as the link text ("see our birthday cakes")

Then write genuinely helpful content: prices, photos, FAQs, how to order, delivery areas. Keyword stuffing ("cakes Thika cakes Thika best cakes Thika") is spam: Google demotes it and customers hate it.

Measure and improve

After a few weeks, check Search Console → Performance:

  • Impressions high but clicks low? Improve the title and description.
  • Ranking positions 8–20? Improve that page's content and add internal links: it's close to page one.
  • New queries appearing? Write content for them.

Why keyword research matters

People find businesses by typing (or speaking) what they need: "plumber in Thika", "best laptop under 50000", "how to register a business in Kenya". Keyword research tells you the words your customers actually use, so you can create pages and content that match. Without it, websites are often written in the business's own jargon that nobody searches for. SEO specialists, content writers, digital marketers and business owners all use keyword research to plan websites, blogs, Google Ads and even product names.

Understanding keyword metrics

MetricMeaningHow to use it
Search volumeRoughly how many searches per monthHigher means more potential visitors, but usually more competition
Keyword difficultyHow hard it is to rank (tool estimate)Beginners and new sites should target easier, specific keywords
Cost per click (CPC)What advertisers pay per click in Google AdsHigh CPC suggests commercial value
TrendRising or falling interest over timePlan seasonal content (school fees, Christmas, KCSE results)
SERP featuresMaps, videos, "People also ask" in resultsShows what kind of content Google prefers

Local Kenyan search volumes are often small in tools; don't ignore a keyword just because a tool shows "0 to 10". Specific local searches can still bring valuable customers.

A step-by-step keyword research process

  1. Brainstorm seed topics from the business: services, products, problems solved, locations served.
  2. Expand with Google Autocomplete (type and see suggestions), "People also ask", "Related searches", and free tools (Google Trends, Google Keyword Planner via a Google Ads account, AnswerThePublic-style tools).
  3. Check the search results for each keyword: what kind of pages rank (service pages, blog posts, directories, videos)? That reveals intent.
  4. Group keywords by topic and intent (keyword clusters).
  5. Assign one main cluster per page in a keyword map.
  6. Prioritise: high intent (ready to buy) and realistic difficulty first.

Create dedicated, genuinely useful pages for each main location you serve (with local details, directions, and photos) rather than copying the same text with only the town name changed.

Google Business Profile: keywords for maps

A complete Google Business Profile helps you appear in Google Maps and the local "map pack":

  • Choose the most accurate primary category (e.g. "Computer repair service").
  • Write a description that naturally mentions your services and areas.
  • Add services/products, opening hours, photos, and your website and WhatsApp/phone.
  • Ask happy customers for reviews and reply to all reviews politely.
  • Post updates and offers regularly.

Content ideas from keywords

Keyword typeContent to create
"How to..." (informational)Step-by-step guides, videos
"Best ... for ..."Comparison and buying guides
"... price in Kenya"Price guides (keep them updated and dated)
"... vs ..."Comparison articles
"[service] [town]" (transactional)Service pages with clear prices or quotes and contact options

An informational article can link to your service page, guiding readers from learning to buying.

Writing for people and search engines

  • Use the main keyword in the title tag, H1, URL, first paragraph and naturally in subheadings.
  • Answer the question fully: include steps, prices, examples, photos and FAQs.
  • Use related terms and synonyms naturally (Google understands meaning, not just exact words).
  • Add internal links to related pages and from older pages to new ones.
  • Keep content updated, especially prices and procedures that change.

Measuring results

  • Google Search Console → Performance: queries, clicks, impressions, average position for each page.
  • Look for queries with many impressions but low clicks: improve titles and descriptions.
  • Look for pages ranking in positions 8 to 20: improving them can move them to page one.
  • Track enquiries and sales from organic search (GA4 key events), not just rankings.

Practice

  1. Choose a local business and brainstorm 20 keywords using Autocomplete and "People also ask".
  2. Group them into clusters and decide which page each cluster belongs to.
  3. Check Google Trends for a seasonal keyword (e.g. "school uniforms") and note the peak months.
  4. Write a title tag, meta description and H1 for a service page targeting a local keyword.
  5. Set up or review a Google Business Profile checklist for the business.
Think about it: A bakery's website homepage targets "cakes", "bread", "wedding cakes", "birthday cakes in Nairobi", "pastry classes" and "cake prices" all at once. Why is it underperforming, and how would you restructure it?Show answer

One page can't fully satisfy many different intents, so it ranks poorly for all of them. Create focused pages: Wedding Cakes (Nairobi), Birthday Cakes, a Cake Price List, Bread and Pastries, and Pastry Classes, each with its own keyword cluster, content, photos and calls to action, and link them from the homepage, which targets the brand and the main "bakery in [area]" keyword.
