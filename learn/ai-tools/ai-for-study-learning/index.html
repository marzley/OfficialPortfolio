Using AI to study and learn faster (the honest way)

AI assistants like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot can be a patient tutor available 24/7. Used well, they help you understand faster. Used badly, they do your thinking for you, give wrong answers confidently, and can get you into trouble for cheating. This lesson shows the difference.

Good uses: AI as a tutor

UseExample prompt
Explain simply"Explain subnetting to a Form 4 student using a housing estate example."
Explain your mistake"Here is my Python code and the error. Don't fix it for me; tell me what the error means and give a hint."
Quiz yourself"Ask me 10 questions on the OSI model, one at a time, and tell me if I'm right."
Different angle"I didn't understand the textbook's explanation of recursion. Give me a real-life analogy and a tiny example."
Summarise your notes"Turn these notes into 10 flashcards (question / answer)."
Plan revision"Make a 3-week revision timetable for these 6 units, 2 hours a day."
Practice problems"Give me 5 SQL JOIN exercises using a school database, with answers hidden until I ask."
Language help"Check the grammar of my paragraph and explain each correction."

The Socratic method prompt

Ask the AI to teach instead of answer:

"I'm learning Python. I'll show you my attempt at the exercise. Don't give me the solution. Ask me guiding questions until I find the answer myself."

You learn far more this way, and it's what good human tutors do.

Always verify: AI makes mistakes

AI can hallucinate: produce confident, wrong facts, fake quotes and references that don't exist, or outdated information (fees, laws, KRA rules). So:

  • Check important facts against your notes, textbooks or official sources (KNEC, KRA, eCitizen, your institution).
  • Run the code it gives you and test it.
  • Ask it to show its reasoning and check each step.
  • Never cite a source you haven't opened yourself.

Academic honesty

Most schools, colleges and universities have rules about AI. Generally:

  • ✅ Using AI to understand, brainstorm, practise and check your grammar is usually fine (check your institution's rules).
  • ❌ Submitting AI-written work as your own, in assignments or exams, is usually plagiarism/cheating and can lead to failing or disciplinary action.
  • ✅ If you're allowed to use AI, say how you used it.

And practically: exams, interviews and jobs test you, not the AI. If AI did the thinking, you won't have the skill when it matters.

A study routine with AI

  1. Before class: "Give me a 5-minute overview of today's topic: [topic]."
  2. After class: write your own notes first, then ask AI to point out gaps or errors.
  3. Practice: ask for exercises; attempt them without help; then ask for feedback.
  4. Revision: flashcards and quizzes from your notes.
  5. Stuck on code: ask for a hint, not the answer.

Privacy

Don't paste personal data (ID numbers, medical information, other people's details) or confidential work documents into public AI tools. Check the tool's privacy settings (some let you turn off using your chats for training).

Try it (on any AI assistant)

  1. Pick a topic from your course that confuses you.
  2. Ask for an explanation at your level with an everyday analogy.
  3. Ask it to quiz you with 5 questions.
  4. Ask it for one common misconception about the topic.
  5. Check one of its facts against your textbook.
