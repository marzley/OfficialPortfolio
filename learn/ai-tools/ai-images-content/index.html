AI for images, design and content creation

AI tools can now create images, remove backgrounds, write captions, generate voice-overs and edit videos in seconds. For freelancers, small businesses and content creators, that's a big productivity boost, as long as you use them with skill, honesty and respect for copyright.

Writing good image prompts

A strong prompt describes subject, setting, style, lighting, composition and format:

"A smiling Kenyan woman selling fresh mangoes at a busy open-air market in Mombasa, natural morning light, colourful fabrics, shallow depth of field, realistic photo style, square format, space at the top for text."

PartExamples
Subjecta boda boda rider, a laptop on a wooden desk, a cup of chai
SettingNairobi skyline at dusk, a school computer lab, a farm in Kericho
Stylerealistic photo, flat illustration, 3D render, watercolour, minimalist logo
Lighting & moodwarm sunset light, bright and clean, dramatic
Compositionclose-up, wide shot, top-down, empty space on the left for text
Formatsquare for Instagram, 16:9 for YouTube thumbnails, 9:16 for Status

Iterate: change one thing at a time ("same image, but at night", "make the background plain white").

Watch out for

  • Hands, text and logos: AI often gets fingers and written words wrong. Add text yourself in Canva.
  • Stereotypes: AI may default to stereotypes. Be specific and check for respectful, accurate representation.
  • Uncanny results: for real products and real people, real photos build more trust.

Copyright and honesty

  • Don't ask for images "in the style of" a living artist or copy brand logos and characters.
  • Don't use AI to create fake photos of real people, fake reviews or misleading adverts. It damages trust and may be illegal.
  • Check each tool's terms for commercial use before using images for a client.
  • Label AI-generated content where appropriate (many platforms now require this for realistic images).

AI for writing marketing content

A workflow that keeps quality high:

  1. Give context: "I run a hardware shop in Kitengela. Customers are builders and homeowners. Tone: friendly, simple English, some Sheng is OK."
  2. Ask for options: "Give me 5 Facebook post ideas for this week's cement offer."
  3. Pick one and ask for a draft with a clear call to action (WhatsApp number, location).
  4. Edit it yourself: check prices, dates and claims, and make it sound like you.
  5. Pair it with a real photo or a Canva design.

Earning with these skills

Clients pay for results, not for pressing "generate". Combine AI with real skills: design sense (see the graphic design tutorial), marketing knowledge, and good communication. Services you can offer: social media content packs, product photo clean-ups, simple ad creatives, presentation design, and short videos with captions.

AI in creative work

AI image and design tools can produce posters, social media graphics, product mock-ups, illustrations and photo edits in seconds. Designers use them to brainstorm ideas, remove backgrounds, extend images and speed up repetitive tasks. Small business owners use them for quick marketing graphics. The skill is in directing the tools, editing the results, and using them honestly and legally.

Writing better image prompts

A strong prompt describes:

ElementExample
Subject"A smiling Kenyan woman selling fresh vegetables at a market stall"
Setting"in an open-air market in Nakuru, morning"
Style"realistic photo" / "flat illustration" / "watercolour"
Lighting and mood"soft natural light, warm and cheerful"
Composition"close-up, subject on the left, empty space on the right for text"
Format"vertical 9:16 for WhatsApp Status"
Exclusions (if supported)"no text, no logos"

Generate several options, pick the best, then refine with follow-up instructions ("make the rider wear a helmet", "use our brand colours").

Combining AI with design tools

  1. Generate or edit images with AI.
  2. Bring them into a design tool (Canva, Figma, Photoshop, Photopea, Affinity).
  3. Add real text with proper fonts (AI-generated text in images is often misspelled).
  4. Apply brand colours, logo and layout grid.
  5. Export in the right format and size.

This hybrid approach gives professional results and keeps text editable for corrections.

Common problems in AI images

  • Hands, fingers, teeth and eyes may look wrong.
  • Text inside images is often garbled.
  • Objects may merge or have impossible details.
  • Cultural details (clothing, food, places) can be stereotyped or inaccurate; review carefully, especially for Kenyan and African contexts.
  • Inconsistent characters across multiple images.

Copyright, likeness and honesty

DoDon't
Check each tool's terms for commercial useAssume every generated image is free to use commercially
Use real photos of your actual products in advertsShow AI images that make products look different from reality
Get consent before editing photos of real peopleCreate fake images of real people (politicians, celebrities, private individuals)
Create original styles and brandsCopy another brand's logo or a living artist's distinctive style closely
Label illustrative AI images where they might be mistaken for real eventsUse AI images as "evidence" or news photos

Deepfakes and misleading images can harm people and may break laws on fraud, defamation and electoral conduct.

AI for marketing copy and video

  • Generate headline and caption options, then edit for your brand voice and local language.
  • Create video scripts and storyboards; record real footage or use AI tools for captions, voiceovers (with disclosure) and editing.
  • Test different versions of posts and see which get more engagement.

Earning with AI design skills

  • Social media graphics packages for small businesses.
  • Product photo editing for online sellers.
  • Thumbnail design for YouTubers.
  • Presentation design and brand kits.

Show before-and-after examples, be honest about your process, and focus on quality and consistency.

Practice

  1. Write three prompts for the same poster idea in different styles and compare results.
  2. Remove the background from a product photo and place it on a branded background.
  3. Create a poster with an AI image plus real text added in a design tool.
  4. Review five AI images for errors in hands, text and cultural details.
  5. Read the commercial-use terms of one AI image tool.
Think about it: A shop uses an AI-generated photo of a perfectly arranged smartphone bundle that doesn't match what customers actually receive. What's the risk?Show answer

Customers feel misled when the product differs, leading to complaints, refunds, bad reviews and possible consumer protection issues. Product adverts should use real photos (well lit and edited honestly) or clearly label illustrations, and descriptions should match what's sold.
