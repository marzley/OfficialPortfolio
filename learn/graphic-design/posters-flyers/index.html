Designing posters, flyers and business cards

Churches, schools, shops and events all need posters, flyers and business cards, which makes them one of the easiest design services to start selling. Here's how to design ones that get results.

Start with the brief

Before opening Canva, ask the client:

  • Goal: what should people do? (Attend, call, visit, buy)
  • Audience: who is it for?
  • Key information: event name, date, time, venue, price, contacts
  • Where it will be used: printed A4/A3 posters, WhatsApp status, Facebook, a banner?
  • Brand: logo, colours, fonts
  • Deadline and quantity (for printing)

Layout principles

  1. Hierarchy: one dominant headline, then the key detail, then the rest.
  2. Alignment: line things up on an invisible grid; centred or left-aligned, consistently.
  3. White space: don't fill every corner. Space makes important things stand out.
  4. Proximity: group related info (date + time + venue together).
  5. Contrast: the call to action (phone number, "Register now") must pop.
  6. Repetition: reuse colours and fonts for a unified look.

The 3-second test

Hold the design at arm's length (or view it tiny on your phone). Can you tell what it is, when/where, and what to do in 3 seconds? If not, cut text and increase the size of the key parts.

Poster checklist

  • [ ] Headline readable from 3 metres away
  • [ ] Date, time, venue correct and double-checked (a wrong date is an expensive reprint)
  • [ ] Contact number/WhatsApp correct
  • [ ] Logo included, not stretched
  • [ ] High-quality photos (not blurry screenshots)
  • [ ] No spelling mistakes (read it backwards, or ask someone else)
  • [ ] Client approved the final proof in writing

Business cards

Front: logo, name, title. Back (or same side): phone, WhatsApp, email, website, location, maybe a QR code linking to your website or WhatsApp.

  • Keep text at least 8 pt.
  • Keep important content inside the safe zone, about 3–5 mm from the edges.
  • Use good card stock (e.g. 300 gsm) and consider matte lamination.

Preparing for print

SettingWhy

Safe zoneKeep text away from edges
300 DPI imagesSharp printing (72 DPI web images look blurry)
CMYK coloursPrinters use CMYK; bright screen colours may print duller

Talk to your printer before designing: ask their preferred file type, bleed and paper options.

Designing for WhatsApp and social media

  • Vertical 1080 × 1920 for Status; big text (phones are small).
  • Put key info in the middle: the top and bottom may be covered by app buttons.
  • Fewer words than print: people swipe in 1–2 seconds.
  • Export as PNG (sharp text) or JPG (photos).

Pricing your design work

Consider: time, number of revision rounds included (e.g. 2), whether you supply print-ready files and editable source files, and rush fees. Always agree the price and revisions before starting, and take a deposit.
