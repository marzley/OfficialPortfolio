Colour theory and typography for graphics

Colour and type do most of the work in a poster, logo or social media post. A few principles will make your designs look intentional instead of random.

The colour wheel

  • Primary colours (for paint): red, yellow, blue.
  • Secondary: orange, green, purple (mix two primaries).
  • Tertiary: mixes like yellow-green, red-orange.

The 60-30-10 rule

A simple way to balance colours:

  • 60% a dominant/background colour,
  • 30% a secondary colour,
  • 10% an accent for the things that must stand out (the price, the "Call now" button).

Contrast: can people read it?

Text must stand out from its background. Dark text on light backgrounds (or the reverse) is safest. Yellow text on white or red on green is hard to read, especially on phone screens outdoors and for colour-blind people (about 1 in 12 men). Online "contrast checkers" test your colour pairs.

Typography: choosing fonts

CategoryLookUse for
Serif (Times, Playfair Display)Small "feet" on lettersElegant, traditional, formal
Sans-serif (Montserrat, Poppins, Inter)Clean, no feetModern, clear on screens
Script (Great Vibes, Pacifico)HandwritingWeddings, beauty; headings only
Display (Bebas Neue, Anton)Bold, decorativeBig poster headlines

Rules:

  1. Use 2 fonts maximum: one for headings, one for body text.
  2. Pair contrasting fonts: a bold display heading with a simple sans-serif body.
  3. Never use script or display fonts for long text.
  4. Create hierarchy with size and weight: the headline biggest, then the key detail, then small print.
  5. Keep text left-aligned or centred consistently, not a mix.
  6. Watch spacing: give lines room (line spacing about 1.3–1.5 for body text).

Hierarchy example (a poster)

  1. HEADLINE (largest): "Back-to-School Sale"
  2. Key offer: "Up to 30% off laptops"
  3. Details: dates, location
  4. Call to action: "WhatsApp 0712 345 678"
  5. Small print: terms

A viewer should get the message in 3 seconds.
