Using computers safely and responsibly: health, ergonomics, ethics, laws and e-waste

Computers and phones are part of our work, school and social lives, often for many hours a day. Used carelessly, they cause back and neck pain, eye strain, poor sleep, addiction, and legal trouble (piracy, cyberbullying, sharing others' data). This unit covers healthy computer use, a safe computer lab or office, digital ethics, the main Kenyan laws every computer user should know, and disposing of old electronics responsibly.

What you will learn

  • Ergonomics: setting up a healthy workstation
  • Preventing eye strain, back pain and repetitive strain injuries
  • Screen time, sleep and digital wellbeing
  • Safety in computer labs and offices (electricity, fire, cables)
  • Computer ethics: piracy, plagiarism, privacy, cyberbullying
  • Key Kenyan laws: Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, Data Protection Act, Copyright Act
  • E-waste: why it matters and how to dispose of devices

Ergonomics: a healthy workstation

Ergonomics means arranging your equipment to fit your body, preventing pain and injury.

AreaGood practice
ChairSupports your lower back; feet flat on the floor (or on a footrest); thighs roughly parallel to the floor
ScreenAbout an arm's length away; top of the screen at or slightly below eye level; no glare from windows behind or in front
Keyboard and mouseElbows at about 90°; wrists straight, not bent up; mouse close to the keyboard
LaptopLong sessions: raise the laptop on a stand or books and use a separate keyboard and mouse, so you're not hunched over
LightingEven room light; screen brightness similar to the surroundings
PhoneHold it closer to eye level instead of bending your neck down for long periods

Common problems and prevention

ProblemCausePrevention
Eye strain (tired, dry eyes, headaches)Staring at screens without breaks, glare, small text20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet (about 6 m) away for 20 seconds; blink; enlarge text; reduce glare; get your eyes tested
Neck and back painPoor posture, low screens, slouchingProper chair and screen height; stand and stretch every 30–60 minutes
Repetitive strain injury (RSI), carpal tunnelRepeating the same motions, bent wristsNeutral wrists; breaks; keyboard shortcuts; stretches
"Text neck"Looking down at phones for long periodsRaise the phone; take breaks
Poor sleepScreens and notifications late at nightStop screens 30–60 minutes before bed; night mode; phone out of the bedroom
Hearing damageLoud earphones for long periodsKeep volume below about 60% and take breaks

Digital wellbeing

  • Screen time: use your phone's Digital Wellbeing (Android) or Screen Time (iPhone) to see usage and set limits.
  • Notifications: turn off non-essential ones; they break concentration.
  • Focus: single-tasking beats constant switching between WhatsApp, TikTok and work.
  • Online and offline balance: exercise, sleep, friends and family.
  • Addiction warning signs: losing sleep, neglecting school/work, anxiety without the phone, gambling or betting apps taking money and time. Seek help from trusted people or counsellors if this sounds familiar.

Safety in computer labs and offices

HazardPrecautions
Electric shockNo exposed wires; don't open power supplies; dry hands; switch off and unplug before cleaning or moving equipment
Overloaded socketsAvoid chaining many extension cables; use proper power distribution and surge protectors
FireDon't block ventilation; keep a CO₂ fire extinguisher (suitable for electrical fires; never use water on electrical fires); know the exits
Trip hazardsRoute cables along walls or under covers
Dust and foodNo eating or drinking near computers; regular cleaning
Heavy equipmentLift with your legs; get help for heavy printers/servers
Power fluctuationsSurge protectors and UPS units; stabilisers in areas with unstable power

Lab rules (common in schools): authorised entry, no food or drinks, proper shutdown, report faults, don't install software without permission, respect others' work.

Computer ethics

Ethics are principles of right and wrong. Computer ethics applies them to technology.

IssueEthical behaviour
Software piracyUse licensed or free/open-source software; don't crack or share paid software
PlagiarismDon't copy others' work (or submit AI-generated work) as your own; cite sources
PrivacyDon't read others' messages, share their photos or personal data without permission
Unauthorised accessNever log into accounts or systems that aren't yours, even "just to see"
Cyberbullying and harassmentDon't post insults, threats, embarrassing content or private images; report abuse
MisinformationCheck before sharing; don't spread fake news
Responsible AI useDisclose AI help where required; don't create deepfakes or impersonate people
Professional honestyIT staff must protect the data and systems they're trusted with
Think about it: A friend asks to borrow your school portal password "just to check their results quickly". Is sharing it okay?Show answer

No. Sharing passwords is a security risk (they could change your details or misuse your account) and usually breaks your institution's rules. Help them recover their own account instead. The same applies to M-Pesa PINs, email and social media passwords.

Kenyan laws every computer user should know

These are summaries for awareness, not legal advice:

LawWhat it covers (in summary)
Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, 2018Crimes such as unauthorised access to computer systems, interfering with data or systems, computer fraud and forgery, cyber harassment, identity theft, child pornography, and publishing false information in certain circumstances. Penalties include fines and imprisonment.
Data Protection Act, 2019How organisations and individuals collect and use personal data: lawful purpose, consent where required, collecting only what's necessary, security, people's rights to access and correct their data. Overseen by the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC), where people can file complaints.
Copyright ActProtects creative works: books, music, films, software, photos. Copying or distributing them without permission (including pirated movies and software) can be an offence.
Kenya Information and Communications ActRegulates communications and electronic transactions, administered with the Communications Authority of Kenya (CA).

Practical consequences: hacking accounts, sending threats online, sharing someone's private images, running fake online "jobs" or scams, or leaking customers' data can lead to criminal charges and civil claims. Businesses that collect customer data (phone numbers, IDs) must protect it and use it only for stated purposes.

E-waste: old phones, computers and batteries

E-waste (electronic waste) includes old phones, computers, TVs, printers, chargers and batteries. It contains toxic substances (lead, mercury, cadmium) that pollute soil and water when dumped or burned, and also valuable recoverable materials (copper, gold).

Responsible steps:

  1. Extend life: repair, upgrade (SSD, RAM, battery) and reuse; donate working devices to schools or community groups.
  2. Wipe your data before giving away or selling: back up, sign out of accounts, then factory reset phones and reset/wipe computer drives.
  3. Recycle properly: use licensed e-waste collectors and recyclers and take-back programmes by manufacturers and telecom companies (check NEMA-licensed handlers); don't burn cables or dump batteries.
  4. Swollen or damaged batteries are a fire risk: don't puncture or throw them in fires; take them to proper collection points.

Summary

  • Set up an ergonomic workstation: back support, screen at eye level an arm's length away, straight wrists, raised laptop for long use.
  • Prevent eye strain (20-20-20 rule), back pain, RSI and poor sleep with breaks, posture and screen limits.
  • Keep labs and offices safe: no food/drinks, safe wiring, surge protection, CO₂ extinguishers for electrical fires.
  • Act ethically: no piracy, plagiarism, unauthorised access, cyberbullying or spreading fake news.
  • Know the Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act (2018), the Data Protection Act (2019, ODPC) and copyright law.
  • Dispose of e-waste responsibly: reuse, wipe data, use licensed recyclers.
