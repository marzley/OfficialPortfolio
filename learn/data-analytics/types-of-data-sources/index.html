Types of data, where data comes from and what makes data good

Before you analyse anything, you need to know what kind of data you have. The type decides which calculations and charts make sense. You can average ages; you can't average county names.

Quantitative and qualitative data

TypeMeaningExamples
Quantitative (numbers you can measure)Amounts, counts, measurementsSales in KSh, number of customers, rainfall in mm
Qualitative (categories or descriptions)Labels, groups, opinionsCounty, payment method, product name, customer comment

Two kinds of numbers

  • Discrete: counts that come in whole steps (number of children, matatus, orders).
  • Continuous: measurements that can take any value (weight, height, temperature, time).

Two kinds of categories

  • Nominal: categories with no order (county, payment method, colour).
  • Ordinal: categories with an order (satisfaction: poor, fair, good, excellent; education level).

What you can do with each type

Data typeGood calculationsGood charts
Continuous / discrete numbersSum, average, median, min, max, spreadHistogram, line chart, box plot
Nominal categoriesCounts and percentagesBar chart
Ordinal categoriesCounts, percentages, median categoryOrdered bar chart
Dates and timesTrends by day, week, monthLine chart

Where data comes from

SourceExamples in Kenya
Business systemsPOS sales, M-Pesa statements, accounting software, CRM
Surveys and formsGoogle Forms, KoboToolbox (popular with NGOs), customer feedback
Websites and appsGoogle Analytics, app usage, social media insights
Government and open dataKenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS), county data, KNBS census reports
International open dataWorld Bank, UN agencies, WHO
Sensors and devicesWeather stations, smart meters, GPS trackers

Primary data is data you collect yourself (a survey). Secondary data was collected by someone else (KNBS reports).

What makes data good? The quality checklist

QualityQuestionBad example
AccurateIs it correct?A sale recorded as KSh 50,000 instead of 500
CompleteIs anything missing?Half the rows have no branch name
ConsistentIs it recorded the same way everywhere?"Nairobi", "nairobi", "NRB" for the same place
TimelyIs it recent enough?Using 2015 prices for a 2026 budget
ValidDoes it follow the rules?An age of 250, a date of 31 February
UniqueAre there duplicates?The same M-Pesa transaction entered twice

"Garbage in, garbage out": bad data gives wrong answers, however clever the analysis.

Personal data

Data about people (names, phone numbers, ID numbers, health, location) is protected by Kenya's Data Protection Act (2019). Collect only what you need, keep it secure, and anonymise it before sharing or analysing when you can.

Think about it: A survey asks "How many people live in your house?" and "Which county do you live in?" Which answer can you average, and what would you do with the other?Show answer

You can average household size (a discrete number). For county (a nominal category), count how many respondents are in each county and show percentages in a bar chart.

Summary

  • Quantitative data is numbers (discrete or continuous); qualitative data is categories (nominal or ordinal).
  • The type decides the calculations and charts you can use.
  • Data comes from systems, surveys, websites, open data and sensors.
  • Check quality: accurate, complete, consistent, timely, valid and unique.
