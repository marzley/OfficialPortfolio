Option 3: Synthetic indices (Deriv and similar)

"Volatility 75", "Boom 1000", "Crash 500", "Step Index"… Synthetic indices are extremely popular among young Kenyan traders, mainly through Deriv (formerly Binary.com), with M-Pesa deposits through payment agents. This lesson explains what they really are, without hype.

1. What it is

Synthetic indices are price series generated by the broker's computer using a random number generator, designed to behave like markets with fixed characteristics (e.g. a set level of volatility, or occasional sudden spikes). They're not connected to any real economy, company or currency, and they trade 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including weekends and holidays.

Examples of names you'll see:

Index familyCharacter (as described by the provider)
Volatility indices (e.g. V10, V25, V75, V100)Constant volatility at different levels; the number indicates how volatile
Boom and Crash indicesMostly steady movement with occasional sudden spikes up (Boom) or down (Crash)
Jump indicesNormal movement with random large jumps
Step indexMoves in fixed-size steps
Range break indicesRange movement with occasional breakouts

Providers say the generator is audited for fairness and randomness. That means there's no news, economy or human order flow behind price moves: past chart patterns on a random series don't give a predictive edge by themselves.

2. How it works: products

ProductHow it works
CFDs on MT5 / Deriv platformsBuy or sell the index with leverage, with stop loss and take profit, like forex CFDs
Options (rise/fall, touch/no touch, digits…)Fixed-payout contracts similar to binary options (see binary options)
MultipliersYour profit or loss is multiplied (e.g. ×100); maximum loss is limited to your stake, but the stake can be lost quickly; commissions apply
AccumulatorsYour stake grows by a percentage each tick while price stays inside a range; if price leaves the range, you lose the stake

Example (multiplier): stake $10, multiplier ×100 on a volatility index. A 1% favourable move ≈ +$10 profit (before commission); a 1% unfavourable move ≈ −$10, which loses the whole stake. On high-volatility indices, 1% can happen in minutes.

3. Costs

Spreads, commissions on multipliers, swaps on some CFDs, built-in payout margins on options, and deposit/withdrawal and payment-agent fees (agents for M-Pesa may apply their own exchange rates and fees).

4. Risk level: very high

  • You trade against the broker's own generated price; the provider designs the products and earns from spreads, commissions and payout margins.
  • 24/7 access removes natural breaks; late-night and weekend trading on phones is a common path to gambling-like behaviour.
  • High volatility + leverage + small accounts = fast losses. "Volatility 75" is famous for blowing accounts.
  • Boom/Crash spikes can wipe out positions in one tick.
  • No fundamental reason for moves: strategies based on news or economics don't apply; many "strategies" sold online are pattern-spotting on randomness.

5. Regulation

Deriv is regulated by authorities in some jurisdictions (for example, the Malta Financial Services Authority for some EU clients, and offshore regulators such as Labuan, Vanuatu and the British Virgin Islands for others). The protection you receive depends on which entity serves your account. Synthetic indices are typically offered to clients outside strict jurisdictions; they're not offered to retail clients in the EU, for example. Check whether any provider is licensed by the CMA in Kenya (on the CMA website) and read the terms of the entity you sign up with.

6. Who it may suit / who should avoid it

  • Avoid: beginners, anyone trading with essential or borrowed money, anyone who trades late at night or feels compelled to "recover" losses.
  • If you're curious: use a demo account only, and study how random series behave. Treat any real money as entertainment spending you expect to lose.

7. How to learn safely

  1. Learn real market foundations first (the forex course): leverage, position sizing and risk.
  2. If you try synthetics, use demo, and test any strategy over hundreds of trades with strict risk per trade.
  3. Set hard limits: no trading after a set hour, maximum daily loss, and no deposits from loans.
  4. Use the provider's responsible trading tools (deposit limits, self-exclusion) if available.

8. Red flags and scams

  • "V75 strategy, 100% accurate", "Boom and Crash spike detector", "never-lose bots"
  • Mentors offering to "pass" your account or trade it for you (account sharing breaks terms and exposes you to theft)
  • Payment agents or individuals who aren't the provider's listed agents
  • Fake "Deriv" apps or websites (always use the official site)
  • Pressure to deposit more to unlock profits

9. Verdict

Synthetic indices are computer-generated random markets traded against the provider, available 24/7 with high leverage. That combination makes them closer to a casino product than to investing. If you trade them at all, do it on demo or with tiny "entertainment" money, with strict limits, and watch carefully for gambling signs.
