Water, sanitation and hygiene services

Clean water and good sanitation are basic needs, and many Kenyan homes, schools and markets still lack them. These businesses earn while improving public health.

What these methods share

  • Public health rules: county public health departments regulate many of these services.
  • Safety: chemicals, sewage and confined spaces need training and protective gear.
  • Trust: reliable service and clean facilities bring repeat customers.

841. Water quality testing

What it is: Testing borehole, tank and tap water for safety using test kits or a partner lab. How it works: Collect samples → test or send to lab → report and advise.

SkillsWater testing, sampling
Cost & equipmentTest kits; lab partnership
Where to find workHouseholds, schools, water vendors, farms
PaymentPer test
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Train in water testing; partner with an accredited lab for full analysis. Pros: Important for health. Cons & risks: Kits don't replace full lab tests. Scam warning: Never issue “safe” certificates without proper tests. Kenya notes: Water vendors and refill stations need regular testing.

842. Water purification systems installer

What it is: Supplying and installing filters, UV and reverse osmosis systems for homes, schools and businesses. How it works: Test water → recommend system → install → service filters.

SkillsPlumbing, water treatment
Cost & equipmentTools, stock or supplier account
Where to find workHomes, schools, clinics, restaurants
PaymentPer installation plus servicing
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Partner with a supplier; learn installation and filter servicing. Pros: Recurring filter sales. Cons & risks: Must match system to water quality. Scam warning: Avoid exaggerated health claims. Kenya notes: High fluoride in parts of the Rift Valley makes treatment valuable.

843. Pay toilets and ablution blocks

What it is: Running clean public toilets and showers at markets, stages and towns. How it works: Build or lease a block → charge per use → keep it clean.

SkillsManagement, cleaning
Cost & equipmentConstruction or lease, water, cleaning supplies
Where to find workMarkets, bus stages, towns
PaymentPer use (often small fees)
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Partner with counties or market committees; prioritise cleanliness. Pros: Daily cash flow. Cons & risks: Needs constant cleaning. Scam warning: Get proper approvals and leases in writing. Kenya notes: Counties sometimes contract operators for public toilets.

844. Biodigester toilet installation

What it is: Installing toilets that treat waste in a sealed digester, suitable where sewers don't reach. How it works: Survey → build or install digester → train users.

SkillsConstruction, sanitation
Cost & equipmentMaterials, tools
Where to find workHomes, schools, rural institutions
PaymentPer installation
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train with a supplier or NGO programme; start with demonstration units. Pros: Solves real sanitation problems. Cons & risks: Upfront cost for clients. Scam warning: Follow public health guidelines for siting. Kenya notes: Many rural and peri-urban areas lack sewer connections.

845. Sanitary bin servicing

What it is: Providing and emptying sanitary bins in schools and offices, with safe disposal. How it works: Install bins → scheduled collection → licensed disposal.

SkillsHygiene, logistics
Cost & equipmentBins, transport, disposal arrangement
Where to find workSchools, offices, malls, hospitals
PaymentMonthly contracts
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with a few offices; partner with a licensed disposal company. Pros: Recurring contracts. Cons & risks: Waste handling rules. Scam warning: Use licensed disposal only. Kenya notes: Girls' schools and offices need dignified menstrual hygiene services.

846. Handwashing station fabrication

What it is: Making foot-operated or tap handwashing stations for schools, markets and businesses. How it works: Design → fabricate (metal or plastic) → deliver.

SkillsWelding or fabrication
Cost & equipmentWorkshop tools, materials
Where to find workSchools, markets, clinics, restaurants
PaymentPer unit
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Make a sample; sell to schools and restaurants. Pros: Simple product. Cons & risks: Demand fluctuates. Scam warning: Agree on specs and deposits. Kenya notes: Foot-operated stations became common during COVID-19 and remain useful.

847. Greywater recycling systems

What it is: Installing systems that reuse shower and laundry water for gardens and flushing. How it works: Assess → install filters and piping → train users.

SkillsPlumbing, design
Cost & equipmentPipes, tanks, filters
Where to find workHomes, schools, hotels in dry areas
PaymentPer project
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Learn safe greywater design; target water-scarce areas. Pros: Saves water. Cons & risks: Must avoid health risks. Scam warning: Never connect greywater to drinking supplies. Kenya notes: Water rationing in towns makes reuse attractive.

848. Septic tank and soak pit construction

What it is: Building septic tanks and soak pits correctly sized and sited. How it works: Site assessment → excavation → construction → inspection.

SkillsMasonry, sanitation design
Cost & equipmentTools, materials, labour
Where to find workHomes, schools, developers
PaymentPer project
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Learn design standards; partner with builders and plumbers. Pros: Constant demand outside sewer areas. Cons & risks: Poor siting contaminates water. Scam warning: Keep tanks away from boreholes and wells as rules require. Kenya notes: Most rural and many peri-urban homes rely on septic systems.

849. Self-service laundromat

What it is: A shop with washing machines and dryers that customers pay to use, plus wash-and-fold service. How it works: Customers bring laundry → pay per load → optional service.

SkillsCustomer service, maintenance
Cost & equipmentHigh: machines, plumbing, premises
Where to find workEstates, student areas, towns
PaymentPer load
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Choose a busy area near rentals or campuses; offer wash-and-fold. Pros: Daily customers. Cons & risks: High setup and power costs. Scam warning: Buy machines from reputable suppliers with service support. Kenya notes: Students and apartment dwellers often lack space for washing.

850. Portable toilet hire

What it is: Supplying and servicing mobile toilets for events, construction sites and gatherings. How it works: Book → deliver → service → collect.

SkillsLogistics, sanitation
Cost & equipmentToilet units, transport, exhauster access
Where to find workEvents, construction sites, funerals, rallies
PaymentPer unit per day
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with a few units; partner with event planners. Pros: Good margins per event. Cons & risks: Transport and cleaning costs. Scam warning: Dispose of waste only through licensed exhausters. Kenya notes: Funerals, weddings and rallies regularly hire mobile toilets.

851. Solar water heater installation

What it is: Installing solar water heaters for homes, hotels and institutions. How it works: Survey → size system → install → maintain.

SkillsPlumbing, solar thermal
Cost & equipmentTools, equipment from suppliers
Where to find workHomes, hotels, hospitals, schools
PaymentPer installation
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train and get the right licence; partner with suppliers. Pros: Saves electricity. Cons & risks: Roof work; quality varies. Scam warning: Use certified equipment. Kenya notes: Kenyan regulations encourage solar water heating in larger buildings; check current requirements.

852. Water dispenser sales and servicing

What it is: Selling, cleaning and servicing water dispensers for offices and homes. How it works: Sell or lease dispensers → regular cleaning → repairs.

SkillsBasic technical skills, hygiene
Cost & equipmentStock, cleaning kits
Where to find workOffices, schools, homes
PaymentSales and service contracts
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with water bottlers; offer quarterly cleaning. Pros: Recurring servicing. Cons & risks: Low margins on sales. Scam warning: Clean with safe, food-grade products. Kenya notes: Offices commonly use dispensers with bottled water.

Summary

  • Water and sanitation businesses include testing, purification, pay toilets, biodigesters, sanitary bins, handwashing stations, greywater, septic tanks, laundromats, portable toilets, solar water heaters and dispenser servicing.
  • County public health rules and NEMA waste rules apply.
  • Protect water sources and use licensed disposal.
