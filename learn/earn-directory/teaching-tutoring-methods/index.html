Teaching, tutoring and training

If you know something well, someone wants to learn it: a Form 3 student struggling with chemistry, a parent wanting their child to code, a business owner learning Excel, a foreigner learning Kiswahili. Teaching can be done face to face, online, or through courses you record once and sell many times.

What these methods share

  • Subject knowledge plus teaching skill: explaining clearly, patience, checking understanding.
  • Safeguarding: when teaching children, follow child-protection good practice (parents present or informed, no private messaging without parents' knowledge, appropriate conduct online).
  • Honesty about outcomes: never promise grades, admission or jobs.
  • Licensing: running a formal school or college requires registration with the relevant authorities; private tutoring and short courses are simpler but still need professionalism.
  • See teaching and expertise earning and online teaching platforms.

204. Home tutoring (primary and secondary)

What it is: One-on-one or small-group tutoring at students' homes or a shared venue. How it works: Assess the learner → plan sessions → regular lessons → progress updates to parents.

SkillsStrong subject knowledge, patience, curriculum familiarity (CBC, 8-4-4 or international)
Cost & equipmentBooks, transport
Where to find workParents in your area, schools' referrals, WhatsApp groups, churches
PaymentPer hour or per month, M-Pesa
Realistic earningsLow to medium (more students, holiday tuition)
Time to first incomeDays to weeks

How to start: Start with one subject you're excellent at; ask satisfied parents for referrals. Pros: Strong local demand. Cons & risks: Travel time; holiday peaks and quiet terms. Scam warning: Agencies charging tutors "registration fees" without delivering clients. Kenya notes: Follow current Ministry of Education guidance on holiday tuition; keep sessions safe and parent-approved.

205. Online tutoring (local and international)

What it is: Tutoring students over video calls. How it works: Use Zoom/Google Meet with a digital whiteboard; schedule regular sessions.

SkillsSubject expertise, online teaching tools, reliable internet
Cost & equipmentLaptop, webcam, headset, stable internet with backup
Where to find workTutoring platforms (check country eligibility), parents, diaspora families, international students
PaymentPer session; platforms pay via PayPal/Payoneer
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Record a short intro video; offer a free trial lesson. Pros: No travel; global market. Cons & risks: Platform competition; time zones. Scam warning: "Tutoring jobs" asking for upfront payment for "verification". Kenya notes: Diaspora families want tutors who know Kenyan curricula and Kiswahili.

206. Exam revision classes and holiday programmes

What it is: Group revision for national exams or holiday enrichment programmes (coding, arts, languages). How it works: Venue or online; structured timetable; past-paper practice.

SkillsExam knowledge, group teaching, organisation
Cost & equipmentVenue, materials, marketing
Where to find workParents, schools, community centres
PaymentPer student per programme
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium (seasonal)
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with other teachers; start small with a clear timetable. Pros: Scales with group size. Cons & risks: Regulations on holiday tuition; venue costs. Scam warning: Never sell "leaked exam papers"; it's illegal. Kenya notes: Check current rules on holiday tuition; enrichment programmes (coding, music, sports) are popular.

207. Teaching English online (ESL)

What it is: Teaching English to learners abroad (adults or children), conversational or exam-focused. How it works: Platforms or private students; lesson materials; regular schedules.

SkillsExcellent English, clear pronunciation, teaching methods; a TEFL/TESOL certificate helps
Cost & equipmentLaptop, headset, quiet space
Where to find workESL platforms (many restrict to certain nationalities), private students via social media
PaymentPer lesson
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Consider a reputable TEFL course; build a teaching profile on platforms open to Kenyans. Pros: Large global demand. Cons & risks: Some platforms only hire "native speaker" passports. Scam warning: Fake TEFL "job guarantees" or expensive unaccredited certificates. Kenya notes: Kenyan English teachers find students in Asia, the Middle East and Africa directly.

208. Teaching Kiswahili to foreigners

What it is: Teaching Kiswahili to expatriates, tourists, missionaries, students and diaspora children. How it works: Online or in-person lessons; cultural context; materials.

SkillsFluent Kiswahili, teaching ability, patience
Cost & equipmentPhone/laptop, teaching materials
Where to find workExpats in Kenya, language platforms, universities, diaspora families
PaymentPer lesson
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Create beginner lesson plans; list on language platforms; network with expat groups. Pros: Niche with less competition. Cons & risks: Seasonal (expats moving). Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Embassies, NGOs and international schools have staff wanting Kiswahili.

209. Teaching other languages (French, German, Chinese, Arabic, sign language)

What it is: Teaching a foreign language or Kenyan Sign Language you're qualified in. How it works: Group or individual lessons, exam preparation.

SkillsProficiency (certified levels help), teaching skills
Cost & equipmentMaterials, laptop
Where to find workStudents, professionals relocating, schools, institutions
PaymentPer lesson or course
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Prove your level (certificates); offer beginner courses. Pros: Demand from people seeking jobs abroad. Cons & risks: Requires high proficiency. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: German and French are popular with people pursuing study and work abroad; Kenyan Sign Language skills are needed for inclusion.

210. Computer and digital skills classes

What it is: Teaching basic computer use, typing, Microsoft Office, internet safety and online services. How it works: Short courses at a centre, cyber café, church hall or online.

SkillsComputer packages (IT basics), patience
Cost & equipmentComputers (or learners' own), projector
Where to find workYouth, adults, job seekers, elderly learners, community groups
PaymentPer course
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Use the hub's free lessons as a structured curriculum; issue certificates of completion (clearly not a government qualification). Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Equipment costs. Scam warning: Don't present your certificate as an official KNEC/TVET qualification. Kenya notes: Computer packages are commonly required for jobs and further studies.

211. Coding classes for children and teens

What it is: Teaching Scratch, Python, web basics, robotics or app making to young learners. How it works: After-school or holiday classes; projects-based.

SkillsCoding, engaging teaching for children, safeguarding
Cost & equipmentLaptops (learners' or yours), internet
Where to find workParents, private schools, holiday camps
PaymentPer term or programme
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Run a pilot holiday camp; show learners' projects to parents. Pros: Growing demand. Cons & risks: Needs child-safe practices. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: CBC emphasises digital literacy; schools seek coding partners.

212. Professional skills training (Excel, data, design, marketing)

What it is: Short workshops and courses for working professionals and organisations. How it works: In-house corporate training or public workshops.

SkillsExpertise, facilitation, practical exercises
Cost & equipmentLaptop, projector, venue (or online)
Where to find workCompanies, NGOs, SACCOs, government agencies, professional bodies
PaymentPer participant or per day
Realistic earningsMedium to medium–high
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Build a strong course outline and run a pilot; collect testimonials. Pros: High value per day. Cons & risks: Requires credibility. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Organisations budget for staff training; some require vendor registration.

213. Recorded online courses

What it is: Creating video courses sold online, recorded once and sold repeatedly. How it works: Plan curriculum → record videos → host on a course platform or your site → market.

SkillsExpertise, video recording, course design, marketing
Cost & equipmentMicrophone, camera/phone, screen recorder, hosting fees
Where to find workCourse marketplaces, your own website, social media audience
PaymentPer sale; local sales via M-Pesa
Realistic earningsVery low to high; depends on audience and marketing
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Validate demand with a live workshop first; then record. Pros: Scalable. Cons & risks: Marketing is the hard part; piracy. Scam warning: "Course creation coaches" promising guaranteed income. Kenya notes: Practical courses (Excel, KRA filing, farming, Canva) sell locally with M-Pesa payments.

214. Live cohort courses and bootcamps

What it is: Teaching a group live over several weeks, online or in person. How it works: Fixed schedule, assignments, community, certificate of completion.

SkillsExpertise, facilitation, organisation
Cost & equipmentLaptop, video platform or venue
Where to find workYour audience, partnerships with organisations
PaymentPer participant
Realistic earningsLow–medium to high
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Run a small first cohort at a discount; improve from feedback. Pros: Higher completion and value than recorded courses. Cons & risks: Time-intensive. Scam warning: Don't promise jobs after bootcamps. Kenya notes: Tech and digital skills bootcamps are popular with youth.

215. Corporate and NGO facilitation

What it is: Facilitating workshops, strategy sessions and training for organisations. How it works: Needs assessment → design → facilitation → report.

SkillsFacilitation, subject expertise, report writing
Cost & equipmentLaptop, materials
Where to find workNGOs, government programmes, companies, development agencies
PaymentDaily rates or consultancy contracts
Realistic earningsMedium to high (experienced)
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Co-facilitate with experienced trainers; build a profile. Pros: Good daily rates. Cons & risks: Irregular; tender processes. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Digital skills facilitation (e.g. Ajira, Jitume) and youth programmes use facilitators (digital skills facilitator).

216. Music, art and creative lessons

What it is: Teaching instruments, singing, drawing, painting, crafts or dance. How it works: Private or group lessons; recitals or exhibitions.

SkillsCreative skill, teaching
Cost & equipmentInstruments/materials, space
Where to find workFamilies, schools, churches, community centres
PaymentPer lesson or term
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays to weeks

How to start: Offer trial classes; showcase students' work. Pros: Rewarding; recurring. Cons & risks: Seasonal demand. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: CBC's creative arts components increase demand.

217. Sports and fitness coaching

What it is: Coaching football, swimming, athletics, chess, martial arts or personal fitness. How it works: Group or individual sessions; academies.

SkillsSport expertise, coaching certification where required, first aid
Cost & equipmentEquipment, venue access
Where to find workSchools, academies, families, gyms
PaymentPer session or term
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Get relevant coaching and first-aid training; start a small academy. Pros: Healthy, community work. Cons & risks: Injury liability; insurance. Scam warning: "Overseas sports scholarship" agents charging fees. Kenya notes: Kenya's athletics and football culture creates demand for youth coaching.

218. Driving school instruction (licensed)

What it is: Teaching people to drive through a licensed driving school. How it works: Theory and practical lessons following official curricula.

SkillsDriving expertise, instructor licence, patience
Cost & equipmentEmployed by a school, or significant investment to open one
Where to find workLicensed driving schools
PaymentSalary or per learner
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths (licensing)

How to start: Meet NTSA instructor requirements; join a registered school. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Regulation and road safety responsibility. Scam warning: Never offer "licences without training"; it's illegal. Kenya notes: NTSA regulates driving schools and instructors.

219. Exam and test preparation (international exams)

What it is: Preparing students for IELTS, TOEFL, SAT, GRE, GMAT and similar exams. How it works: Diagnostic tests, strategy lessons, practice exams.

SkillsHigh scores yourself, exam strategy knowledge
Cost & equipmentMaterials, laptop
Where to find workStudents going abroad, professionals, nurses relocating
PaymentPer course
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Take the exam yourself; build a structured prep course. Pros: Strong demand from people going abroad. Cons & risks: Never guarantee scores. Scam warning: Fake "guaranteed visa/score" agents. Kenya notes: Kenyan nurses and students applying abroad often need IELTS/TOEFL preparation.

220. Educational YouTube channel or podcast

What it is: Teaching through free videos, earning from ads, sponsors, memberships and courses. How it works: Consistent educational content → audience → monetisation.

SkillsTeaching, video production, consistency
Cost & equipmentPhone/camera, microphone
Where to find workYouTube, podcast platforms
PaymentAd revenue, sponsorships, products
Realistic earningsVery low for most; can grow large
Time to first incomeMonths to years

How to start: Pick a focused subject; publish weekly; study what helps viewers (YouTube channel). Pros: Long-term asset. Cons & risks: Slow growth. Scam warning: Buying subscribers or views gets channels penalised. Kenya notes: KCSE revision, CBC help and practical skills channels attract Kenyan audiences.

221. Curriculum and learning material development

What it is: Writing schemes of work, lesson plans, revision books and e-learning content for schools and edtech. How it works: Commissioned work or self-published materials.

SkillsCurriculum knowledge, instructional writing
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workSchools, publishers, edtech companies, NGOs
PaymentPer project or royalties
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Create sample materials aligned to official curricula. Pros: Reusable. Cons & risks: Copyright and approval processes for textbooks. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: CBC and CBET materials are in demand; tools like CBET Planner show the need in TVET.

222. Mentorship and career coaching

What it is: Guiding students and professionals on careers, CVs, interviews and skills. How it works: Paid sessions or programmes; group mentorship.

SkillsRelevant experience, listening, honesty
Cost & equipmentPhone/laptop
Where to find workGraduates, universities, professionals, LinkedIn
PaymentPer session or programme
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Mentor a few people free; build testimonials. Pros: Meaningful. Cons & risks: Must not overpromise outcomes. Scam warning: "Pay for guaranteed job placement" schemes. Kenya notes: Graduates value practical guidance on the Kenyan job market.

Summary

  • Teaching methods include home and online tutoring, revision programmes, English, Kiswahili and other languages, digital and coding classes, professional training, recorded and live courses, facilitation, creative and sports coaching, driving instruction, international exam prep, educational channels, curriculum development and mentorship.
  • Keep children safe, never promise grades, scores or jobs, and follow regulations.
