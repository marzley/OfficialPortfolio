Digital products, software and online assets

Digital products are created once and sold many times. Software products (SaaS), plugins, apps, communities and websites can become assets that earn repeatedly. They take time to build and market, and most don't succeed at first, but they're among the most scalable methods.

What these methods share

  • Solve a real problem for a specific audience.
  • Marketing matters as much as building.
  • Licensing and IP: protect your work; respect others' licences.
  • Payments: M-Pesa for local customers, international gateways for global sales.

599. Micro-SaaS (small software service)

What it is: A small subscription software product solving one problem (e.g. invoicing for salons, SMS reminders for clinics). How it works: Build → launch → subscriptions → support.

SkillsProgramming, product thinking, marketing
Cost & equipmentLaptop, hosting
Where to find workTarget customers directly, online
PaymentMonthly subscriptions (M-Pesa, cards)
Realistic earningsVery low to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Talk to 20 potential users before building; launch a simple version. Pros: Recurring revenue. Cons & risks: Support and churn. Scam warning: As usual: no "guaranteed SaaS income" courses. Kenya notes: Local SaaS with M-Pesa billing and eTIMS features has demand (CBET Planner is one local example of a niche SaaS).

How to start: Use licensed SMS/USSD providers' APIs. Pros: Reaches feature-phone users. Cons & risks: Regulation; costs. Scam warning: Never send spam or premium-rate scams. Kenya notes: USSD reaches users without smartphones; CA rules apply.

Summary

  • Digital products include micro-SaaS, apps, WordPress plugins, extensions, APIs, templates, fonts, presets, communities, directories, job boards, website flipping, domains, 3D assets, course platforms, spreadsheets and SMS/USSD services.
  • Solve real problems, market consistently, respect privacy and licences, and expect slow starts.
