Finance, insurance and money-services work

People and businesses need help with insurance, savings, loans, payments and records. These methods earn commissions or fees by connecting customers with licensed financial products, or by providing financial support services. Many require licences; all require honesty, because you're handling other people's money and futures.

What these methods share

  • Licensing: insurance agents are licensed by the Insurance Regulatory Authority (IRA); investment advisers and fund intermediaries by the CMA; SACCOs by SASRA; banks by CBK. Check what your role requires.
  • Ethics: recommend what suits the customer, disclose commissions, never mis-sell.
  • No guaranteed returns: anyone promising fixed high returns is likely running a scam.
  • Related: trading and investing options, money and tax in Kenya.

331. Insurance agent

What it is: Selling insurance (motor, medical, life, education, business) on behalf of licensed insurers, earning commissions. How it works: Get licensed → contract with insurers → find clients → advise → sell → service policies and claims.

SkillsProduct knowledge, sales, trust, follow-up
Cost & equipmentLicensing exam/fees, phone, transport
Where to find workFamilies, businesses, boda boda and matatu owners, social media
PaymentCommissions from insurers
Realistic earningsLow to medium–high (renewals build income)
Time to first incomeWeeks to months (licensing)

How to start: Meet IRA licensing requirements (agents usually need training and registration through an insurer); start with motor and medical cover. Pros: Renewal commissions grow over time. Cons & risks: Rejection-heavy sales; claims disputes. Scam warning: Never collect premiums into a personal account; never issue fake insurance stickers or certificates. Kenya notes: Motor insurance for boda bodas, matatus and private cars is a large market.

332. Insurance broker support and claims assistant

What it is: Supporting brokerages with client service, renewals and claims processing. How it works: Employed or freelance support within licensed brokerages.

SkillsOrganisation, communication, insurance knowledge
Cost & equipmentLaptop, phone
Where to find workBrokerages, insurers
PaymentSalary or commissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Study insurance basics (e.g. professional insurance certificates); apply to brokerages. Pros: Career path. Cons & risks: Paperwork-heavy. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Medical claims assistance is valued by employers' HR teams.

333. Bank and SACCO sales agent / direct sales representative

What it is: Recruiting customers for accounts, loans, cards and savings products. How it works: Contracted DSR roles with targets and commissions.

SkillsSales, product knowledge, compliance
Cost & equipmentPhone, transport
Where to find workBanks, SACCOs, microfinance institutions
PaymentCommission or salary plus commission
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Apply for DSR roles; learn products thoroughly. Pros: Entry into finance. Cons & risks: Targets; contract insecurity. Scam warning: Never ask customers for PINs or passwords. Kenya notes: Follow customer data protection rules strictly.

334. Bank agent (agency banking)

What it is: Operating an agent outlet for bank deposits, withdrawals and account services. How it works: Apply to the bank's agency programme; maintain float; earn commissions.

SkillsCash handling, accuracy, security
Cost & equipmentPremises, float, required documents
Where to find workYour outlet's customers
PaymentCommissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths (approval)

How to start: Apply through official bank channels. Pros: Steady transactions. Cons & risks: Fraud and security. Scam warning: Watch for impostor "bank officials" asking for reversals. Kenya notes: Agency banking is regulated under CBK guidelines.

335. Loan agent / credit facilitation (licensed lenders only)

What it is: Connecting borrowers with licensed lenders, assisting with applications. How it works: Agent agreements with banks, SACCOs or licensed digital lenders.

SkillsFinancial literacy, honesty
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workLicensed lenders' agent programmes
PaymentCommissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Work only with CBK-licensed or SASRA-regulated lenders. Pros: Helps people access credit. Cons & risks: Pushing debt onto people who can't repay. Scam warning: "Loan processing fee" scams are common; never charge upfront fees for loans you can't guarantee. Kenya notes: Digital credit providers must be licensed by CBK; check the list.

336. Bookkeeping for SMEs (finance focus)

What it is: Keeping books for small businesses (see also method 191), with focus on cash flow and tax readiness. How it works: Monthly records, reconciliations, reports.

SkillsAccounting, software
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workSMEs, NGOs, churches
PaymentMonthly retainer
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Accounting studies (e.g. CPA) plus software skills. Pros: Recurring. Cons & risks: Responsibility for accuracy. Scam warning: Never help falsify accounts. Kenya notes: eTIMS invoicing compliance knowledge helps SMEs.

337. Tax preparation and filing services (qualified)

What it is: Preparing tax returns and handling KRA compliance for individuals and businesses. How it works: Document collection → computation → filing → advice.

SkillsTax knowledge, qualifications for complex cases
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workIndividuals, SMEs, landlords
PaymentPer return or retainer
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Simple returns first; refer complex cases to certified tax practitioners. Pros: Seasonal and recurring. Cons & risks: Liability for errors. Scam warning: Never file false returns or offer to "reduce tax" illegally. Kenya notes: Rental income, turnover tax and eTIMS questions are common.

338. Financial literacy trainer

What it is: Teaching budgeting, saving, debt management and investing basics to groups. How it works: Workshops for chamas, schools, churches, employers.

SkillsFinancial knowledge, teaching
Cost & equipmentMaterials
Where to find workChamas, SACCOs, employers, NGOs
PaymentPer session or contract
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Develop practical, unbiased workshops. Pros: High social value. Cons & risks: Avoid conflicts of interest (selling products while "teaching"). Scam warning: Don't promote pyramid schemes or trading "opportunities". Kenya notes: Groups want practical content on chama investments, T-bills and MMFs.

339. Chama and investment group advisory

What it is: Helping chamas set up constitutions, records, governance and investment plans. How it works: Consultations, templates, training.

SkillsGovernance, finance basics
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workChamas, welfare groups
PaymentPer engagement
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Create a chama starter kit (constitution template, records spreadsheet). Pros: Huge market. Cons & risks: Group disputes. Scam warning: Never hold group funds yourself. Kenya notes: Investment groups may need registration depending on activities.

340. Debt collection support (ethical, licensed)

What it is: Helping businesses recover overdue payments through reminders and negotiation. How it works: Licensed agencies follow legal procedures.

SkillsNegotiation, legal awareness, respect
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workLicensed collection agencies, SMEs
PaymentCommission on recovered amounts
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Work with licensed agencies that follow the law. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Stressful conversations. Scam warning: Harassment and shaming debtors is illegal; never threaten or contact people's contacts. Kenya notes: Data protection rules prohibit sharing debtors' information with their contacts.

341. Payment and POS solutions agent

What it is: Signing up merchants for payment solutions (POS terminals, tills, payment links). How it works: Agent agreements with payment providers; commissions per merchant.

SkillsSales, product knowledge
Cost & equipmentPhone, transport
Where to find workShops, restaurants, hotels
PaymentCommissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with licensed payment providers. Pros: Every business needs payments. Cons & risks: Competitive. Scam warning: Never collect setup fees personally. Kenya notes: Lipa na M-Pesa tills and card POS are widely used.

342. Pension and retirement savings agent

What it is: Signing up individuals for registered retirement schemes. How it works: Agents of licensed pension providers.

SkillsFinancial knowledge, trust
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workInformal workers, SMEs, employees
PaymentCommissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Join licensed providers' agent programmes; understand the products. Pros: Helps people plan for retirement. Cons & risks: Long-term sales. Scam warning: Don't mislead on returns. Kenya notes: Retirement schemes are regulated by the Retirement Benefits Authority (RBA).

343. Money market fund and unit trust agent

What it is: Introducing clients to CMA-licensed fund managers' products. How it works: Tied agent or introducer agreements.

SkillsInvestment basics, ethics
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workLicensed fund managers
PaymentCommissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Understand licensing needed for your role; partner with licensed managers. Pros: Recurring client base. Cons & risks: Market risks must be explained. Scam warning: Never accept client money personally. Kenya notes: Check the CMA register of licensed fund managers.

344. Accounting software implementation consultant

What it is: Setting up accounting systems for businesses and training staff. How it works: Needs analysis → setup → data migration → training.

SkillsAccounting software expertise
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workSMEs, NGOs
PaymentProject fees
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Certify in a popular accounting package. Pros: High-value projects. Cons & risks: Data migration risks. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: eTIMS integration is a common need.

345. Microfinance and SACCO field officer

What it is: Recruiting and supporting borrowers and members in the field. How it works: Employed roles with SACCOs and microfinance institutions.

SkillsFinance basics, community relations
Cost & equipmentTransport
Where to find workSACCOs, MFIs
PaymentSalary plus incentives
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Apply with a business or finance diploma. Pros: Career growth. Cons & risks: Travel; collections pressure. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: SASRA regulates deposit-taking SACCOs.

346. Personal finance content creator

What it is: Teaching money skills through social media, blogs or podcasts. How it works: Educational content → audience → sponsorships (disclosed).

SkillsFinancial knowledge, communication
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workSocial platforms
PaymentAds, sponsorships
Realistic earningsVery low to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Focus on education, not hype; disclose partnerships. Pros: Helps people. Cons & risks: Liability for bad advice; financial promotion rules. Scam warning: Never promote unlicensed investment schemes. Kenya notes: Kenyans actively seek honest information about MMFs, T-bills and SACCOs.

Summary

  • Finance methods include insurance agency and claims, bank and SACCO sales, agency banking, loan facilitation, bookkeeping, tax services, financial literacy training, chama advisory, ethical debt collection, payment solutions, pensions, fund agents, accounting software, field officer work and finance content.
  • Licensing and ethics are essential; never handle customers' money personally or promise returns.
