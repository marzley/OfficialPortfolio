Accessibility and assistive services

Millions of Kenyans live with disabilities, and older people need support too. These services improve independence and inclusion, adding to sign language interpretation (method 428) and web accessibility audits (678).

What these methods share

  • Respect: design with, not just for, people with disabilities.
  • Standards: follow accessibility standards and the Persons with Disabilities Act.
  • Partnerships: disability organisations and NCPWD connect you with users.

937. Mobility aid rental and repair

What it is: Renting and repairing wheelchairs, walkers, crutches and hospital beds. How it works: Stock aids → rent short-term → repair and maintain.

SkillsRepair skills, customer care
Cost & equipmentStock, tools, spare parts
Where to find workHospitals, families, older people, after surgery
PaymentPer day/week or repair fee
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with repairs and a few rental items; partner with hospitals. Pros: Real need. Cons & risks: Cleaning and sanitising required. Scam warning: Sanitise equipment between users. Kenya notes: Many families need short-term aids after surgery.

938. Building accessibility audits

What it is: Checking buildings for ramps, accessible toilets, signage and entrances, and recommending fixes. How it works: Inspect → report → advise on modifications.

SkillsAccessibility standards, construction knowledge
Cost & equipmentLow: measuring tools
Where to find workDevelopers, schools, hospitals, offices
PaymentPer audit
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Learn accessibility standards; partner with architects. Pros: Growing compliance need. Cons & risks: Requires credibility. Scam warning: Give honest reports, not box-ticking. Kenya notes: Kenyan law requires reasonable accessibility in public buildings.

939. Braille transcription and printing

What it is: Converting books, exams and documents into braille. How it works: Receive documents → transcribe → emboss and bind.

SkillsBraille literacy
Cost & equipmentBraille embosser (or partnership), software
Where to find workSchools for the blind, universities, government, banks
PaymentPer page or project
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Learn braille through institutions; partner with organisations with embossers. Pros: Specialised skill. Cons & risks: Expensive equipment. Scam warning: Check transcription accuracy carefully. Kenya notes: Schools and institutions need braille materials.

940. Assistive technology training

What it is: Teaching people to use screen readers, magnifiers and accessibility features on phones and computers. How it works: Assess needs → train one-to-one or in groups.

SkillsAssistive tech knowledge, patience
Cost & equipmentLaptop, phone
Where to find workVisually impaired people, schools, employers, NGOs
PaymentPer session or programme
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn screen readers (NVDA, TalkBack, VoiceOver); partner with disability organisations. Pros: Opens jobs and education. Cons & risks: Funding often from NGOs. Scam warning: Never charge for software that's free. Kenya notes: Free screen readers work on most phones and computers.

941. Home modifications for older people and disabilities

What it is: Installing ramps, grab bars, wider doors and accessible bathrooms. How it works: Assess home → design → build.

SkillsConstruction, accessibility knowledge
Cost & equipmentTools, materials
Where to find workFamilies, older people, hospitals' discharge teams
PaymentPer project
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with physiotherapists and hospitals. Pros: Meaningful work. Cons & risks: Every home is different. Scam warning: Quote clearly; don't oversell. Kenya notes: Ageing parents in Kenyan homes create demand.

942. Adapted transport service

What it is: Transporting wheelchair users and people with limited mobility in adapted vehicles. How it works: Booking → safe pickup → transport → assistance.

SkillsDriving, assisting passengers
Cost & equipmentAdapted vehicle, licences, insurance
Where to find workHospitals, families, disability organisations
PaymentPer trip
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with one adapted vehicle; partner with hospitals and NGOs. Pros: Underserved need. Cons & risks: Vehicle adaptation cost. Scam warning: Train drivers in safe transfers. Kenya notes: Few transport options in Kenya are wheelchair accessible.

Summary

  • Accessibility services include mobility aid rental and repair, building audits, braille, assistive technology training, home modifications and adapted transport.
  • Work with disability organisations and follow accessibility standards.
  • Respect and dignity come first.
