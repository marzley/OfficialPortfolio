Legal, compliance and verification services

Businesses, landlords, NGOs and families need documents served, land checked, risks assessed and rules followed. Many of these roles need a licence or registration, which keeps competition lower and trust higher.

What these methods share

  • Licences first: practise only with the registration your role requires.
  • Confidentiality: you handle sensitive documents and personal data; protect them under the Data Protection Act.
  • Records: keep written instructions, receipts and reports for every job.

817. Process server (licensed)

What it is: Delivering court documents (summons, notices) to the people named, and swearing affidavits of service. How it works: Advocates instruct you → locate and serve → file proof of service.

SkillsKnowledge of court procedure, persistence, honesty
Cost & equipmentLicence from the Judiciary, transport, phone
Where to find workLaw firms, debt recovery firms, courts
PaymentPer document served, plus mileage
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths (after licensing)

How to start: Learn the process server rules, get licensed, then introduce yourself to law firms near the courts. Pros: Steady work from busy law firms. Cons & risks: Hostile recipients; travel costs. Scam warning: Never fake service: it's perjury and ends your licence. Kenya notes: Process servers in Kenya are licensed through the Judiciary; law firms keep lists of reliable servers.

818. Conveyancing clerk

What it is: Supporting advocates with property transfers: searches, consents, valuations, stamp duty and registration. How it works: Prepare and track documents through land offices and county offices.

SkillsAttention to detail, land registration knowledge
Cost & equipmentLow: computer, transport
Where to find workLaw firms handling property
PaymentMonthly salary or per file
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Take a paralegal or conveyancing course; apply to law firms with property practices. Pros: Skill in demand with property sales. Cons & risks: Slow government processes. Scam warning: Never accept cash “to speed up” a land office process: it's bribery. Kenya notes: Ardhisasa and county land offices are central to conveyancing; knowing both is valuable.

819. Company secretary (certified)

What it is: Keeping companies compliant: annual returns, resolutions, registers, beneficial ownership filings and board meeting records. How it works: Retainer with companies → file returns and keep statutory records.

SkillsCompany law, governance
Cost & equipmentCertified secretary qualification and registration
Where to find workSMEs, NGOs, SACCOs, startups
PaymentMonthly retainer or per filing
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Qualify as a Certified Public Secretary (CPS(K)) and register with the Institute of Certified Secretaries; start with small companies. Pros: Recurring retainers. Cons & risks: Responsibility for missed deadlines. Scam warning: Only certified secretaries may act; check credentials before hiring one. Kenya notes: Every Kenyan company must file annual returns and beneficial ownership information with the Business Registration Service.

820. Data protection compliance consultant

What it is: Helping organisations comply with Kenya's Data Protection Act: registration with the ODPC, privacy notices, data audits and staff training. How it works: Gap assessment → policies and registration → training → periodic reviews.

SkillsData protection law, policy writing, training
Cost & equipmentLow: laptop; certification helps
Where to find workSchools, clinics, SACCOs, SMEs, NGOs
PaymentPer project or retainer
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Study the Data Protection Act and ODPC guidance; get a recognised privacy certification; offer audits to SMEs. Pros: Growing demand as enforcement rises. Cons & risks: Must keep up with ODPC rulings. Scam warning: Don't promise organisations they can't be fined: give honest advice. Kenya notes: The ODPC has fined organisations for breaches, which pushes demand for compliance help.

821. Accredited mediator

What it is: Helping people and businesses settle disputes without going to court. How it works: Parties agree to mediation → sessions → written settlement.

SkillsListening, negotiation, neutrality
Cost & equipmentMediation training and accreditation
Where to find workCourts (court-annexed mediation), businesses, families, landlords and tenants
PaymentPer session or per case
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train with a recognised mediation programme and seek accreditation; join court-annexed mediation panels. Pros: Meaningful work. Cons & risks: Income irregular at first. Scam warning: Mediators must stay neutral and keep sessions confidential. Kenya notes: Kenya's Judiciary runs court-annexed mediation to reduce case backlogs.

822. Land search and title verification agent

What it is: Checking land ownership, encumbrances and survey maps for buyers before they pay. How it works: Client gives details → official search → report on findings.

SkillsLand records knowledge, care
Cost & equipmentLow; official search fees
Where to find workLand buyers, diaspora, SACCOs, advocates
PaymentPer search or report
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn how official searches work on Ardhisasa and at land registries; partner with advocates and surveyors. Pros: Protects buyers from fraud. Cons & risks: Liability if you miss something. Scam warning: Never forge or “arrange” search results; fake title schemes are a crime. Kenya notes: Land fraud is common; buyers increasingly verify before paying.

823. Loss adjuster or assessor (licensed)

What it is: Investigating insurance claims (vehicles, property, crops) and reporting the loss to insurers. How it works: Insurer appoints → inspect → assess → report.

SkillsTechnical knowledge (motor, engineering, agriculture), report writing
Cost & equipmentIRA licence, transport, camera
Where to find workInsurance companies
PaymentPer assessment
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Gain relevant experience and get licensed by the Insurance Regulatory Authority. Pros: Varied fieldwork. Cons & risks: Pressure from claimants. Scam warning: Never accept payments from claimants to inflate a loss. Kenya notes: Insurers rely on licensed assessors for motor and property claims.

824. Private investigator (licensed)

What it is: Lawful investigations: background checks, fraud, asset tracing, missing persons. How it works: Client brief → lawful inquiries → written report.

SkillsInvestigation, interviewing, report writing, ethics
Cost & equipmentLicensing, phone, transport
Where to find workLaw firms, insurers, banks, companies
PaymentPer case or day rate
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get the required licence (private security regulation applies) and experience, often from police or audit work. Pros: Interesting work. Cons & risks: Legal limits are strict. Scam warning: Never hack phones, track people illegally or break privacy laws. Kenya notes: The Private Security Regulatory Authority regulates private security services in Kenya; check current licensing requirements.

825. Background screening services

What it is: Verifying job applicants' identity, education certificates, references and work history for employers. How it works: Consent from candidate → checks → report.

SkillsOrganisation, verification, data protection
Cost & equipmentLow: phone, computer
Where to find workEmployers, recruiters, schools, domestic worker agencies
PaymentPer candidate
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Create a clear process with candidate consent; partner with recruiters. Pros: Repeat business. Cons & risks: Slow responses from institutions. Scam warning: Always get written consent; don't sell candidates' data. Kenya notes: Certificate fraud makes verification valuable to Kenyan employers.

826. Occupational safety and health (OSH) advisor

What it is: Helping workplaces meet safety law: risk assessments, safety audits, training and safety committees. How it works: Audit → report → training → follow-up.

SkillsOSH knowledge, auditing
Cost & equipmentRegistration with DOSH, training
Where to find workFactories, construction sites, hotels, schools
PaymentPer audit or retainer
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Study OSH, get registered with the Directorate of Occupational Safety and Health Services, and offer audits. Pros: Required by law for many workplaces. Cons & risks: Responsibility if advice is wrong. Scam warning: Never sign off on a site you didn't inspect. Kenya notes: The Occupational Safety and Health Act requires workplaces to manage risks and audits.

827. Environmental impact assessment (EIA) expert

What it is: Preparing EIA and environmental audit reports for projects that need NEMA approval. How it works: Project brief → site visits → public participation → report submitted to NEMA.

SkillsEnvironmental science, report writing
Cost & equipmentRegistration with NEMA as an expert
Where to find workDevelopers, factories, county projects, farms
PaymentPer report
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Study environmental science and register with NEMA as an associate then lead expert. Pros: Required for many projects. Cons & risks: Strict standards; site travel. Scam warning: Never copy or fake reports: NEMA can deregister you. Kenya notes: Many developments in Kenya need an EIA licence from NEMA before they start.

828. Records management and archiving

What it is: Organising, storing and retrieving physical files for offices, schools and clinics, and setting retention schedules. How it works: Assess records → organise and index → store securely → retrieve on request.

SkillsOrganisation, records management
Cost & equipmentStorage space, shelving, labels
Where to find workLaw firms, clinics, schools, SACCOs, NGOs
PaymentMonthly storage fees or per project
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Start by organising records on-site for clients; add secure storage later. Pros: Recurring storage income. Cons & risks: Space and security costs. Scam warning: Protect confidential files: access logs and locks. Kenya notes: Pairs with document scanning and digitisation (method 263).

Summary

  • Legal and compliance services include process serving, conveyancing support, company secretarial work, data protection, mediation, land searches, loss assessing, private investigation, background screening, OSH, EIA and records management.
  • Most need a licence or registration, which builds trust.
  • Never fake service, searches, reports or inspections.
