Logistics, import, export and trade

Moving goods between farms, factories, towns and countries creates many earning opportunities. Kenya is a regional trade hub (Mombasa port, cross-border trade with Uganda, Tanzania and beyond). Digital tools (WhatsApp, tracking, online marketplaces) make these businesses easier to start and manage.

What these methods share

  • Licences and compliance: customs, KRA, standards (KEBS), and sector-specific permits.
  • Trust and verification: trade fraud is common; verify suppliers and buyers.
  • Cash flow: goods tie up money; plan carefully.

510. Clearing and forwarding agent (licensed)

What it is: Handling customs clearance and shipping logistics for importers and exporters. How it works: Licensed agents process documents and coordinate shipments.

SkillsCustoms procedures, documentation
Cost & equipmentLicences, office
Where to find workImporters, exporters
PaymentFees
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train in customs procedures; work in a licensed firm first. Pros: Essential service. Cons & risks: Regulatory responsibility. Scam warning: Fake agents who disappear with duty payments. Kenya notes: KRA licenses customs agents.

511. Small-scale importing (e.g. from China, Dubai, Turkey)

What it is: Importing goods for resale. How it works: Supplier sourcing → shipping/consolidation → clearing → sales.

SkillsSourcing, negotiation, market research
Cost & equipmentCapital, shipping agents
Where to find workYour sales channels
PaymentSales margins
Realistic earningsLow to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with small consolidated shipments; verify suppliers. Pros: Unique products and margins. Cons & risks: Delays; duties; quality. Scam warning: Fake suppliers and fake shipping agents. Kenya notes: Imports must meet KEBS standards and customs rules.

512. Exporting Kenyan products

What it is: Exporting products like tea, coffee, crafts, avocados, flowers or textiles. How it works: Sourcing → compliance → buyers → shipping.

SkillsExport procedures, buyer networks
Cost & equipmentCapital, certifications
Where to find workInternational buyers, trade fairs, online B2B platforms
PaymentExport contracts
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Study export requirements; join export promotion programmes. Pros: Large markets. Cons & risks: Compliance and payment risks. Scam warning: Fake buyers who ask for samples and "fees". Kenya notes: Export promotion agencies support new exporters.

513. Cross-border trading (East African Community)

What it is: Trading goods across borders with Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda and others. How it works: Buying and selling across markets.

SkillsMarket knowledge, documentation
Cost & equipmentCapital, transport
Where to find workRegional markets
PaymentSales margins
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn EAC simplified trade regimes for small traders. Pros: Regional demand. Cons & risks: Border procedures. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Simplified trade regimes exist for small cross-border traders.

514. Wholesale distribution

What it is: Buying from manufacturers and distributing to retailers. How it works: Distribution agreements, delivery routes.

SkillsLogistics, credit management
Cost & equipmentCapital, transport, storage
Where to find workShops, dukas
PaymentMargins
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with fast-moving goods in one area. Pros: Volume sales. Cons & risks: Credit risk from retailers. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Digital ordering apps for dukas are changing distribution.

515. Warehousing and storage services

What it is: Providing storage space for businesses. How it works: Rental agreements, inventory management.

SkillsInventory management
Cost & equipmentWarehouse space
Where to find workE-commerce sellers, distributors
PaymentStorage fees
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with small storage units. Pros: Recurring income. Cons & risks: Security. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: E-commerce growth increases demand.

516. Last-mile delivery business (fleet)

What it is: Running a fleet of riders delivering for businesses. How it works: Contracts with shops and platforms.

SkillsFleet management
Cost & equipmentMotorbikes, riders, insurance
Where to find workE-commerce, restaurants, pharmacies
PaymentPer delivery or contracts
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with 2–3 riders and reliable clients. Pros: Growing demand. Cons & risks: Accidents; rider management. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Insurance and rider safety are essential.

517. Truck and lorry transport business

What it is: Transporting goods by truck. How it works: Contracts with traders, farmers, manufacturers.

SkillsLogistics management
Cost & equipmentTrucks (high capital), licences
Where to find workBusinesses, logistics platforms
PaymentPer trip
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with one truck and steady contracts. Pros: High demand. Cons & risks: Maintenance and fuel costs. Scam warning: Fake cargo offers used to steal trucks or goods. Kenya notes: Digital freight platforms connect trucks with loads.

518. Cold chain logistics

What it is: Transporting and storing perishable goods (milk, flowers, fish, vaccines). How it works: Refrigerated trucks and storage.

SkillsCold chain management
Cost & equipmentRefrigerated equipment
Where to find workFarmers, exporters, health sector
PaymentContracts
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Partner with producers needing cold storage. Pros: Essential niche. Cons & risks: High capital. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Horticulture exports depend on cold chains.

519. Procurement agent for diaspora and businesses

What it is: Buying and shipping goods for people abroad or businesses that need sourcing help. How it works: Orders → sourcing → quality checks → shipping.

SkillsSourcing, honesty
Cost & equipmentPhone, transport
Where to find workDiaspora, businesses
PaymentService fees
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Offer transparent receipts and photos. Pros: Trust-based clients. Cons & risks: Shipping issues. Scam warning: Always provide receipts; never mix client funds. Kenya notes: Diaspora often need trusted purchasing agents.

520. Courier and parcel agency outlet

What it is: Operating a parcel drop-off and pick-up point for courier companies. How it works: Agency agreements; commissions per parcel.

SkillsOrganisation, record keeping
Cost & equipmentShop space
Where to find workCourier companies
PaymentCommissions
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Apply to courier agency programmes. Pros: Adds foot traffic to a shop. Cons & risks: Liability for parcels. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Combine with cyber or retail shops.

521. Packaging supply business

What it is: Supplying packaging (boxes, bags, containers, labels) to small businesses. How it works: Wholesale buying → reselling to SMEs.

SkillsSourcing, sales
Cost & equipmentStock
Where to find workFood businesses, online sellers
PaymentSales
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Target online sellers and food vendors. Pros: Recurring orders. Cons & risks: Storage. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Follow plastic bag bans; offer compliant alternatives.

522. Inventory and logistics software for SMEs (reselling and setup)

What it is: Setting up inventory and delivery tracking tools for businesses. How it works: Resell or configure software; training.

SkillsSoftware setup, business processes
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workDistributors, shops
PaymentSetup and subscriptions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with software vendors (POS and inventory systems). Pros: Recurring. Cons & risks: Support needs. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: eTIMS-compliant systems are in demand.

523. Trade fair and B2B sourcing facilitation

What it is: Helping businesses attend trade fairs and source suppliers. How it works: Research, travel coordination, supplier vetting.

SkillsResearch, networking
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workSMEs
PaymentFees
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Build supplier verification checklists. Pros: Valued by importers. Cons & risks: Travel costs. Scam warning: Fake trade fair invitations. Kenya notes: Verify events through official organisers.

524. Commodity trading (grain, produce) with warehouse receipts

What it is: Buying and selling agricultural commodities, sometimes using warehouse receipt systems. How it works: Purchase → storage → sale when prices are favourable.

SkillsMarket analysis, quality checks
Cost & equipmentCapital, storage
Where to find workFarmers, buyers, commodity platforms
PaymentMargins
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Learn quality standards and storage. Pros: Seasonal opportunities. Cons & risks: Price risk; spoilage. Scam warning: Fake buyers; insist on proper payment terms. Kenya notes: Warehouse receipt systems exist for some commodities.

525. Logistics consulting for SMEs

What it is: Advising businesses on delivery, routes, warehousing and costs. How it works: Assessment → recommendations.

SkillsSupply chain expertise
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workSMEs, manufacturers
PaymentProjects
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Gain logistics experience; document savings achieved. Pros: Clear value. Cons & risks: Needs track record. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Many SMEs struggle with delivery costs.

Summary

  • Logistics and trade methods include clearing agents, importing, exporting, cross-border trade, wholesale, warehousing, delivery fleets, trucking, cold chains, procurement agents, courier outlets, packaging supply, logistics software, trade facilitation, commodity trading and consulting.
  • Verify suppliers and buyers, follow customs and standards rules, and manage cash flow carefully.
