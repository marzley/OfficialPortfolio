More automotive specialist trades

Adding to automotive trades (methods 431–445), these specialists serve garages and fleet owners who need specific repairs.

What these methods share

  • Skills: technical training and experience matter more than equipment.
  • Honesty: explain faults and show replaced parts.
  • Partnerships: garages refer specialist work.

965. Auto glass replacement

What it is: Replacing and repairing windscreens and vehicle windows. How it works: Source glass → fit → seal → test.

SkillsGlass fitting
Cost & equipmentTools, adhesives, glass supplier
Where to find workMotorists, insurers, fleets
PaymentPer job
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Train under a fitter; partner with insurers and garages. Pros: Insurance work. Cons & risks: Breakage risk. Scam warning: Use quality glass and adhesives. Kenya notes: Insurers pay for windscreen claims.

966. Vehicle air-conditioning repair

What it is: Servicing and repairing car AC systems. How it works: Diagnose → recharge or repair → test.

SkillsAC systems, diagnostics
Cost & equipmentRecharge machine, tools
Where to find workMotorists, fleets, matatu owners
PaymentPer job
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Train in refrigeration and auto AC. Pros: Hot regions need AC. Cons & risks: Equipment costs. Scam warning: Handle refrigerants properly. Kenya notes: Mombasa and other hot towns have strong demand.

967. Exhaust and silencer fabrication

What it is: Repairing and fabricating exhaust pipes and silencers. How it works: Inspect → cut and weld → fit.

SkillsWelding, fabrication
Cost & equipmentWelding equipment, pipe bender
Where to find workMotorists, matatus, trucks
PaymentPer job
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn on the job at an exhaust shop. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Noise and fumes. Scam warning: Don't remove required emissions parts. Kenya notes: NTSA inspections check excessive noise and smoke.

968. Diesel injection specialist

What it is: Testing and repairing diesel injectors and pumps. How it works: Remove → test on bench → repair or calibrate → refit.

SkillsDiesel systems, calibration
Cost & equipmentTest bench, tools
Where to find workTrucks, matatus, tractors, generators
PaymentPer job
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train with an injection specialist; invest in test equipment gradually. Pros: Specialist, well-paid work. Cons & risks: Expensive equipment. Scam warning: Use genuine parts where needed and explain options. Kenya notes: Diesel fleets and tractors need injection specialists.

Summary

  • Automotive specialists include auto glass, AC repair, exhaust fabrication and diesel injection.
  • Training and honest explanations build trust.
  • Follow emissions and safety rules.
