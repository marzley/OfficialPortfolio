Education businesses

Parents in Kenya invest heavily in education. Beyond individual tutoring (methods 204–222), there are owner-level education businesses: daycares, tuition centres, schools, colleges and support services. They require registration and careful compliance because they serve children and learners.

What these methods share

  • Registration and standards: schools register with the Ministry of Education and county authorities; TVET institutions with TVETA; daycares with county governments.
  • Child safeguarding: background checks, safe premises, clear policies.
  • Reputation: results, safety and communication drive enrolment.

557. Daycare centre

What it is: Caring for babies and toddlers while parents work. How it works: Licensed premises, trained caregivers, structured routines.

SkillsChildcare, first aid, management
Cost & equipmentPremises, toys, safety equipment, licences
Where to find workWorking parents in your area
PaymentMonthly fees
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Meet county licensing and safety requirements. Pros: High demand. Cons & risks: Huge responsibility. Scam warning: As usual: honest marketing, no hidden fees. Kenya notes: Estates near business districts have strong demand.

558. Private primary or ECDE school

What it is: Running a private school. How it works: Registration → premises → teachers → curriculum delivery.

SkillsEducation management
Cost & equipmentPremises, staff, materials
Where to find workParents
PaymentSchool fees
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths to years

How to start: Follow Ministry of Education registration standards. Pros: Long-term impact. Cons & risks: High capital and regulation. Scam warning: Don't operate unregistered schools. Kenya notes: CBC requirements shape curriculum and facilities.

559. Tuition and learning centre

What it is: A centre offering after-school and weekend support classes. How it works: Classes, assessments, parent communication.

SkillsTeaching, management
Cost & equipmentPremises, materials
Where to find workParents
PaymentFees
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Hire qualified teachers; comply with regulations on tuition. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Regulatory changes. Scam warning: Don't promise grades. Kenya notes: Check current Ministry rules on tuition.

560. TVET training centre (registered)

What it is: Offering technical and vocational courses. How it works: TVETA registration, accredited courses, assessments.

SkillsEducation management, technical expertise
Cost & equipmentPremises, workshops, equipment
Where to find workYouth, adults seeking skills
PaymentFees (some learners use HELB or sponsorships)
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Register with TVETA; follow CBET curricula (CBET Planner helps trainers with documentation). Pros: National skills demand. Cons & risks: Accreditation requirements. Scam warning: Never issue unaccredited certificates as official. Kenya notes: CBET is the TVET training framework.

How to start: Register appropriately; offer practical courses. Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Equipment maintenance. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Partner with digital programmes where possible.

566. Education consultancy (study abroad and university placement)

What it is: Guiding students on applications to universities locally and abroad. How it works: Consultations, application support (fees transparent).

SkillsAdmissions knowledge, honesty
Cost & equipmentOffice/online
Where to find workStudents, parents
PaymentFees, university commissions (disclosed)
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Build accurate knowledge; disclose commissions. Pros: High demand. Cons & risks: Visa outcomes aren't guaranteed. Scam warning: Never promise visas or admissions; fake agents are common. Kenya notes: Verify institutions' accreditation for students.

Summary

  • Education businesses include daycares, schools, tuition centres, TVET and computer colleges, school transport, supplies, educational toys, reading clubs, education consultancy, exam printing, school software, special needs services and holiday camps.
  • Register properly, protect children, and never promise grades, admissions or visas.
