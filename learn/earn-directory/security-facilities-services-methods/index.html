Security and facilities services

Buildings, estates, events and businesses need protection, cleaning, maintenance and management. These services offer employment and business opportunities, often through contracts with institutions.

What these methods share

  • Licensing: private security providers are regulated by the Private Security Regulatory Authority (PSRA); other services need county permits.
  • Labour law: fair wages, contracts and safety for workers.
  • Reliability: contracts depend on consistent service.

651. Private security guard (employment)

What it is: Working as a licensed security guard. How it works: Employed by registered security firms after training.

SkillsSecurity training, vigilance, integrity
Cost & equipmentUniform provided by employer
Where to find workRegistered security companies
PaymentMonthly wages
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Join PSRA-registered firms; complete required training. Pros: Employment entry point. Cons & risks: Long shifts; risks. Scam warning: Fake security jobs abroad charging fees. Kenya notes: PSRA sets standards and registration for guards.

652. Security company (licensed)

What it is: Running a private security business. How it works: Licensing → recruiting guards → contracts.

SkillsSecurity management, business
Cost & equipmentLicences, staff, uniforms
Where to find workEstates, businesses, schools
PaymentMonthly contracts
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Meet PSRA licensing requirements. Pros: Recurring contracts. Cons & risks: Liability; labour costs. Scam warning: Pay guards fairly and legally. Kenya notes: Minimum wage rules apply to guards.
