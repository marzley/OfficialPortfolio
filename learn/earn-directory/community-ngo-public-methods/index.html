Community, NGO, public programmes and other legitimate earning routes

Some income comes from community work, public programmes, competitions and sharing what you already own. These methods are varied: some are jobs, some are side incomes, some are one-off opportunities. All are legitimate when done honestly.

What these methods share

  • Verify programmes through official websites and offices.
  • Never pay to be "selected" for government programmes, NGO jobs or competitions.
  • Keep records and stay honest with communities.

414. Community health promoter and outreach work

What it is: Supporting community health programmes (education, referrals, data collection). How it works: Recruited through county health departments or NGOs, often with stipends.

SkillsCommunication, community trust, training provided
Cost & equipmentPhone (often provided for data)
Where to find workCounty health departments, NGOs
PaymentStipends/salaries
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeMonths (recruitment)

How to start: Follow official county recruitment announcements. Pros: Community impact. Cons & risks: Low pay. Scam warning: Fake recruitment demanding fees. Kenya notes: Community health promoters are part of Kenya's primary healthcare system.

415. NGO field officer and programme assistant

What it is: Implementing NGO programmes in communities. How it works: Employed roles with field work and reporting.

SkillsRelevant diploma/degree, community work, reporting
Cost & equipmentTransport
Where to find workNGOs, job boards
PaymentSalary
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Volunteer to build experience. Pros: Meaningful. Cons & risks: Contract-based. Scam warning: NGO jobs never require application fees. Kenya notes: Verify NGOs through the NGO regulatory board.

416. Volunteering with stipends (programmes and internships)

What it is: Structured volunteer or internship programmes that pay stipends. How it works: Applications through official programmes.

SkillsVaries
Cost & equipmentVaries
Where to find workGovernment, NGOs, UN agencies, companies
PaymentStipends
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Apply through official portals only. Pros: Experience. Cons & risks: Temporary. Scam warning: Fake "UN jobs" asking for fees are common. Kenya notes: Public service internship programmes are advertised officially.

417. Government youth and digital programmes

What it is: Programmes like Ajira Digital and Jitume that train youth for digital work and connect them to opportunities. How it works: Registration → training → mentorship → job linkages.

SkillsWillingness to learn
Cost & equipmentAccess to centres or a device
Where to find workOfficial programme portals and centres
PaymentProgramme-dependent; income comes from the skills and opportunities you pursue
Realistic earningsDepends on the work you do after training
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Register through official portals (Ajira and Jitume programmes). Pros: Free training. Cons & risks: Training isn't a job guarantee. Scam warning: Fake "Ajira/Jitume recruiters" charging fees. Kenya notes: See how to join a Jitume lab.

418. Census and survey enumeration (official)

What it is: Temporary roles in national surveys and censuses (see method 30). How it works: Official recruitment, training, fieldwork.

SkillsCommunication, local knowledge
Cost & equipmentProvided
Where to find workKNBS and official announcements
PaymentDaily rates
Realistic earningsLow to medium (temporary)
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Watch official announcements. Pros: Good temporary income. Cons & risks: Short-term. Scam warning: Official recruitment is free. Kenya notes: Apply only through official channels.

419. Election officials and polling clerks (seasonal)

What it is: Temporary roles during elections. How it works: Official recruitment by the electoral body.

SkillsIntegrity, training provided
Cost & equipmentNone
Where to find workIEBC announcements
PaymentAllowances
Realistic earningsLow (one-off)
Time to first incomeSeasonal

How to start: Watch official IEBC announcements. Pros: Civic role. Cons & risks: Long hours. Scam warning: Recruitment is free and official. Kenya notes: Only during electoral cycles.

420. Business competitions and grants for entrepreneurs

What it is: Winning funding through legitimate competitions and grant programmes. How it works: Applications, pitches, evaluations.

SkillsBusiness planning, pitching
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workOfficial programme websites, incubators
PaymentGrants/prizes
Realistic earningsOne-off; varies
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Prepare a strong business plan and pitch deck (pitch decks). Pros: Non-repayable funding. Cons & risks: Very competitive. Scam warning: Fake grants demanding "processing fees". Kenya notes: Incubators and hubs announce competitions regularly.

421. Writing and creative competitions

What it is: Winning prizes for writing, art, film, innovation or hackathons. How it works: Entries judged by panels.

SkillsCreativity, skill
Cost & equipmentVaries
Where to find workOfficial competitions
PaymentPrizes
Realistic earningsOne-off
Time to first incomeUnpredictable

How to start: Enter reputable competitions with no entry fees or reasonable fees. Pros: Recognition. Cons & risks: Unpredictable. Scam warning: Competitions with high entry fees and vague judging. Kenya notes: Hackathons often lead to jobs and partnerships.

422. Renting out equipment and assets

What it is: Renting items you own: tools, generators, sound systems, cameras, tents. How it works: Rental agreements, deposits.

SkillsManagement, trust
Cost & equipmentOwned items
Where to find workNeighbours, events, contractors
PaymentPer day
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: List items on social media; use written agreements. Pros: Earn from idle assets. Cons & risks: Damage. Scam warning: Verify renters' identities. Kenya notes: Event equipment rentals are popular.

423. Renting out space (rooms, parking, storage, shops)

What it is: Renting space you control for parking, storage, offices or events. How it works: Agreements and monthly fees.

SkillsProperty management
Cost & equipmentSpace
Where to find workLocal demand
PaymentMonthly
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Check lease terms and county rules. Pros: Passive-ish income. Cons & risks: Tenant issues. Scam warning: Fake tenants with bounced payments. Kenya notes: Declare rental income to KRA.

424. Referral programmes (legitimate)

What it is: Earning rewards for referring customers to legitimate businesses. How it works: Referral links or codes; reward per customer.

SkillsNetworking, honesty
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workBusinesses' official referral programmes
PaymentRewards per referral
Realistic earningsVery low to low
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Only refer services you trust (Marzley referral programme is one example). Pros: Simple. Cons & risks: Small amounts. Scam warning: Pyramid schemes where income comes mainly from recruiting. Kenya notes: Legitimate referral rewards come from real product sales.

425. Selling unused items and decluttering

What it is: Selling things you no longer need. How it works: List online → sell → deliver.

SkillsListing
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workMarketplace, groups
PaymentM-Pesa
Realistic earningsOne-off
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Photograph items well; price fairly. Pros: Quick cash. Cons & risks: One-off. Scam warning: Fake payment messages. Kenya notes: Check M-Pesa balance before handing over items.

426. Mystery app testing for local start-ups and focus feedback

What it is: Paid feedback sessions for local start-ups launching apps and services. How it works: Recruited through start-up communities.

SkillsFeedback skills
Cost & equipmentSmartphone
Where to find workStart-up hubs, social media
PaymentPer session
Realistic earningsVery low
Time to first incomeOccasional

How to start: Join local tech community groups. Pros: Networking. Cons & risks: Occasional. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Nairobi's start-up community runs user research.

427. Interpreter and guide for visiting researchers and journalists (fixer)

What it is: Helping visiting journalists and researchers with logistics, translation and contacts. How it works: Freelance engagements.

SkillsLocal knowledge, languages, networks
Cost & equipmentPhone, transport
Where to find workMedia houses, researchers, networks
PaymentDaily rates
Realistic earningsMedium (irregular)
Time to first incomeOccasional

How to start: Build networks with journalists. Pros: Good daily rates. Cons & risks: Safety in sensitive stories. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Kenya is a regional media hub.

428. Sign language interpretation

What it is: Interpreting between Kenyan Sign Language and spoken languages. How it works: Events, media, courts, education.

SkillsKSL proficiency, training
Cost & equipmentNone
Where to find workEvents, broadcasters, institutions
PaymentPer assignment
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train professionally. Pros: Inclusion; demand. Cons & risks: Training time. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Demand for qualified KSL interpreters exceeds supply.

429. Funeral and event coordination services

What it is: Coordinating funeral logistics: transport, programmes, catering, tents. How it works: Packages for families.

SkillsOrganisation, empathy
Cost & equipmentVendor network
Where to find workFamilies, churches
PaymentPackage fees
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Build vendor partnerships. Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Sensitive situations. Scam warning: Don't overcharge grieving families. Kenya notes: Funerals involve complex logistics across counties.

430. Personal shopping and styling

What it is: Helping clients shop for clothes, gifts and homes. How it works: Consultations and shopping trips (in person or online).

SkillsFashion sense, budgeting
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workProfessionals, brides, diaspora
PaymentFees
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Build a styling portfolio on Instagram. Pros: Creative. Cons & risks: Niche. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Diaspora clients need personal shoppers for gifts sent home.

Summary

  • Community and other routes include health promoters, NGO roles, stipend programmes, Ajira and Jitume, official enumeration and election jobs, competitions and grants, renting assets and space, referral programmes, decluttering, start-up feedback, fixing, sign language interpretation, funeral coordination and personal shopping.
  • Verify official programmes and never pay to be selected.
