More property and finance businesses

Adding to property (methods 357–360) and finance (331–346), these businesses need more capital or licences but can build lasting income.

What these methods share

  • Licences: valuers, forex bureaus, digital lenders and investment advisers are regulated.
  • Due diligence: verify land, titles and approvals before investing.
  • Honesty: transparent pricing and terms protect you and your customers.

943. Student hostel

What it is: Providing accommodation for college and university students near campuses. How it works: Build or lease → furnish → rent per semester → manage.

SkillsProperty management
Cost & equipmentHigh: building or lease, furnishing
Where to find workUniversities, colleges, TVETs
PaymentPer bed per semester
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start near a growing campus; offer Wi-Fi, security and study space. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Vacancies during holidays. Scam warning: Use written tenancy agreements. Kenya notes: Hostels differ from tourist hostels (method 498): students stay for whole semesters.

944. Rental units (being a landlord)

What it is: Building or buying bedsitters, one-bedroom units or shops to rent out. How it works: Acquire property → rent → manage or hire a property manager.

SkillsProperty and tenant management
Cost & equipmentVery high: land and construction, or purchase
Where to find workGrowing towns, near jobs and transport
PaymentMonthly rent
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeYears

How to start: Start small; check approvals, title and demand before building. Pros: Long-term income. Cons & risks: High capital; vacancies; repairs. Scam warning: Check land titles and county approvals before building. Kenya notes: Rental demand is high in fast-growing towns.

945. Land banking and subdivision

What it is: Buying land, getting approvals to subdivide, and selling plots. How it works: Buy → survey and approvals → title processing → sell plots.

SkillsLand law, negotiation, patience
Cost & equipmentVery high capital
Where to find workGrowing peri-urban areas
PaymentProfit on plot sales
Realistic earningsHigh (with risk)
Time to first incomeYears

How to start: Work with an advocate and licensed surveyor; do full due diligence. Pros: Potentially high returns. Cons & risks: Legal and title risks; long timelines. Scam warning: Never sell plots without proper titles and approvals: buyers sue and it can be criminal. Kenya notes: Land fraud is a major problem; honest sellers stand out.

946. Co-working space

What it is: Renting desks, private offices and meeting rooms to freelancers and small teams. How it works: Fit out space → rent by day or month → provide internet and amenities.

SkillsManagement, marketing
Cost & equipmentHigh: lease, furniture, fast internet, backup power
Where to find workFreelancers, startups, remote workers
PaymentDaily or monthly memberships
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start in a busy town with good internet and backup power. Pros: Recurring memberships. Cons & risks: Lease costs. Scam warning: Clear membership terms. Kenya notes: Remote workers want reliable internet and power.

947. Property valuer (registered)

What it is: Valuing land and buildings for banks, buyers, courts and insurers. How it works: Inspection → analysis → valuation report.

SkillsValuation, real estate economics
Cost & equipmentRegistration with the Valuers Registration Board
Where to find workBanks, SACCOs, advocates, insurers
PaymentPer valuation
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeYears (qualification)

How to start: Study land economics; register as a valuer. Pros: Professional fees. Cons & risks: Long qualification path. Scam warning: Never inflate valuations for loans: it's fraud. Kenya notes: Banks need valuations for mortgages and loans.

948. Foreign exchange bureau (licensed)

What it is: Buying and selling foreign currencies. How it works: Get a CBK licence → set up premises → trade currencies.

SkillsFinance, compliance
Cost & equipmentHigh: capital, CBK licence, security
Where to find workTowns, borders, tourist areas
PaymentMargin on exchange
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths to years

How to start: Meet CBK capital and compliance rules, including anti-money laundering checks. Pros: Steady trade in busy areas. Cons & risks: Heavy regulation. Scam warning: Follow anti-money laundering rules strictly. Kenya notes: Forex bureaus in Kenya are licensed and supervised by the Central Bank.

949. Digital credit provider (licensed)

What it is: Offering small loans through apps or USSD. How it works: Get licensed → build credit scoring → lend and collect fairly.

SkillsFinance, technology, compliance
Cost & equipmentHigh: capital, technology, licence
Where to find workIndividuals, SMEs
PaymentInterest and fees
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeYears

How to start: Apply for a Central Bank of Kenya digital credit provider licence. Pros: Large market. Cons & risks: Strict rules on pricing and collection. Scam warning: Debt shaming and harassing borrowers is illegal. Kenya notes: Since 2022, digital lenders must be licensed by the CBK.

950. Investment adviser (licensed)

What it is: Advising clients on investments such as unit trusts, shares and bonds. How it works: Licensed by the Capital Markets Authority → assess clients → advise → review.

SkillsFinance, ethics
Cost & equipmentCMA licence, qualifications
Where to find workProfessionals, SMEs, diaspora
PaymentFees or commissions
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeYears

How to start: Get relevant qualifications and a CMA licence. Pros: Professional income. Cons & risks: Regulation and liability. Scam warning: Never promise guaranteed returns. Kenya notes: The CMA licenses investment advisers in Kenya.

Summary

  • Property and finance businesses include student hostels, rental units, land subdivision, co-working, valuation, forex bureaus, digital lending and investment advice.
  • Many are licensed by CBK, CMA or the Valuers Registration Board.
  • Due diligence, honest terms and fair collection are essential.
