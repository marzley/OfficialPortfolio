Transcription, audio and voice work

Interviews, meetings, court sessions, podcasts, sermons, lectures and videos all produce audio that needs typing, captioning, editing or voicing. This category suits people with good ears, fast typing, clear voices or audio skills.

What these methods share

  • Equipment: good headphones; for voice work, a decent microphone and a quiet space (a wardrobe full of clothes makes a cheap "vocal booth").
  • Skills: listening, accurate typing (typing skills), grammar, and audio software basics.
  • Confidentiality: you'll hear private information; treat it carefully.
  • See transcription and audio work overview.

98. General transcription

What it is: Typing audio or video recordings (interviews, meetings, podcasts) into text. How it works: Listen and type following a style (clean verbatim or full verbatim), with speaker labels and sometimes timestamps; quality checked.

SkillsTyping 50+ wpm with accuracy, listening, grammar
Cost & equipmentLaptop, headphones, foot pedal optional, free transcription software
Where to find workTranscription platforms, agencies, researchers, podcasters
PaymentPer audio minute
Realistic earningsLow; rises with speed and specialisation
Time to first incomeWeeks (tests)

How to start: Practise with public talks; take platform tests; learn style guides. Pros: Flexible; entry-level. Cons & risks: Hard audio pays the same; tests are strict; AI transcription reduces simple work. Scam warning: "Transcription jobs" requiring payment for software, accounts or training. Kenya notes: Accents and multilingual audio are areas where humans still add value.

99. Captioning (closed captions)

What it is: Creating time-synchronised captions for videos, including sound descriptions, for accessibility. How it works: Transcribe and time captions in caption software; follow reading-speed and line rules.

SkillsTranscription, timing, caption standards
Cost & equipmentLaptop, caption software (free options)
Where to find workCaptioning companies, YouTubers, educators, media
PaymentPer video minute
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Caption a short video; learn SRT/VTT formats. Pros: Growing accessibility demand. Cons & risks: Detail-heavy. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Local creators and educators benefit from captions for wider reach.

100. Correcting AI transcripts (transcript editing)

What it is: Reviewing machine-generated transcripts against the audio and fixing errors. How it works: Listen while reading, correct words, names, speakers and punctuation.

SkillsListening, grammar, speed
Cost & equipmentLaptop, headphones
Where to find workTranscription companies, media firms, researchers
PaymentPer audio minute
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn to use auto-transcription tools yourself and offer fast, accurate cleaned transcripts. Pros: Faster than typing from scratch. Cons & risks: Lower rates than full transcription. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Offer cleaned transcripts of Kenyan accents and code-switching (English/Kiswahili).

101. Medical transcription

What it is: Transcribing doctors' dictations and medical records. How it works: Requires medical terminology and strict confidentiality; often through specialist companies.

SkillsMedical terminology, accuracy, data privacy
Cost & equipmentLaptop, headphones, secure setup
Where to find workMedical transcription companies, hospitals
PaymentPer line, minute or salary
Realistic earningsLow–medium (decreasing as speech recognition improves)
Time to first incomeMonths (training)

How to start: Take a recognised medical terminology course; health workers have an advantage. Pros: Specialist demand. Cons & risks: Training cost; strict privacy rules. Scam warning: Overpriced "certification" courses promising guaranteed jobs. Kenya notes: Nurses and clinical officers with typing skills can apply medical knowledge.

102. Legal transcription and court reporting support

What it is: Transcribing legal proceedings, depositions, interviews and legal dictations. How it works: Verbatim accuracy, legal terminology, formatting rules.

SkillsLegal terms, verbatim accuracy, confidentiality
Cost & equipmentLaptop, headphones
Where to find workLaw firms, transcription agencies, research projects
PaymentPer page or minute
Realistic earningsLow–medium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Learn legal terminology; approach law firms with transcription and typing services. Pros: Higher accuracy is rewarded. Cons & risks: Confidentiality obligations. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Law firms and researchers need interview and hearing transcripts.

103. Research interview transcription and translation

What it is: Transcribing and translating qualitative research interviews and focus groups (often in local languages) for universities and NGOs. How it works: Transcribe in the original language, then translate to English, keeping meaning; confidentiality agreements.

SkillsLocal languages, translation, accuracy
Cost & equipmentLaptop, headphones
Where to find workUniversities, research firms, NGOs, consultants
PaymentPer audio hour or project
Realistic earningsLow–medium; local-language skills earn more
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Build relationships with researchers and research firms; offer test transcripts. Pros: Values local languages. Cons & risks: Project-based. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Kiswahili, Kikuyu, Dholuo, Luhya, Kalenjin, Somali and other languages are in demand for research.

104. Voice-over for ads and corporate videos

What it is: Recording your voice for adverts, explainer videos, corporate training, IVR phone systems and documentaries. How it works: Receive a script → record and edit clean audio → deliver files; often directed live.

SkillsClear, expressive voice; reading naturally; basic audio editing
Cost & equipmentUSB/XLR microphone, headphones, quiet treated space, free software (Audacity)
Where to find workVoice marketplaces, ad agencies, production houses, radio stations, freelance platforms
PaymentPer project, word or finished minute; usage rights affect price
Realistic earningsLow to medium; strong demos and repeat clients matter
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Record a 60–90 second demo reel with varied styles; treat your recording space. Pros: Creative; repeat clients. Cons & risks: Equipment investment; competitive. Scam warning: Agencies charging big fees for "voice certification". Kenya notes: Kiswahili and Kenyan English voice-overs are needed for local ads, NGOs and phone systems.

105. Audiobook narration

What it is: Reading entire books aloud and producing finished audio files. How it works: Prepare (read the book), record chapter by chapter, edit, master to platform standards.

SkillsStamina, character voices, consistency, audio editing
Cost & equipmentGood microphone, treated space, software
Where to find workAuthors, publishers, audiobook production marketplaces (availability varies by country)
PaymentPer finished hour or royalty share
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Record sample chapters of public-domain books. Pros: Long projects. Cons & risks: Very time-consuming (editing takes hours per finished hour). Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: African literature and Kiswahili audiobooks are a growing niche.

106. Podcast editing and production

What it is: Editing podcast episodes: removing mistakes and noise, balancing levels, adding music, intros and outros; sometimes full production. How it works: Receive raw audio → edit → master → deliver; optional show notes and publishing.

SkillsAudio editing, listening, workflow
Cost & equipmentLaptop, headphones, Audacity/Reaper/other software
Where to find workPodcasters, companies, churches, media houses
PaymentPer episode or monthly
Realistic earningsLow to medium (monthly clients)
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Edit sample episodes (with permission) and show before/after clips. Pros: Recurring weekly work. Cons & risks: Deadlines every week. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Kenyan podcasts and church sermon recordings need editing.

107. Audio cleanup and restoration

What it is: Removing noise, hum and clicks; improving clarity of recordings (interviews, old tapes, sermons, evidence for professionals). How it works: Use noise reduction and repair tools carefully.

SkillsAudio repair techniques
Cost & equipmentLaptop, headphones, audio software (some paid plugins help)
Where to find workPodcasters, families (old recordings), media, researchers
PaymentPer file
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Practise on noisy recordings; show before/after samples. Pros: Specialised niche. Cons & risks: Limited demand alone; combine with editing. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Digitising and cleaning old cassette recordings for families is a niche.

108. Music production and beat making

What it is: Producing instrumentals and songs for artists, adverts and content creators. How it works: Create beats in a digital audio workstation (DAW); sell or lease beats, or produce songs on commission.

SkillsMusic production, arrangement, mixing basics
Cost & equipmentLaptop, DAW, headphones/monitors, MIDI keyboard
Where to find workLocal artists, beat marketplaces, content creators, ad agencies
PaymentPer beat, lease or production fee; royalties
Realistic earningsVery low to medium; highly variable
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Build a catalogue; share on social media; collaborate with local artists. Pros: Creative; global market. Cons & risks: Competitive; copyright knowledge essential. Scam warning: Fake "record label deals" with upfront fees. Kenya notes: Gengetone, Afrobeats and gospel production have local demand; register works with the relevant copyright and collecting bodies.

109. Mixing and mastering

What it is: Balancing recorded tracks (mixing) and preparing the final song for release (mastering). How it works: Receive stems → mix → master → revisions.

SkillsAdvanced audio engineering, trained ears
Cost & equipmentGood monitors/headphones, DAW and plugins
Where to find workArtists, studios, online marketplaces
PaymentPer song
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Mix free multitrack sessions available online for practice; build a before/after portfolio. Pros: Valued skill. Cons & risks: Needs good equipment and years of practice. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Independent Kenyan artists need affordable mixing.

110. Jingles and sound logos

What it is: Short music pieces for brands, radio ads, shows and events. How it works: Brief → composition → voice and music recording → mix.

SkillsComposition, production, sometimes singing
Cost & equipmentDAW, microphone
Where to find workBusinesses, radio stations, ad agencies, churches, events
PaymentPer jingle (plus usage rights)
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Create sample jingles for imaginary brands. Pros: Short projects. Cons & risks: Clear agreements on rights needed. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Radio advertising remains strong in Kenya; local-language jingles are popular.

111. Sound design and sound effects

What it is: Creating sound effects and soundscapes for games, films, apps and adverts, or selling sound libraries. How it works: Record and design sounds; deliver to projects or sell on audio marketplaces.

SkillsSound design, recording
Cost & equipmentRecorder or microphone, DAW
Where to find workGame developers, filmmakers, audio marketplaces
PaymentPer project or per sale
Realistic earningsVery low to low–medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Build a library of original sounds (nature, city, Kenyan environments). Pros: Sell libraries repeatedly. Cons & risks: Niche. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Authentic African ambience sounds are a distinctive offering.

112. Singing and session vocals

What it is: Recording vocals for songs, adverts, jingles and backing tracks. How it works: Remote recording from home studios or studio sessions.

SkillsSinging, harmony, recording technique
Cost & equipmentMicrophone and quiet space for remote work
Where to find workProducers, artists, ad agencies, online session marketplaces
PaymentPer song or session
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Record a demo of different styles; network with producers. Pros: Creative. Cons & risks: Irregular. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Churches, studios and ad agencies hire session vocalists.

113. Music lessons online and in person

What it is: Teaching instruments, singing or music production. How it works: One-on-one or group lessons, in person or via video calls.

SkillsMusical skill, teaching patience
Cost & equipmentInstrument, phone/laptop for online lessons
Where to find workFamilies, schools, churches, tutoring platforms, social media
PaymentPer lesson or monthly
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays to weeks

How to start: Offer trial lessons; teach beginner packages. Pros: Recurring students. Cons & risks: Scheduling. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Piano, guitar, keyboard and vocal lessons are popular for church musicians and children.

114. Radio and podcast hosting (freelance)

What it is: Hosting shows, MCing recorded segments, or producing sponsored podcast content. How it works: Freelance presenting, guest hosting, or building your own podcast with sponsorships.

SkillsPresenting, interviewing, voice
Cost & equipmentMicrophone, recording setup for podcasts
Where to find workRadio stations, online radio, brands, your own show
PaymentPer show, sponsorship, advertising
Realistic earningsVery low to medium; building an audience takes time
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start a niche podcast; network with stations; create a presenting reel. Pros: Builds a personal brand. Cons & risks: Slow to monetise. Scam warning: "Pay to get airtime as a host" offers. Kenya notes: Community and online radio stations offer entry points.

115. IVR and phone system recordings

What it is: Recording phone menu prompts ("Press 1 for…"), hold messages and announcements for businesses. How it works: Short scripts recorded in clean, neutral voice; delivered in required audio formats.

SkillsClear voice, consistency
Cost & equipmentMicrophone, quiet space
Where to find workBusinesses, call centres, telecom service providers, voice marketplaces
PaymentPer project
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Include IVR samples in your voice demo. Pros: Quick jobs. Cons & risks: Small projects. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Bilingual English/Kiswahili prompts are common requests.

Summary

  • Audio methods include general, research, medical and legal transcription; captions and transcript editing; voice-over, audiobooks and IVR; podcast editing and audio cleanup; music production, mixing, jingles, sound design, session vocals, teaching and hosting.
  • Local languages and good equipment create advantages; AI reduces simple transcription work, so add skills.
  • Beware fees for "certification", software or label deals.
