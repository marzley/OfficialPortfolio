Interior finishing and home specialty services

New buildings, renovations and rentals all need finishing work beyond basic masonry, tiling and painting (methods 348–351). Each specialty below can be a focused business with good margins.

What these methods share

  • Portfolio: before-and-after photos win most jobs.
  • Clear quotes: measure carefully, quote materials and labour separately, take a deposit for materials.
  • Safety: chemicals, dust and heights need protective gear and training.

794. Gypsum ceilings and partitions

What it is: Installing gypsum ceilings, bulkheads and partition walls, including lighting cut-outs. How it works: Measure → design → frame → board → finish.

SkillsGypsum work, measuring, finishing
Cost & equipmentTools, scaffolding access
Where to find workNew homes, offices, shops, churches
PaymentPer square metre or per project
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Work under an experienced fundi; post photos of finished ceilings. Pros: High demand in new builds. Cons & risks: Dusty; competitive pricing. Scam warning: Take deposits only for materials, with receipts. Kenya notes: Gypsum ceilings are standard in new Kenyan homes and offices.

795. Wallpaper and 3D wall panel installation

What it is: Supplying and fitting wallpaper, wall panels and feature walls. How it works: Consult → measure → order → install.

SkillsMeasuring, fitting, finishing
Cost & equipmentTools, sample books
Where to find workHomes, salons, restaurants, offices
PaymentPer roll or square metre
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with wallpaper shops as their installer; build a portfolio. Pros: Quick jobs, visible results. Cons & risks: Damp walls ruin work. Scam warning: Check walls for damp before installing. Kenya notes: Feature walls are popular in Kenyan living rooms, salons and restaurants.

796. Epoxy and resin flooring

What it is: Laying seamless epoxy floors for garages, shops, clinics, kitchens and showrooms. How it works: Prepare surface → prime → pour epoxy → cure.

SkillsSurface preparation, mixing, application
Cost & equipmentGrinder, materials, protective gear
Where to find workShowrooms, clinics, garages, factories, homes
PaymentPer square metre
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train with a supplier or contractor; start with small floors. Pros: Premium niche. Cons & risks: Chemicals need ventilation and gear; mistakes are costly. Scam warning: Use genuine materials; cheap resin peels. Kenya notes: Clinics, food businesses and showrooms value easy-to-clean floors.

797. Wooden floor installation and sanding

What it is: Installing hardwood, laminate and vinyl floors, and sanding and varnishing old wooden floors. How it works: Measure → supply → install or sand → finish.

SkillsCarpentry, sanding, finishing
Cost & equipmentSanding machine (or hire), tools
Where to find workHomes, offices, schools, halls
PaymentPer square metre
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Start with laminate and vinyl; learn sanding under an expert. Pros: Restoring old floors pays well. Cons & risks: Dust; heavy equipment. Scam warning: As usual: deposits only for materials. Kenya notes: Many older Nairobi houses have parquet floors that need restoring.

798. Fitted kitchen cabinets and wardrobes

What it is: Designing, making and fitting kitchens, wardrobes and TV units. How it works: Measure → design → make or order boards → assemble and fit.

SkillsCarpentry, design, measuring
Cost & equipmentWorkshop tools, board cutting service
Where to find workNew homes, apartments, renovations, developers
PaymentPer project
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Use design software to show clients; partner with board suppliers and developers. Pros: Big tickets; referrals. Cons & risks: Cash flow for materials; delays. Scam warning: Put timelines and payment stages in writing. Kenya notes: Developers of apartments order cabinets in bulk.

799. Home staging for sale and rent

What it is: Styling empty or cluttered homes with furniture and décor so they rent or sell faster and photograph well. How it works: Assess → style with own or hired items → photos → remove after sale or rent.

SkillsInterior styling, photography basics
Cost & equipmentDécor and furniture stock or rental partners
Where to find workLandlords, agents, developers, short-stay hosts
PaymentPer project or monthly hire
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Stage your own space or a friend's rental; partner with agents. Pros: Low start-up if you hire items. Cons & risks: Items get damaged; irregular work. Scam warning: Get signed agreements for items left in homes. Kenya notes: Agents and short-stay hosts need good listing photos.

800. Professional home organising and decluttering

What it is: Helping people sort, declutter and organise homes, wardrobes, kitchens and offices. How it works: Consult → sort → donate, sell or discard → organise with storage.

SkillsOrganising, patience, discretion
Cost & equipmentLow: labels, bins (client buys storage)
Where to find workBusy professionals, families moving house, offices
PaymentPer session or project
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with friends; post before-and-after photos (with permission). Pros: Low cost. Cons & risks: Physical work; trust is essential. Scam warning: Never take items without clear permission. Kenya notes: Pairs well with moving services (method 302) and house cleaning (303).

801. Window film and tinting for buildings

What it is: Fitting heat-control, privacy, frosted and safety film on office and home windows. How it works: Measure → choose film → clean and apply.

SkillsFilm application, measuring
Cost & equipmentTools, film stock
Where to find workOffices, homes, shops, schools
PaymentPer square metre
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Practise on small windows; partner with glass and aluminium fabricators. Pros: Quick jobs. Cons & risks: Bubbles and poor finish ruin reputation. Scam warning: Use quality film; cheap film fades. Kenya notes: Hot towns like Mombasa, Kisumu and Garissa value heat-control film.

802. Water tank cleaning and disinfection

What it is: Emptying, scrubbing and disinfecting water tanks for homes, schools, hotels and estates. How it works: Schedule → drain → clean → disinfect → refill and record.

SkillsCleaning, safe chemical use
Cost & equipmentPumps, brushes, protective gear, safe disinfectants
Where to find workHomes, schools, apartments, hotels, hospitals
PaymentPer tank or contract
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn safe disinfection practice; offer schools and estates regular contracts. Pros: Recurring work. Cons & risks: Confined spaces are dangerous: never enter a tank alone. Scam warning: Don't use unsafe chemicals; rinse properly. Kenya notes: Tanks are everywhere in Kenya and regular cleaning protects health.

803. Solar panel cleaning

What it is: Cleaning solar panels on homes, farms, schools and commercial roofs so they produce more power. How it works: Schedule → safe roof access → clean with proper equipment → report.

SkillsSafe roof work, cleaning methods
Cost & equipmentSoft brushes, water system, safety harness
Where to find workHomes, schools, farms, businesses with solar
PaymentPer panel or contract
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with solar installers who want to offer maintenance. Pros: Growing with solar adoption. Cons & risks: Roof safety risks. Scam warning: Don't use harsh tools that scratch panels. Kenya notes: Dust in dry areas reduces solar output, so regular cleaning matters.

804. Rope access and high-rise window cleaning

What it is: Cleaning and maintaining tall buildings' windows and facades using certified rope access. How it works: Survey → rig → clean or repair → inspect.

SkillsCertified rope access (e.g. IRATA), cleaning
Cost & equipmentCertification, harnesses, ropes (often employer-provided)
Where to find workOffice towers, hotels, apartments
PaymentDaily rate or contract
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get certified rope access training; join a facade maintenance company. Pros: Specialist, well-paid niche. Cons & risks: Very dangerous without training. Scam warning: Never work at height without certification and a rescue plan. Kenya notes: Nairobi, Mombasa and other towns have more high-rises needing facade maintenance.

805. Waterproofing and damp-proofing

What it is: Stopping leaks and damp in roofs, basements, bathrooms, water tanks and walls. How it works: Inspect → find cause → apply membranes or coatings → warranty.

SkillsWaterproofing systems, diagnosis
Cost & equipmentTools, materials
Where to find workHomes, apartments, developers, schools
PaymentPer square metre or project
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train with a materials supplier; start with bathrooms and flat roofs. Pros: Problem-solving work with referrals. Cons & risks: Callbacks if the cause wasn't fixed. Scam warning: Fix the real cause, not just the stain. Kenya notes: Flat roofs and long rains make waterproofing a common need.

Summary

  • Interior specialties include gypsum ceilings, wallpaper and wall panels, epoxy and wooden floors, fitted kitchens and wardrobes, home staging, organising, window film, tank and solar panel cleaning, rope access and waterproofing.
  • Before-and-after photos, careful measuring and clear quotes win work.
  • Chemicals, confined spaces and heights need training and protective gear.
