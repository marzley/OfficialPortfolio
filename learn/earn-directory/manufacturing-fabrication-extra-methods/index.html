More manufacturing and fabrication

Adding to manufacturing businesses (methods 616–632), these fabrication businesses serve farmers, builders and households.

What these methods share

  • Quality: strong, durable products win repeat orders.
  • Standards: KEBS standards apply to many products.
  • Safety: welding and machinery need protective gear.

972. Chain-link fencing production

What it is: Making chain-link fencing and barbed wire products. How it works: Wire → weave on machine → roll and sell.

SkillsMachine operation
Cost & equipmentFencing machine, wire stock
Where to find workFarmers, homeowners, schools, hardware shops
PaymentPer roll
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with one machine; sell to hardware shops and farmers. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Wire price changes. Scam warning: Use the wire gauge you advertise. Kenya notes: Land fencing is common across Kenya.

973. Concrete posts and culverts

What it is: Casting fencing posts, culverts and ventilation blocks. How it works: Moulds → mix and cast → cure → sell.

SkillsConcrete work
Cost & equipmentMoulds, materials, curing space
Where to find workFarmers, counties, contractors
PaymentPer piece
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Cure products properly for strength. Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Heavy transport. Scam warning: Don't sell weak, uncured products. Kenya notes: Counties and contractors buy culverts for roads.

974. Metal jiko (charcoal stove) fabrication

What it is: Making improved charcoal stoves (jikos) from metal and ceramic liners. How it works: Cut metal → shape → fit liners → sell.

SkillsMetalwork
Cost & equipmentTools, metal sheets, liners
Where to find workHouseholds, markets, hardware shops
PaymentPer jiko
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Make efficient designs that save charcoal. Pros: Daily household need. Cons & risks: Low prices. Scam warning: Don't claim efficiency you can't show. Kenya notes: Improved jikos cut charcoal use and smoke.

975. Ferro-cement water tanks

What it is: Building water tanks from cement and wire mesh on site. How it works: Build mesh frame → plaster → cure → test.

SkillsMasonry, ferro-cement technique
Cost & equipmentMaterials, tools
Where to find workHomes, schools, farms
PaymentPer tank
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Train with a water NGO or experienced mason. Pros: Cheaper than plastic for big tanks. Cons & risks: Skill needed to avoid cracks. Scam warning: Cure properly and test for leaks. Kenya notes: Rainwater harvesting programmes use ferro-cement tanks.

976. Poultry and livestock equipment fabrication

What it is: Making feeders, drinkers, cages, incubator frames and pig pens. How it works: Design → fabricate → sell.

SkillsFabrication
Cost & equipmentWorkshop tools
Where to find workPoultry and livestock farmers, agrovets
PaymentPer item
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Sell through agrovets and farmer groups. Pros: Growing farming market. Cons & risks: Competition from imports. Scam warning: Use safe, non-toxic materials. Kenya notes: Poultry farming growth drives demand.

977. Tent and canvas making and repair

What it is: Making and repairing tents, tarpaulins, truck covers and awnings. How it works: Measure → cut canvas → stitch → fit.

SkillsHeavy-duty sewing
Cost & equipmentIndustrial sewing machine, canvas
Where to find workEvent tent owners, truckers, shops, campsites
PaymentPer item or repair
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with repairs for tent hire businesses. Pros: Repeat repair work. Cons & risks: Bulky materials. Scam warning: Quote materials and labour clearly. Kenya notes: Event tent hire (method 317) depends on repairs.

Summary

  • Fabrication businesses include fencing, concrete products, jikos, ferro-cement tanks, livestock equipment and tents.
  • Quality, standards and safety matter.
  • Honest specifications build trust.
