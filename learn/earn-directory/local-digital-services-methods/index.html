Local digital services

Not all digital income is "online". Across Kenya, people earn by bringing digital services to their neighbourhood: printing and typing, online applications, phone repairs, M-Pesa agencies, photo studios, Wi-Fi hotspots and more. These businesses often need a location, some equipment and licences, but serve steady local demand.

What these methods share

  • Location and trust: a visible, accessible spot and a reputation for honest service.
  • Licences and permits: county business permits and, for some services, specific licences (e.g. M-Pesa agent requirements, CA licensing for some telecom services). Check what applies.
  • Customer data: handle IDs, documents and passwords responsibly; never keep customers' passwords.
  • See local services online and online government services.

246. Cyber café / business centre

What it is: A shop offering internet access, printing, photocopying, scanning, typing, lamination and online applications. How it works: Walk-in customers pay per service.

SkillsComputer skills, customer service, knowledge of online services
Cost & equipmentComputers, printer/copier, scanner, laminator, internet, premises, permits
Where to find workWalk-in customers near schools, offices, markets
PaymentCash/M-Pesa per service
Realistic earningsLow to medium (location matters)
Time to first incomeImmediately once open; setup takes weeks

How to start: Research the location; start with essential equipment; add services over time. Pros: Steady daily demand. Cons & risks: Equipment maintenance; competition. Scam warning: Don't help customers with fraudulent applications or fake documents. Kenya notes: KUCCPS, HELB, eCitizen, KRA and job applications drive traffic seasonally.

247. Printing, photocopying and binding services

What it is: Printing documents, photos, posters, booklets; binding and laminating. How it works: Equipment-based service, often part of a cyber or stationery shop.

SkillsEquipment operation, basic design
Cost & equipmentPrinters/copiers, binding machines, consumables
Where to find workStudents, offices, churches, events
PaymentPer page/job
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeImmediate (after setup)

How to start: Near schools and offices; bulk-print deals for churches and schools. Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Toner and maintenance costs. Scam warning: Never print fake certificates, IDs or currency. Kenya notes: Exam seasons and funerals create printing peaks.

248. Online application and form-filling services

What it is: Helping people complete online applications: KUCCPS, HELB, KRA, eCitizen, job portals, NHIF/SHA registrations. How it works: Customer brings documents → you guide or complete with them → print confirmations.

SkillsPortal knowledge (e-services subject), accuracy
Cost & equipmentComputer, printer, internet
Where to find workWalk-ins, schools, communities
PaymentService fee + official fees paid by customer
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Master common portals; post a clear price list showing official fees separately. Pros: Constant need. Cons & risks: Errors affect people's lives; be careful. Scam warning: Never claim you can "guarantee" placements, loans or approvals. Kenya notes: Peak times: KUCCPS application windows, HELB, KRA June deadline.

250. Phone repair and accessories shop

What it is: Repairing phones (screens, batteries, charging ports, software) and selling accessories. How it works: Diagnosis → quote → repair → warranty.

SkillsElectronics repair, software flashing, honesty
Cost & equipmentTools, spare parts, premises
Where to find workWalk-ins, referrals
PaymentPer repair
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks (training)

How to start: Train with an experienced technician; start with common repairs. Pros: Huge demand. Cons & risks: Fake parts; customer data on phones. Scam warning: Never unlock stolen phones or change IMEIs; it's illegal. Kenya notes: Respect customers' privacy; never browse their data.

252. M-Pesa and mobile money agent

What it is: Running an agent outlet for deposits, withdrawals and other mobile money services. How it works: Apply through the operator's agent process (often via a dealer), meet requirements, maintain float, earn commissions.

SkillsCash handling, accuracy, security awareness
Cost & equipmentFloat capital, premises, permits, documents required by the operator
Where to find workYour outlet's customers
PaymentCommissions from the operator
Realistic earningsLow to medium (location and float matter)
Time to first incomeWeeks to months (application)

How to start: Apply through official channels; learn fraud prevention. Pros: Steady transactions. Cons & risks: Fraud and robbery risks; float management. Scam warning: Fraudsters impersonate customer care to trick agents into reversing transactions; follow official procedures only. Kenya notes: Follow Safaricom's and other operators' official agent requirements and anti-fraud rules.

253. Airtime, data and utility payments reselling

What it is: Selling airtime, data bundles and paying bills (electricity tokens, water, TV) for customers. How it works: Dealer/reseller accounts; small commissions per transaction.

SkillsAccuracy, customer service
Cost & equipmentFloat, phone or POS
Where to find workWalk-ins, neighbourhood
PaymentCommission
Realistic earningsVery low to low (volume business)
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Sign up with legitimate reseller programmes. Pros: Simple add-on to a shop. Cons & risks: Thin margins. Scam warning: Unofficial "cheap bundle" schemes. Kenya notes: A useful add-on for cyber cafés and shops.

What it is: Selling internet access by time or data to neighbours, estates or public spaces using hotspot billing. How it works: Internet line → routers/access points → hotspot billing system with M-Pesa → customers buy vouchers.

SkillsNetworking, customer support
Cost & equipmentInternet connection, routers, billing system, power backup
Where to find workEstates, hostels, markets
PaymentVoucher sales via M-Pesa
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Understand the internet provider's terms and Communications Authority of Kenya licensing requirements before reselling internet. Pros: Recurring income. Cons & risks: Licensing, power outages, support. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: See the Wi-Fi hotspot billing guide; check CA licensing for internet resale.
