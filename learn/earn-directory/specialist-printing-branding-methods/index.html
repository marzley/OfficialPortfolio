Specialist printing and branding

Every business, school, church and event needs branded items: banners, uniforms, stamps, signs, ID cards and packaging. Beyond a general print shop (method 260), each specialist printing skill can be a business of its own.

What these methods share

  • Quality and deadlines: clients come back when work is sharp and on time.
  • Deposits: take a deposit before printing custom orders.
  • Rights: don't print trademarks, club logos or designs you don't have permission to use.

771. Screen printing

What it is: Printing designs on T-shirts, bags and fabrics with screens. How it works: Design → screen → print → cure.

SkillsScreen printing
Cost & equipmentScreens, inks, press, drying
Where to find workSchools, events, companies, churches
PaymentPer item
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn from a printer; start with simple one-colour designs. Pros: Cheap for bulk orders. Cons & risks: Messy; setup per design. Scam warning: Deposits for bulk orders. Kenya notes: Election, school and event seasons bring bulk orders.

772. Embroidery

What it is: Stitching logos and names on uniforms, caps and bags. How it works: Digitise design → machine embroidery.

SkillsMachine operation, digitising
Cost & equipmentEmbroidery machine, software
Where to find workSchools, companies, hotels, churches
PaymentPer item
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with a single-head machine; target school uniforms. Pros: Premium finish. Cons & risks: Machine cost. Scam warning: As usual. Kenya notes: School uniforms and corporate wear need embroidery.

773. Large-format printing (banners, roll-ups)

What it is: Printing banners, posters, roll-up banners and backdrops. How it works: Design → print → finish.

SkillsDesign, machine operation
Cost & equipmentLarge-format printer, finishing tools
Where to find workBusinesses, events, churches, politicians
PaymentPer square metre
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start by outsourcing printing; buy a machine when volume grows. Pros: High demand. Cons & risks: Expensive machines and ink. Scam warning: Check designs for spelling before printing. Kenya notes: Events and campaigns create peaks.

774. DTF (direct-to-film) garment printing

What it is: Printing full-colour designs onto film, then heat-pressing them onto T-shirts, hoodies, caps and bags of almost any fabric. How it works: Design → print on DTF film → powder and cure → heat press onto the garment.

SkillsDesign, machine operation
Cost & equipmentDTF printer and curing unit (or buy printed transfers from a DTF supplier), heat press
Where to find workSchools, events, churches, brands, individuals
PaymentPer item
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start by ordering printed DTF transfers and pressing them yourself; buy a printer when orders grow. Pros: Works on cotton and blends; no minimum order. Cons & risks: Printer maintenance and ink costs. Scam warning: Don't print brand logos or copyrighted designs without permission. Kenya notes: DTF suits small custom orders that screen printing can't do cheaply.

775. Sublimation printing (mugs, jerseys, gifts)

What it is: Printing full-colour designs on mugs, jerseys, cushions and gifts. How it works: Design → sublimation print → heat press.

SkillsDesign, heat press
Cost & equipmentSublimation printer, heat presses, blanks
Where to find workGift shops, events, corporates, individuals
PaymentPer item
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with mugs and expand to jerseys. Pros: Low setup cost. Cons & risks: Works only on polyester or coated items. Scam warning: As usual. Kenya notes: Personalised gifts sell well around holidays.

776. Laser engraving and cutting

What it is: Engraving and cutting wood, acrylic and leather for signs, awards and gifts. How it works: Design → laser → finish.

SkillsDesign software
Cost & equipmentLaser machine, ventilation
Where to find workBusinesses, schools, event planners, gift shops
PaymentPer item
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with awards and signs; show samples. Pros: Premium products. Cons & risks: Machine cost; fumes need ventilation. Scam warning: Use safety goggles and ventilation. Kenya notes: Acrylic signs and wooden gifts are popular.

777. Rubber stamps and seals

What it is: Making company stamps, seals and self-inking stamps. How it works: Order → design → produce → deliver.

SkillsDesign, production
Cost & equipmentStamp-making equipment
Where to find workBusinesses, schools, churches, SACCOs
PaymentPer stamp
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn stamp making; verify customers' documents. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Misuse risk. Scam warning: Ask for registration documents before making official company or government stamps; never make stamps for impersonation. Kenya notes: Every registered business needs stamps.

778. Signage fabrication (3D letters, lightboxes)

What it is: Making shop signs, 3D letters and lit signs. How it works: Design → fabricate → install.

SkillsFabrication, electrics
Cost & equipmentWorkshop, materials
Where to find workShops, offices, churches, schools
PaymentPer sign
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Work with a signage shop first. Pros: High-value jobs. Cons & risks: Installation at heights. Scam warning: County signage permits may be required. Kenya notes: Check county outdoor advertising rules for big signs.

779. ID cards and lanyards

What it is: Printing staff, student and event ID cards and lanyards. How it works: Collect details and photos → print → deliver.

SkillsDesign, data handling
Cost & equipmentCard printer, lanyard supplier
Where to find workSchools, companies, events
PaymentPer card
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with schools and events. Pros: Repeat yearly orders. Cons & risks: Data privacy. Scam warning: Protect personal data and photos. Kenya notes: Schools reprint student IDs every year.

780. Packaging printing (boxes, labels, bags)

What it is: Printing branded packaging for products. How it works: Design → print → cut → deliver.

SkillsDesign, print management
Cost & equipmentPrinter or print partners
Where to find workFood processors, cosmetics makers, shops
PaymentPer unit or batch
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with packaging printers; offer design. Pros: Recurring orders. Cons & risks: Minimum quantities. Scam warning: Ensure labels include required information (KEBS rules for food). Kenya notes: Small manufacturers need affordable branded packaging.

781. Corporate gifts and promotional items

What it is: Supplying branded pens, diaries, bags, umbrellas and gifts. How it works: Source → brand → deliver.

SkillsSales, sourcing
Cost & equipmentSupplier network
Where to find workCompanies, NGOs, banks
PaymentPer order
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Build a catalogue; target end-year orders. Pros: Large orders. Cons & risks: Payment delays. Scam warning: Get purchase orders and deposits. Kenya notes: End-year and conference seasons are busiest.

Summary

  • Specialist printing includes screen printing, embroidery, large-format, DTF garment printing, sublimation, laser engraving, stamps, signage, ID cards, packaging and corporate gifts.
  • Quality, deadlines and deposits matter.
  • Respect trademarks, data privacy and rules (county signage, KEBS labels).
