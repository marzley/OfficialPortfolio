More food and hospitality businesses

Adding to the food businesses in methods 446–462, these serve events, offices and other food businesses.

What these methods share

  • Food safety: food handler medical certificates and county public health licences.
  • Consistency: the same quality every time builds loyalty.
  • Costing: price for ingredients, labour, transport and waste.

917. Mobile bar service

What it is: Providing bartenders, drinks setup and cocktails at weddings, parties and corporate events. How it works: Book → set up bar → serve → clear.

SkillsBartending, customer service
Cost & equipmentBar equipment, licences where needed
Where to find workEvents, weddings, corporate parties
PaymentPer event
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Train in bartending; partner with event planners. Pros: Good event margins. Cons & risks: Alcohol licensing rules. Scam warning: Never serve minors; follow licensing for selling alcohol. Kenya notes: Weddings and corporate events often hire mobile bars.

918. Popcorn and candy floss vending

What it is: Selling popcorn and candy floss at events, schools and malls. How it works: Machine → set up at events → sell per pack.

SkillsFood hygiene, selling
Cost & equipmentMachines, ingredients, packaging
Where to find workEvents, schools, malls, parks
PaymentPer pack
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Start with one machine at kids' parties and events. Pros: Cheap to start. Cons & risks: Seasonal and event-dependent. Scam warning: Keep machines clean. Kenya notes: Kids' parties and fun days create demand.

919. Vending machines

What it is: Placing snack and drink vending machines in offices, campuses and hospitals. How it works: Buy machines → place with host → restock → collect cash or M-Pesa.

SkillsLogistics, maintenance
Cost & equipmentMachines, stock
Where to find workOffices, hospitals, campuses, gyms
PaymentPer sale
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Negotiate placement deals; use cashless payments. Pros: Passive-ish income. Cons & risks: Machine costs; vandalism. Scam warning: Buy from suppliers who provide maintenance. Kenya notes: Cashless M-Pesa payments make vending practical.

920. Staff canteen operator

What it is: Running canteens for factories, offices and schools. How it works: Win a contract → cook and serve daily meals.

SkillsCatering, management
Cost & equipmentKitchen equipment (often provided), staff
Where to find workFactories, offices, colleges, hospitals
PaymentPer meal or contract
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with a small office canteen; tender for bigger contracts. Pros: Steady daily volumes. Cons & risks: Thin margins; strict hygiene. Scam warning: Get contracts in writing with payment terms. Kenya notes: Many factories and offices outsource canteens.

921. Food safety and hygiene trainer

What it is: Training food handlers and businesses in hygiene, safe storage and food safety systems. How it works: Workshops → audits → certificates of attendance.

SkillsFood science, training
Cost & equipmentLow: training materials
Where to find workRestaurants, hotels, schools, food processors
PaymentPer session or audit
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get recognised food safety qualifications; partner with public health officers. Pros: Helps businesses pass inspections. Cons & risks: Must stay current with standards. Scam warning: Don't issue government certificates you aren't authorised to. Kenya notes: County inspections push businesses to train staff.

922. Shared commercial kitchen

What it is: Renting a licensed kitchen by the hour or month to small food businesses. How it works: Set up a licensed kitchen → rent time slots → provide storage.

SkillsManagement, hygiene
Cost & equipmentHigh: kitchen equipment, licences
Where to find workHome bakers, caterers, meal prep businesses
PaymentHourly or monthly rent
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Fit out a kitchen that meets public health standards; market to home-based food sellers. Pros: Helps many small businesses. Cons & risks: High setup costs. Scam warning: Clear rules on cleaning and storage. Kenya notes: Differs from a cloud kitchen (method 446): you rent the kitchen to others.

923. Chapati and mandazi wholesale production

What it is: Making chapatis, mandazis and samosas in bulk for kiosks, schools and offices. How it works: Early-morning production → deliver to buyers.

SkillsCooking, hygiene, logistics
Cost & equipmentKitchen, fryers, packaging
Where to find workKiosks, offices, schools, canteens
PaymentPer piece or dozen
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Start with a few kiosks; deliver on time every day. Pros: Daily orders. Cons & risks: Early hours; cooking gas costs. Scam warning: Use fresh oil and good hygiene. Kenya notes: Mandazi and chapati are breakfast staples.

Summary

  • More food businesses include mobile bars, popcorn and candy floss, vending machines, staff canteens, food safety training, shared kitchens and wholesale chapati and mandazi.
  • Public health licences and food handler certificates apply.
  • Cost carefully and stay consistent.
