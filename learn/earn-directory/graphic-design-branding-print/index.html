Graphic design (part 1): branding, print and event design

Every business, church, school, family event and political campaign in Kenya needs designs: logos, posters, funeral programmes, wedding invitations, menus, banners, labels. Graphic design is one of the most accessible earning skills: you can start with Canva on a phone and grow into professional tools and higher-paying brand work.

This lesson covers branding and print design. Part 2 covers digital products, social media, illustration and specialist design.

What all design methods share

  • Core skills: layout, typography, colour, hierarchy, and listening to clients (design basics, colour and typography).
  • Tools: Canva (free/Pro), Adobe Illustrator/Photoshop/InDesign (paid), Affinity apps (one-time purchase), free alternatives (Inkscape, GIMP, Photopea, Figma free plan).
  • Portfolio first: clients buy what they can see (design portfolio and clients).
  • Legal use of assets: only use fonts, photos and icons you have the right to use; never sell designs built from stolen images or copied logos.
  • Payment: M-Pesa from local clients (take a deposit), PayPal/Payoneer from international platforms.

See also: graphics and design earning overview and graphic design with Canva.

34. Logo design

What it is: Creating a business's main symbol or wordmark: simple, memorable, and usable in many sizes and colours. How it works: Brief (business, audience, values, competitors) → research → sketches → 2–3 concepts → revisions → final files (vector, PNG, colour and black/white versions).

SkillsConcept development, simplicity, typography, vector drawing
Cost & equipmentLaptop; Illustrator, Affinity Designer or Inkscape (free) for vector files
Where to find workLocal businesses, start-ups, churches, schools, freelance marketplaces, design contests
PaymentFixed project fee; deposit for local clients
Realistic earningsLow to medium; very competitive at the cheap end, better with strategy and branding packages
Time to first incomeWeeks (portfolio first)

How to start: Create 5–10 concept logos for realistic businesses (logos and branding); learn to deliver proper vector files. Pros: High demand. Cons & risks: Price undercutting; clients wanting "just a quick logo" for very little. Scam warning: Never copy existing logos or use AI-generated images that imitate brands; legal and reputation risk. Kenya notes: Many SMEs, chamas, SACCOs and churches need first logos; sell logo + basic brand kit packages.

35. Brand identity kits

What it is: A complete visual identity: logo variations, colour palette, fonts, patterns, and a brand guideline document showing correct use. How it works: Discovery session → moodboard → logo and system → applications (business card, social post, letterhead) → guideline PDF.

SkillsBranding strategy, consistency, presentation
Cost & equipmentLaptop, design software
Where to find workGrowing SMEs, start-ups, NGOs, schools, agencies (subcontracting)
PaymentProject fee in milestones
Realistic earningsMedium; more valuable than logos alone
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Build 2–3 full concept brand kits; present them as case studies. Pros: Higher value; leads to ongoing design work. Cons & risks: Longer projects; needs good client communication. Scam warning: Clients requesting "free concepts" from many designers before choosing. Kenya notes: Start-ups and established SMEs rebranding are good targets.

36. Business cards, letterheads and stationery

What it is: Designing business cards, letterheads, envelopes, email signatures, receipt books and invoice templates that match a brand. How it works: Use brand assets; prepare print-ready files with correct size, bleed and resolution (print production).

SkillsLayout, typography, print preparation
Cost & equipmentLaptop or phone (Canva); software for print files
Where to find workLocal businesses, printers, professionals (lawyers, doctors, consultants)
PaymentSmall fixed fees; often bundled with printing
Realistic earningsLow; good as add-ons
Time to first incomeDays to weeks

How to start: Partner with a local printer; offer design + print packages. Pros: Quick jobs; steady local demand. Cons & risks: Low prices; print errors can be costly (always send proofs). Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Cyber cafés and printers in every town need designers for walk-in customers.

37. Flyers and posters

What it is: Promotional flyers and posters for businesses, events, sales, schools, churches and campaigns. How it works: Get text and photos → design for the medium (print or WhatsApp/social) → proof → deliver print-ready and digital versions.

SkillsHierarchy (what's read first), bold typography, photo editing
Cost & equipmentPhone or laptop (Canva works well)
Where to find workLocal businesses, churches, schools, event organisers, WhatsApp marketing
PaymentPer design, M-Pesa
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium with volume and regular clients
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Design 10 sample posters for different industries (posters and flyers); post them on WhatsApp status and Facebook. Pros: Fast to learn; very high local demand. Cons & risks: Tight deadlines; clients may send poor photos. Scam warning: Clients who disappear after receiving unwatermarked files: send watermarked previews until paid. Kenya notes: Weekend events, church crusades, sales and school functions create constant demand.

38. Event branding (banners, roll-ups, backdrops, tickets)

What it is: Designing a coordinated set for an event: roll-up banners, stage backdrops, wall branding, tickets, badges, signage and social media graphics. How it works: Requires exact print dimensions from the printer, high-resolution images and consistent branding across items.

SkillsLarge-format print knowledge, consistency, deadlines
Cost & equipmentLaptop with professional or capable design software
Where to find workEvent planners, corporates, NGOs, churches, schools, printers
PaymentPackage fee per event
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium per event
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn large-format specs (resolution, bleed, safe zones); partner with printers and event planners. Pros: Bigger projects; repeat corporate clients. Cons & risks: Last-minute changes; print mistakes are expensive. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Conferences, graduations, product launches and corporate events need full branding packages.

39. Funeral programmes, eulogies and memorial posters

What it is: Designing funeral and memorial programmes (order of service, eulogy, tributes, photos), memorial posters and social media announcements. How it works: Sensitive, fast work: families provide photos and text, often within 1–3 days; you design, proofread carefully, and coordinate printing.

SkillsLayout of multi-page booklets, photo restoration and editing, careful proofreading, empathy
Cost & equipmentLaptop (or Canva on phone), printer partnerships
Where to find workFamilies, churches, funeral homes, printers, referrals
PaymentPer programme, often with printing
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Create respectful templates; partner with printers and churches; offer quick turnaround with clear proofs. Pros: Constant demand in Kenya. Cons & risks: Emotional context; very tight deadlines; errors in names or dates are hurtful, so always get written approval. Scam warning: Avoid overcharging grieving families; build trust and referrals. Kenya notes: Funeral programmes are a major local design market across the country.

40. Wedding invitations and wedding stationery

What it is: Invitations, save-the-dates, programmes, menus, seating charts, welcome signs and thank-you cards, printed or digital (WhatsApp invitations). How it works: Consultation on theme and colours → designs → proofs → print or digital delivery.

SkillsElegant typography, colour, attention to detail
Cost & equipmentLaptop or phone; printer partners
Where to find workCouples, wedding planners, churches, social media
PaymentPackage fees; deposits
Realistic earningsLow to medium (packages and peak seasons)
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Build template collections; show them on Instagram/Facebook; partner with planners. Pros: Creative; higher-value packages. Cons & risks: Many revisions; seasonal. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Ruracio, traditional ceremony and church wedding stationery are popular requests; digital WhatsApp invitations are common.

41. Church and event programmes, bulletins and orders of service

What it is: Weekly bulletins, service programmes, conference booklets and announcement slides for churches and organisations. How it works: Templates updated weekly; recurring clients.

SkillsTemplates, consistency, quick turnaround
Cost & equipmentLaptop/phone, Canva or InDesign
Where to find workChurches, mosques, conferences, associations
PaymentPer issue or monthly retainer
Realistic earningsLow; good as recurring income
Time to first incomeDays to weeks

How to start: Offer a branded template set and a monthly package to your own church or local institutions. Pros: Recurring work. Cons & risks: Weekly deadlines. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Many churches outsource weekly designs and social media graphics.

42. Menu design

What it is: Printed menus, wall menus, digital menus with QR codes, and promotional cards for restaurants, cafés, hotels and food vendors. How it works: Organise the menu logically, highlight profitable items, make it readable, deliver print and digital versions.

SkillsInformation layout, food photography editing, typography
Cost & equipmentLaptop/phone, design software
Where to find workRestaurants, hotels, cafés, food trucks, caterers
PaymentPer project; updates paid separately
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Redesign 3 real (anonymised) menus as concepts; approach local eateries. Pros: Visible work; updates bring repeat business. Cons & risks: Frequent price changes. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: QR menus and WhatsApp menus became common; offer both.

43. Packaging and label design

What it is: Designing product labels, boxes, pouches and bottles that attract buyers and meet labelling requirements. How it works: Get exact packaging dimensions (die-lines) from the printer/manufacturer; design front and back; include required information; print proofs.

SkillsBranding, print production, understanding labelling information
Cost & equipmentLaptop with vector software
Where to find workSmall manufacturers, farmers and agro-processors, cosmetics makers, juice and honey producers
PaymentProject fees
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Create concept labels for local products; learn about die-lines and finishes. Pros: Higher value; brands come back for new products. Cons & risks: Mistakes on large print runs are costly; clients must confirm regulatory text. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Small producers seeking supermarket listing need professional labels; clients are responsible for regulatory approvals and required details (e.g. standards marks, ingredients); you design what they confirm.

44. Signage and shopfront design

What it is: Designing shop signs, billboards, directional signs, office signage and wall branding. How it works: Measure or receive exact sizes; consider viewing distance and materials; work with sign makers.

SkillsLarge-format design, legibility at distance, vector work
Cost & equipmentLaptop, vector software
Where to find workBusinesses, sign makers, property managers, schools
PaymentPer design; often through sign makers
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with sign-making businesses; build a portfolio with mock-ups. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Must understand production limits. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: County signage and advertising regulations may apply; the business or sign maker handles permits.

45. Vehicle branding and wraps

What it is: Designing graphics for company vehicles, matatus, delivery motorbikes and food trucks. How it works: Use vehicle templates with exact measurements; design for curves and windows; coordinate with branding companies.

SkillsLarge-format and vector design, spatial thinking
Cost & equipmentLaptop, vector software, vehicle templates
Where to find workBranding companies, businesses with fleets, matatu owners
PaymentPer design
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Learn to apply designs to vehicle templates; approach branding companies. Pros: Eye-catching portfolio pieces. Cons & risks: Technical; production partner essential. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Matatu art culture is a distinctive Kenyan design niche; follow regulations on vehicle markings that owners must comply with.

46. T-shirt and merchandise design

What it is: Designs for T-shirts, hoodies, caps, mugs and tote bags for events, companies, churches, schools, campaigns or your own brand. How it works: Create designs suited to printing methods (screen print, heat transfer, DTF), limited colours where needed, correct file formats.

SkillsIllustration or typography, print method knowledge
Cost & equipmentLaptop/phone and design software
Where to find workEvent organisers, companies, schools, churches, merchandise printers, your own brand
PaymentPer design or per printed item (if you resell)
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium; more if you build a merch business
Time to first incomeDays to weeks

How to start: Partner with local printers; design event shirts; consider your own small brand. Pros: Fun; scalable with a brand. Cons & risks: Copyright issues with famous characters and logos. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Team-building, graduations, harambees and campaigns create demand for printed merchandise.

47. Calendar design

What it is: Wall and desk calendars for businesses, schools, churches and NGOs, often as year-end corporate gifts. How it works: Branding + photos + 12 monthly layouts + holidays; print-ready files.

SkillsMulti-page layout, consistency
Cost & equipmentLaptop, design software
Where to find workCorporates, SACCOs, schools, printers
PaymentProject fee
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium (seasonal)
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Prepare templates by September; approach printers and companies before December. Pros: Seasonal volume. Cons & risks: Very seasonal; check Kenyan public holidays carefully. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Include accurate Kenyan public holidays; confirm any newly gazetted ones.

48. Certificate design

What it is: Certificates for schools, training centres, competitions, churches and events, sometimes with serial numbers or QR verification. How it works: Template design + data merge (names) for bulk certificates.

SkillsFormal layout, mail merge or bulk-create features
Cost & equipmentLaptop; Canva bulk create, Word mail merge (mail merge)
Where to find workSchools, colleges, training providers, event organisers
PaymentPer template plus per batch
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Create template sets; learn mail merge for bulk names. Pros: Quick recurring work. Cons & risks: Spelling of names must be perfect. Scam warning: Never design fake academic or professional certificates; it's illegal. Kenya notes: Graduations and training programmes (including Ajira and Jitume trainings) need certificates.

49. School materials and ID cards

What it is: Student ID cards, report card templates, timetables, prospectuses, admission letters and school branding. How it works: Templates plus data; coordinate with ID printers.

SkillsTemplates, data merge, layout
Cost & equipmentLaptop, design software
Where to find workPrivate and public schools, colleges
PaymentPer project or term
Realistic earningsLow to low–medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Approach schools at the start of terms with a sample pack. Pros: Recurring by term. Cons & risks: Handles student data; protect it. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Follow the Data Protection Act when handling student photos and details.

50. Brochures and company profiles

What it is: Multi-page documents presenting a company's services, history, team and projects, printed or as PDFs for tenders and clients. How it works: Structure content → layout in InDesign/Affinity Publisher/Canva → images and infographics → print and digital versions.

SkillsMulti-page layout, typography, editing content, infographics
Cost & equipmentLaptop, layout software
Where to find workContractors, consultants, NGOs, SMEs bidding for tenders
PaymentProject fee
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Create a sample company profile for a fictional contractor; approach SMEs. Pros: Valuable to clients (tenders). Cons & risks: Clients may provide messy content; offer editing as an extra. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Contractors and suppliers preparing tender documents often need professional profiles.

51. Annual reports and NGO reports

What it is: Designing reports with charts, infographics, photos and long text for NGOs, companies, SACCOs and government agencies. How it works: Long-document layout with styles, data visualisation, accessibility, print and web PDFs.

SkillsAdvanced layout, data visualisation, attention to detail
Cost & equipmentLaptop, InDesign or similar
Where to find workNGOs, development agencies, SACCOs, corporates, consultancies
PaymentProject fee in milestones
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Redesign a public report section as a portfolio piece; learn chart design. Pros: Larger budgets; repeat annual clients. Cons & risks: Long projects with many reviewers. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Nairobi's many NGOs and development organisations produce regular reports.

52. Book cover design and interior layout (typesetting)

What it is: Designing book covers (front, spine, back) and laying out book interiors for printing or ebooks. How it works: Cover: concept, typography, imagery, exact spine width from the printer. Interior: styles, margins, page numbers, chapter openings.

SkillsTypography, layout software, print specifications
Cost & equipmentLaptop, design and layout software
Where to find workSelf-publishing authors, publishers, churches and schools publishing books
PaymentPer cover or per page
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Design covers for public-domain classics as practice; learn print-on-demand specifications. Pros: Growing self-publishing market. Cons & risks: Image licensing needs care. Scam warning: Don't use images without proper licences. Kenya notes: Kenyan authors, pastors and schools increasingly self-publish; local printers and online platforms both need files.

Summary

  • Branding and print design methods range from quick flyers and funeral programmes to brand identities, packaging, reports and book design.
  • Start with Canva and a portfolio; grow into professional tools, print knowledge and higher-value packages.
  • Take deposits, send watermarked previews, proofread carefully, and only use assets you have rights to.
