Beauty and fashion businesses

Beauty and fashion are powered by social media in Kenya: Instagram and TikTok portfolios, WhatsApp bookings and influencer marketing. These methods build on the creative skills in earlier categories (hair, makeup, tailoring) with business models that can grow.

What these methods share

  • Hygiene and safety: sterilised tools and safe, registered products.
  • Portfolio content: before/after photos and short videos are your shop window.
  • Bookings and deposits via WhatsApp or booking apps.

463. Nail technician

What it is: Manicures, pedicures, gel and acrylic nails. How it works: Salon, home studio or mobile service.

SkillsNail techniques, hygiene
Cost & equipmentTools, products, UV lamp
Where to find workSocial media, salons, events
PaymentPer service
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays to weeks

How to start: Train; build a nail art portfolio on Instagram/TikTok. Pros: High demand. Cons & risks: Chemical exposure; use ventilation. Scam warning: As usual: fake product suppliers. Kenya notes: Bridal and event packages sell well.

464. Lash and brow technician

What it is: Eyelash extensions, brow shaping, tinting and lamination. How it works: Appointment-based services.

SkillsPrecision, hygiene, training
Cost & equipmentProducts, tools
Where to find workSocial media, salons
PaymentPer service; refills
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Professional training; patch tests for allergies. Pros: Recurring refills. Cons & risks: Allergic reactions. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Use safe, registered products.

465. Beauty salon or barbershop business (owner)

What it is: Owning a salon and employing stylists. How it works: Premises, staff, booking systems, marketing.

SkillsBusiness management, customer service
Cost & equipmentPremises, equipment, licences
Where to find workLocal customers, online bookings
PaymentService revenue
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start small; use booking and M-Pesa systems. Pros: Scalable with staff. Cons & risks: Rent and staff management. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: County business and health permits apply.

466. Beauty training academy

What it is: Training others in hair, makeup, nails or barbering. How it works: Short courses with certificates (registered where required).

SkillsExpertise, teaching
Cost & equipmentTraining space, kits
Where to find workYouth, career changers
PaymentCourse fees
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Register appropriately (e.g. as a TVET institution for formal courses). Pros: High demand for skills. Cons & risks: Accreditation. Scam warning: Don't promise jobs after courses. Kenya notes: Formal training institutions require TVETA registration.

467. Skincare product brand (registered)

What it is: Creating and selling your own skincare products. How it works: Formulation → testing → approvals → branding → sales.

SkillsFormulation knowledge, regulation
Cost & equipmentLab or contract manufacturing, certification
Where to find workOnline, shops, salons
PaymentSales
Realistic earningsLow to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Work with qualified formulators; get approvals. Pros: Brand value. Cons & risks: Regulation; liability. Scam warning: Never sell harmful lightening products. Kenya notes: Cosmetics need KEBS standards compliance.

468. Wig making and hair products business

What it is: Making and selling wigs, hair extensions and hair care products. How it works: Production → online sales → installation services.

SkillsWig making, styling
Cost & equipmentHair materials, tools
Where to find workSocial media, salons
PaymentSales
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Content showing wig making and installation. Pros: Strong demand. Cons & risks: Supplier quality. Scam warning: Honest descriptions of hair types. Kenya notes: Wig businesses thrive on TikTok.

469. Clothing brand (your own label)

What it is: Designing and selling your own fashion line. How it works: Design → production (yourself or tailors/manufacturers) → marketing.

SkillsDesign, branding, marketing
Cost & equipmentProduction costs
Where to find workSocial media, pop-ups, online store
PaymentSales
Realistic earningsLow to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Launch a small capsule collection; pre-orders. Pros: Creative brand. Cons & risks: Inventory risk. Scam warning: Don't copy other brands' designs and logos. Kenya notes: "Made in Kenya" fashion has growing appeal.

470. School uniform and corporate uniform supply

What it is: Producing and supplying uniforms to schools and companies. How it works: Contracts → production → delivery.

SkillsTailoring, production management
Cost & equipmentMachines, staff
Where to find workSchools, companies
PaymentContracts
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with local schools. Pros: Bulk orders. Cons & risks: Deadlines at term starts. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Peak demand before school terms.

471. Embroidery and branding business

What it is: Embroidering logos on uniforms, caps and bags. How it works: Digitising designs → machine embroidery.

SkillsEmbroidery machines, digitising
Cost & equipmentEmbroidery machine
Where to find workSchools, companies, teams
PaymentPer piece
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Combine with uniform supply. Pros: Repeat orders. Cons & risks: Equipment cost. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: School and corporate branding is steady.

472. Shoe reselling and sneaker business

What it is: Selling shoes, especially sneakers, online. How it works: Source → content → sales.

SkillsProduct knowledge, authentication
Cost & equipmentCapital
Where to find workSocial media
PaymentM-Pesa
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Be clear about originals vs replicas. Pros: Popular. Cons & risks: Counterfeits. Scam warning: Never sell fakes as originals. Kenya notes: Honest labelling builds loyal buyers.

473. Bag and accessories making

What it is: Making handbags, leather goods and accessories. How it works: Production → online sales.

SkillsLeatherwork or sewing
Cost & equipmentMaterials, tools
Where to find workSocial media, markets, tourists
PaymentSales
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Develop a signature product. Pros: Unique products. Cons & risks: Material costs. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Kenyan leather and kitenge bags appeal to tourists.

474. Fashion stylist and image consultant

What it is: Styling clients for events, photoshoots and professional image. How it works: Consultations and styling sessions.

SkillsFashion sense, communication
Cost & equipmentPhone
Where to find workProfessionals, influencers, brands
PaymentFees
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Build a styling portfolio. Pros: Creative. Cons & risks: Niche. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Corporate image consulting is growing.

475. Fashion photography and modelling

What it is: Modelling for brands or photographing fashion campaigns. How it works: Agencies, freelance shoots, social media.

SkillsModelling or photography
Cost & equipmentPortfolio
Where to find workBrands, agencies, designers
PaymentPer shoot
Realistic earningsVery low to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Work with reputable agencies; build portfolios. Pros: Creative industry. Cons & risks: Exploitation risks. Scam warning: Fake modelling agencies charging "portfolio fees" or asking for inappropriate photos. Kenya notes: Verify agencies thoroughly; never pay to be "signed".

476. Second-hand designer and vintage fashion

What it is: Curating vintage and designer second-hand items for resale. How it works: Sourcing → authentication → online sales.

SkillsFashion knowledge, authentication
Cost & equipmentCapital
Where to find workSocial media
PaymentSales
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Focus on quality and honest descriptions. Pros: Sustainable fashion trend. Cons & risks: Authenticity checks. Scam warning: Don't sell counterfeits. Kenya notes: Builds on Kenya's mitumba culture.

477. Fashion events and pop-up markets organiser

What it is: Organising markets where designers and sellers showcase products. How it works: Venue → vendor fees → marketing.

SkillsEvent planning, marketing
Cost & equipmentVenue costs
Where to find workDesigners, sellers
PaymentVendor fees, tickets
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with a small monthly market. Pros: Community building. Cons & risks: Attendance risk. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Pop-up markets are popular in Nairobi and other towns.

Summary

  • Beauty and fashion methods include nails, lashes and brows, salon ownership, beauty academies, skincare brands, wigs, clothing labels, uniforms, embroidery, sneakers, bags, styling, modelling and photography, vintage resale and pop-up markets.
  • Use safe registered products, honest labelling and strong social media portfolios; avoid counterfeits and fake agencies.
