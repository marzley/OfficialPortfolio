Smart technology and telecom installation trades

Homes, offices, estates and schools keep adding internet, security and backup power. Each installation trade below can be a job, a contract role or your own small business. They build on the basics of network installation (method 173), CCTV (174), TV and satellite dishes (257) and solar (382).

What these methods share

  • Safety first: work at height, mains electricity and batteries can kill. Get proper training and protective equipment.
  • Licences: electrical and solar work in Kenya needs the right EPRA licence; work for ISPs usually comes with their own vetting and training.
  • Warranty and records: give written quotes, receipts and warranty terms, and keep photos of every installation.

782. Fibre-to-the-home (FTTH) installation technician

What it is: Connecting homes and offices to fibre internet: running drop cables, fitting termination boxes and setting up routers. How it works: An internet provider or its contractor assigns jobs → install → test → customer signs off.

SkillsCable handling, basic networking, ladder safety
Cost & equipmentTools and test kit are often provided by the contractor; your own ladder, toolkit and transport help
Where to find workISP installation contractors, estate fibre projects
PaymentPer installation or monthly wage
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks (after training)

How to start: Apply to ISP contractors in your area; take a short fibre or networking course; be reliable and neat. Pros: Steady demand as fibre spreads. Cons & risks: Long days, heights, targets. Scam warning: Fake 'job placement' fees: real contractors don't charge you to work. Kenya notes: Fibre is spreading fast in towns and estates across Kenya, so installers are needed beyond Nairobi.

783. Fibre splicing and OTDR testing

What it is: Joining fibre cables with a fusion splicer and testing links with an OTDR (a fault-finding meter). How it works: Contractors hire splicers for new routes, repairs after cable cuts and building backbones.

SkillsFusion splicing, fibre testing, reading OTDR traces
Cost & equipmentHigh if you own a splicer and OTDR; many start by using the employer's
Where to find workTelecom contractors, ISPs, county and road projects
PaymentDaily rate or per joint
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train on splicing (short practical courses exist), work under an experienced splicer, then build a reputation. Pros: Skilled, better-paid niche. Cons & risks: Expensive equipment; emergency call-outs at night. Scam warning: Cheap 'certificates' without hands-on practice won't get you hired. Kenya notes: Cable cuts from roadworks are common, so repair splicers stay busy.

784. Telecom tower and rooftop site technician

What it is: Maintaining mobile network sites: antennas, power systems, generators and batteries. How it works: Tower companies and their maintenance contractors send technicians to scheduled and fault visits.

SkillsElectrical basics, climbing safety, telecom equipment
Cost & equipmentTraining and safety certification (work at height); usually employer-provided gear
Where to find workTower companies, maintenance contractors
PaymentMonthly salary or contract
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get an electrical or telecom certificate, then a work-at-height safety course; apply to maintenance contractors. Pros: Structured career path. Cons & risks: Dangerous if safety rules are ignored; travel to remote sites. Scam warning: Never climb without certified harness training, whatever you're promised. Kenya notes: Thousands of sites across Kenya need regular maintenance.

785. Satellite internet installation (e.g. Starlink)

What it is: Mounting satellite internet dishes, running cables and setting up Wi-Fi for homes, lodges, farms and schools far from fibre. How it works: The client buys the kit → you survey for a clear view of the sky → mount, cable and configure.

SkillsMounting, cabling, Wi-Fi setup
Cost & equipmentLadder, drill, mounting hardware, cable
Where to find workRural homes, lodges, farms, schools, NGOs
PaymentPer installation
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn the kit's setup guide, install for a few clients at a fair price, and collect reviews. Pros: Big need in areas without fibre. Cons & risks: Travel costs; you depend on the client buying kit. Scam warning: Don't resell kits or subscriptions in ways the provider doesn't allow; buy from official channels. Kenya notes: Satellite internet has been available in Kenya since 2023 and suits remote lodges, farms and schools.

786. Electric fence installation and maintenance

What it is: Installing and servicing electric fences for homes, farms, schools and wildlife-conflict areas. How it works: Survey → quote → install energiser, wires and earthing → service contracts.

SkillsElectrical basics, fencing, earthing
Cost & equipmentTools, tester, stock of wire and insulators
Where to find workHomes, estates, farms, schools
PaymentPer project plus service fees
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Train with an experienced installer; learn safety standards and proper earthing. Pros: Security demand is constant. Cons & risks: Liability if installed badly; theft of materials. Scam warning: Use quality energisers; cheap fakes fail. Kenya notes: Common in Kenyan homes and farms; fences near wildlife areas need extra care and community agreement.

787. Automatic gate and barrier installation

What it is: Fitting gate motors, remotes, barriers and safety sensors for homes, estates and car parks. How it works: Quote → supply motor → install, wire and programme → maintenance visits.

SkillsElectrical, mechanical fitting, welding helps
Cost & equipmentTools, welding access, motor stock
Where to find workHomes, estates, schools, hotels, offices
PaymentPer installation plus repairs
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Start as an assistant to an installer; learn popular gate motor brands. Pros: Repairs bring repeat income. Cons & risks: Power outages and surges damage motors; customer complaints. Scam warning: Avoid grey-market motors with no warranty. Kenya notes: Gated homes and estates are growing in every Kenyan town.

788. Access control, intercom and video door phones

What it is: Installing card or code door locks, intercoms and video door phones for offices, estates and apartments. How it works: Survey → design → install and configure → train users → support contract.

SkillsLow-voltage wiring, configuration
Cost & equipmentTools, test equipment
Where to find workApartments, offices, schools, estates
PaymentPer project plus support
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Learn alongside CCTV or network work; partner with security companies. Pros: Fits well with CCTV and networking. Cons & risks: Fast-changing technology. Scam warning: Respect privacy: don't record or share residents' data. Kenya notes: Apartment blocks and offices increasingly use access cards and video door phones.

789. Biometric attendance and time systems

What it is: Supplying and setting up fingerprint or face attendance machines and their software for schools, factories and offices. How it works: Supply device → install → connect to payroll or reports → train HR.

SkillsDevice setup, basic networking, Excel or software reporting
Cost & equipmentDevice stock (or order per client)
Where to find workSchools, factories, hospitals, offices
PaymentPer installation plus support
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with a supplier; learn a couple of common devices and their reports. Pros: Simple installs; recurring support. Cons & risks: Software problems land on you. Scam warning: Follow the Data Protection Act: biometric data is sensitive personal data, so explain how it's stored and protected. Kenya notes: Schools and employers use these widely; Data Protection Act compliance is a selling point.

790. Smart home automation installation

What it is: Setting up smart lights, sockets, sensors, cameras and voice assistants that work together from a phone. How it works: Consultation → design → install and configure → hand over with training.

SkillsNetworking, electrical basics, app setup
Cost & equipmentDemo kit, tools
Where to find workNew homes, apartments, Airbnbs, offices
PaymentPer project
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Automate your own home first; partner with electricians and interior designers. Pros: Premium clients; fun work. Cons & risks: Small market; devices vary in quality. Scam warning: Use reputable devices and secure them (change default passwords). Kenya notes: Popular with new apartments and short-stay rentals in Nairobi and Mombasa.

791. Inverter and battery backup installation

What it is: Installing inverters and batteries so homes and businesses keep power during blackouts. How it works: Assess load → size system → install → test → maintain.

SkillsElectrical installation, sizing calculations
Cost & equipmentTools, meters; licensed electrician status
Where to find workHomes, shops, clinics, offices
PaymentPer installation
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Qualify as an electrician (EPRA licence), learn sizing, start with small systems. Pros: Blackouts drive demand. Cons & risks: Battery fire and electrocution risks if done badly. Scam warning: Watch for fake batteries and inflated capacity labels. Kenya notes: Power cuts make backup power a common purchase for Kenyan homes and businesses.

792. Laptop and computer refurbishing and resale

What it is: Buying used or ex-corporate laptops, repairing, cleaning and upgrading them, and reselling with a warranty. How it works: Source → test and fix → upgrade (SSD, RAM) → clean and reinstall legal software → sell.

SkillsHardware repair, software setup
Cost & equipmentStock money, tools, parts
Where to find workStudents, small businesses, online buyers
PaymentPer sale
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with a few units; give an honest condition report and a short warranty. Pros: Affordable computers help students. Cons & risks: Stock risk; slow sales. Scam warning: Never buy stolen devices; ask for proof of ownership. Don't install pirated software. Kenya notes: Ex-corporate laptops are popular with Kenyan students; honest warranties build trust.

793. E-waste refurbishing and collection (licensed)

What it is: Collecting old electronics, refurbishing what can be reused and passing the rest to licensed recyclers. How it works: Collection drives → sort → refurbish or harvest parts → licensed recycler.

SkillsElectronics repair, sorting, record keeping
Cost & equipmentStorage, transport; licences for handling e-waste
Where to find workOffices, schools, homes, repair shops
PaymentSales of refurbished items and parts, collection fees
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Learn the NEMA rules for e-waste handling; partner with a licensed recycler. Pros: Environmental impact. Cons & risks: Toxic materials need safe handling. Scam warning: Don't burn cables or dump batteries: it's harmful and illegal. Kenya notes: NEMA regulates e-waste; licensed recyclers exist in Nairobi and other towns.

Summary

  • Installation trades include fibre (FTTH) installation, fibre splicing, tower maintenance, satellite internet, electric fences, gate automation, access control, biometric attendance, smart homes, inverter backup, laptop refurbishing and licensed e-waste handling.
  • Safety training, the right licences (EPRA for electrical and solar work) and neat, documented work build a reputation.
  • Respect privacy and the Data Protection Act when installing systems that collect personal or biometric data.
