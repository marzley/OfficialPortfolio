Tourism and hospitality businesses

Kenya's safari parks, beaches, culture and conferences attract visitors from around the world and within the country. Online booking platforms, reviews and social media let small operators compete. Most tourism businesses need licences from tourism regulators.

What these methods share

  • Licensing: tourism businesses and tour guides are licensed by the Tourism Regulatory Authority (TRA); check requirements.
  • Reviews: online reputation drives bookings.
  • Seasonality: plan for high and low seasons.

495. Tour operator

What it is: Organising safaris, excursions and tour packages. How it works: Packages → bookings → logistics → guiding.

SkillsLogistics, destination knowledge, customer service
Cost & equipmentLicences, vehicles or partnerships
Where to find workTourists, travel agents, online platforms
PaymentPackage fees
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get licensed; partner with lodges and drivers. Pros: High-value bookings. Cons & risks: Seasonality; capital. Scam warning: Fake tour operators take deposits online; legitimate ones are licensed and verifiable. Kenya notes: TRA licensing and KATO-type association membership add credibility.

496. Travel agent

What it is: Booking flights, hotels and packages for clients. How it works: Agency accreditation or partnerships; commissions/fees.

SkillsBooking systems, customer service
Cost & equipmentLicences, booking tools
Where to find workIndividuals, companies
PaymentFees/commissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train in travel and tourism; join an agency. Pros: Steady corporate clients. Cons & risks: Online booking competition. Scam warning: Fake visa and job "travel agents"; verify thoroughly. Kenya notes: Corporate and religious travel are niches.

497. Guesthouse, homestay or B&B

What it is: Hosting guests in your home or a small property. How it works: Listings → bookings → hospitality.

SkillsHospitality
Cost & equipmentProperty, furnishing, licences
Where to find workBooking platforms, social media
PaymentPer night
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Meet licensing and safety requirements. Pros: Uses existing property. Cons & risks: Guest management. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Homestays near parks and towns attract travellers.

498. Hostel and budget accommodation

What it is: Budget accommodation for students, backpackers and workers. How it works: Rooms/beds rented nightly or monthly.

SkillsProperty management
Cost & equipmentProperty, furnishing
Where to find workStudents, travellers
PaymentRent
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Near universities or transport hubs. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Maintenance. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Student hostels near universities are in demand.

499. Campsite and glamping

What it is: Offering camping or "glamping" (comfortable tents) experiences. How it works: Land → facilities → bookings.

SkillsHospitality, outdoor management
Cost & equipmentLand, tents, facilities
Where to find workTourists, local travellers
PaymentPer night
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start small with good reviews. Pros: Growing domestic tourism. Cons & risks: Weather and access. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Domestic weekend getaways are popular.

500. Cultural experiences and village tours

What it is: Offering authentic cultural experiences: cooking, crafts, farm visits. How it works: Bookings via platforms and tour operators.

SkillsCultural knowledge, hosting
Cost & equipmentLow
Where to find workTourists, experience platforms
PaymentPer person
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Work with community members fairly; share income. Pros: Unique experiences. Cons & risks: Respect and authenticity matter. Scam warning: Don't exploit communities. Kenya notes: Community-based tourism benefits local areas.

What it is: Driving and guiding tourists on safaris. How it works: Employed by operators or freelance.

SkillsWildlife knowledge, driving, guiding certification
Cost & equipmentLicences
Where to find workTour operators, lodges
PaymentSalary, tips
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get guiding certification and experience. Pros: Rewarding career. Cons & risks: Seasonal. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Professional guide certification adds credibility.

502. Hotel and restaurant digital marketing services

What it is: Managing online presence for hotels and restaurants. How it works: Listings, reviews, social media, booking optimisation.

SkillsDigital marketing
Cost & equipmentLaptop
Where to find workHotels, lodges, restaurants
PaymentMonthly retainer
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Improve one lodge's listings and reviews. Pros: Recurring. Cons & risks: Seasonal budgets. Scam warning: Never post fake reviews. Kenya notes: Many lodges have weak online presence.

503. Conference and events venue

What it is: Renting space for meetings, conferences and events. How it works: Bookings, catering partnerships.

SkillsHospitality, event management
Cost & equipmentVenue, equipment
Where to find workOrganisations, NGOs

Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Market through event planners. Pros: High-value bookings. Cons & risks: Capital. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: NGO and corporate workshops drive demand.

What it is: Transport between airports, hotels and towns. How it works: Bookings via websites, hotels and platforms.

SkillsDriving, punctuality

Where to find workHotels, tour operators, travellers

Realistic earningsLow to medium

505. Travel content creation

What it is: Creating travel videos, blogs and guides about Kenya. How it works: Content → audience → sponsorships from tourism businesses.

SkillsStorytelling, video
Cost & equipmentCamera/phone
Where to find workPlatforms, tourism brands
PaymentSponsorships, ads
Realistic earningsVery low to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Document affordable local travel. Pros: Promotes local tourism. Cons & risks: Travel costs. Scam warning: Disclose sponsored stays. Kenya notes: Domestic tourism content is popular.

506. Wedding destination planning

What it is: Planning weddings at beaches, lodges and destinations. How it works: Packages with venues and vendors.

SkillsEvent planning, tourism knowledge
Cost & equipmentVendor network
Where to find workCouples (local and international)
PaymentPlanning fees
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Partner with destination venues. Pros: High-value. Cons & risks: Complex logistics. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Coastal and lakeside venues are popular.

507. Housekeeping and hospitality staffing agency

What it is: Providing trained staff to hotels and events. How it works: Recruitment, training, placement.

SkillsRecruitment, training
Cost & equipmentOffice, licences
Where to find workHotels, events
PaymentPlacement or management fees
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Build a pool of trained workers. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Labour law compliance. Scam warning: Never charge workers illegal fees. Kenya notes: Follow employment laws.

508. Souvenir and curio shop (online and physical)

What it is: Selling crafts and souvenirs to tourists. How it works: Shop + online orders.

SkillsRetail, sourcing
Cost & equipmentStock, shop
Where to find workTourists, online buyers
PaymentSales
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Source fairly from artisans. Pros: Tourist demand. Cons & risks: Seasonal. Scam warning: Never sell wildlife products; it's illegal. Kenya notes: Trade in ivory and wildlife products is prohibited.

509. Language guide for tourists and business visitors

What it is: Guiding visitors who speak specific languages (French, German, Chinese, Italian). How it works: Freelance with tour operators and businesses.

SkillsLanguage fluency, guiding
Cost & equipmentLicences
Where to find workTour operators, embassies, businesses
PaymentDaily rates
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Combine language skills with guiding certification. Pros: Premium rates. Cons & risks: Seasonal. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Multilingual guides are in demand.

Summary

  • Tourism methods include tour operation, travel agency, homestays, hostels, glamping, cultural experiences, safari guiding, hospitality marketing, venues, transfers, travel content, destination weddings, staffing, souvenirs and language guiding.
  • Licensing, reviews and fair treatment of communities matter; never trade wildlife products.
