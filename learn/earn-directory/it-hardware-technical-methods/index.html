IT hardware and technical services

Adding to computer and phone repair (methods 250–251 and 172) and installation trades (782–793), these technical services serve offices, schools and data-heavy businesses.

What these methods share

  • Data care: you'll handle clients' devices and data: protect them.
  • Genuine parts and software: no pirated software or counterfeit parts.
  • Service contracts: recurring maintenance beats one-off repairs.

930. Printer and photocopier servicing

What it is: Repairing and maintaining printers and copiers for offices, schools and cyber cafés. How it works: Service contracts → scheduled maintenance → repairs and toner supply.

SkillsPrinter repair, electronics
Cost & equipmentTools, spare parts stock
Where to find workOffices, schools, cyber cafés, print shops
PaymentPer service or contract
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train with a technician; offer monthly maintenance contracts. Pros: Recurring income. Cons & risks: Parts availability. Scam warning: Use genuine or quality compatible parts and be honest about which. Kenya notes: Every office and school relies on printers.

931. Data centre technician

What it is: Maintaining servers, racks, cooling and power in data centres and server rooms. How it works: Shift work monitoring and maintaining equipment.

SkillsHardware, networking, power and cooling basics
Cost & equipmentTraining and certifications
Where to find workData centres, telcos, banks, cloud providers
PaymentMonthly salary
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get networking or data centre certifications; apply to data centres in Nairobi. Pros: Growing sector. Cons & risks: Shift work. Scam warning: Real employers don't charge for placement. Kenya notes: Nairobi hosts several large data centres.

932. Data recovery service

What it is: Recovering files from damaged or failed hard drives, phones and memory cards. How it works: Diagnose → recover with software or lab tools → return data.

SkillsTechnical skill, care
Cost & equipmentRecovery software, tools; lab equipment for advanced work
Where to find workIndividuals, businesses, photographers
PaymentPer job
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with logical recovery (deleted files) before physical repairs. Pros: Clients value it highly. Cons & risks: Not always possible. Scam warning: Keep recovered data confidential and delete your copies. Kenya notes: Pairs with computer repair shops.

933. ICT equipment leasing to schools

What it is: Leasing computers, projectors and tablets to schools with maintenance included. How it works: Buy equipment → lease contract → maintain → replace.

SkillsFinance, IT support
Cost & equipmentHigh: equipment capital
Where to find workSchools, colleges, training centres
PaymentMonthly or termly lease payments
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with one school; include maintenance and training. Pros: Recurring income. Cons & risks: Capital and payment risk. Scam warning: Use clear contracts and insurance. Kenya notes: Schools want ICT but lack upfront budgets.

934. Licensed software reseller

What it is: Selling genuine software licences (office suites, antivirus, accounting) and setting them up. How it works: Become an authorised partner → sell and install → renew yearly.

SkillsProduct knowledge, sales
Cost & equipmentPartner registration
Where to find workSMEs, schools, NGOs
PaymentMargin and renewal commissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Register with vendors' partner programmes. Pros: Yearly renewals. Cons & risks: Margins can be thin. Scam warning: Never sell cracked or pirated software. Kenya notes: Businesses need genuine licences for compliance.

935. Drone mapping and surveying

What it is: Using drones to map land, monitor crops and inspect sites, producing maps and models. How it works: Plan flight → capture images → process into maps → report.

SkillsDrone piloting, mapping software
Cost & equipmentDrone, software, KCAA licensing
Where to find workSurveyors, farms, construction firms, counties
PaymentPer project
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get the Kenya Civil Aviation Authority's required licences and registration before commercial flights. Pros: High-value outputs. Cons & risks: Regulation and equipment costs. Scam warning: Never fly commercially without KCAA approval. Kenya notes: Different from drone videography (method 122): the product is maps and data.

936. IoT prototyping and electronics assembly

What it is: Building connected devices and prototypes: sensors, trackers, automation. How it works: Design → assemble → program → test.

SkillsElectronics, programming (Arduino, ESP32)
Cost & equipmentTools, components
Where to find workStartups, farms, factories, researchers
PaymentPer prototype or project
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Build a few demo projects (soil moisture sensor, water tank level alarm). Pros: Creative, growing field. Cons & risks: Component sourcing. Scam warning: Be clear about prototype vs production quality. Kenya notes: Agritech and water monitoring are popular IoT uses in Kenya.

Summary

  • IT services include printer servicing, data centre work, data recovery, ICT leasing, licensed software reselling, drone mapping and IoT prototyping.
  • Use genuine parts and software and protect client data.
  • Drone work needs KCAA approval.
