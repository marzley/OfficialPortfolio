Mining, quarrying and building materials (licensed)

Kenya's building boom needs stone, ballast, sand and murram, and several counties have gold, gemstones and other minerals. These are physical, regulated businesses: done legally and safely they create good jobs; done illegally they destroy land and rivers and risk lives.

What these methods share

  • Licences: minerals are governed by the Mining Act (2016) through the Ministry of Mining, with permits for artisanal and small-scale miners. Quarries and sand harvesting also need county permits and NEMA environmental approval.
  • Safety: collapsing pits and quarries kill workers every year. Protective gear, safe slopes and no work in unstable pits are essential.
  • Land and community: get the landowner's written agreement and engage the community.
  • Rehabilitation: restore land after extraction (backfilling, trees).

722. Artisanal gold mining (licensed)

What it is: Small-scale gold mining by individuals or groups with an artisanal mining permit. How it works: Dig and process ore → sell gold to licensed dealers.

SkillsMining skills, safety awareness
Cost & equipmentTools, permit, safety gear
Where to find workLicensed gold dealers and buying centres
PaymentPer gram
Realistic earningsLow to medium; very uncertain
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Join or form a registered artisanal miners' group; get the county permit. Pros: Possible high value. Cons & risks: Dangerous pits; mercury poisoning. Scam warning: Sell only to licensed dealers; 'gold' sold to you outside the system is a common scam. Kenya notes: Migori, Kakamega, Siaya, Turkana and West Pokot have artisanal gold mining. Avoid mercury; safer methods exist.

723. Gemstone mining and dealing (licensed)

What it is: Mining or trading gemstones such as tsavorite, ruby, tourmaline and garnet. How it works: Mine or buy rough stones → grade → sell to licensed buyers or cutters.

SkillsGem identification
Cost & equipmentLicences (mining or dealer's licence), tools, testing equipment
Where to find workLicensed dealers, exporters, jewellers
PaymentPer carat or piece
Realistic earningsMedium to high; risky
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Learn gemology basics; partner with a licensed dealer before trading. Pros: High value. Cons & risks: Fraud and fake stones. Scam warning: Fake and glass 'gems' are common; test before paying. Kenya notes: Taita Taveta and parts of the Rift Valley are known for gemstones; dealing needs a licence.

724. Building stone quarrying and cutting

What it is: Cutting machine-cut building stones in a licensed quarry. How it works: Quarry → cut stones → sell to builders and hardware stores.

SkillsQuarry work, machine operation
Cost & equipmentCutting machine, licences, safety gear
Where to find workBuilders, contractors, hardware stores
PaymentPer stone or lorry load
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Work in an established quarry first; then lease a licensed site. Pros: Steady construction demand. Cons & risks: Accidents; dust. Scam warning: Avoid unlicensed quarries: they can be closed overnight. Kenya notes: Machakos, Juja, Kiambu and Nyeri supply Nairobi's building stones.

725. Ballast crushing and supply

What it is: Producing crushed stone (ballast) for concrete. How it works: Crush by hand or machine → sell by lorry load.

SkillsPhysical work or crusher operation
Cost & equipmentHammers or a crusher, transport
Where to find workContractors, builders, hardware stores
PaymentPer tonne or lorry
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Start by supplying local builders; consider a group crusher. Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Hard physical work; dust. Scam warning: Agree quantities and prices before delivery. Kenya notes: Many youth and women's groups earn from hand-crushing; machines raise output.

726. Sand harvesting and supply (licensed)

What it is: Harvesting and transporting building sand from approved sites. How it works: Licensed site → load → deliver to construction sites.

SkillsLogistics
Cost & equipmentLicences, transport
Where to find workBuilders, contractors
PaymentPer lorry
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Use only approved harvesting sites with county and NEMA permits. Pros: High demand. Cons & risks: Environmental damage if illegal; crackdowns. Scam warning: Illegal riverbed sand mining is banned in many areas and destroys rivers. Kenya notes: Counties regulate sand harvesting; check the latest rules before starting.

727. Murram and hardcore supply

What it is: Supplying murram and hardcore for roads, foundations and compounds. How it works: Source from licensed pits → deliver by lorry.

SkillsLogistics, negotiation
Cost & equipmentTransport, supplier agreements
Where to find workRoad contractors, builders, counties
PaymentPer lorry
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with licensed pit owners and truck owners. Pros: Big projects. Cons & risks: Payment delays on tenders. Scam warning: Get written orders; avoid paying 'agents' for tenders. Kenya notes: Road and estate developments need regular supplies.

728. Paving block (cabro) installation

What it is: Laying interlocking paving blocks for driveways, compounds and parking. How it works: Quotes → excavation and base → laying → finishing.

SkillsPaving skills
Cost & equipmentTools, compactor, labour
Where to find workHomeowners, schools, churches, businesses
PaymentPer square metre
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn with an experienced team; photograph finished jobs. Pros: Visible work brings referrals. Cons & risks: Poor base causes sinking. Scam warning: Don't skimp on the base and edging; failures ruin reputations. Kenya notes: Pairs well with block making (method 620).

729. Lime and agricultural lime supply

What it is: Supplying lime for farming (soil acidity) and construction. How it works: Buy from producers → distribute to farmers and builders.

SkillsSales, soil knowledge
Cost & equipmentStock, transport
Where to find workFarmers, agrovets, builders
PaymentPer bag
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn soil testing basics; partner with agrovets. Pros: Helps farmers raise yields. Cons & risks: Seasonal demand. Scam warning: Sell genuine, labelled lime only. Kenya notes: Many Kenyan soils are acidic; agronomists often recommend liming after soil tests.

730. Mining and quarry equipment hire

What it is: Renting out crushers, compressors, generators and tools. How it works: Hire contracts → deliver → maintain.

SkillsEquipment maintenance
Cost & equipmentEquipment (high capital)
Where to find workQuarries, contractors, miners
PaymentDaily or weekly hire
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with one in-demand machine; use written hire agreements and deposits. Pros: Recurring income. Cons & risks: Breakdowns and theft. Scam warning: Track equipment and require deposits. Kenya notes: Insurance and maintenance records protect your investment.

731. Mineral testing and assay services (licensed lab)

What it is: Testing ore and gold purity for miners and buyers. How it works: Samples → testing → certificates.

SkillsChemistry or laboratory training
Cost & equipmentLab equipment, accreditation
Where to find workMiners, dealers, investors
PaymentPer test
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Train as a lab technician; work in an accredited lab first. Pros: Trusted, essential role. Cons & risks: Expensive setup. Scam warning: Fake certificates are a fraud tool; keep strict records. Kenya notes: Accurate testing helps small miners get fair prices.

732. Geology and mining field assistant

What it is: Assisting geologists and exploration companies in the field. How it works: Employment or contracts: sampling, mapping, logging.

SkillsScience background, fitness
Cost & equipmentField gear
Where to find workExploration companies, consultants, universities
PaymentDaily rate or salary
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks to months

How to start: Study geology or earth sciences; register with exploration firms. Pros: Path to a geology career. Cons & risks: Remote, contract-based work. Scam warning: As usual. Kenya notes: Exploration projects advertise through official company channels.

Summary

  • Mining and building materials include artisanal gold, gemstones, building stone, ballast, sand, murram, paving, lime, equipment hire, testing and field work.
  • Licences (Mining Act, county permits, NEMA) and safety are non-negotiable.
  • Rehabilitate land and avoid mercury and illegal sand mining.
