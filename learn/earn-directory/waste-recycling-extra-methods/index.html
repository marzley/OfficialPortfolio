More waste and recycling businesses

Adding to waste collection, recycling and composting (methods 385–389), these turn specific waste streams into income.

What these methods share

  • Licences: NEMA licences for waste handling and transport.
  • Safety: sorting waste needs gloves and protective gear.
  • Buyers: know who buys your recovered material first.

955. Used cooking oil collection

What it is: Collecting used cooking oil from restaurants and selling it to biodiesel or soap makers. How it works: Collect in containers → filter → sell in bulk.

SkillsLogistics
Cost & equipmentContainers, transport, NEMA licence
Where to find workRestaurants, hotels, fast-food outlets
PaymentPer litre sold
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Sign up restaurants; find a reliable buyer. Pros: Keeps oil out of drains and street food. Cons & risks: Messy logistics. Scam warning: Never resell used oil as cooking oil: it's dangerous and illegal. Kenya notes: Some used oil is illegally recycled into food; legitimate collection protects public health.

956. Glass recycling

What it is: Collecting and processing waste glass for recyclers or into crafts and beads. How it works: Collect → sort by colour → crush or craft.

SkillsSorting, crafts
Cost & equipmentCollection points, crusher or tools
Where to find workBars, hotels, households, glass recyclers
PaymentPer kilo or per product
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with bars and hotels for collection. Pros: Abundant waste. Cons & risks: Low prices for raw glass. Scam warning: Wear protective gear when handling glass. Kenya notes: Recycled glass beads are a craft product.

957. Tyre recycling

What it is: Turning old tyres into products: sandals, mats, planters, flooring and furniture. How it works: Collect tyres → cut and process → make products.

SkillsFabrication
Cost & equipmentCutting tools, workspace
Where to find workTyre shops, garages; buyers of products
PaymentPer product
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with simple products like planters and furniture. Pros: Free raw material. Cons & risks: Labour-intensive. Scam warning: Never burn tyres: it's toxic and illegal. Kenya notes: Akala sandals made from tyres are a long Kenyan tradition.

958. Paper recycling and egg trays

What it is: Collecting waste paper and producing egg trays or other moulded products. How it works: Collect paper → pulp → mould → dry → sell.

SkillsMachine operation
Cost & equipmentEgg tray machine, drying space
Where to find workOffices, schools; poultry farmers buy trays
PaymentPer tray
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Secure paper supply and poultry farmer buyers. Pros: Steady demand from poultry farms. Cons & risks: Machine costs; drying space. Scam warning: Buy machines from reputable suppliers. Kenya notes: Poultry farms need large numbers of egg trays.

959. Scrap metal dealing (licensed)

What it is: Buying and selling scrap metal to recyclers and steel mills. How it works: Licensed yard → buy with records → sort → sell in bulk.

SkillsMetal identification, record keeping
Cost & equipmentYard, scale, Scrap Metal Council licence
Where to find workGarages, construction sites, households
PaymentMargin per kilo
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Get licensed under the Scrap Metal Act and keep seller records. Pros: Big market. Cons & risks: Heavy regulation due to theft. Scam warning: Never buy vandalised infrastructure (power cables, rail parts): it's a serious crime. Kenya notes: Kenya regulates scrap dealing under the Scrap Metal Act.

Summary

  • Waste businesses include used cooking oil, glass, tyres, paper egg trays and licensed scrap metal.
  • Get NEMA and scrap licences and keep records.
  • Never resell used oil as food or buy stolen metal.
