Delivery, transport and local gig work through apps

App-based gig work connects people who need a service (a ride, a delivery, a cleaner, a plumber) with workers nearby. It offers flexibility and fast starts, but income depends on demand, commission rates, fuel and vehicle costs. These methods are mostly offline work found and paid through apps.

What these methods share

  • Know your real earnings: subtract commissions, fuel, maintenance, data and insurance from what the app pays.
  • Safety first: road safety, protective gear, and verified customers.
  • Compliance: licences, insurance and vehicle requirements (NTSA rules for drivers and riders).
  • Platform terms change: commission rates and incentives vary; read terms carefully.

297. Ride-hailing driver (car)

What it is: Driving passengers through ride-hailing apps. How it works: Register with an app → vehicle and document checks → accept rides → earn per trip minus commission.

SkillsSafe driving, navigation, customer service
Cost & equipmentCar (owned or rented), valid driving licence, PSV-related requirements as applicable, insurance, smartphone
Where to find workRide-hailing apps operating in your city
PaymentApp payouts and cash/M-Pesa from riders
Realistic earningsLow to medium after costs
Time to first incomeWeeks (registration)

How to start: Calculate costs carefully, especially if renting a car; meet all legal requirements. Pros: Flexible hours. Cons & risks: High costs, commissions, safety risks at night. Scam warning: Car "rent-to-own" deals with unfair terms; read contracts carefully. Kenya notes: Driver associations and regulators have debated commission rates; check current rules and NTSA requirements.

298. Boda boda rider (motorbike taxi and app-based)

What it is: Transporting passengers and parcels by motorbike, independently or via apps. How it works: Licensed rider → stage or app jobs → per trip payment.

SkillsSafe riding, local knowledge
Cost & equipmentMotorbike, licence, insurance, helmet and reflective gear
Where to find workStages, apps, regular customers
PaymentCash/M-Pesa
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays (once licensed)

How to start: Get proper training and a licence; join a registered stage/SACCO. Pros: Immediate income. Cons & risks: Road accidents; bike theft. Scam warning: Loan deals for bikes with hidden charges; compare carefully. Kenya notes: NTSA rules on licences, helmets and passenger safety apply.

299. Food delivery rider

What it is: Delivering restaurant orders through food delivery apps. How it works: Accept orders → pick up → deliver → paid per delivery.

SkillsNavigation, timeliness
Cost & equipmentMotorbike/bicycle, delivery box, smartphone
Where to find workFood delivery apps
PaymentPer delivery plus incentives
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Register with apps; plan peak hours. Pros: Flexible. Cons & risks: Weather, traffic, accidents. Scam warning: Fake "rider recruitment" charging fees. Kenya notes: Demand is concentrated in major towns.

300. Parcel and courier delivery

What it is: Delivering parcels for online shops and individuals. How it works: Courier apps or direct contracts with sellers.

SkillsReliability, route planning
Cost & equipmentMotorbike/vehicle
Where to find workOnline sellers, courier apps
PaymentPer delivery
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Offer reliable same-day delivery to Instagram shops. Pros: Growing e-commerce. Cons & risks: Lost parcels; cash-on-delivery handling. Scam warning: Fake customers using stolen goods schemes. Kenya notes: Online sellers value reliable riders.

312. Matatu and PSV investment and management

What it is: Owning or managing public service vehicles through a registered SACCO. How it works: Vehicle → SACCO membership → daily operations.

SkillsBusiness management
Cost & equipmentVehicle investment, SACCO membership
Where to find workRoutes through SACCOs
PaymentDaily earnings
Realistic earningsMedium (with risks)
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Research routes; join a reputable SACCO. Pros: Daily cash flow. Cons & risks: Regulation, accidents, maintenance. Scam warning: Fraudulent vehicle sales. Kenya notes: NTSA rules require PSVs to operate under registered SACCOs.

Summary

  • Gig and transport methods include ride-hailing, boda boda, food and parcel delivery, errands, moving, cleaning, laundry, trades, car washing, mechanics, tours, short-term rentals, co-hosting, car hire and PSV operations.
  • Calculate real earnings after costs; prioritise safety; follow licensing and insurance rules.
