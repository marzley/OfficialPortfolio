Small-scale manufacturing

Making everyday products locally (detergents, briquettes, paper bags, building blocks, snacks) can be a solid business with steady local demand. Online marketing, WhatsApp orders and M-Pesa help small manufacturers reach shops and households.

What these methods share

  • Standards and permits: many products need KEBS standardisation marks before they can be sold in shops and supermarkets; county business permits and sometimes NEMA approvals apply.
  • Safety: chemicals, machines and heat are dangerous; train properly and use protective gear.
  • Costing: calculate raw materials, packaging, labour, power and transport per unit.
  • Quality consistency keeps distributors and shops buying.

616. Liquid soap and detergent making

What it is: Producing liquid soap, dishwashing liquid and multipurpose detergents. How it works: Mix ingredients by tested formulas → bottle and label → sell to households, schools, offices.

SkillsFormulation, safe chemical handling
Cost & equipmentMixing containers, raw materials, protective gear, bottles
Where to find workHouseholds, schools, hotels, offices, shops
PaymentPer litre or bulk orders
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Take a practical training course; start with bulk supply to schools and offices. Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Chemical safety; competition. Scam warning: Overpriced "detergent making kits" promising guaranteed markets. Kenya notes: KEBS standards apply for retail sale.
