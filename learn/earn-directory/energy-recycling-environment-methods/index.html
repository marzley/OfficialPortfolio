Energy, recycling and environmental work

Kenya is a leader in renewable energy, and waste management and climate projects are growing. These methods turn environmental needs into income, from collecting recyclables to installing solar and selling clean cookstoves. Some need technical training and licences; others start with very little capital.

What these methods share

  • Safety: electrical work, chemicals and waste handling require training and protective equipment.
  • Licences: solar technicians (EPRA), waste handling (NEMA and county rules) and others.
  • Honest claims: avoid exaggerated "carbon credit" or savings promises.

381. Solar product sales (lamps, home systems)

What it is: Selling solar lanterns, home systems and pay-as-you-go products. How it works: Agent or reseller for solar companies; commissions per sale.

SkillsSales, product knowledge
Cost & equipmentPhone, transport, sample products
Where to find workRural and peri-urban households, solar company agent programmes
PaymentCommissions
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Join reputable solar companies' agent networks. Pros: Meaningful product. Cons & risks: Customer repayment issues for PAYG products. Scam warning: Avoid substandard, uncertified products. Kenya notes: Pay-as-you-go solar is widespread in Kenya.
