More fashion and textile businesses

Adding to fashion and beauty (methods 463–477) and tailoring (267), these textile businesses suit both artisans and traders.

What these methods share

  • Quality: neat finishing and durable materials bring repeat buyers.
  • Deposits: take deposits on custom orders.
  • Originality: don't copy other brands' designs or logos.

924. Tie-dye and batik

What it is: Dyeing fabric into colourful patterns for clothes, décor and souvenirs. How it works: Prepare fabric → wax or tie → dye → finish.

SkillsDyeing techniques
Cost & equipmentDyes, wax, fabric
Where to find workBoutiques, tourists, online buyers
PaymentPer piece
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn from artisans; sell online and at craft markets. Pros: Unique products. Cons & risks: Dye chemicals need safe handling. Scam warning: Use colour-fast dyes so products don't run. Kenya notes: Batik and tie-dye are popular with tourists and young buyers.

925. Knitting and crochet products

What it is: Making sweaters, scarves, hats, baby clothes and décor by hand or machine. How it works: Take orders or make stock → sell.

SkillsKnitting, crochet
Cost & equipmentYarn, needles or knitting machine
Where to find workParents, boutiques, online
PaymentPer item
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with baby items and beanies; post on social media. Pros: Low start-up. Cons & risks: Time-consuming by hand. Scam warning: Get deposits for custom orders. Kenya notes: Cold-season demand in highland towns.

926. Bridal gown and dress rental

What it is: Renting wedding gowns, bridesmaid dresses and evening wear. How it works: Buy quality stock → rent with deposit → clean and alter.

SkillsFashion sense, customer service
Cost & equipmentStock, fitting space, dry cleaning
Where to find workBrides, wedding planners
PaymentPer rental
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with a small range; partner with wedding planners. Pros: Each dress earns many times. Cons & risks: Damage and cleaning costs. Scam warning: Use deposits and written rental terms. Kenya notes: Many couples prefer renting gowns.

927. Suit and formal wear hire

What it is: Renting suits, tuxedos and formal wear for weddings, proms and events. How it works: Stock sizes → rent with deposit → clean.

SkillsFitting, customer service
Cost & equipmentStock, alteration access
Where to find workGrooms, groomsmen, graduates
PaymentPer rental
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Partner with tailors for alterations. Pros: Repeat event demand. Cons & risks: Sizing variety needed. Scam warning: Written terms for late returns and damage. Kenya notes: Weddings and graduations drive demand.

928. Mitumba bale wholesaling

What it is: Importing or buying bales of second-hand clothes and selling them to retailers. How it works: Source bales → sell whole or graded to traders.

SkillsTrade knowledge, grading
Cost & equipmentHigh: capital, storage
Where to find workMitumba retailers, online sellers
PaymentPer bale
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Learn the trade at major markets before investing. Pros: Large volumes. Cons & risks: Import rules and quality risk. Scam warning: Follow import regulations and pay duties. Kenya notes: Kenya's mitumba trade is huge; retailers rely on wholesalers.

929. School sweater knitting

What it is: Machine-knitting school sweaters in school colours. How it works: Get school specs → knit in bulk → deliver.

SkillsMachine knitting
Cost & equipmentKnitting machines, yarn
Where to find workSchools, uniform shops
PaymentPer sweater
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Approach schools and uniform shops with samples. Pros: Bulk orders. Cons & risks: Seasonal around school openings. Scam warning: Match exact school colours and specs. Kenya notes: Uniform suppliers need reliable sweater makers.

Summary

  • More textile businesses include tie-dye and batik, knitting and crochet, bridal and suit hire, mitumba bale wholesaling and school sweater knitting.
  • Quality, deposits and written rental terms matter.
  • Respect originality and import rules.
