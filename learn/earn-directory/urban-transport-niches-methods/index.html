Urban transport niches

Beyond matatus, boda bodas and ride-hailing (methods 297–298 and 312), towns need many other ways to move people, water and goods.

What these methods share

  • Licences: passenger transport needs NTSA and county licensing.
  • Insurance: protect vehicles, riders and cargo.
  • Maintenance: downtime is lost income.

861. Tuk-tuk (three-wheeler) passenger business

What it is: Running tuk-tuks for short trips in towns. How it works: Own or lease tuk-tuks → hire drivers or drive → daily earnings.

SkillsDriving, fleet management
Cost & equipmentTuk-tuk, insurance, NTSA and county licences
Where to find workTowns, coast, estates
PaymentPer trip or daily driver remittance
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Start with one vehicle; agree clear terms with drivers. Pros: Cheaper than cars. Cons & risks: Licensing and route rules. Scam warning: Use written agreements for hired drivers. Kenya notes: Tuk-tuks are common in Mombasa, Malindi and many towns.

862. Water bowser delivery

What it is: Delivering water by truck to homes, construction sites and estates. How it works: Source from licensed points → deliver → charge per litre or trip.

SkillsDriving, logistics
Cost & equipmentWater bowser, licences, source agreements
Where to find workEstates, construction sites, hotels
PaymentPer trip or volume
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Use licensed water sources and keep tanks clean. Pros: High demand during shortages. Cons & risks: Fuel costs; water quality responsibility. Scam warning: Never sell untreated water as drinking water. Kenya notes: Water shortages in Nairobi and other towns keep bowsers busy.

863. Handcart (mkokoteni) goods transport

What it is: Moving goods by handcart in markets and town centres. How it works: Hire or own a cart → move goods per trip.

SkillsStrength, reliability
Cost & equipmentHandcart, county permit where required
Where to find workMarkets, wholesalers, shops
PaymentPer trip
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Get a strong cart and build relationships with traders. Pros: Very low start-up. Cons & risks: Physically demanding. Scam warning: Agree prices before loading. Kenya notes: Busy markets like Gikomba and Marikiti rely on handcarts.

864. Motorbike work-to-own leasing

What it is: Buying motorbikes and leasing them to riders who pay daily until they own the bike. How it works: Buy bikes → sign lease agreements → collect daily payments → transfer ownership.

SkillsFinance management, contracts
Cost & equipmentCapital for bikes, tracking devices, insurance
Where to find workBoda boda riders
PaymentDaily repayments
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Use clear written contracts, trackers and insurance; start with a few trusted riders. Pros: Helps riders own assets. Cons & risks: Default and theft risks. Scam warning: Be fair: hidden charges ruin trust and may break consumer laws. Kenya notes: Many Kenyan boda riders work towards owning their bikes.

865. Bicycle delivery service

What it is: Delivering parcels, food and documents by bicycle in busy towns. How it works: Take orders from shops and apps → deliver by bike.

SkillsFitness, navigation
Cost & equipmentBicycle, phone, delivery bag
Where to find workRestaurants, shops, offices
PaymentPer delivery
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Partner with nearby restaurants and pharmacies. Pros: Cheap to run; beats traffic. Cons & risks: Weather and safety. Scam warning: Wear a helmet and reflective gear. Kenya notes: Short-distance deliveries in town centres suit bicycles.

Summary

  • Urban transport niches include tuk-tuks, water bowsers, handcarts, motorbike leasing and bicycle delivery.
  • Get NTSA and county licences, insurance and written agreements.
  • Treat riders and drivers fairly.
