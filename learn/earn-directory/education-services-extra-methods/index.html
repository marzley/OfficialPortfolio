More education services

Schools, parents and adult learners need more than tutoring. These add to teaching and education businesses (methods 204–220 and 557–570).

What these methods share

  • Child safety: background checks and safeguarding for anyone working with children.
  • Curriculum fit: align with CBE (competency-based education) where relevant.
  • Relationships: head teachers and parents' associations open doors.

910. KNEC examiner, marker and supervisor roles

What it is: Seasonal work marking national exams or supervising and invigilating exam centres. How it works: Qualified teachers apply or are appointed → training → marking or supervision sessions.

SkillsTeaching qualification, subject expertise, integrity
Cost & equipmentNone beyond being a qualified teacher
Where to find workKenya National Examinations Council, TSC
PaymentPer session or script
Realistic earningsLow to medium (seasonal)
Time to first incomeMonths (exam season)

How to start: Register and keep your TSC and KNEC details updated; apply when invitations open. Pros: Extra income for teachers. Cons & risks: Seasonal; strict confidentiality. Scam warning: Exam leaks are serious crimes: never share papers or marking schemes. Kenya notes: KNEC recruits examiners and supervisors every exam season.

911. Adult literacy and numeracy teacher

What it is: Teaching adults to read, write and count, often for business and daily life. How it works: Groups or one-to-one classes → practical lessons.

SkillsPatience, teaching skills
Cost & equipmentLow: books, venue
Where to find workChurches, chamas, NGOs, county adult education programmes
PaymentPer class or monthly
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Partner with community groups and adult education centres. Pros: Life-changing impact. Cons & risks: Low pay in community settings. Scam warning: Never charge for “certificates” you can't legitimately issue. Kenya notes: Adult education programmes exist through counties and the national government.

912. STEM and robotics clubs for schools

What it is: Running after-school clubs where children build robots and science projects. How it works: Bring kits → weekly sessions → competitions.

SkillsSTEM knowledge, teaching children
Cost & equipmentRobotics kits, laptops
Where to find workPrivate schools, academies, holiday camps
PaymentPer child per term
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with one school; enter local science and robotics competitions. Pros: Popular with parents. Cons & risks: Kit costs. Scam warning: Background checks and safeguarding are essential. Kenya notes: Fits CBE's focus on practical skills.

913. Debate and public speaking coaching

What it is: Coaching students and professionals in debate, speeches and presentations. How it works: Workshops or weekly coaching → competitions or events.

SkillsPublic speaking, coaching
Cost & equipmentLow
Where to find workSchools, universities, professionals
PaymentPer session or term
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Coach a school debate team to a competition; use results as proof. Pros: Valuable skill for life. Cons & risks: Competitive market. Scam warning: Be wary of “guaranteed scholarship” promises. Kenya notes: School debate competitions are popular in Kenya.

914. School trips organiser

What it is: Planning educational trips for schools: transport, venues, permits, meals and safety. How it works: Package trips → handle logistics → supervise.

SkillsPlanning, safety management
Cost & equipmentLow upfront (pay suppliers from deposits); insurance
Where to find workSchools, colleges
PaymentPer student or package
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Partner with transport and venues; get insurance. Pros: Repeat school clients. Cons & risks: High responsibility for safety. Scam warning: Use licensed, inspected vehicles only. Kenya notes: Trips to parks, museums and industries are common for schools.

915. Science lab equipment and chemical supply

What it is: Supplying schools with lab apparatus, chemicals and models. How it works: Take orders → source from licensed suppliers → deliver.

SkillsProduct knowledge, procurement
Cost & equipmentStock or supplier accounts, transport
Where to find workSecondary schools, colleges
PaymentPer order
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Register as a supplier with schools; follow chemical handling rules. Pros: Repeat termly orders. Cons & risks: Payment delays from schools. Scam warning: Handle chemicals safely and legally. Kenya notes: Schools buy lab supplies every term.

916. Early childhood teaching aids production

What it is: Making charts, flashcards, counters and learning materials for ECDE centres. How it works: Design → print or craft → sell to schools and parents.

SkillsDesign, crafts
Cost & equipmentPrinting access, materials
Where to find workECDE centres, daycares, parents
PaymentPer item or set
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Design materials aligned to the CBE early years curriculum. Pros: Low cost to start. Cons & risks: Competition from printed imports. Scam warning: Don't copy publishers' copyrighted content. Kenya notes: Many ECDE centres need affordable materials.

Summary

  • Education services include KNEC roles, adult literacy, STEM clubs, debate coaching, school trips, lab supplies and ECDE materials.
  • Safeguarding and background checks matter whenever you work with children.
  • Exam integrity and copyright must be respected.
