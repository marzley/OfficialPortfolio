Fishing, marine and lake economy

Lake Victoria, Lake Turkana, Lake Naivasha, the Indian Ocean coast and thousands of fish ponds support a big fishing economy. Beyond catching fish, there is money in trading, processing, ice, gear, boats, transport and tourism. These methods suit people living near water, and some (trading, processing, cold chain) work inland too.

What these methods share

  • Licences and rules: fishing and fish trading are regulated by the Kenya Fisheries Service (KeFS) and county governments, and landing beaches are run with Beach Management Units (BMUs). Passenger boats are regulated by the Kenya Maritime Authority (KMA).
  • Hygiene and cold chain: fish spoils within hours without ice; buyers and supermarkets demand clean handling.
  • Seasons and closed seasons: catches vary by season, and some species or areas have closed seasons to let stocks recover.
  • Safety on water: life jackets, weather checks and seaworthy boats save lives.

708. Licensed small-scale fishing

What it is: Catching fish with a boat and legal nets or lines and selling at the landing beach. How it works: Fishing trips → landing → sale to traders, hotels or processors.

SkillsFishing skills, swimming, boat handling
Cost & equipmentBoat (or crew share), legal nets, licence, life jackets
Where to find workLanding beaches, fish traders, hotels
PaymentCash or M-Pesa per catch
Realistic earningsLow to medium; varies with season
Time to first incomeDays (as crew)

How to start: Start as crew with an experienced fisher; join your BMU; use only legal mesh sizes. Pros: Daily cash income. Cons & risks: Dangerous weather, falling catches, theft of nets. Scam warning: Don't buy 'permits' from people who aren't BMU or KeFS officials. Kenya notes: Undersized mesh and monofilament nets are illegal and destroy fish stocks.

709. Fish trading (buying at the beach, selling in towns)

What it is: Buying fresh fish at landing beaches and selling to markets, estates and restaurants. How it works: Buy early at the beach → ice → transport → sell same day.

SkillsNegotiation, quality checking, customer care
Cost & equipmentCool boxes, ice, transport, trading capital
Where to find workTown markets, estates, hotels, WhatsApp groups
PaymentCash, M-Pesa
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Start small with one cool box and a few regular customers; build trust with beach suppliers. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Spoilage without ice; price swings. Scam warning: Beware of 'supplier' who asks for full payment before you see the fish. Kenya notes: Omena, tilapia and Nile perch are the big sellers; many traders are women.

710. Fish smoking, drying and processing

What it is: Smoking, sun-drying or frying fish to last longer and sell further away. How it works: Buy fresh fish → clean → smoke/dry on racks → pack → sell.

SkillsProcessing and hygiene
Cost & equipmentDrying racks (raised, off the sand), smoking kiln, packaging
Where to find workMarkets, supermarkets (with certification), inland towns
PaymentPer kg or packet
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Use raised racks and improved kilns; package in clean branded packs. Pros: Longer shelf life, sells inland. Cons & risks: Firewood costs; quality complaints. Scam warning: Never sell spoiled or chemically treated fish. Kenya notes: Dried omena sells across Kenya and to neighbouring countries.

711. Ice supply for fishermen and traders

What it is: Making or reselling ice for fish preservation. How it works: Ice machine or bulk purchase → sell blocks or flakes at beaches and markets.

SkillsBasic business and maintenance
Cost & equipmentIce machine or freezers, power (solar or grid), insulated storage
Where to find workLanding beaches, fish traders, events
PaymentPer block or bag
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Survey demand at your nearest beach; start by reselling, then invest in a machine. Pros: Essential and recurring. Cons & risks: Power costs and outages. Scam warning: As usual: keep records and avoid credit to unknown buyers. Kenya notes: Solar-powered ice and cold rooms are expanding on Lake Victoria beaches.

712. Fishing nets and gear sales and repair

What it is: Selling legal nets, hooks, lines, floats and life jackets; repairing nets. How it works: Stock gear → sell to fishers; mend nets for a fee.

SkillsProduct knowledge, net mending
Cost & equipmentStock, workspace
Where to find workFishers, BMUs, beach shops
PaymentPer item or repair
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn net mending; stock only legal mesh sizes. Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Credit sales. Scam warning: Selling illegal nets can lead to confiscation and fines. Kenya notes: Life jackets are a valuable stock item: many fishers still lack them.

713. Boat building and repair

What it is: Building and repairing wooden or fibreglass fishing and transport boats. How it works: Orders → construction → launching; repairs at the beach.

SkillsCarpentry or fibreglass work
Cost & equipmentTools, timber or fibreglass materials, workspace
Where to find workFishers, transporters, tourism operators
PaymentPer boat or job
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths (training), then per job

How to start: Apprentice with a boat builder; learn fibreglass repair. Pros: Skilled trade. Cons & risks: Material costs; slow payments. Scam warning: Agree deposits in writing. Kenya notes: Boats carrying passengers must meet KMA safety standards.

714. Outboard engine repair and servicing

What it is: Servicing and fixing outboard motors for fishing and transport boats. How it works: Call-outs to beaches; workshop repairs; spare parts.

SkillsSmall-engine mechanics
Cost & equipmentTools, spares
Where to find workFishers, boat operators, hotels
PaymentPer job
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Train in small engines; partner with a parts supplier. Pros: Repeat clients. Cons & risks: Fake spares. Scam warning: Use genuine parts and show customers old ones you replaced. Kenya notes: Mobile mechanics who come to the beach are in demand.

715. Passenger and cargo boat transport (licensed)

What it is: Carrying people and goods between islands, beaches and towns. How it works: Scheduled trips or hire; fares per person or cargo.

SkillsBoat handling, safety, navigation
Cost & equipmentSeaworthy boat, life jackets for all, KMA licence and insurance
Where to find workIsland communities, traders, tourists
PaymentFares
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths (licensing)

How to start: Get KMA registration, safety equipment and insurance; train crew. Pros: Steady income on busy routes. Cons & risks: Accidents are deadly if overloaded. Scam warning: Never overload; life jackets for every passenger, every trip. Kenya notes: Overloading and no life jackets cause many lake tragedies; safety builds trust and bookings.

716. Sport fishing charters

What it is: Taking tourists deep-sea or lake fishing. How it works: Bookings via hotels and online → trip → photos and catch-and-release.

SkillsFishing expertise, hospitality, boat skills
Cost & equipmentSuitable boat, gear, licences, insurance
Where to find workCoastal hotels, tourists, online travel platforms
PaymentPer trip
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Crew on an established charter; build reviews online. Pros: High-value clients. Cons & risks: Seasonal; costly equipment. Scam warning: Take deposits only through clear booking terms. Kenya notes: Malindi, Watamu and Shimoni are known for sport fishing; follow catch-and-release where required.

717. Seaweed farming

What it is: Growing seaweed in shallow coastal waters for sale to processors. How it works: Plant on lines → harvest after weeks → dry → sell.

SkillsFarming, patience
Cost & equipmentRopes, stakes, seedlings, drying space
Where to find workProcessors, cosmetics and food buyers, cooperatives
PaymentPer kg
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Join a seaweed farmers' group (common in Kwale). Pros: Low capital; suits women's groups. Cons & risks: Price depends on few buyers. Scam warning: Avoid buyers who change prices after harvest; use group contracts. Kenya notes: Kwale County seaweed farming has grown with community groups and processors.

718. Ornamental fish breeding

What it is: Breeding aquarium fish (goldfish, guppies, koi) for pet shops and hobbyists. How it works: Breeding tanks or ponds → raise → sell.

SkillsFish care, water quality
Cost & equipmentTanks or small ponds, pumps, feed
Where to find workPet shops, aquarium installers, online
PaymentPer fish
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with hardy species; partner with aquarium installers (method 598). Pros: Higher price per fish. Cons & risks: Disease losses. Scam warning: Don't import species without permits; some are invasive. Kenya notes: Growing demand from homes, offices and hotels.

719. Fish cold chain distribution

What it is: Moving fish in insulated or refrigerated transport to supermarkets and institutions. How it works: Contracts → collection → cold transport → delivery.

SkillsLogistics, hygiene
Cost & equipmentInsulated or refrigerated vehicle, ice, permits
Where to find workSupermarkets, hotels, schools, hospitals
PaymentPer delivery or contract
Realistic earningsMedium to high
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Start with an insulated van and a few hotel contracts. Pros: Big contracts. Cons & risks: High costs; strict hygiene. Scam warning: Keep temperature records; buyers reject warm fish. Kenya notes: Demand for fish inland (Nairobi, Central, Rift) keeps growing.

720. Fish feed and fingerling supply

What it is: Supplying quality fish feed and fingerlings (young fish) to fish farmers. How it works: Buy or produce → deliver to pond farmers → advise.

SkillsAquaculture knowledge
Cost & equipmentStock, transport, certified hatchery source
Where to find workFish farmers, cooperatives, counties
PaymentPer kg or fingerling
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Source from certified hatcheries; learn basic pond management. Pros: Repeat orders each cycle. Cons & risks: Fingerling deaths in transport. Scam warning: Buy fingerlings only from certified hatcheries; poor stock ruins farmers. Kenya notes: Pond farming has spread in Central, Western and Nyanza.

721. Landing beach services (cleaning, weighing, storage)

What it is: Paid services at landing beaches: weighing, cleaning fish, cold storage, porters. How it works: Charge fishers and traders per service.

SkillsReliability, basic hygiene
Cost & equipmentScales, cleaning area, storage
Where to find workFishers, traders, BMUs
PaymentPer service
Realistic earningsLow
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Work with the BMU to offer an organised service. Pros: Daily cash. Cons & risks: Competition. Scam warning: Keep fair, visible prices. Kenya notes: Clean, organised beaches attract better buyers.

Summary

  • Fishing jobs go far beyond catching fish: trading, processing, ice, gear, boats, engines, transport, tourism, seaweed and supply.
  • Follow KeFS, BMU and KMA rules; use legal nets; respect closed seasons.
  • Ice and hygiene protect profits; life jackets protect lives.
