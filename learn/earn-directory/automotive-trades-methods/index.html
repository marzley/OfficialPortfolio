Automotive trades and services

Kenya has millions of vehicles and motorbikes, and they all need maintenance, parts and services. Skilled automotive workers increasingly find customers through Google Maps, WhatsApp, TikTok repair videos and Facebook groups.

What these methods share

  • Training: TVET courses and apprenticeships build real skills; certifications add trust.
  • Genuine parts and honest quotes build long-term customers.
  • Online presence: a Google Business Profile, before/after videos and reviews bring customers (Google Business).
  • Safety and law: tools, chemicals and road safety; vehicle modifications must stay legal.

431. Auto electrician

What it is: Diagnosing and repairing vehicle electrical systems: wiring, lights, starters, alternators, sensors. How it works: Workshop or mobile call-outs with diagnostic tools.

SkillsAutomotive electrical training, diagnostics
Cost & equipmentMultimeter, diagnostic scanner, tools
Where to find workCar owners, garages, fleets, matatu operators
PaymentPer job
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks (after training)

How to start: Train at a TVET; apprentice with an experienced auto electrician. Pros: Modern cars need electrical specialists. Cons & risks: Keeping up with new technology. Scam warning: Never tamper with odometers or emissions systems illegally. Kenya notes: Hybrid and imported cars bring demand for diagnostic skills.

432. Panel beating and spray painting

What it is: Repairing body damage and repainting vehicles. How it works: Workshop jobs, insurance-assessed repairs.

SkillsBodywork, painting, colour matching
Cost & equipmentWorkshop, compressor, spray booth (ideal)
Where to find workCar owners, insurers' approved garages, fleets
PaymentPer job or insurance payments
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Apprentice; seek insurer approval when established. Pros: Accidents create steady demand. Cons & risks: Paint fumes; environmental rules. Scam warning: Don't inflate insurance claims. Kenya notes: Follow environmental rules for paint waste.

433. Tyre services and wheel alignment

What it is: Selling and fitting tyres, puncture repair, balancing and alignment. How it works: Roadside or shop-based services.

SkillsTyre fitting, alignment equipment operation
Cost & equipmentTools, compressor; alignment machine for advanced services
Where to find workMotorists, boda boda riders, fleets
PaymentPer service
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Start with puncture repair; expand to tyre sales. Pros: Constant demand. Cons & risks: Roadside safety. Scam warning: Don't sell worn tyres as new. Kenya notes: Boda boda tyre services are a huge market.

434. Motorbike repair and servicing

What it is: Repairing and servicing motorbikes. How it works: Roadside or workshop.

SkillsMotorbike mechanics
Cost & equipmentTools, spare parts
Where to find workBoda boda riders, delivery riders
PaymentPer job
Realistic earningsLow to medium

How to start: Apprentice; locate near boda stages. Pros: Huge market. Cons & risks: Price competition. Scam warning: Use genuine parts; fake parts cause accidents. Kenya notes: Electric motorbikes are growing; learn their systems.

435. Electric vehicle and e-bike servicing

SkillsEV electrical training, battery safety

PaymentPer job or employment
Realistic earningsLow–medium to medium
Time to first incomeMonths (training)

How to start: Seek training from e-mobility companies or TVET programmes. Pros: Future-proof skill. Cons & risks: High-voltage safety. Scam warning: As above. Kenya notes: Electric motorbikes are spreading in Nairobi and other towns.

SkillsAuto electrical, device configuration
Cost & equipmentTools, devices
Where to find workCar owners, fleets, car hire businesses, insurers' requirements

Realistic earningsLow to medium

How to start: Partner with licensed tracking providers. Pros: Insurers often require tracking. Cons & risks: Wiring liability. Scam warning: Never install trackers on vehicles without the owner's consent. Kenya notes: Car hire and logistics firms require trackers.

438. Spare parts reselling online

SkillsParts knowledge, customer service
Cost & equipmentCapital, stock
Where to find workMechanics, car owners, groups

How to start: Specialise in popular models; sell genuine parts with clear labels. Pros: High demand. Cons & risks: Counterfeits. Scam warning: Never sell stolen parts. Kenya notes: Clearly label genuine vs aftermarket parts.

What it is: Towing broken-down vehicles and roadside help. How it works: Calls from motorists and insurers.

441. Vehicle import facilitation (licensed clearing agents)

What it is: Helping people import vehicles: sourcing, shipping, clearing. How it works: Licensed clearing and forwarding processes.

SkillsCustoms procedures, licensing
Cost & equipmentLicences
Where to find workBuyers, dealers
PaymentFees
Realistic earningsMedium
Time to first incomeMonths

How to start: Work with licensed clearing firms. Pros: Steady demand. Cons & risks: Regulatory compliance. Scam warning: Fake car importers taking deposits; verify licences and company history. Kenya notes: Vehicle age limits and KRA customs rules apply.

442. Used car dealing and brokerage

What it is: Buying and selling used cars or connecting sellers and buyers. How it works: Online listings, inspections, transfers.

SkillsCar valuation, negotiation, paperwork
Cost & equipmentCapital (dealing) or none (brokerage)
Where to find workOnline marketplaces, networks
PaymentMargins or commissions
Realistic earningsLow to high
Time to first incomeWeeks

How to start: Learn ownership verification and NTSA transfers. Pros: High value per deal. Cons & risks: Fraud risks. Scam warning: Verify logbooks and ownership through official systems. Kenya notes: Ownership transfers go through NTSA's online system.

443. Pre-purchase car inspection services

What it is: Inspecting used cars for buyers before purchase. How it works: Inspection checklist, diagnostics, report.

SkillsMechanical expertise, diagnostics
Cost & equipmentScanner, tools
Where to find workCar buyers, diaspora buyers
PaymentPer inspection
Realistic earningsLow to medium
Time to first incomeDays

How to start: Offer independent, written reports. Pros: Builds trust. Cons & risks: Liability for missed faults. Scam warning: Stay independent from sellers. Kenya notes: Diaspora buyers often need trusted inspectors.

Summary

  • Automotive methods include auto electrics, panel beating, tyres, motorbike and EV servicing, tracking, accessories, parts reselling, upholstery, towing, import facilitation, used car dealing, inspections, valuation and battery/lubricant retail.
  • Train properly, use genuine parts, follow NTSA and customs rules, and market with real before/after content.
