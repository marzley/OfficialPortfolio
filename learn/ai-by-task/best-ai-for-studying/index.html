Best AI for studying and learning: tutors, notes, flashcards and exam revision

AI can be the best study partner you've ever had: patient, available at midnight, and able to explain the same idea ten different ways. It can also make you lazy if it does your thinking for you. The difference is how you use it.

The best AI tools for studying

ToolBest for
NotebookLMUpload your notes, past papers or a textbook chapter; ask questions, get study guides, quizzes and audio summaries based only on your material
Chat assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)Explaining topics, step-by-step help, practice questions. Several now have learning or study modes that guide you instead of just giving answers
Khanmigo (Khan Academy)A tutor built for learning maths and science, designed not to just hand out answers
QuizletFlashcards and practice tests, with AI help to create sets
AnkiSpaced-repetition flashcards: the best way to remember facts long-term (free on computers and Android)

Study methods that actually work (with AI)

Research on learning shows two methods beat re-reading notes:

  1. Active recall: testing yourself.
  2. Spaced repetition: reviewing at growing intervals (1 day, 3 days, a week…).

Accuracy and honesty

AI can make mistakes in maths and facts, especially in local syllabus details. Check answers against your textbook and teacher. Follow your school's rules on AI; when in doubt, ask your teacher.

Studying on a budget

Most of these tools work on a phone. NotebookLM, Anki (Android), Quizlet's basic features and the big chat assistants have free plans. Download notes and flashcards when you have Wi-Fi to save data.

Think about it: A student uses AI to answer every assignment and scores well, but fails the exam. Why, and what should they change?Show answer

In exams there's no AI: they never practised recalling and applying the knowledge themselves. They should use AI for explanations, hints, quizzes and feedback on their own work, and practise past papers without help.

Summary

  • NotebookLM for your own notes; chat assistants and Khanmigo as tutors; Anki and Quizlet for flashcards.
  • Use AI for active recall (quizzes) and spaced repetition (flashcards).
  • Ask for hints and feedback, not finished answers.
  • Check accuracy and follow school rules.
