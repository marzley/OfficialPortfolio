Content creation

Creators earn from ads, sponsorships/brand deals, affiliate links, selling their own products and fan support. Ads alone are rarely enough in Kenya at the start; brand deals and your own products usually pay more.

YouTube

  • YouTube Partner Program (ad revenue) usually needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. A lower tier for fan-funding features opens earlier. Check YouTube's current requirements.
  • Long videos earn far more per view than Shorts.
  • Good niches for Kenyan creators: tech reviews and tutorials, farming and agribusiness, personal finance and investing (be careful and honest), education (KCSE/CBC revision, coding), cooking, travel, comedy, Kiswahili content.

TikTok and Instagram

  • Great for growing fast and getting brand deals.
  • Direct payment programmes (e.g. TikTok's Creator Rewards) are not available in every country; check whether Kenya is eligible before counting on them.
  • Earn through brand promotions, TikTok Shop/affiliate where available, live gifts, and selling your own services.

Facebook

  • Facebook content monetisation and in-stream ads have eligibility and country rules that change often; check Meta's Creator Studio/Professional Dashboard.
  • Facebook groups and pages are powerful for selling local services.

How to actually grow

  1. Choose one niche and one main platform.
  2. Post consistently (e.g. 3 short videos a week or 1 long video a week) for at least 6 months.
  3. Hook viewers in the first 3 seconds; make thumbnails and titles clear.
  4. Study your analytics: which videos keep people watching? Make more like those.
  5. Build an email list or WhatsApp channel so you own your audience.

Equipment

Start with your phone. Good sound (a KSh 1,500–3,000 lapel mic) and light (a window or ring light) matter more than an expensive camera.
