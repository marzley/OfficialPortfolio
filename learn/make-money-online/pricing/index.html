Pricing your work

Charging too little is the most common beginner mistake. It attracts difficult clients and burns you out.

Three ways to price

  1. Hourly: good when the work is unclear or ongoing (support, VA work). Track time honestly.
  2. Fixed price per project: good when the scope is clear ("5-page website"). Clients prefer it. Write exactly what's included.
  3. Value or package pricing: price by the result for the client ("Online shop with M-Pesa: KSh 40,000"). Offer 3 packages (Basic, Standard, Premium); most clients pick the middle.

Work out your minimum rate

Your rate must cover what you want to earn, your costs (internet, power, software, laptop), the platform fee and tax, and only part of your time is billable (the rest is finding clients, admin and learning).

Ways to raise your income

  • Specialise: "M-Pesa integration developer" earns more than "web developer".
  • Raise your price by 10–20% every few new clients while demand is good.
  • Sell monthly retainers: maintenance, social media, SEO. Predictable income.
  • Deposits: ask 50% upfront for new clients. Never start big work without a deposit.
  • Put scope and revisions in writing ("includes 2 rounds of changes").

Talking about price

  • Give the price confidently, then stop talking.
  • If the client says it's too much, reduce the scope, not the price: "For KSh 20,000 I can do 3 pages instead of 6."
