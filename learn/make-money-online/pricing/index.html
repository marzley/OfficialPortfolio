Pricing your work: hourly vs project rates, packages, deposits and raising your prices

Pricing is where many freelancers lose money: charging too little out of fear, underestimating time, giving unlimited revisions, or starting work without a deposit. This unit gives you practical methods to set prices that cover your costs and grow with your skills, plus packages, quotes, contracts and how to raise rates confidently.

What you will learn

  • What a price must cover
  • Calculating your minimum hourly rate
  • Hourly vs fixed-price vs value-based vs retainer pricing
  • Researching market rates (local and international)
  • Building packages (good, better, best)
  • Writing quotes and simple contracts; deposits and milestones
  • Handling discounts, scope creep and late payments
  • When and how to raise your prices

What your price must cover

Your price isn't just "time spent". It must cover:

  • Your living costs and savings
  • Business costs: data/internet, electricity, equipment (laptop wear), software subscriptions, platform fees, M-Pesa/transfer fees
  • Non-billable time: finding clients, proposals, admin, learning (often 30–50% of a freelancer's time)
  • Taxes
  • Risk: months with less work

Calculating your minimum hourly rate

Example:

ItemKSh per month
Living costs40,000
Business costs (data, power, tools, equipment fund)8,000
Savings7,000
Tax allowance5,000
Total needed60,000

If you can bill 80 hours a month (about 4 hours a day, 20 days, because the rest goes to admin and finding work):

60,000 ÷ 80 = KSh 750 per hour minimum

This is your floor: below it you're losing money. Charge more when the market, your skill and the value allow.

Pricing models

ModelHow it worksBest forWatch out
HourlyPaid per hour worked (tracked)Ongoing support, unclear scope, VA workIncome capped by hours; clients may watch the clock
Fixed-price (project)One price for defined deliverablesWebsites, logos, articles, videosMust define scope precisely; estimate time carefully
Per unitPer word, per audio minute, per product listing, per pageWriting, transcription, data entryMake sure unit price covers effort
Value-basedPriced on the value to the clientCopywriting, marketing, systems that save/make moneyNeeds confidence and understanding of client's business
RetainerMonthly fee for ongoing workSocial media management, maintenance, bookkeepingDefine what's included each month

Fixed-price is often better for skilled freelancers: as you get faster, you earn more per hour, and clients prefer knowing the total cost.

Researching market rates

  • Platforms: look at profiles similar to yours (experience, reviews, location) on Upwork/Fiverr.
  • Local market: ask peers, check what agencies charge, see price lists of competitors.
  • Job posts: budgets in posts show what clients expect (though many lowball).
  • Communities: freelancer groups often discuss rates.

International clients often pay more than local ones for the same skill, but competition is global. Local clients value proximity, local knowledge (M-Pesa, KRA, Kiswahili) and in-person meetings.

Tips:

  • Most clients choose the middle option; make it the best value.
  • Clearly list what's included and not included.
  • Add upsells: extra pages, express delivery, content writing, logo design, hosting.

Quotes, contracts, deposits and milestones

A good quote includes

  1. Client and project name, date, quote number, validity (e.g. 14 days)
  2. Scope: exact deliverables
  3. Exclusions: what's not included (content writing, hosting, extra pages)
  4. Timeline and what you need from the client (content, photos, access) and by when
  5. Price and payment terms: deposit, milestones, final payment, methods (M-Pesa till/paybill, bank)
  6. Revisions included and the cost of extra revisions
  7. Ownership: rights transfer on full payment

Deposits and milestones

  • New clients: deposit before starting (e.g. 40–50%).
  • Larger projects: milestones (e.g. 40% start, 30% design approval, 30% launch).
  • Platforms: use escrow milestones.
  • Never deliver final files (source files, full website access) before final payment from unknown clients; share previews/watermarked versions instead.

Scope creep, discounts and late payments

Scope creep

Clients often ask for "one small extra thing" repeatedly. Protect yourself:

  • Refer back to the agreed scope politely: "That's a great idea; it's outside the current scope. I can add it for KSh X and 2 extra days."
  • Use a change request for additions.

Discounts

  • Prefer adding value over cutting price ("I can't reduce the price, but I can include an extra revision").
  • If you discount, reduce scope too, or offer discounts for prepayment or longer retainers.
  • Avoid very cheap prices to "get started": they attract difficult clients and are hard to raise later.

Late payments

  • Clear due dates on invoices; reminders before and after the due date.
  • Pause work if payments are overdue (state this in your terms).
  • For platform work, use the platform's dispute processes.

Raising your prices

Raise prices when:

  • You're consistently busy (more demand than time).
  • You've gained skills, results and reviews.
  • Your costs increased.

How:

  • New clients get new prices immediately.
  • Existing clients: give notice (e.g. 30 days) and explain the value you provide.
  • Increase in steps (e.g. 10–25%), and watch the response.
Think about it: A designer charges KSh 2,000 per logo, spends about 10 hours on each including revisions, and wonders why they're always broke. What's wrong, and what would you suggest?Show answer

They earn about KSh 200 per hour before costs, far below a sustainable rate. Suggest calculating a minimum hourly rate, limiting revisions in packages, raising prices (or offering packages like logo + brand kit), improving the portfolio to target better-paying clients, and taking deposits.

Summary

  • Prices must cover living costs, business costs, non-billable time, taxes and risk.
  • Minimum hourly rate = total monthly needs ÷ realistic billable hours.
  • Choose models wisely: hourly, fixed-price, per unit, value-based or retainers.
  • Research market rates; build good/better/best packages with clear inclusions and upsells.
  • Use written quotes with scope, exclusions, timeline, payment terms and revisions; take deposits and use milestones.
  • Manage scope creep with change requests, add value instead of discounting, and raise prices as demand and skills grow.
