Sell physical products online: sourcing, pricing, shop setup, payments and delivery in Kenya

Selling physical products online is the most familiar kind of online business in Kenya: clothes on Instagram, phone accessories on TikTok, cosmetics on WhatsApp, electronics on Jumia. It works because people already buy this way. It is also a real business with stock, cash flow, delivery and returns, so this unit treats it seriously: from choosing a product to getting it to the customer's door.

What you will learn

  • Business models: own stock, made-to-order, marketplace selling, dropshipping
  • Choosing products that sell and make a profit
  • Sourcing locally (Gikomba, Eastleigh, wholesalers) and importing
  • Calculating cost, margin and price correctly
  • Where to sell: social media, WhatsApp Business, marketplaces, your own store
  • Payments: M-Pesa Till/Paybill, pay on delivery, cards
  • Delivery and logistics across Kenya
  • Customer service, returns, legal registration and records

Step 1: Choose a product

A good product to sell online:

  • Solves a problem or fits a clear desire (looks, convenience, status, gifts)
  • Is light and hard to break (cheaper delivery, fewer returns)
  • Has a margin of at least 30–50% after all costs
  • Isn't sold everywhere at the same price
  • Photographs and films well

Examples that often work: phone accessories, fashion for a specific style or size, beauty and hair products, baby items, home organisers, kitchen gadgets, fitness gear, branded merchandise, school supplies before term opens.

Avoid regulated or risky products at the start

Medicines, supplements with health claims, alcohol, counterfeit brands, cosmetics with banned ingredients and electrical goods without standards marks can bring KEBS/PPB/county problems. Start with simple, legal goods.

Order a small test batch first. Sell it, learn what customers ask for, then reorder.

Step 3: Price correctly

Most beginners lose money because they forget costs. Calculate the landed cost of one unit:

Cost itemExample (KSh)
Buying price400
Transport/shipping to you (share per unit)50
Packaging20
Marketing (ads ÷ units sold)80
Payment fees and losses10
Total cost per unit560

If you sell at KSh 1,000, your profit is 1,000 − 560 = KSh 440, a margin of 44%.

Delivery is usually charged separately to the customer, but if you offer "free delivery", add it to your costs.

Step 4: Set up where you sell

  1. WhatsApp Business: business profile, catalogue with prices and photos, quick replies, labels for "new order", "paid", "delivered".
  2. Instagram / TikTok / Facebook: post products in use, behind the scenes, customer reviews, short videos; link to WhatsApp or your store. TikTok Shop and Facebook/Instagram shops have country restrictions, so check what is available in Kenya.
  3. Marketplaces (Jumia, Kilimall and others): register as a seller, upload products with good photos and descriptions; you pay commissions but get ready-made traffic.
  4. Your own online store: WordPress + WooCommerce, Shopify, or a custom site with M-Pesa checkout. This builds your brand and you own the customer relationship.

Product photo checklist: natural daylight, plain background, several angles, the product in use, size comparison, and a short video.

Reduce fake orders

For pay-on-delivery, confirm orders by phone, ask for a small delivery deposit for far locations, and keep a list of numbers that refused deliveries.

Step 6: Deliver

  • Nairobi: boda-boda riders, delivery apps and courier companies; agree a fixed price per zone.
  • Countrywide: bus/shuttle parcel services, courier companies and pick-up point networks; share the tracking or receipt photo with the customer.
  • Package well: waterproof bag, the item wrapped, a thank-you note and your social handles.

Step 7: Customer service and returns

  • Reply quickly (within an hour during business hours).
  • Have a clear exchange/return policy and stick to it.
  • Ask happy customers for a review or photo; it's your best marketing.
  • Track repeat customers and offer them early access to new stock.

Legal and records

  • Register a business name on eCitizen (Business Registration Service) when you grow; get a KRA PIN and check whether you need a county business permit.
  • Keep a sales and expenses record (a simple Excel or Google Sheet): date, item, quantity, price, cost, customer, payment reference.
  • Learn about turnover tax and eTIMS invoicing requirements in the taxes lesson.
Think about it: You sell a phone case at KSh 500. It costs you KSh 250 to buy, KSh 30 packaging, and you spend KSh 3,000 on ads that bring 20 sales. Are you making money?Show answer

Ads per unit = 3,000 ÷ 20 = KSh 150. Total cost = 250 + 30 + 150 = KSh 430. Profit = 500 − 430 = KSh 70 per case (14%). It's barely profitable: raise the price, bundle (case + screen protector), or improve ads so they bring more sales per shilling.

Summary

  • Choose a model (own stock, made-to-order, marketplace, supplier delivery, pre-orders) that fits your capital and risk.
  • Pick light, legal products with 30–50% margin; test with small batches.
  • Always calculate total cost per unit (buying, transport, packaging, ads, fees) before pricing.
  • Sell through WhatsApp Business, social media, marketplaces and eventually your own store.
  • Offer trusted payments (M-Pesa, pay on delivery), reliable delivery, great service and clear returns; register and keep records as you grow.
