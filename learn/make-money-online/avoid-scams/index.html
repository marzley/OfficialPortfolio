Avoiding online money scams

Scammers target people who want to earn online. Learn the patterns and you'll save money and time.

Common scams in Kenya

ScamHow it looksThe truth
Investment/"daily earnings" apps"Deposit KSh 1,000, earn KSh 250 daily"Pays early users with new users' money (Ponzi), then disappears
Forex / crypto "account managers""Send me money, I'll trade for you, 50% monthly returns"No real trader guarantees returns; they vanish with your money
Pyramid / network schemesEarn by recruiting others who pay to joinMost people lose money
Task scamsLike videos/rate products, then "top up" to unlock earningsYou'll keep topping up and never withdraw
Fake jobsHigh-pay job, asks for a fee for training, medical, visa or uniformReal employers don't charge you
Fake clientsWants you to install "their software", or sends a cheque/overpaymentMalware or a reversed payment
M-Pesa reversal"I sent money to you by mistake, please return it"Check your actual M-Pesa balance, not the SMS; call 100/Safaricom
Account takeover"Share the code we just sent you"The code gives them your WhatsApp or M-Pesa

Rules that protect you

  1. Guaranteed high returns = scam. Real investments (Treasury bills, money market funds, SACCOs, stocks) have modest returns and are regulated.
  2. You should never pay to get a job or to withdraw your own money.
  3. Never share OTP codes, PINs or passwords.
  4. Verify: is the company registered? Is the investment licensed by the Capital Markets Authority (CMA) or the Central Bank of Kenya? Search the name + "scam".
  5. Slow down: scammers create urgency ("offer ends today").
  6. If it sounds too good to be true, it is.

If you've been scammed

  • Report to your bank or Safaricom immediately (they can sometimes freeze funds).
  • Report to the DCI and keep screenshots and transaction codes.
  • Warn others; don't try to "recover" your money through people who promise to get it back for a fee. That's the next scam.
