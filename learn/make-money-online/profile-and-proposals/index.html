A winning profile and proposals: templates, examples and what clients look for

On freelance platforms and LinkedIn, your profile and proposals are your shop window and your sales pitch. Clients scan dozens of applicants in minutes; most proposals are generic copy-paste messages they ignore. This unit shows exactly how to build a profile that attracts the right clients and write proposals that get replies, with templates and before/after examples.

What you will learn

  • How clients choose freelancers
  • Profile photo, title, overview, portfolio, skills and rates
  • Writing a specialised headline that ranks in search
  • The proposal formula, with templates for different jobs
  • Before/after proposal examples
  • Interviews and follow-ups
  • Tracking and improving your win rate

How clients choose

Clients ask: Does this person understand my need? Can they do it well? Can I trust them? Is the price reasonable? They look at:

  1. Relevance: your title and first lines match their job.
  2. Proof: portfolio samples and reviews in the same type of work.
  3. Communication: clear, correct, specific writing.
  4. Reliability signals: verified identity, complete profile, response time, job success.
  5. Price and value.

Building your profile

Photo

Clear, friendly, professional headshot: good light (face a window), plain background, your face filling most of the frame, smiling, no sunglasses or group photos. It builds trust immediately.

Title / headline

Specific titles beat general ones and match what clients search for.

WeakStrong
"Freelancer""Shopify & WooCommerce Developer: Fast, Mobile-Ready Online Stores"
"Writer""SEO Blog Writer for Fintech & Personal Finance Brands"
"Graphic designer""Brand Identity Designer: Logos & Brand Kits for Restaurants and Cafés"
"Virtual assistant""Executive Virtual Assistant: Inbox, Calendar & Travel Management"

Overview (About)

Structure:

  1. Hook: who you help and the result ("I help small e-commerce brands turn browsers into buyers with clear, persuasive product pages.").
  2. What you offer: 3–5 services.
  3. Proof: experience, results, notable clients or projects (numbers where possible).
  4. Process/why you: how you work (communication, deadlines, revisions).
  5. Call to action: "Message me with your project details and I'll reply within a few hours."

Keep paragraphs short; the first 2 lines matter most (they show in search previews).

Example:

I build fast, mobile-friendly WordPress and WooCommerce websites for small businesses, with M-Pesa and card payments, SEO basics and easy content editing. What I do: business websites · online shops · speed optimisation · bug fixes · website maintenance Recent work: an online shop for a Nairobi cosmetics brand (300+ products, M-Pesa checkout); a booking site for a Mombasa tour company that cut phone enquiries in half. How I work: clear milestones, daily updates, and 30 days of free fixes after launch. Send me your project details and I'll reply with ideas and a timeline.

Portfolio

  • 3–6 best, relevant pieces (not everything you've ever done).
  • Each with a title, the problem, what you did, tools used and results.
  • Concept/practice work is fine if labelled honestly.

Skills, rates and other sections

  • Add relevant skills/tags (they affect search).
  • Set a reasonable rate (see the pricing lesson); you can adjust per project.
  • Complete education, certifications, languages (Kiswahili and others can be an advantage), and verify your identity.

The proposal formula

Clients receive many proposals; yours must show you read their job in the first two lines.

Keep it short (100–200 words), specific and free of errors.

Template: web development

Hi [Name], you need a WooCommerce shop for your skincare products with M-Pesa payments and a clean, mobile-first design. I recently built a similar store for a Nairobi beauty brand (link): 120 products, M-Pesa checkout, and page speed under 2 seconds on mobile. For your shop I'd: 1. Set up WooCommerce with your products, categories and variations 2. Integrate M-Pesa (STK push) and card payments 3. Optimise images and speed for mobile users 4. Train you to add products yourself I can deliver in about 2–3 weeks. Do you already have product photos and descriptions, or will you need help with those? Best regards, [Your name]

Template: writing

Hi [Name], you're looking for weekly SEO articles on personal finance for a Kenyan audience. I write for [publication/blog] on saving, SACCOs and money market funds; one of my articles ranks on page one for "best money market funds in Kenya" (sample link). My process: keyword research, an outline for your approval, a well-sourced draft with headings and meta description, and one revision. Which keywords or topics are your priority for the first month? Thanks, [Your name]

Template: virtual assistance

Hi [Name], managing your inbox and calendar while travelling between clients sounds overwhelming; I can take that off your plate. I currently support a real estate agent with inbox management, scheduling and CRM updates, saving her about 10 hours a week. I'm available 9am–5pm EAT, which overlaps with your UK mornings. I'm comfortable with Google Workspace, Calendly and HubSpot. Would you like me to start with a short paid trial week? Kind regards, [Your name]

Before and after

Before (generic, ignored):

Dear Sir/Madam, I am a hardworking and experienced freelancer with many skills. I have read your job and I can do it perfectly. Please give me a chance. I am available 24/7. Thank you.

Problems: no evidence of reading the job, no proof, no specifics, sounds desperate, generic greeting.

After (specific, gets replies):

Hi Sarah, you need 10 Instagram carousel designs to launch your new juice brand next month, matching your green and orange branding. Here are carousels I designed for a Nairobi café's launch (link); their posts got 3× more saves than usual. I'd create a template set first for your approval, then the 10 carousels with your copy, delivered as PNG and editable Canva links. Could you share your brand guidelines or logo files? Thanks, Achieng

Interviews and follow-ups

  • Reply fast when a client messages: speed matters.
  • Prepare: reread the job, prepare questions, have portfolio links ready.
  • Video calls: good light, quiet room, test internet; be professional and friendly.
  • Clarify scope: deliverables, deadlines, revisions, communication, payment milestones.
  • Follow up once politely if no reply after a few days, adding value ("I had another idea for your homepage...").

Track and improve

Keep a simple spreadsheet: date, job, platform, proposal version, reply (yes/no), hired (yes/no). After 20–30 proposals, review:

  • Which job types reply most? Focus there.
  • Which openings work? Reuse the structure (not the exact text).
  • Are you applying too late? Apply early to fresh jobs.
  • Is your price wildly off?
Think about it: You've sent 40 proposals in two weeks with no replies. What would you check and change?Show answer

Check relevance (are you applying to jobs that match your specialisation and portfolio?), the first two lines (personalised to the client's need?), proof (relevant samples linked?), profile completeness and photo, timing (applying early?), and length (short and specific?). Improve, then track results on the next 20 proposals.

Summary

  • Clients choose based on relevance, proof, communication, reliability and value.
  • Profile: professional photo, specialised searchable title, results-focused overview, 3–6 relevant portfolio pieces, verified details.
  • Proposals: personal opening about their need, relevant proof, specific approach, timeline, a smart question, short sign-off.
  • Avoid generic, desperate messages; reply fast; clarify scope in interviews; follow up once.
  • Track proposals and improve based on data.
