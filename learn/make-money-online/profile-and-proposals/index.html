A profile and proposals that win work

Your profile headline

Say what you do + for whom + a result. Bad: "Hardworking, honest freelancer". Good:

  • "WordPress & M-Pesa developer | Fast, secure websites for small businesses"
  • "Logo & brand designer for restaurants and cafés"
  • "Virtual assistant | Email, calendar & customer support for busy founders"

Your overview (bio): a simple structure

  1. The client's problem in their words.
  2. How you solve it, with specific skills and tools.
  3. Proof: results, examples, numbers.
  4. Call to action: "Send me a message with your project and I'll reply within an hour."

The proposal formula

Clients get 30–50 proposals and read the first two lines. Make them about the client, not you.

Why it works: uses their name, repeats their need, shows matching proof, gives a clear plan, price and time, and ends with a question so they reply.

Common mistakes

  • Copy-paste proposals ("Dear Sir/Madam, I have read your job…").
  • Talking about yourself for three paragraphs.
  • No samples, or samples unrelated to the job.
  • Applying to 50 jobs a day. Instead, apply to 5–10 good matches with custom proposals.

Getting your first reviews

  • Take small, quick jobs you can do perfectly.
  • Over-deliver on the first 3–5 jobs.
  • After delivering, ask politely: "If you're happy, a short review would really help me."
