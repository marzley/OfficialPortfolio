Working as a virtual assistant or online admin support

A virtual assistant (VA) helps businesses and busy professionals with admin, customer support, scheduling, research and social media, remotely. It's one of the most accessible online jobs: you need reliable internet, a laptop, good communication and organisation, and everyday digital skills taught on this learning hub.

What VAs do

AreaTasks
AdminEmail inbox management, calendar scheduling, data entry, filing documents
Customer supportReplying to emails, chats and WhatsApp messages; handling orders
ResearchFinding suppliers, leads, prices, travel options; summarising findings
Social mediaScheduling posts, replying to comments, simple Canva graphics
E-commerce supportUploading products, updating stock, processing orders
Bookkeeping supportRecording receipts and invoices in spreadsheets or tools like QuickBooks
ContentTranscription, proofreading, formatting documents and presentations

Soft skills matter as much: clear written English, reliability, confidentiality, time management, and asking good questions.

Build proof before you apply

  1. Pick a focus: e.g. "Email and calendar management for real estate agents" or "Customer support for online shops".
  2. Create sample work: an organised inbox system with labels, a cleaned-up spreadsheet, a social media calendar, a research report. Put them in a simple portfolio (Google Drive folder or website).
  3. Take free certificates: Google's free courses, HubSpot Academy (customer service, social media), and others.
  4. Practise locally: help a local business, church or school with admin for a testimonial.

Where to find work

  • Freelance platforms: Upwork, Fiverr, OnlineJobs.ph-style boards, LinkedIn Jobs (filter "remote").
  • Remote job boards: We Work Remotely, Remote OK, and company career pages.
  • Direct outreach: offer help to local businesses, agencies and entrepreneurs on LinkedIn.
  • Referrals from satisfied clients (the best source long-term).

See the Freelancing platforms and Profile and proposals lessons for writing winning proposals.

Pricing

Start with an hourly rate or a monthly package ("20 hours per month of inbox and calendar support"). Use the rate calculator in the Pricing your work lesson, factor in internet and power costs, platform fees and taxes, and raise your rates as you gain reviews and experience.

Working professionally

  • Agree on the scope, hours, communication channel and response times in writing.
  • Use a password manager or the client's shared-access tools; never ask clients to send passwords in plain messages.
  • Keep client information confidential (and follow data protection rules).
  • Send a short weekly report: what you did, what's pending, questions.
  • Track your time (Toggl, Clockify) if billing hourly.
  • Have backup power and internet (a power bank, a mobile data bundle) for reliability.

Getting paid in Kenya

Common options include payment through the platform to your bank or M-Pesa (via supported withdrawal methods), Payoneer, PayPal (withdrawal to M-Pesa is available), and direct bank transfers. Compare fees and exchange rates, and keep records for tax purposes.

Avoid VA scams

  • No legitimate job asks you to pay to get hired or to buy "training materials" first.
  • Be careful with "clients" who want you to receive money and forward it, or to buy gift cards.
  • Keep communication and payment on the platform until trust is established.
