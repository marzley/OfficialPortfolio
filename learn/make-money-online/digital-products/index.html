Sell digital products: e-books, templates, courses and printables, from idea to M-Pesa payment

A digital product is something you create once and sell many times as a file or online access: an e-book, a CV template, an Excel budget planner, a course, a set of design assets, a revision pack. There's no stock, no delivery rider and no limit on how many copies you can sell. That is why creators love them.

The catch: making the product is the easy part. Finding buyers who trust you enough to pay is the real work. This unit walks you through the whole process, from validating an idea to getting paid by M-Pesa and card.

Who buys digital products?

People pay to save time, avoid mistakes or get a result faster:

  • Job seekers (CV templates, interview guides)
  • Small business owners (invoice templates, marketing calendars, stock trackers)
  • Students and parents (revision notes, worksheets)
  • Farmers and aspiring entrepreneurs (business plans, step-by-step guides)
  • Designers and creators (presets, templates, mockups)

Step 1: Find and validate an idea

Never spend two months building something nobody wants. Validate first:

  1. List problems you can solve. What do people ask you for help with? What have you learned the hard way?
  2. Look for demand signals: questions repeated in Facebook groups, TikTok comments, Reddit, Google "People also ask", and products already selling on Etsy, Gumroad or Selar (competition proves demand).
  3. Make a small test: post a free tip or a mini-version and see who engages.
  4. Pre-sell: "I'm making a complete Excel chama tracker. Early price KSh 300 until Friday." If people pay, build it. If nobody does, you saved weeks.

The "one painful problem" rule

"How to cook" is too broad. "30 cheap healthy lunches for campus students under KSh 150 each" is a specific problem with a clear buyer.

Step 2: Create the product

  • Outline first: list the outcome, then the steps to get there. Each step becomes a chapter, module or sheet.
  • Make it practical: checklists, examples, screenshots, worked numbers, templates the buyer fills in.
  • Design for readability: big headings, short paragraphs, consistent fonts and colours. Canva has free e-book and workbook layouts.
  • Test it: give it to 2–3 people from your audience and fix anything confusing.
  • Export properly: PDF for e-books, .xlsx with locked formula cells for spreadsheets, editable Canva template links for templates, MP4 for videos.

For a course, keep videos short (5–12 minutes), show the screen, and give a downloadable workbook. Good audio matters more than camera quality: a cheap clip-on microphone makes a huge difference.

These are rough ranges, not rules. Pricing tactics:

  • Bundles: CV template + cover letter + interview guide for less than separately.
  • Tiers: Basic (e-book), Standard (+ templates), Premium (+ a 30-minute call).
  • Launch discount with a real deadline.
  • Price by value of the result, not by page count.

Manual selling doesn't scale

Sending files by hand after confirming each M-Pesa message works for your first 20 sales, but it's slow and error-prone. Move to a platform that delivers automatically once you have regular sales.

Step 5: Deliver and protect

  • Platforms deliver a download link automatically after payment.
  • Add your name/website on every page so shared copies still advertise you.
  • Expect some piracy; don't waste energy fighting it. Focus on updates, support and community, which pirates don't get.
  • Write a clear refund policy (e.g. refunds within 7 days if the file is faulty or not as described).

Step 6: Market it

The best digital product sellers build an audience first:

  • Post free, useful content daily on one platform (TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, X or Facebook).
  • Give away a free lead magnet (a mini checklist) in exchange for an email or WhatsApp channel follow.
  • Share testimonials and results from buyers (with permission).
  • Write a sales page: problem → promise → what's inside → who it's for → price → guarantee → FAQ → buy button.
  • Run affiliate deals: let others promote your product for a commission.

A simple sales page outline:

Legal and ethical basics

  • Only sell what you created or have rights to. Reselling KNEC papers, copyrighted textbooks or "PLR" content you didn't check can get you banned or sued.
  • Don't promise income results you can't back up ("make KSh 100k in a week").
  • Keep records of sales for tax (see the taxes lesson).
Think about it: A creator spends three months recording a 40-video course on "everything about computers", launches it, and sells two copies. What went wrong, and what should they have done?Show answer

The topic was too broad, it wasn't validated, and there was no audience. They should have picked one specific problem (e.g. "Excel for office job interviews"), tested demand with free content, pre-sold to a small group, then built the course with buyers' feedback.

Summary

  • Digital products are made once and sold many times: e-books, templates, spreadsheets, courses, printables, assets.
  • Validate before building: find a specific painful problem, look for demand, pre-sell.
  • Create practical, well-designed products with free tools, and test them on real users.
  • Price by the value of the result, use bundles and tiers, and sell on Selar, Gumroad, Etsy or your own site.
  • Build an audience with free content and lead magnets; sell only what you own the rights to.
