Your 30-day action plan: from zero to your first online income, week by week

You've learned about many ways to earn online. Knowledge alone doesn't pay; action does. This final unit turns everything into a practical 30-day plan with daily tasks. It won't make you rich in a month (nobody honest can promise that), but if you follow it, by day 30 you'll have a skill direction, a portfolio, profiles, a pipeline of applications or content, and very possibly your first paid work.

What you will build in 30 days

  • A clear choice of one main earning path
  • A basic skill level and 3 portfolio samples
  • Professional profiles (LinkedIn + one platform)
  • A payment setup (M-Pesa, PayPal/Payoneer or bank)
  • 30+ applications, pitches or published pieces of content
  • A simple tracker and weekly review habit

Week 1: Choose, learn and set up (days 1–7)

DayTasks
1Write down your goal (e.g. "earn my first KSh 5,000 online by day 30"). Choose your path. Create your tracker.
2Find one free course for your skill (Ajira Digital, Google, freeCodeCamp, HubSpot, YouTube or this learning hub). Study 2 hours.
3Study 2 hours. List 10 people/companies who pay for this skill and how much (search job boards and Fiverr gigs).
4Study 2 hours. Practise: create your first rough sample.
5Set up LinkedIn properly: photo, headline, about, skills. Connect with 20 people in your field.
6Set up payments: confirm M-Pesa details; open PayPal and/or Payoneer if you'll work internationally (use your real details).
7Review: what did you learn? Adjust your study plan. Rest a bit.

Week 2: Build your portfolio (days 8–14)

DayTasks
8Study the work of top people in your field. Note what makes it good.
9Create portfolio sample 1 (a real-looking project: a blog post, a logo, a landing page, a spreadsheet, a short video).
10Get feedback from someone experienced (a community, a friend, a mentor). Improve it.
11Create sample 2.
12Create sample 3.
13Put your samples in one place: Google Drive folder, Behance, GitHub, Canva PDF or a simple website.
14Review: are your samples good enough that you'd pay for them? Fix the weakest one.

Real projects beat imaginary ones

Offer to do one small project for a local business, church, chama or friend at a low price or free in exchange for a testimonial. A real client name on your portfolio builds trust fast.

Week 3: Go to market (days 15–21)

DayTasks
15Create a profile on one platform (Upwork, Fiverr, a tutoring site or a local job board) or set up your content channel. Use the profile lesson's templates.
16Send 5 tailored applications/pitches or publish 1 piece of content.
17Send 5 more / publish 1 more. Tell your network what you offer (WhatsApp status, LinkedIn post).
18Approach 5 local businesses directly with a specific offer.
19Send 5 more / publish 1 more. Follow up on earlier messages politely.
20Improve your profile based on what you've learned (headline, gig images, pricing).
21Review: how many replies? What's working? Adjust your pitch.

Week 4: Deliver, follow up and grow (days 22–30)

DayTasks
22Keep sending 5 applications or publishing daily. Reply to every enquiry within hours.
23If you get a job: confirm scope, price, deadline and deposit in writing. Deliver early and well.
24Ask satisfied clients for a review/testimonial and referrals.
25Learn one advanced topic in your skill.
26Create sample 4 based on the type of work clients ask for most.
27Record all income and expenses in your tracker; set aside a share for tax and savings.
28Follow up with every warm lead.
29Plan your next 30 days: income goal, skills to learn, number of pitches.
30Big review: total applications, replies, clients, income. Celebrate progress.

Tracking your progress

Your numbers tell you what to fix:

MetricIf it's low...
Applications sentSend more: aim for 5+ a day
Reply rateImprove targeting and personalise your pitch
Interviews/calls → jobsImprove portfolio and how you explain your process
Repeat clientsImprove quality, communication and deadlines

Common reasons people quit (and how to push through)

  • "No one is replying." Normal at the start. Improve one thing at a time and keep volume up.
  • "I'm not good enough yet." Start with simpler jobs; you improve by doing paid work.
  • "Everyone is doing it." Be specific: a niche, a local market or a better service beats competing on everything.
  • "I spent money on a course and got nothing." Use free learning first; avoid guaranteed-income programmes.
  • "I earned, then nothing." Build a pipeline: keep pitching and creating even when busy.
Think about it: After 30 days, Wanjiru has sent 70 applications, got 6 replies, 1 small job worth KSh 3,000 and a good review. Should she switch to a new skill?Show answer

No. One job and a review is real progress. Her reply rate (about 9%) suggests her pitch is working somewhat; she should keep the same skill, improve her portfolio with the job, specialise further, and keep the volume up. Switching resets her progress to zero.

Summary

  • Choose one path for 30 days and track everything in a simple sheet.
  • Week 1: learn and set up profiles and payments. Week 2: build 3 portfolio samples. Week 3: go to market with 5 pitches or content pieces daily. Week 4: deliver, ask for reviews, follow up and plan the next month.
  • Use metrics (applications, reply rate, job rate, average per job) to know what to fix.
  • Expect slow starts; consistency and specialisation beat switching skills.
