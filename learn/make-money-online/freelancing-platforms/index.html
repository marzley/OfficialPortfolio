Freelancing platforms compared: Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer, LinkedIn and local options

Freelance marketplaces connect you with clients worldwide, handle contracts and payments, and protect both sides. Each platform works differently: on Upwork you send proposals to jobs; on Fiverr clients buy your packaged "gigs". This unit compares the main platforms, their fees and how payment works from Kenya, and gives step-by-step guidance for getting started on each.

Fees and rules change

Platform fees, connects/credits pricing and policies change. Check each platform's current fee pages and terms before relying on the numbers here.

What you will learn

  • How freelance marketplaces work
  • Upwork: proposals, connects, fees, Job Success Score
  • Fiverr: gigs, levels, fees, getting the first order
  • Freelancer.com, PeopleperHour, Guru and niche platforms
  • LinkedIn, direct clients and local work
  • Payment methods and withdrawing to Kenya
  • Platform rules that get accounts banned

How marketplaces work

  1. You create a profile (skills, portfolio, rates).
  2. Clients post jobs (you apply) or browse services (you're found).
  3. Work happens under a contract on the platform; payments are held in escrow or tracked hourly.
  4. You deliver; the client approves; funds are released (after a security period); you withdraw.
  5. Both sides leave reviews, which drive future work.

Why use platforms: clients come to you, payment protection, dispute resolution. Downsides: fees, competition, platform rules, dependency.

Upwork (upwork.com)

Model: clients post jobs; freelancers send proposals using Connects (a paid credit system; some free connects may be given).

FeatureNotes
FeesA service fee is deducted from your earnings (check Upwork's current fee structure; it has changed over time)
ContractsFixed-price (milestones in escrow) or hourly (time tracked with the desktop app)
ReputationJob Success Score (JSS), badges (Rising Talent, Top Rated)
Payment outBank transfer, Payoneer, PayPal (options vary by country)

Getting started:

  1. Complete your profile 100%: professional photo, specialised title, overview, portfolio, skills, rates, ID verification.
  2. Apply to jobs that match your specialisation; quality over quantity.
  3. Write personalised proposals (see the proposals lesson).
  4. Start with smaller jobs to earn your first reviews; deliver excellently.
  5. Keep communication on the platform.

Fiverr (fiverr.com)

Model: you create gigs (packaged services with prices: Basic, Standard, Premium); clients find and buy them.

FeatureNotes
FeesFiverr keeps a percentage of each order (commonly cited as 20%; check current terms)
LevelsNew seller → Level 1 → Level 2 → Top Rated, based on performance
RankingRelevance, conversion, ratings, response time, on-time delivery
Payment outPayoneer, bank transfer, PayPal (availability varies), after a clearance period

Getting started:

  1. Choose a specific service ("I will design a modern restaurant menu", not "I will do any design").
  2. Research competitors: titles, prices, gig images, keywords.
  3. Create a strong gig: clear title with keywords, attractive gig images/video, packages, FAQ, requirements.
  4. Price to win first orders, but don't undervalue drastically.
  5. Respond fast; deliver early; ask satisfied buyers for reviews.
  6. Promote gigs on social media and to your network.

LinkedIn and direct clients

  • LinkedIn: optimise your profile (headline with your service, About, Featured portfolio), post useful content, connect with potential clients, use the "Services" page feature, and search for "freelance" and "contract" roles.
  • Direct clients: local businesses, referrals, past employers, communities. No platform fees, but you handle contracts, invoicing and payment collection yourself (use deposits and written agreements).
  • Social media: show your work (before/after, tutorials) on TikTok, Instagram, Facebook groups.

Many successful freelancers start on platforms for reviews, then gradually add direct clients.

Local and Kenyan options

  • Ajira Digital and Jitume programmes provide training, mentorship and sometimes connections to work.
  • Kenyan businesses (SMEs, schools, churches, NGOs) hire freelancers directly: approach them with specific offers.
  • Kenyan job boards and agencies post contract and remote roles.

Getting paid from platforms

MethodHow it works for Kenya
PayoneerCommonly used with Upwork and Fiverr; withdraw to a Kenyan bank account
PayPalWithdraw to M-Pesa via the PayPal option in M-Pesa, or to a bank
Direct bank transferSome platforms pay to Kenyan banks (fees apply)
WiseFor direct clients abroad

Use accounts in your own name, matching your ID. See the "Getting paid in Kenya" lesson for step-by-step setup.

Think about it: A client on Upwork offers to pay you directly through M-Pesa to "avoid fees" before you've done any work together. What are the risks?Show answer

It breaks Upwork's terms (you could lose your account), and you lose payment protection: the client could disappear without paying, or it could be a scam. Keep the work and payments on the platform, especially with new clients.

Choosing your first platform

You are...Start with
A specialist with a clear service (design, writing, video)Fiverr (gigs) + Upwork
Good at writing proposals, want bigger projectsUpwork
A tutor/language teacherPreply/italki
An experienced developerUpwork, then selective networks (Toptal etc.)
Focused on local clientsDirect outreach + LinkedIn + WhatsApp Business

Summary

  • Marketplaces provide clients, contracts and payment protection, at the cost of fees and competition.
  • Upwork: proposals with connects, fixed-price or hourly contracts, JSS; Fiverr: packaged gigs, levels, ranking factors.
  • Other platforms: Freelancer, PeopleperHour, Guru, selective networks, niche sites; plus LinkedIn and direct clients.
  • Withdraw via Payoneer, PayPal (to M-Pesa), bank transfer or Wise, using accounts in your own name.
  • Follow platform rules: no off-platform payments with platform clients, no multiple or borrowed accounts, no fake identities.
