Starting a YouTube channel that grows

YouTube is the world's second-biggest search engine. A channel can build an audience, promote a business, and eventually earn through ads, sponsorships and your own products. It's a long game: most successful creators uploaded consistently for a year or more before it paid. Here's how to start right.

1. Pick a niche you can sustain

Choose a topic where you can make 50+ videos and that people actively search for. Good niches combine what you know, what you enjoy, and what people need:

  • Tech tutorials (Excel, phone tips, coding, KRA iTax guides)
  • Farming and agribusiness (poultry, dairy, greenhouse)
  • Cooking Kenyan meals, budget recipes
  • Education (KCSE revision, CBC projects)
  • Small business and money (M-Pesa tips, side hustles, saving)
  • Local reviews (phones under KSh 20,000, travel spots)

A clear niche helps YouTube know who to recommend your videos to. "Excel tutorials for Kenyan offices" beats "a bit of everything".

3. Plan videos people search for

  • Use YouTube's search suggestions: type "how to" + your topic and note what appears.
  • Look at the most-viewed videos in your niche: what questions do the comments ask?
  • Make videos that answer a specific question: "How to file KRA nil returns in 2026 (step by step)".

A simple video structure:

  1. Hook (first 10–15 seconds): the result or the problem. "In 5 minutes you'll file your nil returns without going to a cyber."
  2. Content: clear steps, no long intros.
  3. Call to action: subscribe, a related video, or your WhatsApp for services.

4. Titles, thumbnails and descriptions

  • Title: clear benefit plus keywords, under ~60 characters.
  • Thumbnail: a close-up face or the result, 3–5 big words, high contrast (design in Canva, 1280 × 720).
  • Description: first two lines summarise the video with keywords; add timestamps (chapters), links and contacts.
  • Tags matter much less than the title, thumbnail and how long people keep watching.

5. Consistency and analytics

  • Upload on a regular schedule you can keep (e.g. one video a week).
  • In YouTube Studio → Analytics, watch:
  • Click-through rate (CTR): are thumbnails/titles attracting clicks?
  • Average view duration / retention: where do people leave? Improve the hook and pacing.
  • Traffic sources: search, suggested videos, external (WhatsApp shares).
  • Reply to comments: it builds community and gives you new video ideas.

Rules and safety

  • Use only music and clips you have rights to (YouTube Audio Library is free).
  • Don't use misleading thumbnails or titles (clickbait loses trust and viewers).
  • Follow YouTube's community guidelines; protect your account with 2-step verification.
  • Declare sponsored content.
  • Income from YouTube is taxable: keep records.

How YouTube creators actually earn

YouTube can become a real income source, but it takes consistency, patience and serving a specific audience. Kenyan creators earn from cooking, comedy, tech reviews, education, farming, travel, faith, beauty, business tips and local news commentary. Most successful channels grow slowly at first; the creators who last treat it like a small business: planning, learning from analytics and diversifying income.

Planning content people want

  • Search-driven videos: answer questions people type into YouTube ("how to apply for a KRA PIN", "best budget phone 2026"). Use YouTube's search suggestions for ideas.
  • Series: weekly episodes viewers come back for ("Shamba Diaries week 12").
  • Trends with your angle: react to news or trends relevant to your niche, adding value.
  • Shorts: under-60-second vertical videos to reach new viewers; link to longer videos.

Keep a content calendar with titles, keywords and filming dates.

Rules that protect your channel

  • Only use music, clips and images you have rights to (YouTube Audio Library, licensed libraries); copyright strikes can remove your channel.
  • Follow community guidelines: no harmful misinformation, hate, harassment or dangerous content.
  • Be careful with reaction content: add commentary and value, and respect fair use limits.
  • Don't buy subscribers or views; fake engagement can lead to penalties.
  • Disclose paid promotions using YouTube's settings and in the video.
  • Protect your account with 2-step verification; creators are targeted by phishing ("sponsorship" emails with malicious attachments).

Getting paid in Kenya

YouTube earnings are paid through Google AdSense; you'll need to verify your identity and address and set up a payment method (such as a bank account that can receive international payments). Keep records of income for tax purposes and understand your obligations with KRA.

Practice

  1. Define your niche, audience and five video ideas based on real search suggestions.
  2. Script a 5-minute video with a strong 15-second hook.
  3. Film and edit a short video with captions using a free editor.
  4. Design three thumbnail versions and ask friends which they'd click.
  5. After publishing, check CTR and retention and write one improvement for the next video.
Think about it: A new creator uploads irregularly, covers random topics each time, and gives up after two months with 80 subscribers. What would you advise?Show answer

Choose a clear niche and audience, plan a realistic schedule (for example one video a week plus Shorts), make videos that answer specific searches, improve titles and thumbnails using analytics, and commit to at least six months to a year. Growth usually compounds slowly, and consistency with steady improvement matters more than early numbers.
