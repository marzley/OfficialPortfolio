Remote jobs from Kenya: roles, job boards, CVs, interviews, contracts and working well remotely

Freelancing means many short projects for different clients. A remote job is different: one employer, regular hours or tasks, and a monthly salary or contract fee, but you work from home or a co-working space instead of an office. Kenyans work remotely for local companies, regional firms and international companies in support, sales, tech, content, finance and operations.

Remote jobs are competitive because one posting can receive thousands of applicants from around the world. This unit shows what roles exist, where to find them, how to apply so you stand out, and how to avoid fake "remote job" scams.

Many international companies hire Kenyans as contractors or through employer-of-record services (companies like Deel or Remote that legally employ you on their behalf). Read the contract carefully either way.

Where to find remote jobs

  • LinkedIn Jobs: filter by "Remote" and location "Kenya" or "Africa"; follow companies that hire in Africa.
  • Remote job boards: We Work Remotely, Remote OK, Remotive, Working Nomads, JustRemote, FlexJobs (paid). Check whether a posting says "worldwide" or limits countries.
  • Africa-focused: companies and boards that hire across Africa; Kenyan job sites (BrighterMonday, MyJobMag, Fuzu) with remote filters.
  • Company career pages: many remote-first companies list "work from anywhere" roles.
  • Communities: Twitter/X, Slack and Discord tech communities, Ajira Digital clubs, alumni networks.
  • Referrals: most hires still come through people. Tell your network exactly what role you want.

Search smarter

Use searches like "remote" "Africa" customer support or "EMEA" remote support Kenya. "EMEA" roles (Europe, Middle East, Africa) often accept Kenyan applicants because the time zone matches Europe.

Step 1: A remote-ready CV

  • One to two pages, clean layout, no photo needed for most international roles.
  • At the top: name, role title ("Customer Support Specialist"), email, phone, LinkedIn, portfolio, "Nairobi, Kenya (EAT, UTC+3)".
  • Bullet achievements with numbers: "Resolved 60+ chats daily with 95% satisfaction" beats "Responsible for customer care".
  • Mention remote skills: Slack, Zoom, Google Workspace, Notion, Trello/Asana, Zendesk/Freshdesk, async communication.
  • Tailor keywords to each job description; many companies use applicant tracking systems (ATS) that scan for them.

Step 2: LinkedIn and portfolio

  • Professional photo, headline that states the role you want and your key skill.
  • An "About" section in first person: what you do, results, tools, availability.
  • Turn on Open to work (visible to recruiters only if you prefer).
  • Post occasionally about what you're learning or building.
  • A portfolio for creative and tech roles (website, GitHub, Behance, Google Drive folder with samples).

Step 3: Apply well

  • Apply within the first few days of a posting.
  • Write a short, specific cover letter: why this company, what you'll bring, one relevant result.
  • Complete skills tests carefully (typing tests, writing samples, coding tasks). They often decide the shortlist.
  • Keep a tracker sheet: company, role, link, date applied, status, follow-up date.

Step 4: Interviews

  • Test your setup: stable internet, charged laptop, quiet room, good light facing you, headphones with a mic.
  • Expect: a recruiter screen, a skills interview or test, a final interview, sometimes a paid trial task.
  • Prepare STAR stories (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for common questions: a difficult customer, a mistake you fixed, working without supervision.
  • Ask good questions: "How does the team communicate?", "What does success look like in the first 90 days?"

Contracts, payment and tax

  • Get a written contract before starting: role, pay, currency, payment schedule, hours, notice, equipment, confidentiality.
  • Payment methods: local bank transfer, Wise, Payoneer, Deel/Remote payouts, PayPal (see the getting-paid lesson).
  • As a contractor you must declare income to KRA yourself (see the taxes lesson).
  • A legitimate employer never asks you to pay for training, equipment, "application processing" or a work permit for a remote job.

Working well remotely

  • Over-communicate: daily updates in the team channel, say when you're online and offline.
  • Respect time zones; put your hours in your profile ("Available 9am–6pm EAT").
  • Write clearly; remote work is mostly written.
  • Deliver on time; if you'll be late, say so early.
  • Set boundaries to avoid burnout: a start time, breaks, an end time.

Fake remote job red flags

  • You must pay for training, a "starter kit", equipment or registration.
  • Interviews only on Telegram/WhatsApp chat with no video and no company email.
  • Salary far above the market for very little work ("KSh 5,000 per hour typing").
  • Requests for your M-Pesa PIN, ID photos with selfies before any contract, or bank login.
  • "Task" jobs where you deposit money to "unlock" higher-paying tasks.
Think about it: A company emails: "Congratulations! You've been hired as a remote data entry clerk at $40/hour. To begin, buy our approved laptop from our vendor and we'll reimburse you." What do you do?Show answer

This is a classic fake-cheque/equipment scam. Real employers send equipment or reimburse through payroll after proper hiring. Don't pay, verify the company and recruiter independently (official website, LinkedIn employees), and report the message.

Summary

  • Remote jobs give one employer and regular pay while you work from home; roles span support, sales, admin, content, tech, finance, AI data and education.
  • Know the difference between employee and contractor arrangements; read contracts.
  • Find jobs on LinkedIn, remote boards, Kenyan job sites, company pages and through referrals; "EMEA" roles often suit Kenya's time zone.
  • Use a tailored, results-focused CV, a strong LinkedIn, a portfolio, careful tests and STAR interview stories.
  • Prepare internet and power backup, communicate a lot, and never pay to get a job.
