Start a web design business: skills, services, pricing, finding clients and delivering projects

Many Kenyan businesses still have no website, or have an old, slow one that doesn't work on phones. Others need online shops, booking systems, M-Pesa payments, Google visibility and maintenance. A web design business can start with one laptop and grow into an agency. This unit covers everything: the skills, services to offer, tools, pricing, finding clients, the project process, contracts, hosting, maintenance income and scaling.

Build a portfolio first

  1. Build 3–5 sample sites for different industries (salon, restaurant, school, hardware shop, consultant), using realistic content.
  2. Deploy them online (free static hosting or cheap hosting) so clients can open them on their phones.
  3. Offer 1–2 discounted or free sites to real businesses (a relative's shop, your church) in exchange for a testimonial and permission to show the work.
  4. Create a simple portfolio website with your services, samples, testimonials and contacts (WhatsApp button).

Finding clients

Local

  • Walk-in visits: businesses near you (show a sample on your phone; offer a free quick audit of their Google presence).
  • Google Maps research: businesses with no website or old sites; call or WhatsApp them with a specific offer.
  • Networks: church, chama, alumni, family businesses; ask for referrals.
  • Partnerships: printers, graphic designers, accountants, business consultants who meet businesses needing websites.
  • Social media: before/after showcases, tips videos (TikTok/Instagram), WhatsApp Status.

Online

  • Upwork/Fiverr (website and WordPress jobs), LinkedIn, Facebook business groups.

A simple outreach message

Hello [Name], I came across [Business] on Google Maps; your reviews are great! I noticed you don't have a website yet, so customers searching "[service] in [town]" may not find your prices and location. I build fast, mobile-friendly websites with WhatsApp and call buttons for local businesses (example: [link]). Would you like a free 10-minute chat about what a website could do for you? [Your name], [phone]

The project process

  1. Enquiry and discovery: goals, audience, pages, features, examples they like, budget, deadline.
  2. Proposal/quote: scope, timeline, price, payment terms, what you need from them (logo, photos, text).
  3. Agreement and deposit.
  4. Content collection: often the slowest part; provide a content checklist and deadline.
  5. Design: wireframe/mockup or homepage draft for approval.
  6. Build: pages, responsive design, forms, integrations, SEO basics.
  7. Review: client feedback within the agreed revision rounds.
  8. Launch: domain/DNS, SSL, final testing (phones, forms, speed), Google Search Console, Google Business Profile link.
  9. Handover and training: how to edit content; logins; documentation.
  10. Final payment and ongoing care plan offer.

Contracts and ethics

  • Written agreement: scope, timeline, payment schedule, revisions, ownership, hosting responsibilities, support period.
  • Register domains in the client's name (with you as technical contact); give them access to hosting. Holding domains hostage damages trust and can be legally risky.
  • Licensed assets only: themes, plugins, fonts and images must be properly licensed (no nulled/pirated themes: they often contain malware).
  • Security: strong passwords, updates, backups; never store client passwords insecurely.
  • Data protection: forms collecting personal data need privacy notices and secure handling.
  • Honesty: don't promise "number one on Google".

Maintenance and growth

  • Offer care plans at launch (updates, backups, uptime monitoring, small changes, monthly report).
  • Hosting reselling (if you can provide reliable support).
  • Upsells: SEO, content writing, social media, Google Ads, M-Pesa integration, booking systems.
  • Scale: templates and checklists, then hire or partner with designers, writers and developers; register a business name or company; use project management tools.
Think about it: A client asks you to register their domain under your own name "to make things easier". Why should you register it in the client's name instead?Show answer

The domain is the client's business asset. If it's in your name and you become unavailable or the relationship ends, the client could lose access to their website and email, causing disputes. Registering in the client's name (with you as technical contact) protects them and your reputation.

Summary

  • Core skills: HTML/CSS, WordPress, hosting, design basics, SEO; add JavaScript and M-Pesa integration to grow.
  • Offer business sites, landing pages, shops, booking sites, redesigns, hosting/email setup, SEO and care plans; recurring income stabilises the business.
  • Build 3–5 samples and get testimonials before charging full prices.
  • Price with packages, clear scope and deposits; follow a structured project process.
  • Find clients locally (walk-ins, Google Maps research, referrals, partnerships) and online.
  • Use contracts, client-owned domains, licensed assets, good security and honesty; grow with care plans, upsells and partners.
