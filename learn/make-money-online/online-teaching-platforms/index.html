Online tutoring, teaching and selling courses

If you can explain something well (a language, maths, coding, music, Excel, exam preparation), people will pay you to teach them. You can tutor live, one to one, teach classes, or record a course once and sell it many times.

What makes a good online teacher

  1. Clear audio matters more than video quality (use a cheap clip-on microphone).
  2. Short lessons (5 to 15 minutes per video) with one goal each.
  3. Practice: exercises, quizzes and real examples (like this hub).
  4. A friendly intro video on your profile: who you are, who you help, how lessons work.
  5. Reviews: ask your first students to leave one.

Course idea checklist

  • Is it a skill people pay for? (Search the topic on Udemy and YouTube: lots of content = demand.)
  • Can you teach it better for a specific audience? ("Excel for Kenyan SACCO accountants", "Kiswahili for medical volunteers")
  • Can you show a result? ("Build your first website in a weekend")

Selling photos, videos and music (stock media)

If you take good photos or videos, or make music, you can license them to businesses, bloggers and video makers worldwide through stock agencies. Every download earns you a royalty. Authentic African photos and videos (everyday life, business, farming, cities, nature) are in demand because there are too few of them.

Envato (AudioJungle, VideoHive)Music, video templatesLarge creator marketplace.

Rules to know:

  • Model releases: if a person can be recognised, you need their signed permission for commercial use.
  • No logos or brands in commercial images (blur or avoid them).
  • Keywords matter: describe what's in the image (who, what, where, mood) so buyers find it.
  • Upload consistently: portfolios with hundreds of good images earn steadily; a few images earn little.
