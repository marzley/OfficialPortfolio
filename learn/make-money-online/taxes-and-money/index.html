Taxes, records and managing your money

Tax basics (Kenya)

This is general guidance, not tax advice. Rules and rates change; always confirm on kra.go.ke or with an accountant.

  • Get a KRA PIN on iTax. You need it for bank accounts, contracts and many clients.
  • Income earned online by a Kenyan resident, including from foreign clients and platforms, is generally taxable in Kenya.
  • Individuals file an annual income tax return (deadline 30 June for the previous year).
  • Small businesses with turnover within the set range can use Turnover Tax (a percentage of gross sales, filed monthly); check the current rate and thresholds.
  • Keep your personal and business money separate (a separate M-Pesa till/business account makes records easy).

Keep simple records

A spreadsheet with: date, client/customer, description, amount, fees, amount received, payment method, and your expenses (internet, equipment, software). Keep invoices and receipts. Good records also show you which work actually pays.

Managing irregular income

Online income goes up and down. Protect yourself:

  1. Pay yourself a fixed "salary" from your business account each month.
  2. Emergency fund: save 3–6 months of expenses.
  3. Set aside tax from every payment (e.g. put 10–15% in a separate account).
  4. Save and invest regularly: money market funds (licensed by CMA), SACCOs, Treasury bills/bonds (via CBK DhowCSD), and later stocks. Understand risk; avoid anything promising guaranteed high returns.
  5. Invest in yourself: a better laptop, internet, courses. The best return early on.

The 50/30/20 rule (a starting point)

  • 50% needs (rent, food, transport, internet)
  • 30% wants
  • 20% savings and investments

Adjust it for your situation, but always pay your savings first.
