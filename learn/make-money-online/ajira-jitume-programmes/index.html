Ajira Digital, Jitume and other free government programmes

Kenya's government runs free programmes to help young people learn digital skills and find online work. Many people don't know they exist, or think they're only for university graduates. They're not: if you're willing to learn, they can give you training, equipment access, internet, mentorship and connections.

Programme names, partners and application steps change. Use the official websites and your county's ICT office for current details.

Ajira Digital Programme

Ajira means "employment" in Kiswahili. The Ajira Digital Programme is a government initiative (run by the Ministry responsible for ICT with partners) to get young Kenyans working online.

What it offers:

  • Free training in online work: freelancing basics, digital marketing, transcription, data entry, content writing, virtual assistance and more.
  • Ajira Clubs in universities and TVETs, and Ajira centres in some counties and youth empowerment centres.
  • Mentorship from people already earning online.
  • Information about real platforms and scams, so beginners don't lose money.

How to join: register on the Ajira website, look for a centre or club near you (universities, TVETs, county youth centres), and attend the induction and training.

Jitume

Jitume ("push yourself") is a government digital skills and jobs programme run through the Konza Technopolis Development Authority with the ICT Authority and partners. It sets up Jitume Digital Labs: computer labs with internet in TVETs, universities, constituencies and other public institutions, where young people get:

  • Free access to computers and internet to learn and work online.
  • Training in digital work: the online earning categories covered in this subject (AI data annotation, transcription and writing, social media management, content creation, graphic design, programming and web development, e-commerce, micro-tasks, virtual assistance and freelance marketplaces).
  • Facilitators who guide learners from basic computer skills to their first online jobs.
  • Links to partner companies offering digital work.

Our founder Kelvin Wanyoike is a Jitume facilitator: read Jitume facilitator in Kenya for how a Jitume lab session works and how to make the most of it.

How to get the most from a Jitume lab

  1. Find the nearest lab: ask your nearest TVET, university or constituency office, or follow Jitume's official social media pages.
  2. Come with a goal: pick one category from the map of online work.
  3. Use the time to build: your profile, samples, portfolio and applications, not just browsing.
  4. Keep learning between sessions on your phone with free resources like this hub.
  5. Ask the facilitator to review your profiles and proposals.

Getting the most from free government programmes

Government digital skills programmes and public digital hubs can provide free or low-cost training, internet access and computers, which removes major barriers for young people starting online work. The difference between those who benefit and those who don't is usually what happens after the training: building a portfolio, applying consistently and continuing to learn. Always confirm current details, eligibility and locations on official websites and social media accounts, since programmes change over time.

Before you join a programme

QuestionWhy it matters
What exactly will I learn, and how long does it take?Choose a track that matches your goals
Is it free, and are there any genuine costs?Avoid fake "registration fee" scams using programme names
Where is the nearest hub or training centre?Access to computers and internet
Will I get a certificate, mentorship or job linkages?Helps credibility and next steps
What equipment do I need (laptop, phone, internet)?Plan ahead

Register only through official websites, verified social media accounts or physical centres. Programme names are often used by scammers asking for "processing fees".

Making the most of digital hubs

  • Book or plan your sessions; use the time for focused learning and applications.
  • Bring your own flash drive or use cloud storage for your work; never leave accounts logged in.
  • Join or form study groups with other users; share opportunities and feedback.
  • Ask hub staff about events, mentorship and partner opportunities.
  • Respect equipment and other users; hubs serve many people.

Turning certificates into income

A certificate alone rarely wins clients. Combine it with:

  1. Portfolio: 3–5 samples showing what you can do (designs, articles, websites, reports).
  2. Profiles: LinkedIn and relevant freelance platforms with a clear headline and samples.
  3. Local clients: offer services to businesses near you (social media, websites, data entry).
  4. Consistency: apply to opportunities every week; track applications.
  5. Testimonials: ask satisfied clients for short reviews.

Staying safe and ethical

  • Never pay "agents" to get you accounts, jobs or programme slots.
  • Don't buy, sell or rent freelance accounts; it violates platform rules and leads to bans.
  • Be honest about your skills and experience.
  • Protect your accounts with strong passwords and 2-step verification.
  • Respect clients' confidentiality and deadlines.

  2. Choose one skill track and write why it suits you.
  3. Create three portfolio samples for that skill.
  4. Get or confirm your KRA PIN and learn your basic tax obligations from official sources.
  5. Write your 90-day plan with weekly goals.
Think about it: Someone on WhatsApp offers to "fast-track" your enrolment in a free government digital skills programme for KSh 1,500. What should you do?Show answer

Don't pay. Genuine free programmes don't require payments to agents for enrolment. Verify through the programme's official website, verified social media or a physical centre, report the message if possible, and warn others in the group.
