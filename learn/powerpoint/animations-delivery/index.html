Animations, transitions and presenting with confidence: rehearsal, delivery and handling questions

Great slides are only half of a great presentation; the other half is you. This unit covers using animations and transitions purposefully (not decoratively), the presenter tools in PowerPoint and Google Slides, and the delivery skills that turn nervous speakers into confident presenters: preparation, rehearsal, voice, body language, handling questions, presenting online, and dealing with technical problems in Kenyan venues.

What you will learn

  • When animations help and when they distract
  • Applying entrance, emphasis and exit animations; timing and the Animation Pane
  • Morph and simple transitions
  • Presenter View, speaker notes and keyboard controls
  • Rehearsing and timing your talk
  • Voice, body language and managing nerves
  • Handling questions
  • Presenting online (Zoom/Meet/Teams) and handling technical problems

Part 1: Animations and transitions

Use motion with a purpose

Good usesBad uses
Revealing points one at a time to keep focusEvery bullet flying and spinning
Showing a process step by stepRandom effects on every slide
Highlighting a key numberSlow animations that waste time
Showing change (before/after)Sound effects (rarely appropriate)

Rule: if an animation doesn't help understanding, remove it.

Transitions (between slides)

Transitions tab → choose an effect (Fade or Push are subtle and professional) → set Duration → Apply To All for consistency. Avoid flashy ones (Vortex, Origami) in professional settings.

Animations (objects on a slide)

Animations tab:

TypeUseExamples
EntranceMake something appearAppear, Fade, Wipe
EmphasisDraw attentionPulse, Grow/Shrink, Colour change
ExitMake something disappearFade out
Motion pathsMove along a pathShow movement on a map

Steps:

  1. Select the object (text box, picture, chart).
  2. Choose an animation; set Effect Options (e.g. text "By Paragraph" to reveal one bullet per click; charts "By Series/Category").
  3. Start: On Click, With Previous, After Previous.
  4. Duration (0.3–0.5 s is usually enough) and Delay.
  5. Open the Animation Pane to see, reorder and fine-tune all animations.
  6. Animation Painter copies an animation to other objects.

Morph (PowerPoint for Microsoft 365)

Morph creates smooth movement between two slides: duplicate a slide, move/resize/recolour objects on the second, then apply the Morph transition to it. Great for zooming into a map, building a diagram or showing growth. Use it subtly.

Google Slides: Insert → Animation (or View → Motion) for object animations and transitions (fewer options).

Presenter View and speaker notes

  • Add speaker notes below each slide (View → Notes): key points, stories, numbers to mention.
  • Presenter View (Slide Show → Use Presenter View, with a projector or second screen) shows you the current slide, the next slide, your notes and a timer, while the audience sees only the slide.
  • Don't read notes word for word; use them as prompts.

Saving and sharing

  • Keep fonts consistent (embed fonts: File → Options → Save → Embed fonts) if presenting from another computer.
  • Bring a PDF copy as backup (it opens on any computer, without animations).
  • Save to cloud and a flash disk; email it to yourself.

Part 3: Delivering with confidence

Preparation

  1. Know your material: you should be able to explain each slide without reading it.
  2. Know your audience and time limit.
  3. Prepare the opening and closing carefully: they're remembered most.

Strong openings: a question ("How many of you paid for something with M-Pesa today?"), a surprising fact, a short story, or the problem you'll solve.

Strong closings: summarise 3 key points, then a clear call to action ("Approve the pilot budget", "Register by Friday", "Visit our stand").

Rehearse

  • Rehearse out loud, standing, at least 3 times.
  • Use Slide Show → Rehearse Timings (PowerPoint) to see how long each slide takes; aim to finish slightly under time.
  • Record yourself (phone or Record in PowerPoint) and watch: notice filler words ("eh", "like", "so"), speed and gestures.
  • Practise transitions between slides ("Now that we've seen the problem, let's look at the solution").

Voice

  • Pace: slow down; nerves make people rush. Pause after important points.
  • Volume: speak to the back row; use a microphone in big rooms (test it first).
  • Variety: change tone and speed to keep attention; avoid monotone.
  • Clarity: simple words; explain jargon; for mixed audiences, consider key terms in Kiswahili if appropriate.

Body language

  • Stand tall, feet steady; avoid swaying or pacing constantly.
  • Eye contact: look at different sections of the room, not the screen or the floor.
  • Gestures: natural and open; avoid crossing arms or hands in pockets.
  • Face the audience: glance at the screen briefly; use Presenter View or a laptop in front of you.
  • Smile where appropriate; it relaxes you and the audience.

Managing nerves

Nervousness is normal; even experienced speakers feel it.

  • Prepare and rehearse (the best cure).
  • Arrive early; check equipment; meet some audience members.
  • Breathe slowly (in for 4, hold, out for 6) before starting.
  • Focus on helping the audience, not on being judged.
  • Have water nearby; a pause is fine if you lose your place.
Think about it: You're halfway through presenting your final-year project and your mind goes blank. What can you do?Show answer

Pause and take a breath (a short silence feels longer to you than to the audience), glance at your speaker notes or the slide title to recover the thread, summarise what you've covered so far ("So far we've seen..."), and continue to the next point. Rehearsing transitions in advance makes this much easier.

Handling questions

  1. Listen fully without interrupting.
  2. Repeat or rephrase the question so everyone hears it (and you confirm you understood).
  3. Answer briefly and directly; link back to your main message.
  4. If you don't know: say so honestly and offer to follow up ("Good question; I'll confirm the exact figure and email it to you").
  5. Hostile questions: stay calm and polite; acknowledge the concern; answer the facts.
  6. Prepare likely questions in advance, with backup slides after your conclusion (jump to them by typing the slide number).

Presenting online (Zoom, Google Meet, Teams)

  • Test your internet, camera, microphone and screen sharing beforehand; close other apps and notifications.
  • Camera at eye level, face lit from the front (a window in front of you, not behind).
  • Share only the presentation window (not your whole screen with private notifications).
  • Use Presenter View on one screen if possible, or keep notes on paper.
  • Engage: ask questions, use the chat or polls, pause for questions more often (online attention drops faster).
  • Bandwidth: if the connection is weak, turn off your video while sharing slides, or send the PDF in advance.
  • Record (with permission) and share the recording/slides afterwards.

Handling technical problems (common in real venues)

ProblemPrevention / fix
Projector doesn't connectBring adapters (HDMI, USB-C to HDMI, HDMI to VGA); arrive early to test
Laptop power cut / no socketFully charge; bring a charger and extension; have the PDF on a flash disk
Venue computer lacks fonts or PowerPoint versionUse standard fonts, embed fonts, bring a PDF
Videos don't playEmbed videos in the file (not online links) or download them; test sound
Internet failsDon't depend on live websites; use screenshots
Clicker failsUse the keyboard; ask a colleague to advance slides

If everything fails, you should be able to give the presentation without slides: another reason to know your material.

Practice tasks

  1. Animate a 4-bullet slide to reveal one bullet per click, and a chart to appear by category.
  2. Try the Morph transition to "zoom" into part of a picture.
  3. Write speaker notes for every slide and rehearse with Rehearse Timings.
  4. Record a 3-minute presentation and note three things to improve.
  5. Prepare five likely questions with answers and two backup slides.

Summary

  • Use animations and transitions to aid understanding (reveal, emphasise, show change); keep them subtle and consistent.
  • Know the presenter controls (F5, Shift+F5, B, Esc), use speaker notes and Presenter View, and keep a PDF backup.
  • Prepare a strong opening and closing; rehearse aloud with timings and recordings.
  • Speak slowly and clearly, make eye contact, use open body language, and manage nerves with preparation and breathing.
  • Handle questions by listening, repeating, answering briefly and being honest.
  • Prepare for online presenting and technical problems (adapters, power, PDF, embedded videos).
