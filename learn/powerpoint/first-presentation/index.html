Build your first presentation step by step

Presentations are everywhere: class projects, church and chama meetings, business pitches, job interviews and trainings. This lesson walks you through making a clear presentation from scratch in PowerPoint (Google Slides and Canva work almost the same way).

Step 1: plan before you open PowerPoint

Answer three questions on paper:

  1. Who is the audience? (Classmates, investors, farmers, a panel)
  2. What is the ONE message they must remember?
  3. What should they do after? (Vote yes, buy, register, approve the budget)

Then outline 5–10 slides. A reliable structure:

SlideContent
1Title, your name, date
2The problem or question
3–6Your main points (one per slide), with evidence
7Summary / recommendation
8Call to action, contacts, "Questions?"

Step 2: create the file and choose a design

  1. Open PowerPoint → Blank Presentation (or a simple template).
  2. Design tab: pick a clean theme and a colour Variant. Avoid busy backgrounds.
  3. Design → Slide Size: Widescreen (16:9) for projectors and screens.

Step 3: add slides with the right layout

Home → New Slide (Ctrl + M) and choose a layout: Title Slide, Title and Content, Two Content, Section Header, Comparison, Picture with Caption. Layouts keep fonts and positions consistent. Type into the placeholders rather than adding random text boxes.

Step 4: write less, show more

  • One idea per slide. The title should state the point: "Sales grew 30% after we added M-Pesa" beats "Sales".
  • Short bullet points, about 3–5 per slide, a few words each. You speak the details.
  • Big text: titles 32–44 pt, body at least 24 pt.
  • Pictures, icons, charts instead of paragraphs (Insert → Pictures / Icons / Chart).

Step 5: add images properly

  • Insert → Pictures (from your device or stock images).
  • Resize from the corners (dragging the sides stretches faces).
  • Picture Format → Crop to remove edges; Crop to Shape for circles.
  • Use Designer (Home → Designer) for automatic layout ideas.

Step 6: speaker notes

Type what you'll say in the Notes pane under each slide. In Presenter View (Slide Show → Use Presenter View) you see your notes and the next slide while the audience sees only the slide.

Step 7: check and practise

  • Slide Sorter view to check the order.
  • Review → Spelling (F7).
  • Rehearse out loud with Slide Show → Rehearse Timings: a common guide is about 1–2 minutes per slide.
  • Save as .pptx, and also export a PDF copy (File → Export) in case the venue computer can't open your file or fonts.

Who presents, and why it matters

Presentations are everywhere: students defending projects, job candidates presenting in interviews, entrepreneurs pitching to investors or county funds, NGOs reporting to donors, sales teams showing products, teachers delivering lessons, church and chama meetings. A clear presentation helps people understand and remember your message and makes you look prepared and confident. A crowded, hard-to-read one loses the audience in minutes, however good the content.

Structure that works for most presentations

PartSlidesContent
Opening1 to 2Title, who you are, a hook (a question, a striking fact, a short story)
Problem / context1 to 2Why this matters to the audience
Main points3 to 5One idea per slide, with evidence (data, examples, images)
Solution / recommendation1 to 2What you propose and how
Summary1The 3 key points
Call to action and questions1What you want them to do; contact details

A common guideline is roughly one slide per minute or two, but it depends on the content. A 10-minute pitch might have 10 to 12 slides.

The 10-20-30 idea for pitches

A popular guideline for business pitches: about 10 slides, 20 minutes, and fonts no smaller than 30 points. It forces you to focus on the essentials: problem, solution, market, business model, competition, team, finances, and the ask.

Writing slide titles that tell the story

Use titles that state the point, not just the topic:

Topic title (weak)Message title (strong)
SalesSales grew 40% after we added M-Pesa payments
Survey results7 in 10 customers want delivery within 24 hours
BudgetWe need KSh 500,000 to open the second branch

If someone reads only your titles, they should understand your whole argument.

Design basics: consistency and contrast

  • Use the Slide Master (View → Slide Master) to set fonts, colours and logo once for all slides.
  • Pick 2 or 3 colours (for example your brand colours) and use them consistently.
  • Use one font family (or two at most), at least 24 points for body text and 36+ for titles.
  • Dark text on a light background (or the opposite) with strong contrast; avoid text on busy photos.
  • Leave white space; don't fill every corner.
  • Align objects using Smart Guides and Arrange → Align.

Designer (Design → Designer) in Microsoft 365 suggests professional layouts automatically when you add images.

Images that help

  • Use high-quality images that support the point, not generic clip art.
  • Your own photos (your product, your shop, your community project) are more convincing than stock photos.
  • Free sources with clear licences: Unsplash, Pexels; PowerPoint's Insert → Stock Images (Microsoft 365).
  • Crop to remove distractions; compress pictures (Picture Format → Compress Pictures) to keep the file small.
  • Add alt text (right-click → View Alt Text) for accessibility.

Transitions and animations: less is more

UseAvoid
A simple Fade or Morph transition between slidesSpinning, bouncing, random transitions
Revealing bullet points one by one when you discuss eachAnimating every object
Morph to zoom into a part of a diagramSound effects on transitions

Morph (Transitions → Morph) smoothly moves objects between two slides: duplicate a slide, move or resize objects on the second slide, and apply Morph for a professional effect.

Presenting with confidence

  1. Practise aloud at least three times; time yourself.
  2. Know your first two minutes by heart: nerves are strongest at the start.
  3. Use Presenter View (Slide Show → Use Presenter View) to see notes, the next slide and a timer.
  4. Face the audience, not the screen; make eye contact across the room.
  5. Speak slowly and pause after key points.
  6. Prepare answers to likely questions; it's fine to say "I'll find out and get back to you".
  7. Have a backup: the file on a flash drive and in email/cloud, plus a PDF version.

Presentation day checklist

  • Arrive early; test the projector, HDMI/VGA adapter, clicker and sound.
  • Embed fonts (File → Options → Save → Embed fonts) if using a different computer.
  • Open the file from the local disk, not directly from a flash drive, to avoid freezing.
  • Turn off notifications (Focus Assist / Do Not Disturb) and close other apps.
  • Have water and printed notes in case of technical failure.

Online presentations (Zoom, Meet, Teams)

  • Share only the PowerPoint window, not your whole screen, to avoid showing private notifications.
  • Use a good microphone or earphones with a mic; sound quality matters more than video.
  • Look at the camera when making key points.
  • Check your background and lighting (light in front of you, not behind).
  • Pause regularly for questions in chat.

Practice

  1. Plan a 5-minute presentation on a topic you know, with message titles for each slide.
  2. Set up a Slide Master with your colours, font and a logo.
  3. Create two slides and use Morph to animate a change between them.
  4. Rehearse with Presenter View and the timer, then record yourself (Slide Show → Record) and watch it back.
  5. Present to a friend and ask for one thing to keep and one to improve.
Think about it: A student's slides are full paragraphs copied from the report, in 14-point font, and they read every word aloud. How would you improve the presentation?Show answer

Cut text to short phrases or a single message per slide in at least 24-point font, move the detail to speaker notes or a handout, add visuals (charts, images, diagrams) that support each point, and practise speaking naturally to the audience using notes in Presenter View instead of reading the slides.
